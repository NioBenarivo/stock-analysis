**The Salim family's integrated palm oil and cooking-oil company**: 280,975 ha of nucleus plantations (oil palm, rubber, sugar cane, timber, cocoa, tea), 27 palm oil mills, 5 CPO refineries and the Bimoli/Palmia consumer brands — plus a 59.51% controlling stake in the separately-listed PT PP London Sumatra Indonesia Tbk (LSIP). Listed IDX June 2011. Market cap ~Rp8.9–9.3 trillion (August 2026, price ~Rp580–600, 15.50bn shares). FY2025 sales Rp21.06tn, attributable profit Rp2.07tn (EPS Rp133), net gearing 0.04x.

**Period covered:** FY2018–FY2025 headline series + 1H2026; audited detail FY2022–FY2025. **Sources in hand:** Annual Reports 2023/2024/2025 (each with full audited FS), results highlights FY2019–FY2025 + 1H2026, public expose decks 2024/2025/2026, web research. Full source table at the end.

## THE ONE THING TO UNDERSTAND FIRST

**The consolidated P&L you read is not the business SIMP's shareholder owns.** Three layers of distortion sit between the reported numbers and the economics:

1. **71% of reported revenue is the wrong 29% of the profit.** The Edible Oils & Fats (EOF) division — Bimoli cooking oil, Palmia margarine, industrial oil sold to Indofood — is 71% of external sales but ran a **4.7% operating margin in FY2025** (Rp702bn profit on Rp15.0tn division revenue). The money is made upstream: the Plantations division earned Rp3.36tn at a 23.2% margin. SIMP's topline is a distribution business; its bottom line is a plantation business.

2. **Over half of that plantation profit — and roughly half of SIMP's entire market value — is LSIP, of which SIMP owns only 59.51%.** LSIP contributed ≈Rp1.12tn of SIMP's Rp2.07tn FY2025 attributable profit [calculated: NCI profit Rp764.4bn ÷ 40.49% × 59.51%]. At market, SIMP's LSIP stake is worth ≈Rp4.6–4.9tn against SIMP's own ~Rp9tn market cap. Meanwhile the group's cash pile (Rp8.35tn at Dec-25, Rp9.01tn at Jun-26) sits substantially **inside LSIP** (LSIP's own cash ≈Rp7.8tn per market data — approximate, web-sourced), while essentially all of the group's Rp9.5tn interest-bearing debt sits at the SIMP parent level. The parent borrows short-term at 5.7–8.0% rather than upstream LSIP's cash, because every rupiah dividended out of LSIP leaks 40.49% to LSIP's minorities. SIMP is simultaneously net-cash on paper and structurally levered in fact.

3. **The whole stack is itself a minority position inside a deeper pyramid.** Anthoni Salim → First Pacific (HK) → 50.07% of Indofood (INDF) → IndoAgri (SGX, ~74–86% held, see §1.2) → 73.46% of SIMP (+6.68% held directly by INDF). IndoAgri itself trades at a ~54% discount to NAV (analyst estimate, Mar 2026). Discounts stack: the market prices LSIP below its cash, SIMP barely above its LSIP stake, and IndoAgri barely above its SIMP stake.

And hanging over FY2025 is the single largest item in the filing set: on **1 December 2025 the Ministry of Forestry imposed a Rp2,337,480m (Rp2.34tn) forestry administrative charge on the Group** — equal to **113% of FY2025 attributable profit** [calculated] — under the Satgas PKH forest-area enforcement programme. SIMP paid it in full into a state escrow on 30 December 2025, filed an objection, and **booked the entire amount as an asset** ("Other Non-Current Assets", Note 14), not an expense. If the objection fails, FY2025's record profit was materially overstated. Evidence in §2.3, §3.7.

Points worth holding onto:

- **Two Salim vehicles hold direct SIMP stakes** (IndoAgri 73.46% + INDF 6.68% = 80.14% combined control). The 6.68% INDF direct stake means INDF captures a slice of SIMP economics without sharing it with IndoAgri's own minorities.
- **The INDF stake in IndoAgri is creeping up**: 72.94% → 73.67% → 74.35% effective across the three expose decks (Dec-23/24/25), driven by rising direct purchases (12.98% → 13.71% → 14.39%). A March 2026 analyst note (FPA Financial) cites INDF's "total interest" in IndoAgri at **85.90%** and Anthoni Salim's at 86.04% as of Dec 31, 2025 — the gap to SIMP's own 74.35% figure likely reflects SGX deemed-interest conventions vs. effective economic interest; flagged, not reconciled. Either way, IndoAgri's free float is thin and shrinking.
- **IndoAgri delisting is a live, dormant catalyst.** INDF's April 2019 offer at S$0.28 (revised S$0.3275) failed to reach the 90% compulsory-acquisition threshold. IndoAgri remains SGX-listed at S$0.395 (Mar 2026); the same analyst pegs a minimum credible privatization price at S$0.525. Any renewed attempt would put a value marker on the whole stack, SIMP included.
- SIMP's FS name INDF and IndoAgri as "penultimate parents" and **First Pacific as ultimate parent**; the AR front section adds that Anthoni Salim holds the interest in First Pacific. No individual is named UBO in the FS itself.
- Shareholder mix (Dec-25): foreign institutions 75.50% of shares held, local institutions 11.78%, local individuals 12.70% — 16,253 holders total, up from 9,659 at end-2023 (the Lo Kheng Hong effect drawing retail).

Readings:

- **Roughly 40–49% of consolidated revenue, every year, is sold inside the Salim ecosystem** — Indofood's factories (ICBP, Indolakto), Indofood's distributor (Indomarco Adi Prima), and the Salim retail chains (Indomaret, Indogrosir, Superindo). The EOF division is, to a first approximation, the captive fats supplier of the Salim consumer complex.
- **The FY2025 "improvement" to 39.5% is arithmetic, not structural**: related sales *grew* Rp432bn; the ratio fell only because total revenue jumped 32%. Absolute RPT sales hit a record Rp8.31tn.
- **The SRIT collapse is the interesting move**: Rp1.51tn (9.4% of revenue) in 2024 → Rp79.9bn in 2025. SRIT is the group's China-side related trader; its disappearance matches the China geographic line (Rp1.57tn → Rp202bn) and the export share collapse (13.9% → 5.4% of sales). SIMP's entire export/trading channel through its affiliate was switched off in one year — consistent with export levies (12.5% + US$178/t duty) and B40/B50 domestic pull making exports uneconomic. The residual company is ~95% domestic.

### The related-party bank

Cash parked at **PT Bank Ina Perdana Tbk** (Salim-affiliated bank): Rp694bn (2022) → Rp1,216bn (2023) → Rp1,006bn (2024) → **Rp1,898bn (2025, 4.6% of total assets)**, including Rp703bn of time deposits. Bank Ina supplied 18.7–29.2% of the Group's finance income across the window, and also issued the bank guarantee SIMP posted in its KPPU antitrust appeal. A fifth of the group's cash earns its interest inside the family's bank.

A related-party transaction that took eight years to not happen.

Given IndoAgri also controls other planters (via SIMP it doesn't), the feedstock side of the RPT picture is simply not visible. No supplier exceeded 10% of revenue, per the note.

# 02 — MODEL

## 2.1 Product

SIMP sells four things: **crude palm oil and palm kernel products** (upstream), **branded and industrial cooking oil, margarine and shortening** (downstream — Bimoli, Bimoli Spesial, Happy; Palmia, Royal Palmia, Amanda; industrial brands Delima, Malinda, Simas), **sugar** (two mills), and a tail of **rubber, oil palm seeds, timber, cocoa and tea**. The business model: grow FFB on ~237k ha of nucleus oil palm, mill it in 27 mills (7.2mt FFB capacity), pipe most of the CPO into its own 5 refineries (1.9mt capacity after the Tanjung Priok line-3 completion), and sell the refined products overwhelmingly into the domestic market — about half of it to Salim-affiliated buyers (§1.4).

### Revenue by product, FY2025 (external, post-elimination; anchor: revenue Rp21,056,904m)

| Product | Rp mn | % of revenue | FY2024 | % |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Edible oils & fats (cooking oil, margarine, shortening) | 14,981,075 | 71.1% | 12,339,389 | 77.3% |
| Palm kernel & related (PK, PKO, PKE) | 2,089,798 | 9.9% | 1,235,791 | 7.7% |
| CPO (sold externally) | 1,827,575 | 8.7% | 506,780 | 3.2% |

The external CPO line more than tripled in FY2025 — with export share *falling*, this is CPO sold domestically outside the group (B40/B50-driven domestic demand absorbing spot CPO at good prices). Internally, the Plantations division transferred **Rp8.38tn of product to the EOF division** (inter-segment sales, eliminated) — the group's single largest "customer" is itself. 78% of the EOF division's CPO feedstock came from own plantations in 2024 (77% in 2023; FY2025 figure not disclosed — with external FFB purchases up 14%, integration likely slipped).

Geographic split FY2025: **domestic 94.6%** / export 5.4% (was 86.1/13.9 in FY2024; ~14% in 2023, ~17% in 2022). China went from Rp1.57tn to Rp202bn in one year. New/remaining export markets are small: Singapore Rp386bn, India Rp99bn (new), Nigeria, Timor Leste, Philippines.

## 2.5 Distribution — how the product is sold

### Customer concentration

- **ICBP: 19.6% of consolidated revenue (FY2025), 21.9% (FY2024)** — the only disclosed >10% customer, a common-control affiliate, buying industrial cooking oil and fats for the Indofood noodle/food complex. Payment terms 1–35 days.
- The Salim retail/distribution ring (Indomarco Adi Prima, Indomaret, Indogrosir, Superindo) takes a further ~Rp3.3tn (FY2025).
- Consumer sales run through **Indofood's distribution network** (explicit in the AR) — SIMP does not own its route to market; it rents its ecosystem from its parent group at every step: distribution (Indofood), packaging (ICBP), logistics/vehicles (Indomobil), sites (Aston Inti Makmur), banking (Bank Ina), insurance (ACA).
- Export channel: dismantled in 2025 (5.4% of sales; SRIT wound down, §1.4).

### Pricing mechanism

Upstream realisations track the KPB/domestic tender price (company discloses only benchmarks: domestic CPO avg Rp13,190/kg 2024, **Rp14,234/kg 2025, +8%**; CIF Rotterdam US$1,113/t 2024, US$1,283/t 2025). ASP achieved is **not disclosed** — a persistent gap in SIMP's reporting (peers publish it). Downstream, Bimoli is a price-taker to policy as much as to CPO: HET caps on subsidised channels, DMO obligations (the source of the export-quota income), and competitive pressure from Wilmar (Sania/Fortune) and Sinar Mas (Filma). The EOF margin series (6.2% → 5.9% → 5.5% → 4.7% of division revenue, 2022–25) says SIMP absorbs CPO cost inflation rather than passing it fully through.

## 2.6 Key variables — what actually moves the model

**1. The CPO price (domestic KPB / global).** Plantations division revenue Rp14.46tn on ~733kt CPO + 166kt PK plus sugar/rubber/others. Sensitivity: ±Rp1,000/kg on CPO ≈ ±Rp733bn revenue, and — because nucleus cash costs are largely fixed — most of the nucleus-volume share (~562kt [calculated: 2,714 × 20.7%]) drops through to operating profit: ≈Rp560bn per Rp1,000/kg, or ~14% of FY2025 group operating profit. The FY2024 (+17% KPB → plantations op profit +116%) and FY2025 legs show the gearing both ways.

**2. Nucleus FFB volume — the decaying annuity.** −2%/yr trend (2,714kt in 2025; −4% in 1H26). Every lost nucleus tonne is replaced either by external FFB at market price (margin ≈ milling spread only) or not at all. This variable is structural, worsening, and management guides growth on it anyway ("volume growth from maturing trees" — with 6.7% of the estate immature, there is very little maturing).

**3. External FFB/CPO purchase price and share.** 27% of FFB and ~22% of refinery feedstock now bought in. Raw materials 46.5% of COGS. A price rally is partially self-defeating (§2.4); a price fall cushions less than peers because nucleus volume is low-yield.

**4. The EOF spread (Bimoli price − CPO cost − policy burden).** 71% of revenue earning 4.7–6.2% margin. Policy-dependent (HET/DMO/MinyaKita); the KPPU episode shows the state actively polices this margin. Every 1pp on EOF margin ≈ Rp150bn operating profit [calculated on Rp15.0tn division revenue].

**5. Biodiesel policy — B40 (Jan 2025) → B50 (Jul 2026).** The domestic CPO price bid: GAPKI projects biodiesel absorbing ~14.6mt of CPO in 2026 (vs 12.7mt under B40) against production growth \<2% — structurally supportive of variable #1, and the main reason FY2025/1H26 prices held. SIMP has no biodiesel plant; it benefits only through price.

**6. The Rp2.34tn forestry charge resolution (binary).** Full recovery, full loss, or loss-plus-hectares. Swings equity by up to 8.8% and could remove producing area (§3.7).

**7. LSIP's dividend policy and the 40.49% leak.** SIMP's own liquidity depends on what LSIP upstreams: LSIP dividends paid to its NCI were Rp107.7–185.6bn/yr in the window, i.e. LSIP distributes little and hoards cash — which keeps the parent borrowing (§3.1).

**8. Rupiah** — now minor on revenue (95% domestic) but still prices imported fertiliser/fuel inside cost lines; no hedging policy (stated).

## 2.7 Demand

Indonesian CPO demand is being re-plumbed by the state. Domestic consumption now takes ~60% of production [calculated from GAPKI 2026 projections: ~14.6mt biodiesel + ~11mt food/oleochemicals of ~51.6mt output], led by the biodiesel mandate's step-ups (B30 2020 → B35 Feb 2023 → B40 Jan 2025 → **B50 July 2026, full rollout by Oct 2026**). Food demand (cooking oil — SIMP's downstream market) grows with population and GDP at low single digits, but its *price* is politically capped through HET/MinyaKita mechanisms whenever it threatens headline inflation. Export demand (India, China, EU) is the residual — squeezed by levies (12.5% PE + US$178/t BK as of mid-2026) and EUDR friction in Europe. GAPKI projects **2026 exports declining** as B50 pulls supply inward; early-2026 exports actually jumped ~26% (front-running the mandate) before the July cutover. For SIMP specifically, demand risk is not volume — ICBP and the domestic market absorb everything — it is **the policy-set price** of what it sells.

## 2.8 Supply

Indonesia produced ~50–53mt of CPO in 2023–2025 (GAPKI's own figures conflict: 52.76mt for 2024 in one release, ~48mt implied in another — flagged, not reconciled; 2025 ≈ 51.6mt, +7.5%). Growth is ending: GAPKI guides **\<2% production growth in 2026** on El Niño, aging national tree stock and slow replanting — the whole country has SIMP's problem in milder form. The moratorium on new forest conversion, plus Satgas PKH actively clawing back planted forest-area land (688k–890k ha "recovered" sector-wide by mid-2026), caps area growth. Malaysian supply is similarly constrained (Aug 2026 Bursa CPO at RM4,562–4,724/t, four-month highs). Structural undersupply of vegetable oil vs. biodiesel-inflated demand is the bull case for the price variable; the bear case is soybean-oil oversupply from South America, which capped CPO through 2023–24 and can again.

## 3.7 Scenario: the Rp2.34tn forestry charge

The one open item that can move book value double-digits. Arithmetic per scenario, on FY2025 bases (attributable profit Rp2,068bn; attributable equity Rp20,444bn):

**A. Objection succeeds — full refund.** Escrow returns as cash; the asset was real. P&L impact nil (it was never expensed); ~Rp2.3tn cash arrives at whichever entities paid it. Book value unchanged, quality of FY2025 earnings retroactively validated. Probability: history is not kind — AALI's Rp571bn analogue (this series' AALI teardown) was expensed, not capitalised; sector-wide, Satgas PKH has *kept* Rp11.4tn. SIMP's own auditor let the asset stand, which says the objection has some legal basis (likely procedural/tariff grounds), not that recovery is probable in substance.

**B. Objection fails — charge expensed.** Write-off Rp2,337bn pre-tax. Attributable impact depends on the undisclosed entity split: if borne pro-rata to planted area, roughly 60% parent-side / 40% LSIP-side [assumption], attributable hit ≈ Rp1,402bn + 59.51% × Rp935bn ≈ **Rp1.96tn ≈ 9.6% of attributable equity, ≈ one full year of earnings** [calculated]. Restated FY2025 attributable profit would be ~Rp0.5tn and the 4.5x P/E becomes ~18x on that year.

**C. Charge stands AND land is lost.** The charge is for occupying designated forest area; Satgas PKH's toolkit includes *penguasaan kembali* (state repossession). If the underlying hectares (undisclosed — at the very rough Rp25–75mn/ha range implied by reported sector charges, Rp2.34tn ↔ ~30–90k ha [calculated, weak assumption]) are producing estates, SIMP loses both the money and ~10%+ of nucleus output, permanently. This is the scenario the disclosure's silence about hectares makes impossible to price — which is itself the finding.

Watch: Satgas PKH decision letters (any quarter now — objection filed Dec 2025), LSIP's own FS for its share, and whether the FY2026 interim statements quietly move the asset.

# SUMMARY VERDICT

**What it is:** the Salim family's palm oil estate-and-cooking-oil complex — an aging, shrinking, low-yield plantation base (12.3t FFB/ha, 6.7% immature, area −6% since 2018) welded to a captive 5%-margin branded-fats business that exists substantially to supply the family's food companies (40–49% of revenue to related parties, ICBP alone ~20%), inside a four-deck listed pyramid (First Pacific → INDF → IndoAgri → SIMP → LSIP) where every deck trades at a discount to the one below.

**What it earns:** through the cycle, ~4% ROE — losses at the 2018–19 trough, records in 2024–25 only because Indonesian biodiesel policy re-priced CPO. The FY2025 record (EPS Rp133, +33%) is real cash but flattered: it books a Rp2.34tn state fine as an asset, and 1H2026 core profit is already negative yoy. The genuinely excellent numbers — 0.04x gearing, Rp8.3tn cash, Rp5.6tn EBITDA — describe a group whose cash sits 59.51%-owned at LSIP while its debt sits 100%-owned at the parent.

**What would have to be true for it to work as an investment:** (a) CPO prices hold at B50-era levels long enough for the ~20% payout on rising EPS to compound (the Rp26 dividend = 4.3% yield, growing); (b) the forestry objection succeeds or costs only money, not hectares; and (c) at some point the controller acts on the structure — IndoAgri squeeze-out, SIMP-LSIP simplification, or a payout step-change — converting one of the three stacked discounts into cash. (a) is plausible near-term; (b) is genuinely open; (c) has been available and declined for 15 years.

**What breaks it:** a CPO down-cycle finds a company with half its peers' yield per hectare, a heavier bought-fruit bill, a politically capped downstream, and (ex-LSIP cash) a levered balance sheet — the 2019 loss-maker is still in there under the price tailwind. The estate math is unforgiving: at ~0.7% replanting, nucleus volume declines ~2%+ per year indefinitely, and every point of decline converts fixed-cost leverage into reverse.

**Position:** SIMP at 4.5x earnings and 0.45x book is cheap the way pyramid stubs are always cheap — correctly, for the base case. It is not a bet on palm oil; it is a bet on the Salim family's capital-allocation calendar, with a 4.3% coupon while waiting and a binary Rp2tn fine attached. Within this universe, the operationally superior ways to own the CPO price remain the young-estate names; the interesting trade *inside* the Salim stack is LSIP (the cash and the trees) rather than SIMP (the debt and the brands) — unless and until the controller moves, in which case SIMP is the lever. As a business teardown: a formerly great estate being run for cash, quietly, patiently, and not for you. *(Not investment advice; figures as extracted and calculated above.)*
