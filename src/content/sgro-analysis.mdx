**Business:** Oil palm grower and miller — 8 CPO mills (510 tph), ~127,000 planted ha (nucleus + plasma) in South Sumatra and West/Central Kalimantan, plus one of Indonesia's leading private oil-palm seed producers ("DxP Sriwijaya"). Until November 2025 also sago (Riau) and rubber (West Kalimantan) — both divested.
**Listed:** IDX: SGRO, since 2007. HQ Palembang, South Sumatra.
**Share price:** Rp4,760 (19 Aug 2026); 1,818,622,000 shares; market cap ≈ Rp8.66tn [calculated — see §3.6 for a conflicting third-party figure]. Free float ~1.3% after the March 2026 mandatory tender offer.
**Period covered:** FY2019–FY2025 audited (FY2019–2020 from comparative columns only), quarterly to 1H26.
**Sources in hand:** five annual reports (FY2021–FY2025, each with audited FS), June 2025 bond/sukuk prospectus, three public-expose decks (2023–2025), five monthly newsletters (Oct 2022 – Jul 2026), plus web research dated 20 Aug 2026. Full source table at the end.

## THE ONE THING TO UNDERSTAND FIRST

**The company this report describes no longer exists in the form the five annual reports describe it. In nineteen months — August 2024 to March 2026 — SGRO was sold twice, stripped to a pure-play palm company, fined Rp700bn for occupying forest land, drained of an 80%-payout dividend by its departing owner, and then 98.7% absorbed by a Korean conglomerate, leaving 1.3% of the shares in public hands.**

The sequence, in order:

1. **27 Aug 2024** — the Sampoerna family's Singapore vehicle (Sampoerna Agri Resources Pte Ltd, controller since 2006) transfers its entire 69.68% to **Twinwood Family Holdings SFO Limited**, a Dubai family office. Same ultimate owner — Putera Sampoerna — no price disclosed. This looks cosmetic; in hindsight it was staging.
2. **5 Jun 2025** — a **Rp600.14bn dividend** (Rp330/share) is paid from FY2024 profit: an **80.2% payout [calculated] against a stated 5–30% policy**, the largest in the company's history, months before control changes hands.
3. **9 May 2025** — an EGMS converts the listco's charter to a **pure holding company** and approves transferring its directly-held plantations, mills and biogas plants to subsidiaries. The corporate box is being tidied for sale.
4. **19–24 Nov 2025** — in one week: **AGPA Pte Ltd, a 100% subsidiary of POSCO International (Korea), buys Twinwood's 65.72% for ≈Rp9.44tn (Rp7,903/share)**; and the loss-making sago (PT NSP, Rp316bn) and rubber (PT HKI, Rp1.57tn) businesses are sold **back to Sampoerna-family entities** as common-control transactions, with the combined **Rp283bn loss booked straight to equity (APIC), never touching the P&L**.
5. **~12 Nov 2025** — days before completion, the group books a **Rp700.5bn administrative fine** and surrenders **2,986 ha to the Satgas PKH forest-area task force**. This single item is why FY2025 profit halved. Three smaller PKH fines land on subsidiaries the same week.
6. **13 Jan 2026** — the EGMS renames the company **PT Prime Agri Resources Tbk**; the Sampoerna-era President Commissioner resigns and a POSCO nominee takes the seat.
7. **3 Mar 2026** — the mandatory tender offer closes: ~599.7m shares tendered at Rp7,903, AGPA reaches **98.695%**, free float falls to **~1.3%**. OJK rules require the float restored to **≥20% by March 2028**.

Why this reframes everything: the FY2021–FY2024 record — the Sampoerna era — is evidence about the *assets*, not about the *company you can buy today*. The go-forward entity is a Korean-owned, nearly-private, palm-only estate business with net debt near zero (cash Rp2.9tn at end-2025 [§3.1]), no stated dividend policy under the new owner, a fresh state-imposed land loss, and a hard March 2028 deadline that forces either a large secondary distribution, a rights issue, or a delisting decision. The 1.3% float means the quoted price is close to meaningless as a valuation signal — the only prices that carry information are the Rp7,903 POSCO paid and whatever the 2028 re-float clears at.

Evidence: ownership chain §1.2, divestments and the APIC hit §1.4, dividend history §1.5, Satgas PKH §2.3 and §12-timeline items throughout, float and valuation §3.6, the 2028 scenario §3.7.

Three observations. First, the August 2024 SAR→Twinwood hop changed nothing economically (same UBO, disclosed as an "affiliated party" transfer, no price) — its function appears to have been to reposition the stake in a Dubai family-office vehicle ahead of a sale process. Second, the stake fell 69.68% → 65.72% between the transfer and the sale — this is dilution of the *percentage* by the Apr 2025 buyback authorization arithmetic and small sell-downs (Twinwood sold 3.96% by March 2025, per the FY2024 subsequent events), i.e. the family was already trimming. Third, the basis-of-consolidation note names the post-deal ultimate parent as **POSCO Holdings Inc.** — a listed Korean chaebol holding company, a categorically different kind of controller from a family office: SGRO/PAR is now a line item in a corporate strategy (POSCO International already ran PT Bio Inti Agrindo, a Papua palm estate with 210kt/y of milling, since 2011, and a 500kt/y Balikpapan refinery), not a family's estate.

**What POSCO paid [calculated]:** Rp7,903/share × 1,195.2m shares ≈ Rp9.45tn for 65.72%, implying Rp14.37tn for 100% of the equity — ≈ 2.5× end-2025 book equity attributable to owners (Rp5.04tn), ≈ 19.2× FY2024 parent profit, ≈ Rp113m per planted-palm hectare on ~127k ha (§3.6). The MTO added ≈Rp4.74tn, so POSCO's total cash outlay approaches **Rp14.2tn** for 98.7%.

## 1.4 Related party transactions

**The disclosed RPT note is almost empty — and that is the finding.** Across FY2020–FY2025 the group reports **zero related-party sales and zero related-party purchases of goods** in every single year (0% of revenue, 0% of COGS [calculated each year]). Disclosed RPT was confined to: deposits at PT Bank Sahabat Sampoerna (peak Rp258.6bn, 2.5% of assets, 2022), an office lease from PT Sampoerna Land (≈Rp6.8–7.1bn/yr of ROU depreciation, ~2.5–3.5% of G&A), and donations accrued to Yayasan Putera Sampoerna (Rp16bn approved 2022, Rp20bn 2023, Rp9.5bn 2024). For a family-group listco this is an unusually clean trading record — SGRO genuinely did not buy from or sell to the Sampoerna ecosystem.

But the clean note is not the whole related-party picture. The economically significant related-party activity happened **outside the RPT note**, in three places:

**1. The November 2025 common-control divestments — priced by the seller's family, loss to equity.** NSP (sago) went to PT Sampoerna Strategic for **Rp316bn** (fairness opinion Rp303bn); HKI (80%, rubber) went to Sampoerna Agri Resources Pte Ltd for **Rp1.57tn** (opinion Rp1.52tn). Because both buyers were entities under common control (the outgoing controller), PSAK common-control accounting routed the combined **Rp283.1bn loss directly through APIC — additional paid-in capital nearly halved, Rp583.8bn → Rp300.7bn — with no P&L recognition**. The transactions happened *the same week* control passed to POSCO: the family took back the two failed diversifications at prices a hair above the fairness floor, and the loss on a decade of sago/rubber capital allocation was permanently buried in an equity line most readers never open. The split of the Rp283bn equity loss is itself informative [calculated from the FY2025 notes]: **NSP accounts for Rp258.1bn of it — sold at ~45% below carrying value** (Rp316bn price vs ≈Rp574bn implied carrying) — while HKI, whose Rp2.43tn of rubber bearer plants had been a **Key Audit Matter for impairment two years running** (FY2023, FY2024), crystallized only a Rp25.0bn loss against carrying value. Either the rubber carrying value held up better than the auditor's flag suggested, or the family paid close to book for an asset the market had been told to doubt — with a related buyer, the price proves less than it would at arm's length.

**2. The Aquarius loan, disclosed as a contingency rather than an RPT.** HKI's 20% shareholder, Aquarius Plantations Pte Ltd (Singapore), lent HKI Rp470.7bn (2021) → Rp511.1bn (2022) → Rp345.1bn (2023) → Rp145.9bn (2024), at 0.25% over the group's cost of debt, capitalized annually — and per the FY2025 notes, **"repayable only from environmental credits or court-ordered bankruptcy."** A shareholder loan on those terms is quasi-equity wearing debt clothing, and it sat in the contingencies note, not the related-party note, every year. Aquarius also injected Rp1.0tn of capital into HKI in 2023 while HKI was losing Rp232.5bn. Who is behind Aquarius is never disclosed in any filing in hand.

**3. The Danantara placement — a related-party-flavoured transaction with the state.** On 21 Oct 2025, weeks before completion, the group placed **Rp500bn into PT Danantara Investment Management (Persero) bonds at a 2% fixed coupon** (5–7yr tenors) — so far below market that the group booked an immediate **Rp90.6bn day-one loss** (Level 3 fair value). A 2% coupon from a brand-new state investment vehicle, purchased by a company that had just been fined Rp700bn by a state task force and was awaiting regulatory clearance of a foreign takeover, invites an obvious reading: this is not treasury management, it is a goodwill payment to the state, priced at a Rp90.6bn cost, booked without ever being called that. No filing connects the two events explicitly; the timing does.

The FY2025 notes formally de-recognize the Sampoerna ecosystem: Sampoerna Land, Bank Sahabat Sampoerna and the Yayasan are footnoted as "no longer related parties" from the AGPA acquisition date, and the Sampoerna Land office lease was terminated early, effective 30 Jun 2026. The clean break is complete — the new related-party universe will be POSCO entities, and the thing to watch in FY2026 is whether CPO offtake starts flowing to POSCO's Balikpapan refinery at disclosed-or-undisclosed prices (§3.8).

NCI), and the balance into the cash pile — which is precisely what made the company saleable. What the retained cash did *not* buy: any new planted hectares of consequence (planted area *shrank* every year, §2.2), any downstream processing, any acquisition. The Sampoerna era ended with the estate smaller, older, and debt-free — harvested, in every sense, for sale.

Cash-conversion check (the checklist test): cumulative FY2021–FY2025 net profit ≈ Rp3.31tn vs cumulative CFO ≈ Rp7.69tn [calculated]. Operating cash covered reported earnings 2.3× — earnings here are real and conservatively stated (heavy D&A from bearer plants). The quality problem at SGRO was never cash conversion; it was what the cash was, and wasn't, used for.

# 02 — MODEL

## 2.1 Product

What is actually sold, in one paragraph: SGRO plants oil palm on its own (nucleus) estates, buys additional fresh fruit bunches (FFB) from tied smallholders (plasma) and independent third parties, mills all of it into **crude palm oil (CPO)** and **palm kernel (PK)**, and sells both — almost entirely to domestic Indonesian refiners, at prices set off the CPO benchmark. A small but strategically distinctive sideline sells **germinated oil-palm seeds** (the "DxP Sriwijaya" varieties bred by subsidiary Binasawit Makmur) to other planters. Until Nov 2025 there were also sago starch (Riau) and natural rubber (West Kalimantan) segments; both were divested at a loss to the departing controller (§1.4).

### Revenue by product, FY2025 (continuing operations, total Rp6,446,671m)

| Product | Revenue | % of total | Volume | ASP |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CPO | ≈Rp4.9tn | ≈76% | 339,735 t sold | Rp14,513/kg (+9.3%) |
| Palm kernel | ≈Rp1.1tn | ≈17% | 87,270 t sold | Rp12,610/kg (+50%) |
| Germinated seeds | Rp205bn | ≈3% | 24.0m seeds | Rp8,538/seed (flat) |
| FFB/other | balance | ≈3% | — | — |

Three product-level observations:

**CPO is the model; everything else is texture.** Palm products have been 91–97% of revenue every year since 2020. The company describes itself, accurately, as a **price taker** — it publishes no revenue or profit guidance, only operational targets.

**The PK windfall is doing quiet heavy lifting.** PK's ASP rose 59% in 2024 and another 50% in 2025 (Rp12,610/kg), with the PK/CPO price ratio at 65–70% versus a normal 45–50% band — a lauric-oils squeeze (cosmetics, oleochemicals, tight PKO supply). PK revenue grew from ~Rp0.5tn (2023) to ~Rp1.1tn (2025) on essentially flat volume [calculated]. This is a commodity-ratio anomaly, not a business improvement: if the ratio mean-reverts to ~47%, PK revenue at 2025 CPO prices would fall by roughly Rp0.45tn [calculated: 87kt × (12,610 − 14,513×0.47)] — most of a year's net profit.

**The seed business is small revenue, outsized signal.** 22–27m germinated seeds/yr at a stable Rp8,400–8,550/seed makes SGRO one of Indonesia's leading private seed producers (the newsletters claim "second largest domestic producer"; the company's own pubex market-share charts show it **fourth**, behind PPKS, Asian Agri and others — an unreconciled self-contradiction, flagged in the data pack). Seeds carry ~75% gross margin [calculated: revenue Rp205bn vs seed COGS Rp50.5bn, FY2025] and tie SGRO into the national replanting program (PSR) as a supplier — a genuine moat-let, and plausibly part of what POSCO was buying: proprietary genetics plus a breeding R&D operation, not just hectares.

The flow exposes the model's central tension: **only two-thirds of the fruit is the company's own**. The mills need ~1.7mn tonnes to run economically; the nucleus estates at their age produce ~1.1mn; the balance must be bought at market-linked prices from plasma farmers (whose price is set by a government TBS formula) and independents (who shop around). When CPO prices rise, purchased-fruit cost rises nearly in lockstep — the buy-in share caps margin torque in both directions (§2.4, §2.6).

## 2.4 Process — how the product is made and what it costs

### The physical process

Oil palm yields FFB year-round from year ~4; bunches must reach a mill within ~24–48h of harvest or free fatty acids destroy quality. Mills sterilize, thresh, press and clarify: 1 tonne of FFB → ~208 kg CPO (OER 20.8%) + ~51 kg PK + biomass/effluent (the methane from which two Sumatra PLTBg plants burn for 4 MW of power). Estate work (upkeep, fertilizer, harvest labour) is the own-crop cost; the mill is a throughput machine whose economics depend on running full.

Mill utilisation [calculated, rough]: 1,714,124 t ÷ (510 tph × 20 h/day × 300 days ≈ 3.06mt effective capacity) ≈ **56%** — the mills have structural headroom of ~1.3mt of FFB. That spare capacity is why buying external fruit is rational even at thin margins: bought FFB that covers more than its marginal cost defrays mill fixed costs. It is also why FFB supply, not mill capacity, is the binding constraint on output.

## 2.6 Key variables — what actually moves the model

Ranked by profit sensitivity; anchors restated from §2.4 (FY2025: revenue Rp6,447bn, COGS Rp4,311bn, operating profit Rp961bn incl. the Rp700bn fine).

**1. CPO price (≈76% of revenue).** ±Rp1,000/kg on 339,735 t sold = ±Rp340bn revenue [calculated]. Pass-through claws back roughly the purchased-FFB share — bought fruit reprices with CPO via the government TBS formula — so the operating-profit sensitivity is closer to ±Rp200–230bn [calculated: own-fruit share ~66% of throughput retains the full move; bought fruit retains only the mill margin], still ±20% of a normal year's operating profit per Rp1,000. Range experienced FY2019–FY2025: Rp6,844 → Rp14,513/kg.

**2. Own-FFB volume (the aging-estate variable).** Nucleus FFB fell 1,242,866 t (2022) → 1,131,320 t (2025), −9% [calculated], on a 15-yr-old estate with 25% of mature area past 20 years. Each 100kt of lost own fruit ≈ 21kt of CPO whose margin reverts from grower margin to (at best) bought-in mill margin — order Rp150–200bn of operating profit [calculated at FY2025 prices]. This is the variable replanting exists to fix, and the reason the 5,000 ha/yr FY2026 target matters more than any single price print.

**3. Purchased-FFB share (44–53% of fruit input).** Governs margin torque in both directions (§2.3). Watch it drift up as nucleus ages — every point of share shifts ~Rp40bn of cost from controlled to market-priced [calculated on Rp4.0tn combined FFB pool].

**4. PK/CPO price ratio (65–70% vs normal 45–50%).** Mean reversion costs ~Rp0.4–0.5tn of revenue (§2.1) at constant volumes — the single largest downside asymmetry in the current P&L.

**5. OER (20.5–21.9% over the period).** ±0.5pt on 1.71mt of FFB = ±8.6kt CPO ≈ ±Rp125bn revenue [calculated at Rp14,513/kg]. Driven by fruit ripeness/logistics and external-fruit quality — external FFB is systematically lower-OER, so the buy-in share drags this too.

**6. State/land liabilities (the new wildcard).** FY2024–FY2025 delivered Rp319bn (NSP settlement paid), Rp86bn (strict-liability settlement), Rp700.5bn (PKH fine) — Rp1.1tn of state-driven charges in 24 months after three years of "no sanctions" boilerplate. The exposure that remains unquantified: the never-provisioned Rp753.7bn NSP restoration order (status after the 2024 settlement is undisclosed — though the NSP entity itself now belongs to the Sampoerna family again), any further PKH tranches, and the KSO's revenue-share terms with Agrinas (undisclosed).

**7. B50 biodiesel / export levy (the policy overlay).** Mandatory B50 from 1 Jul 2026 and a 12.5% export levy (from Mar 2026) both push domestic CPO demand and hold the domestic price relationship to the Malaysian benchmark — supportive for a 99.7%-domestic seller, embedded in variable #1 rather than separate.

## 2.7 Demand

SGRO's demand is Indonesian domestic demand, one step removed: its refiner customers serve cooking oil, oleochemicals and — increasingly — the biodiesel program. The structural story of 2024–2026 is the state absorbing ever more of the domestic crop: **B40 rolled out through 2025 (~15.6mn kl allocation), and mandatory B50 launched 1 Jul 2026** (full national coverage targeted 1 Oct 2026, per the July 2026 program status), each 10-point step adding roughly 3–4mn t of CPO demand [industry estimates via web research]. The export levy was raised to 12.5% (Mar 2026) partly to fund the subsidy gap. For a domestic price-taker this is nearly all tailwind: it firms the domestic clearing price and guarantees a buyer of last resort. The offsetting global facts: vegetable-oil substitution (soy/sunflower price competition set the 2Q26 CPO recovery, per the company's own newsletter), Indian/Pakistani import demand elasticity, and an August 2026 Kemendag reference price cut below US$1,000/t on "weak global demand" — the first sub-$1,000 print in the research arc, a reminder that the levy-funded mandate sits on top of a soft global market, not instead of it.

## 2.8 Supply

Indonesian CPO production (GAPKI-reported) has been recovering — the web pass found conflicting GAPKI narratives (Dec 2025: 2026 exports "pressured by B50"; Apr 2026 coverage: exports up sharply) that could not be resolved into a clean tonnage table [open item]. The supply facts that matter for SGRO specifically: the **national estate is old** (Indonesia's replanting program chronically behind target — which is SGRO's seed-business demand), **new planting is effectively frozen** (moratoria + PKH enforcement — 5.89m ha reclaimed nationally, 2.37m ha handed to state-owned Agrinas), and **El Niño 2023-24 cut everyone's 2024 crop** (SGRO's FFB −9%, an industry-wide print). Supply discipline is externally imposed and durable: barring policy reversal, Indonesian CPO supply growth for the rest of the decade is a yield/replanting story, not an area story — mildly bullish for incumbents holding titled, planted land, and a persistent bid under the seed franchise.

## 3.7 Scenario: the March 2028 re-float

The OJK/IDX rule behind the MTO: a controller that crosses into a public company via tender must restore free float to **≥20% within two years** — here, by **March 2028** — or face delisting-track consequences. AGPA holds 98.695%. The arithmetic of the three paths:

**Path A — sell-down to 20% float.** AGPA must place ≈18.7% of the company ≈ **340m shares [calculated: 20% − 1.3% of 1,818.6m]**. At the MTO's Rp7,903 that is a ≈Rp2.7tn placement; at the current quote, ≈Rp1.6tn. A placement of that size into a name with this float history clears only with a story — which gives POSCO a direct financial incentive to spend 2026–2027 making the numbers presentable: replanting ramped, fine behind it, B50 tailwind, possibly refinery integration announced. *If this path is taken, the two published years before the placement will be the shop-window years.* The re-float price, not today's quote, is where minority value gets set.

**Path B — go private.** Buy the remaining ~1.3% (≈Rp190bn at the MTO price [calculated]) and delist. Cheap and clean; the cost is losing the IDX listing POSCO just implicitly paid for (local-currency funding, the idA bond program shelf, Indonesian-flag optics for a company operating state-adjacent land under an Agrinas KSO). The bond program's disclosure obligations persist either way.

**Path C — re-IPO-scale equity raise.** A primary rights/new-share issue that dilutes AGPA below 80% while funding expansion (replanting, refinery integration capex). Same float outcome as A but the cash goes into the company. This is the path a growth-minded chaebol would choose, and the Rp600–800bn FY2026 capex guidance plus net-cash balance sheet is consistent with building toward it.

No filing states which path is intended [open item]. What the November 2025 – March 2026 behaviour reveals: POSCO paid the same price to minorities as to the family (no squeeze at a discount), immediately deleveraged, and kept the listing — behaviour most consistent with A or C, not B.

## 3.8 Roadmap and what to watch

In rough order of information value:

1. **The first related-party note of the POSCO era (FY2026 FS).** Watch for CPO offtake to POSCO International / Balikpapan refinery entities, and its pricing basis. SGRO historically had zero related-party sales; if that flips, the entire §2.5 customer structure — and the minority-shareholder value question — changes shape. This is the single most important disclosure to read in the FY2026 report.
2. **Re-float mechanics before March 2028** (§3.7) — any placement announcement, or a delisting proposal.
3. **Replanting execution:** ~5,000 ha/yr promised for FY2026 vs ~3.9k ha/yr age-neutral requirement [calculated §2.3]. The nucleus estate's 15-year age and 25% >20yr band is the core operating problem; POSCO's capex guidance (Rp600–800bn) says they intend to fix it.
4. **Satgas PKH follow-through:** the Rp700bn provision is an *estimate pending the Forestry Minister's final determination*; the KSO revenue-share terms with Agrinas are undisclosed; and further PKH tranches on the remaining landbank are possible (5.89m ha reclaimed nationally). Also note the Rp753.7bn NSP restoration order — never provisioned — left the group *with the divested NSP entity*: the sale to the family took the group's largest unbooked contingency with it.
5. **PK/CPO ratio mean reversion** (§2.1) — the quiet Rp0.4–0.5tn revenue risk.
6. **B50 execution** — the 1 Oct 2026 full-coverage milestone and the December 2026 program review; plus the export-levy/reference-price mix (Aug 2026 reference already below US$1,000).
7. **Dividend policy under POSCO** — none stated since the takeover; the old 5–30% policy was honoured only in the breach (§1.5).
8. **The Danantara bonds** — whether the Rp500bn placement stays a one-off or becomes a pattern of state-directed balance-sheet use.
9. **Seed franchise under POSCO** — DxP Sriwijaya volumes (26.7m seeds 2025, record) and whether POSCO invests in or starves the R&D program; national PSR replanting funding is the demand driver.

# SUMMARY VERDICT

**What this business is:** a mid-scale, mid-quality South Sumatra/Kalimantan palm estate — 127k planted ha, two-thirds self-supplied, 15-year-old nucleus trees, mid-table yields, eight under-utilised mills — with one genuinely differentiated asset (the DxP Sriwijaya seed franchise) and, since November 2025, a new identity as POSCO International's Indonesian palm platform. The Sampoerna era ran it as a cash harvest: deleverage, under-replant, pay escalating dividends, absorb the accumulated environmental liabilities in two final years of fines, hand back the failed diversifications at family prices, and sell at a strategic premium. It was a well-executed exit — for the seller.

**What it earns through the cycle:** operating profit Rp0.9–1.7tn across the FY2021–FY2025 CPO range, parent profit Rp360bn–1.05tn, FCF consistently above earnings (Rp0.8–1.6tn) — with the caveat that part of that FCF was the estate aging in place. ROE clears an Indonesian cost of equity only in the top half of the CPO cycle. Underlying FY2025 — ex-fine — was the best year in the record (op profit ≈Rp1.66tn [calculated]), which is worth holding onto: the operating business POSCO bought is in good shape, at a good point in the policy cycle (B50), with a net-cash balance sheet.

**What would have to be true for the equity to work from here** (for a minority holder, which is now an almost theoretical category): (1) POSCO chooses the re-float path and spends 2026–27 dressing the shop window — replanting visibly ramped, no new state charges, possibly refinery integration announced; (2) the re-float clears somewhere between the current Rp4,760 and the Rp7,903 control price; (3) related-party offtake to POSCO's refinery, if it starts, is priced and disclosed honestly. Under those assumptions the rump equity has an anchored upside marker ~65% above the current quote [calculated]. The bonds are the simpler instrument: idA affirmed, net cash, POSCO parentage, early repayments already happening — the credit has never been better in the company's history.

**What would break it:** a delisting decision at an opportunistic price (the 1.3% float has no defence); a second Satgas PKH tranche on the remaining landbank; undisclosed transfer pricing into the POSCO system hollowing out the listco's P&L before the re-float; PK ratio mean reversion plus a CPO downturn compressing the model back to its 2023 shape (Rp450bn profit) just as the placement window opens; and — the tail risk this history earns — the pattern of liabilities surfacing only at ownership transitions repeating itself, in either direction.

**Position:** as an operating business, better than its reputation and better than its reported FY2025 — the fine obscured a record underlying year, and the policy backdrop (B50, frozen national supply) favours titled incumbents. As a listed equity, it is not currently investable in any conventional sense: 1.3% float, no dividend policy, controlled by a strategic owner whose interests will be expressed through channels (offtake, capex, the re-float structure) that minorities can watch but not influence. The March 2028 deadline is the whole investment case: everything between now and then is either shop-window dressing for a placement — in which case the rump is cheap — or preparation for going private — in which case the rump is stranded. Watch item #1 in §3.8 (the FY2026 related-party note) is the earliest reliable tell of which.
