## THE ONE THING TO UNDERSTAND FIRST

**The company this report covers ceased to exist in May 2025.** For 32 years ANJT was the
Tahija family's agribusiness — the listed rump of Julius Tahija's Austindo group, run by his
sons George and Sjakon, marketed as Indonesia's most conscientious RSPO planter. On 7 May
2025, First Resources Limited (Singapore-listed, controlled by the **Fangiono family**)
bought 91.17% from the Tahijas — press figure ~US$329m, never disclosed in ANJ's own filings
— then took the stake to **95.92%** through a mandatory tender offer completed 3 October
2025. Free float is 4.08% and shrinking.

Everything else in the FY2025 filing set is the new owner cleaning house, and the speed is
the story:

- **The books were restated.** EY (replacing the KPMG-network firm that had audited the
  Tahija-era accounts) found FY2024 fixed assets overstated by **US$103.25m** and equity by
  **US$103.59m** — a functional-currency remeasurement error plus Papua impairments that
  were never booked. FY2023 equity was cut US$91.67m. The prior auditor had issued unmodified
  opinions on both years. (§3.2)
- **Papua was given away.** The two West Papua plantation companies (PMP, PPM) — US$72.08m
  of net assets, ~91,210 ha of concession — were sold on 1 October 2025 for a combined,
  literal **US$24,316** to a buyer whose ultimate ownership nobody has been able to
  establish. Loss booked: US$72.06m. (§1.3)
- **Sago and edamame were shut, not sold.** ANJAP, GMIT and ANJB discontinued 31 Dec 2025;
  GSB (South Sumatra) sold two months after year-end. The diversification story ANJ told
  for a decade is over. (§1.3)
- **The customer base was swapped in a single quarter.** Related-party revenue went from
  **zero (FY2024) to 57.5% of sales (FY2025)**; one First Resources affiliate, PT Adhitya
  Serayakorita, now takes 55% of revenue with no disclosed pricing basis. The old
  Wilmar-linked buyers vanished from the >10% list. (§1.4, §2.5)
- **The balance sheet was re-plumbed into the parent.** US$131m of OCBC NISP/SMBC/CIMB
  Niaga term debt was repaid in May 2025 and replaced by a single BRI overdraft at 5.25%
  that is part of a **First Resources Group cash-pooling arrangement**. (§3.1)

So the analytical question has changed shape. Underneath the one-offs, FY2025 was
operationally the *best* year in the covered window — gross margin 35.3%, EBITDA US$68.3m,
net debt/equity 0.19×, and Q1 2026 net profit up 189% — but a minority shareholder no longer
owns a claim on an independent business. They own 4.08% of a captive First Resources
subsidiary that sells through the parent's trading arm at undisclosed prices, banks through
the parent's cash pool, pays no dividend, and whose remaining float is one corporate action
away from a squeeze-out. The minorities who declined the Rp2,365/share tender [calculated,
§3.6] have since watched the price fall to Rp1,450.

**NEW IN v2 — what the original filings add.** With the FY2021–FY2024 annual reports in
hand, the restatement is no longer one event; it is a habit. Every single year in the
covered window was rewritten by a later filing: FY2020's profit (+US$2.2m) became a loss
(−US$1.2m) under a standard change; FY2021 was cut US$39.7m → 36.6m; FY2023 was published
three times with three different answers — **+US$1.9m as filed, +US$4.4m after the FY2024
filing's own restatement, −US$93.7m after the FY2025 restatement**; end-2023 equity was
rewritten from US$425.3m to US$391.9m to US$300.3m across three consecutive filings, a
cumulative −US$125m re-answer to the same question [calculated, §3.2]. And the FY2023
auditor's report shows this was not an oversight: impairment indicators on the Papua assets
were *identified, tested, and dismissed* — in the Key Audit Matters, by name — two years
before EY concluded a US$97.76m impairment had been missed. The Tahija-era books were clean
on related parties and honest about operations, but wrong — repeatedly, materially,
audited-and-signed wrong — about what Papua was worth.

# 01 — MANAGEMENT

## 1.1 History and milestones

The listed entity is the agribusiness residue of one of Indonesia's oldest pribumi
conglomerates. Julius Tahija (1916–2002) — decorated soldier, Caltex Pacific chairman,
co-founder of Bank Niaga (1955) — built the Austindo/Indrapura group into Indonesia's
9th-largest conglomerate by 1994 (~US$2.9bn assets, per Forbes/Wikipedia; web-sourced). His
sons George Santosa Tahija and Sjakon George Tahija inherited control. (An "ANZ Panin"
banking link could not be verified and is likely a mistaken premise — flagged by the web
research.)

### The Papua divestment — the strangest transaction in this year's IDX plantation sector

On 1 October 2025 — the same week the MTO closed — ANJT and ANJA sold 100% of PMP and PPM
to **PT Harmoni Agri Mandiri (HAM)**, described only as "a third party":

| | Shares sold | Price (full US$) |
|---|---|---|
| PMP — Company 34% + ANJA 66% | 1,952,371,000 | 4,325 + 11,992 = **16,317** |
| PPM — Company 32% + ANJA 68% | 1,716,853,000 | 1,982 + 6,017 = **7,999** |
| **Total consideration** | | **US$24,316** |
| Net assets at transfer | | US$72,083,188 |
| **Loss on divestment** | | **US$(72,058,872)** |

The FS is "Expressed in United States Dollars" — full units, not thousands — and the
arithmetic is self-consistent to the dollar (72,083,188 − 24,316 = 72,058,872). Trade press
reported the price as Rp405.59bn (~US$24.3m), which is almost exactly the FS figure ×1,000 —
a units mangle on one side or the other. The FS's internally consistent loss calculation is
treated as authoritative here: **the estates were transferred for a token sum.** One press
source frames the substance: HAM assumed ~US$80.1m of PMP/PPM liabilities as of 31 Jul 2025
— this was a debt-assumption exit, not a sale. The entities represented **36.18% of ANJT's
total assets** (bisnis.com) and ~91,210 ha of concession, of which only ~9,024 ha was
planted.

What the filings do *not* say:

- **Who bought it.** HAM's directors (Christian Jonathan; Hingdi Pangestu, per press) are
  publicly traceable but its ultimate ownership is not — sawitindonesia.com called it
  "misterius". For a transfer of a third of the balance sheet for US$24,316, the absence of
  any UBO disclosure is the single largest governance gap in the filing set.
- **Why now, and why for nothing.** The stated rationale is portfolio "re-focusing". The
  economic rationale is visible only in the restatement: PMP/PPM fixed assets had already
  been impaired **US$97.76m (against FY2023)** and **US$9.03m (against FY2024)** — booked as
  *prior-period corrections*, i.e. the Tahija-era accounts never recognized them (§3.2). Add
  the divestment loss and the Papua palm adventure consumed **~US$179m of book value
  [calculated: 97.76+9.03+72.06]** against 2013 acquisition + a decade of build-out — before
  counting years of operating losses.
- **Any warranty, indemnity or guarantee to the buyer.** The notes are silent.

`NEW IN v2` — **the original filings now show the giveaway was a decade in the making.**
Four threads, each visible only in the pre-2025 documents:

1. **The carrying value was always absurd against the planted area.** PMP + PPM were
   carried (pre-elimination) at US$267.9m (2021) → 231.6m (2022) → 227.4m (2023) → 207.8m
   (2024) — against a planted footprint that never exceeded **8,122 ha**. That is
   US$28,000–33,000 per planted hectare [calculated] in a country where entire planted
   estates change hands at US$5,000–10,000/ha. The balance was land bank (~76,000 ha of
   the group's 155k-ha "cadangan lahan" sat in Papua, essentially unplanted for a decade)
   plus capitalized development cost. Papua yields never justified it: 13.6 → 16.1 → 15.3
   → 16.3 → **11.1** t/ha (2020–2024) against 18–24 at the mature estates.
2. **The land tenure was clouded from January 2022.** MOEF decree SK 01 (5 Jan 2022) put
   the Company's, PPM's and PMP's Papua concessions on a revocation list. PPM/PMP were
   excluded by a final decree on 14 Jun 2022; the Company's directly-held **30,515.75-ha
   Womba HGU** (expiring 2050) sat at "status quo" until a resolution noted as 21 Jun 2023.
   A third ~36,506-ha Papua concession was earmarked for conservation, pending MOEF
   decision. The FY2025 filing is silent on what happened to the Womba HGU after the
   PMP/PPM sale — **open question: does ANJT still directly hold 30,516 Papua hectares?**
3. **The accounting defended the value to the end.** FY2022: blanket "no impairment."
   FY2023: impairment indicators identified on the bearer plants and plasma receivables of
   "two subsidiaries" (Note 20 names PPM and PMP), tests run, value-in-use declared higher —
   zero booked; the same year, US$10.5m was reclassified out of PPM's bearer plants into
   plasma receivables, shrinking the asset base under test. FY2024 (signed 14 Mar 2025):
   still "no impairment," on every line. FY2025 (EY): US$97.76m of it should have been
   impaired against FY2023 and US$9.03m more against FY2024. §3.2 has the full cascade.
4. **The planted area itself misbehaved.** Southwest Papua planted hectares *fell* from
   8,122 (2022) to 7,407 (2023) — a 715-ha net disappearance in the year immature area
   mechanically converts to mature, with no write-off or explanation in the notes. Even
   the production history got rewritten: FY2020 West Papua output was revised ~30% upward
   between the FY2020 and FY2021 newsletters (FFB 46,444t → 60,356t) with no footnote.

Context worth carrying: these South Sorong concessions are where ANJ admitted a 2015-era
RSPO New Planting Procedure breach after Greenomics satellite analysis caught forest
clearing (ANJ: "an infringement has occurred"), the blemish on its best-in-class ESG
narrative. The link between that history and the 2025 exit is plausible but not documented
in any source in hand — marked unverified. The pubex decks add their own tell: Papua CPO
milestones were headline achievements in the 2020/2021 decks, stopped being tracked in
2022, and by the 2024 deck Papua appeared only as a static map entry `NEW IN v2`.

## 1.4 Related party transactions

The cleanest before/after in any teardown so far: related-party revenue was **nil in
FY2024** and **US$135.98m = 57.5% of revenue in FY2025** [calculated].

| Counterparty | Relationship | Nature | Rev. FY2025 | Trade receivable 31 Dec 25 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| PT Adhitya Serayakorita | Common control (FR Group) | CPO + PK offtake | US$129.94m (**55% of sales**) | US$26.78m |
| PT Swadaya Mukti Prakarsa | Common control (FR Group) | PK | US$6.05m | US$0.62m |

Mechanics of the switchover: the legacy CPO/PK sale commitments (formula-priced monthly
deliveries up to 25,500 t CPO/8,125 t PK — the arrangement that fed the Wilmar-linked
buyers, PT Synergy Oil Nusantara 52% and PT Ivo Mas Tunggal 11% of FY2024 sales) **ended 16
May 2025**, nine days after completion. The related-party sales began in the same window.
The customer base was not transitioned; it was replaced.

`NEW IN v2` — **the Tahija-era RPT record, for contrast, is the cleanest in this teardown
series.** Across FY2021–FY2024 the consolidated related-party note contains exactly one
arrangement: GMIT's *rent-free* use of an AKJ/MDN-owned property in Jember — the family
lending an asset to the company, not extracting from it. No related-party sales, purchases,
loans or guarantees; management fees and dividends flowed only intra-group (parent-only
appendix: management fee charged to subsidiaries US$2.4–3.0m/yr; dividends up from ANJA
~US$6.5m/yr, US$11.2m in 2024). The one lever the insiders did pull was pay: **key
management compensation ran US$6.37m (2021) → 6.26m (2022) → 7.73m (2023) → 6.80m (2024)**
— the +23% raise landing in FY2023, the year profit fell 91% and the dividend was skipped.
Under First Resources it dropped to US$3.74m. So the ownership change traded one governance
profile for another: from zero related-party business but board pay uncorrelated with
results, to lean pay but 57.5% of revenue priced inside the controlling group.

What is missing is the pricing. The FS discloses **no pricing basis** for the affiliate
sales — no arm's-length statement, no formula, no benchmark reference. The receivable from
the two affiliates is **99.6% of all trade receivables** [calculated], unimpaired on
management's assertion of collectability. There are no related-party loans or guarantees in
Note 6 — but the Group's *entire* post-refinancing debt facility is a BRI overdraft that is
explicitly part of a **First Resources Group cash-pooling arrangement** (Rp2tn limit,
several FR companies, 5.25%), which is related-party treasury in substance sitting outside
the RPT note (§3.1). The only external check available: FY2025 CPO ASP of US$871/t against
the year's benchmark path (§2.5) is not obviously off-market — but one year, one number, no
formula. This line — affiliate ASP vs benchmark, year by year — is now the single most

## 2.6 Key variables — what actually moves the model

1. **CPO price (≈84.5% of revenue).** FY2025 realized US$871/t. Sensitivity: ±US$100/t on
   254k t of CPO ≈ ±US$25m revenue; netting the pass-through on bought fruit, roughly
   ±US$15–18m EBITDA on a US$68m base [calculated] — a ±25% EBITDA swing per US$100. This
   is, as at every upstream planter, most of the model.
2. **Affiliate pricing (57.5% of revenue).** A 3% ASP discount to arm's-length on affiliate
   volume ≈ US$4m of pre-tax profit ≈ 8% of operating profit [calculated] — economically
   invisible in any single year, cumulatively decisive. No formula disclosed. Watch
   ASP-vs-benchmark annually.
3. **Bought-fruit share (38.2% of volume, 56.3% of COGS).** Compresses both margin
   volatility and margin level. If FR routes more third-party fruit through ANJT's mills
   (or less), reported margin moves without the estates changing at all.
4. **Own-estate yield (21.4 t/ha print, but −16% lfl in Q1 2026).** The 2023 El Niño echo,
   the unnamed disease at ANJAS, and estate age (14.2 yr avg) all sit here. Each 1 t/ha on
   ~33k mature ha ≈ 33k t FFB ≈ 6,800 t CPO ≈ US$6m revenue at US$871 [calculated].
5. **Biodiesel policy.** B40 in force from early 2025 (~+1.7m t/yr CPO demand); B50
   status contradictory in press (postponed-indefinitely per Jan 2026 reporting vs
   in-effect-July-2026 per later reporting — unresolved). Every incremental mandate
   percentage point tightens domestic supply and supports ANJT's 100%-domestic realization.
6. **The squeeze-out option.** Not an operating variable, but the variable that dominates
   minority returns: FR at 95.92% (98.39% with CLP), float ~4%, delisting/compulsory
   acquisition explicitly speculated in press. The exit price, if it comes, anchors on
   recent trade prices — a reason the Rp1,450 print matters (§3.6).

## 2.7 Demand

Domestic-first story now: Indonesian biodiesel mandates (B40 running, B50 pending) plus
food/oleochemicals absorb ANJT's entire book — the FS shows zero direct exports. Globally,
palm demand growth is Indonesia + India + biodiesel; ANJ's own MD&A leans on the B40→B50
sequence as the demand thesis, and First Resources cited the same mandate expansion as an
acquisition rationale. The structural picture (from prior teardowns in this series):
Indonesian CPO production plateauing near 47–50m t, domestic biodiesel takes an
ever-larger share, export surplus shrinks — bullish domestic CPO realization, which is
exactly the price ANJT now captures via the affiliate.

## 3.7 Scenario: what is the residual float actually worth?

The only scenario that matters for a minority is the squeeze-out. Arithmetic, line by line:

```

  Rp2,365 × 136.96m public shares  = Rp324bn cost to FR  (0.9% of FR's mkt cap — trivial)

  Value = discounted dividends = 0 for the foreseeable  ⇒ drift toward Case B
```

The range is Rp1,100–2,365 depending entirely on FR's choice of mechanism and timing.

book-value appraisal would allow. There is no operating scenario — yield recovery, B50,
CPO at US$1,100 — that accrues to minorities except through that door.

# SUMMARY VERDICT

**What the business is:** a clean, mid-scale, RSPO-certified upstream palm producer — four
estates, 40k planted ha, 254k t CPO, US$428/t net cash cost, near-net-cash balance sheet —
wrapped inside the worst minority-shareholder structure in this teardown series.

**What actually happened in FY2025:** the Tahija era ended with a US$103.6m restatement
that revealed the old books had overstated equity for years, and the Fangiono era began by
amputating everything the Tahijas built after 2010 — Papua palm given away for US$24,316 to
a buyer with no traceable owner, sago and edamame shut, GSB sold — leaving a core that
immediately printed its best margins in the covered window (35.3% gross, US$68.3m EBITDA)
and a record Q1 2026. The reported net loss of US$59.6m is the funeral bill for the old
strategy, not a statement about the surviving assets.

**What v2 adds — the Tahija era was not what its reputation said** `NEW IN v2`: twelve
listed years produced a 0.6% lifetime net margin [calculated §3.2], ~US$16.8m of total
dividends, a share price below the IPO print for most of a decade, and a set of accounts in
which every year was later rewritten — FY2023 three times, ending US$95.6m worse than first
told. The ESG halo was real (SPOTT #1 in Indonesia, CDP A ratings, PROPER Gold) and so was
the operational honesty of the newsletters; the capital allocation was the failure. Papua
consumed ~US$179m of book value against 8,000 planted hectares yielding 11–16 t/ha, sago
and edamame lost money at the gross line for at least five straight years, and the
impairment that would have admitted all this was tested and declined annually until a buyer
arrived who had no reason to pretend. First Resources didn't break ANJT; it marked it to
market.

**What it earns through the cycle:** at mid-cycle CPO (US$700–750), plausibly US$45–55m
EBITDA and US$25–35m of pre-tax cash earnings on the current perimeter [calculated,
§3.5] — real, but volume risk is live (Q1 2026 own-fruit −16% lfl) and 38% of throughput
is thin-margin bought fruit.

**What would have to be true for the equity to work:** First Resources choosing to exit
the float at or near the Rp2,365 MTO precedent — a ~US$20m decision for them, +63% from
Rp1,450. Secondarily: honest affiliate pricing (unverifiable annually except by
ASP-vs-benchmark), and CPO staying ≥US$850.

**What breaks it:** a book-anchored squeeze-out (~Rp1,100), years of captivity with zero
dividends while the parent extracts economics through pricing and cash-pooling that
minorities cannot audit, or simply nothing happening at all — Case C is the default, and
it pays nothing.

**Position:** this is no longer investable as a business; it is a special situation on
First Resources' corporate-action calendar. The operating teardown says the residual core
is good and priced cheaply; the structure says the cheapness isn't yours to unlock. Anyone
holding at Rp1,450 is making a governance bet on the Fangiono family's treatment of a 4%
float — with the PMP/PPM disposal as the available evidence of how this group transacts
when nobody can see the counterparty. I would not make that bet; the interesting trade
was tendering at Rp2,365, and it closed in September 2025.
