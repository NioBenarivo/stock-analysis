## THE ONE THING TO UNDERSTAND FIRST

**SMAR is not a plantation company. It is the Indonesian processing-and-distribution node of the Widjaja family's palm oil chain, and both sides of its P&L are transactions with entities the same family controls.**

The scale mismatch tells you what the business actually is. SMAR's own estates produced 524,740 tonnes of CPO in FY2025. The company sold **5.33 million tonnes** of product. Roughly ten tonnes move through SMAR's refineries, biodiesel plants and trading desks for every tonne its own land grows. Revenue of Rp86.9tn makes it by far the largest IDX-listed palm company by sales — three times AALI — on the *smallest* planted area of any major peer (136k ha vs GAR group's own 531k, Wilmar's 267k, Indofood Agri's 329k).

Now look at who is on each side of the flows:

- **49.79% of FY2025 net sales (Rp43.3tn) went to related parties.** One counterparty — Golden Agri International Pte Ltd (GAI), Singapore, the GAR group trading arm — took **33.28% of consolidated revenue** alone (30.46% in FY2024; ~32.31% in FY2023 per a partially-captured note line).
- **Rp13.97tn of palm-product purchases came *from* related parties** — 18.2% of COGS [calculated] (the company itself, unusually, quotes this as "16.07% of *net sales*" — a denominator that flatters the ratio every year).
- SMAR also earns **Rp2.22tn of management and commission fees** from sister companies, most of which is *netted inside G&A as a negative cost line* rather than shown as revenue.
- Its assets are insured by **PT Asuransi Sinar Mas** (related party). Its bank loans carry corporate guarantees from **Purimas Sasmita and GAR** — and one loan is collateralised by land belonging to another related party. Its domestic branded distribution runs through **PT Sinarmas Distribusi Nusantara** (affiliate); its export bulk runs through GAI.

In other words: the family sits upstream of SMAR (selling it fruit and CPO), downstream of SMAR (buying half its output), beside it (insuring it, distributing for it, guaranteeing its debt) and above it (92.40% of the register, with the public holding 7.60% — one notch above the 7.5% OJK free-float minimum). The reported 11.7% gross margin is therefore not a market outcome; it is where the family chooses to let profit fall inside a chain it controls end-to-end. Disclosure thins precisely where that discretion lives: no product-level margins, no own/affiliate/third-party sourcing split, no litigation register, no audit fee, and a related-party purchase ratio benchmarked against the wrong denominator.

And yet — this is not a value-extraction story like some of its IDX peers. Over FY2022–FY2025 cumulative operating cash flow (Rp16.3tn) exceeded cumulative net income (Rp10.3tn); the dividend has been a disciplined ~30% of earnings every single year [calculated — §1.5]; leverage has come down from 4.2x to 2.4x net debt/EBITDA [calculated]; Pefindo rates it idAA-; and each new bond prices cheaper than the last (10.5% in 2020 → 6.2% in Feb 2026). The structure is built to make SMAR a **reliable, conservatively-geared cash conduit** — which is exactly what a bondholder wants and roughly what a 7.6% minority equity holder is allowed to ride along with. Sections 1.4, 2.6 and 3.7 carry the evidence.

GAI (Golden Agri International Pte Ltd, Singapore) is the single largest customer of the company at roughly **Rp28.9tn in FY2025 [calculated: 33.28% × 86,946,068]** — the group's own trading arm intermediates a third of SMAR's revenue before it ever reaches an external market. Other named sales counterparties include Shining Gold Oilseed Crushing and Shining Gold Foodstuffs (Ningbo — the group's China downstream), Golden Agri-Resources Europe B.V., and domestically PT Sinarmas Distribusi Nusantara (branded distribution). The prospectus explicitly disclaims "dependency" on GAI; it does not quantify the volume that flows through it, and it applies no such disclaimer to the domestic distributor.

The FY2025 drop in the RP ratio (53.16% → 49.79%) is partly mechanical: from January 2025 four pulp-and-paper side entities — **PT Indah Kiat Pulp & Paper Tbk, PT Pindo Deli, PT Cakrawala Mega Indah, PT Kreasi Kotak Megah** (the Asia Pulp & Paper wing of the family) — were **reclassified from related parties to third parties**, on the stated basis that no significant influence/common control/common key management was found. Whatever cooking-oil, packaging and paper volume those four represent simply stopped counting as related. The Rupiah effect of the reclassification is not quantified anywhere (MISSING DATA #6). The reclassification itself is a quietly significant document: the agri and pulp wings of the Widjaja family have formally told the auditor they are no longer under common control.

### The fee machine hidden inside G&A

SMAR bills the group for shared services — manpower, accounting, IT, sales/purchase handling — and earns tolling fees for processing sister companies' product (counterparties include SMII, PRIMATAMA, BAHANA, BPL, SKIP, BAP, MITRANUSA and others):

| Rp mn | FY2022 | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Management/commission fee income (RPT note) | 1,423,677 | 1,857,409 | 1,866,255 | **2,224,158** |
| G&A contra-line "allocation to mgmt/commission fees" | (1,075,298) | (1,268,882) | (1,371,500) | (1,572,521) |
| Unreconciled gap [calculated] | 348,379 | 588,527 | 494,755 | 651,637 |

Two things are wrong with this presentation. First, a Rp2.2tn service business — which would be material revenue for most listed companies — is booked as a *negative expense*, flattering the reported G&A ratio. Second, the income figure exceeds the G&A offset by Rp350–650bn every year with no reconciliation; the remainder presumably nets against COGS or selling expenses, unitemised. Gross G&A before the offset was ~Rp3.1tn in FY2025 [calculated: 1,537,137 + 1,572,521]; the printed Rp1.5tn is a net number.

### Everything else the family provides

- **Insurance:** essentially the whole insured asset base (refineries, mills, bulking stations — a combined policy up to Rp4.69tn) sits with **PT Asuransi Sinar Mas**; premiums Rp60.0bn (FY2025).
- **Guarantees downward:** GAR (the Singapore grandparent) guarantees SMAR's facilities at BRI, MUFG, BNP Paribas, SMBC, Citibank, Danamon and Maybank; Purimas guarantees the Panin money-market line. The support flows *down* the chain — unusual and credit-positive for SMAR, credit-entangling for GAR.
- **Collateral sideways:** the Bank Permata term loan is secured by land and buildings of **PT Bahana Karya Semesta — a related party**, not a subsidiary. Conversely, the legal opinion warns that TAPIAN/KRESNA/SOCI assets are pledged for obligations of the parent or of SOCI, and that enforcement "dapat berdampak material terhadap kelangsungan usaha" (could materially affect the going concern) of those subsidiaries — unusually blunt language for a legal opinion.
- **Leases:** SMAR leases tanks/mills to PT Ivo Mas Tunggal (GAR sister); PT Sinar Mas Tjipta provides "diplomacy & advocacy consulting" to two estate subsidiaries; PT Mitra Ekasukses Abadi manages warehouses.

### What the RPT section adds up to

Take FY2025: Rp43.3tn of sales to affiliates + Rp14.0tn of purchases from affiliates + Rp2.2tn of fees earned from affiliates ≈ **Rp59.5tn of gross related-party flow against Rp86.9tn of revenue [calculated]**. The pricing basis disclosed is boilerplate ("terms agreed between the parties"). Nothing in the record suggests value is being *tunnelled out* — the cash arrives (§3.2), receivables from affiliates have fallen from 15.0% of assets to 2.3%, and the parent guarantees rather than borrows. But the margin SMAR reports is, at this RPT density, a policy variable of the Widjaja family, not a price discovered in a market. That is the correct lens for every number in Part 02.

**The business model in one paragraph:** SMAR grows a base load of its own fruit (enough for ~10% of throughput), buys the rest of its feedstock from family plantations and third parties, converts it in a 4m-tonne-class refining/biodiesel/oleochemical complex into products spanning government-subsidised FAME to branded margarine, and sells half of the output to its own family's trading network (GAI for export bulk, Sinarmas Distribusi Nusantara for domestic branded), plus a state-mandated, state-subsidised biodiesel tranche. It is a margin-on-volume processor with a plantation kicker — not the reverse.

## 2.6 Key variables — what actually moves the model

**1. The CPO price (FOB Belawan).** Weight: sets ~22% of revenue directly and re-prices the whole feedstock/product stack. Range in period: $901 (FY2023) to $1,248 (FY2022); FY2025 $1,093; Aug-2026 reference price $996.52. Sensitivity, upstream only [calculated]: own CPO+PK ≈ 665kt; $100/t ≈ US$66m ≈ **Rp1.1tn of pre-tax swing** at Rp16,300/US$ — roughly a third of FY2025 pre-tax profit. For the bought-in 90%, price passes through with a lag and shows up as variable #2.

**2. The refining/processing spread.** Weight: the downstream segment is ~91% of revenue but swung between Rp1.2tn and Rp4.4tn of result in four years — a Rp3tn+ range that dwarfs every cost line. Driver: Indonesian refining overcapacity vs export-levy structure (the levy differential subsidises refined exports; when levies were reshuffled in 2022 and capacity overbuilt, spreads collapsed through FY2023–FY2024). 1H2026's 15.8% gross margin says the spread has reopened. Not separately disclosed — inferable only from the segment note, which is why §2.4 leans on it.

**3. The biodiesel mandate and BPDPKS.** Weight: 14% of revenue (~Rp12.2tn) at an administered margin, 1.33m kL allocation = 8.4% of the national programme. B40 held through 2026; **B50 postponed indefinitely (March 2026 decision)** pending the crude-vs-CPO spread and BPDPKS funding. Each mandate step-up has added ~25% to SMAR's allocation; a B50 launch would add roughly another fifth to biodiesel volume; a mandate cut in a fiscal squeeze is the symmetrical risk. The Rp1.2tn receivable spike (FY2024) shows the fund's payment timing is itself a working-capital variable.

**4. The export levy/tax regime.** Weight: Rp2.68tn of FY2025 selling expense (3.1% of revenue [calculated]), and the *funding source* of variable #3 — the state taxes SMAR's exports to subsidise SMAR's biodiesel. Levy raised 10%→12.5% (March 2026) precisely to keep the mandate funded: the two variables hedge each other inside SMAR's own P&L, a circularity unique to Indonesian palm. The 2022 export ban (Apr–May) sits in this bucket as the tail risk — it built the FY2022 windfall and then the Rp3.47tn levy bill.

**5. Related-party pricing discretion.** Weight: ~50% of revenue, ~18% of COGS, 100% of insurance, the export window, and the fee machine (§1.4). Not quantifiable by construction — which is the point. Any thesis on SMAR's margin is a thesis on Widjaja family allocation policy. Watch item, not model input.

**6. FX.** The prospectus quantifies it: ±10% IDR/USD → **±Rp820bn pre-tax**. USD-linked debt (BNI US$438m is the single biggest facility) against partly USD-linked product prices; the company runs cross-currency swaps on the DBS loan. Interest rates: ±0.5% → ±Rp42bn — minor.

**7. Own-estate FFB trajectory.** Weight: the plantation segment's Rp2.56tn result rides on 2.2m t of FFB that is falling ~1–7% a year until replanting cohorts mature. The 45%-of-nucleus-over-19-years profile is the slow-burn threat; the ~9.4k ha/yr replanting pace is the response; the payoff window is 2028–2030. 1H2026's −10.3% FFB is the cost of that medicine being taken now.

## 2.7 Demand

Domestic Indonesia is the anchor: 53% of sales, held up by three legs — cooking oil (population staple; SMAR sells branded, unbranded and the DMO Minyakita), the biodiesel mandate (14.2m kL of national B40 consumption in 2025, state-guaranteed offtake), and food-industry fats. Export demand (47%) is intermediated by GAI into India, China and Europe; the deck's list of mandate-driving countries quietly dropped the US from PE2025 onward (US biofuel policy uncertainty) and now reads Indonesia/Brazil/India. EUDR — the EU deforestation regulation that would bite a Kalimantan-heavy supplier — was postponed/simplified into a 2026–2027 timeline (Dec 2025 EU decision); no SMART/GAR-specific EUDR readiness commentary exists in any document in this set, an odd silence for a group with GAR's European B.V. in the customer list (MISSING DATA #10). Structural demand risk is mild: palm is the cheapest oil and Indonesia mandates its own consumption; the demand-side variables that matter (mandate size, India tariffs, China restocking) are policy variables, consistent with the pattern across this peer group.

## 2.8 Supply

Indonesia produced 52.76m t of CPO in 2024 (GAPKI); SMAR's own 525kt is ~1.0% of national output [calculated] — but its ~4m t of refining throughput is a meaningful share of national processing. The national supply story mirrors SMAR's own: aging estates, weak replanting, flat-to-declining output — which supports prices and processing spreads for whoever holds modern capacity. The policy overlay is the live risk: the **Satgas PKH** forest-area task force had reclaimed 5.9m ha of plantations-in-forest-zones by 2026, transferring 4.1m ha to the new state operator Agrinas. No SMART/GAR estate was found linked to a seizure in the searches run (explicitly "not found", not "cleared" — MISSING DATA #5); but the prospectus ranks land-rights revocation as its #3 risk factor, PANIGORAN's HGU sits expired-mid-renewal, and the SIMP teardown in this series showed what a Satgas PKH charge looks like when it lands (Rp2.34tn). The legal opinion's cluster of simultaneously expired B3 waste permits (parent + SATYA) and expired collective labour agreements across five Sumatra entities reads as an administrative-renewal backlog — small items individually, but a pattern of letting paperwork ride that is worth watching in a land-tenure enforcement climate.

# SUMMARY VERDICT

**What this business is:** the family-owned toll road between Indonesia's third-largest private plantation base and the world's cooking-oil, biodiesel and oleochemical demand — with the family collecting at both on-ramps and both off-ramps. SMAR grows ~10% of its own throughput at ~25% margins, processes the rest at ~3%, and sells half of everything to itself. The listed entity is engineered to be *adequate*: adequate margin (3% net), adequate ROE (~13% through-cycle), adequate disclosure (thinning exactly where family discretion operates), and an utterly reliable 30% payout.

**What it earns through the cycle:** Rp4–6tn of EBITDA at mid-cycle, Rp9.5tn at the 2022 peak, Rp3.8tn at the 2023 trough; net profit Rp1–5.5tn across the same span; cash conversion above 1x cumulatively (Rp16.3tn OCF vs Rp10.3tn earnings, FY2022–FY2025). Leverage 2.4x net debt/EBITDA and falling; idAA-; each bond cheaper than the last.

**What has to be true for the equity to work:** only that the family keeps running the conduit as it has — 30% payout on rising earnings, no value extraction beyond the transfer-pricing dial, refining spreads staying merely normal, and the biodiesel mandate not shrinking. At 3.9x trailing earnings and 0.72x book with a prospective ~8% yield on FY2026 numbers, nothing heroic is priced in. The upside scenarios (B50, replanting inflection, a family buyout of a Rp1.2tn float) are free options.

**What would break it:** the same concentration that makes it cheap. A GAI repricing decision, a levy/mandate regime turn, a refining-spread relapse, or a land-tenure event on Kalimantan estates would each hit a P&L that minorities have no mechanism to defend — no float, no activist path, a 79-year-old average independent bench, and an audit committee chaired from inside the group's own insurance ecosystem. And the position has no exit except the family's bid: 7.6% float is an entrance with no door back out at size.

**Position:** this is the highest-quality operator in the IDX palm set on cash generation and credit trajectory, wrapped in the sector's most minority-indifferent structure. The instrument the structure is *designed for* is the bond (idAA-, 6.2–7.5% coupons, guaranteed facilities, covenant headroom everywhere) — and the bond market has already priced that in. The equity is a deep-value coupon-clipper: paid ~30% of whatever the family reports, at 4x a partially-delivered year, with takeout optionality. It works as a yield instrument sized to what you can sell back into a 7.6% float on a bad day — and not as anything else.
