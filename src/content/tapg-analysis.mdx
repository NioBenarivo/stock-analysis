## THE ONE THING TO UNDERSTAND FIRST

TAPG reports two different businesses in the same set of numbers, and nearly every published
figure mixes them up:

- **The consolidated business** — 82,682 ha of planted oil palm, ~659k tonnes of CPO sold,
  Rp11.40 trillion of revenue, Rp3.85 trillion of profit. This is what the P&L and balance
  sheet describe.
- **The equity-accounted business** — a 50/50 joint venture, **PT Union Sampoerna Triputra
  Persada (USTP)**, plus its plantations. None of its revenue, cost, tonnes or hectares appear
  on TAPG's P&L. Only one line does: **"share of profit from joint ventures and associates" of
  Rp1,066,889m**, which is **22.9% of pre-tax profit and 27.7% of net profit**.

Every operational number TAPG publishes — 159,707 ha planted, 3.04m tonnes FFB, 953k tonnes
CPO, 18 mills, 23 estates — is footnoted *"including associate company"* and therefore
**includes the JV**. The financial numbers do not. About **31% of the group's physical palm oil
operation sits outside the consolidated accounts.**

Any per-hectare or per-tonne analysis that divides consolidated revenue by group-wide hectares
will understate unit economics by roughly a third. This report keeps the two apart.

# 01 — MANAGEMENT

## 1.1 History and milestones

TAPG is a young corporate entity sitting on top of an older set of plantations. It was
incorporated 24 January 2005 as **PT Alam Permata Indah** and renamed **PT Triputra Agro
Persada** on 31 March 2005 (approved 6 May 2005). The estates themselves were bought in, not
planted from scratch — several subsidiaries have commercial start dates well before the parent
existed (BBB 1995, HHK 1999, EBL and DLJ 2000, GCM 2002).

| 2007 | Acquires MIK. **Forms the USTP joint venture with PT Union Sampoerna** — the single most important structural decision in the company's history |
| 2008 | Acquires FLTI, HJA, SKM, EBL, DLJ, YWA, AAPA |

| 2018 | Divests the remaining West Kalimantan estates |

Read as a strategy, the arc is: **buy → consolidate → build mills → prune the geography →
list → harvest.** The last new-land acquisition was 2014. Since 2017 the company has been
shrinking its footprint, not expanding it. Nucleus planted area has actually *fallen*: 136.4k ha
(2023) → 135.6k (2024) → 132.8k (2025).

## 1.3 Group structure and subsidiaries

TAPG itself is a **holding company** — its stated line of business is holding-company activity,
management consulting, and wholesale trade in oil-bearing fruit, vegetable oils and fats,
agricultural machinery, and fertiliser/agrochemicals. It owns no plantation directly.

The structure is deliberately layered:

```
TAPG
├─ 94.93%  AMP — PT Agro Multi Persada (holding co, Rp8,774,823m assets)   ← THE REAL GROUP
│    └─ 20 operating subsidiaries, all 98.46–99.99% owned
├─ 99.90%  ATS — PT Alam Teduh Sentosa (ecosystem restoration sub-holding)
│    └─ ABM, SAK, BSM
├─ 50.00%  USTP — PT Union Sampoerna Triputra Persada (JOINT VENTURE)
├─ 49.00%  PT ATP Bio Indonesia (associate, with Aisin Takaoka)
└─ (via TAPAS) 22.87%  Coldspace PTE. LTD, Singapore (associate)
```

**AMP holds essentially everything.** Its Rp8.77 trillion of pre-elimination assets is 60% of
group total assets. The 5.07% of AMP that TAPG does *not* own is the source of the
Rp478,406m non-controlling interest on the balance sheet and Rp143,081m of the profit — i.e.
**minorities take 3.7% of group profit before it reaches TAPG shareholders.**

The four sub-1% direct holdings (HHK, SMG, SLM, GCM) are USTP's operating estates in which
TAPG also holds token direct stakes. They are inside USTP's consolidation — don't double-count.

**USTP is a very good business.** Standalone FY2025: net sales Rp4,962,369m, net profit
Rp2,119,488m — a **42.7% net margin against TAPG's own 33.7%** — on net assets of Rp5,224,588m,
i.e. **~40.6% ROE**. TAPG's half is worth Rp2.61 trillion on the books.

It is also being **harvested harder than it earns.** USTP distributed Rp2,719,742m of dividends
in FY2025 (TAPG's 50% = Rp1,359,871m) against Rp2,119,488m of profit — a **128% payout**. USTP's
net assets fell from Rp5,828,140m to Rp5,224,588m, and TAPG's carrying value fell from
Rp2,944,729m to Rp2,640,217m. This is exactly why TAPG's non-current assets *declined* 3.1% in a
year of record profit: the JV is being drained to fund the parent's dividend.

**What to watch:**

1. **Affiliate FFB purchases jumped 66% YoY** (Rp176.1bn → Rp292.7bn), from 2.96% to 4.24% of
   COGS. TAPG's mills are buying more fruit from the JV estates. The stated pricing basis is
   *"agreed prices depending on the type of product involved regarding market prices"* — which
   is not a formula, and is the weakest disclosure in the whole related-party note. There is no
   published transfer-price mechanism.
   **TL;DR:** the group buys ~Rp293bn of fruit a year from entities it half-owns, at prices set
   by negotiation, with no disclosed benchmark. Immaterial today; worth monitoring if the
   trajectory continues.

2. **No management fee, no royalty, no trademark licence, no intercompany loan, no guarantee to
   any affiliate.** This is the notable absence. Compare with peers where a Singapore affiliate
   charges 1%+ of net sales as a "royalty."

3. **PT Borneo Konservasi Restorasi** appears for the first time in FY2025 with a Rp5,150m
   receivable, related by *"the same key management personnel."* Consistent with the ATS
   ecosystem-restoration build-out.

4. **PT Kreasi Pangan Samadhi** (formerly PT Kedai Pangan Sejahtera) — a Rp26bn equity stake in
   a food business under the same key management, up 44% in a year. Small, non-core, and worth a
   question at the AGM.

5. **Dana Pensiun Triputra** runs the group defined-contribution pension (4.0% employer / 2.4%
   employee of pensionable income; Rp11,993m contributed in FY2025).

but it means the terminal value depends entirely on replanting discipline, not expansion.

**The FY2025 profit story was not CPO — it was the kernel.** CPO ASP rose 8%; PK ASP rose **52%**
and PKO **37%**. PK + PKO went from 12.9% of revenue in FY2024 to **17.0% in FY2025**, adding
Rp698bn of incremental revenue at what is essentially by-product cost. The 2023 decision to
build the SKM kernel crushing plant — turning PK into PKO — landed in exactly the right price
cycle: PKO volume went 6k t (2023) → 20k t (2024) → 22k t (2025) and its revenue is now larger
than the entire rubber segment by 25×.

**Rubber is a rounding error that loses money.** Revenue Rp23,549m (0.21%) and a **segment
operating loss of Rp5,137m** in FY2025 versus +Rp562m in FY2024 — a 1,014% swing. It consumed
Rp9,817m of capex and carries Rp162,460m of segment assets earning nothing. FY2025 deliveries
fell 29% on wintering and *fusicoccum* fungus. There is a clean, obvious disposal candidate here
(it sits inside BBB in Jambi), and management has not addressed it.

This is the single most important operational fact about TAPG and it cuts both ways.

**The good:** 82.7% of the estate is in the 7–20 year prime yield window, at an average age of
13.8 years — almost dead centre of peak productivity. FFB yield of **24.3 t/ha** (2025, up from
23.0) is at the top of the Indonesian listed peer range. This is a portfolio at maximum output
with minimum immature drag (only 6.9% immature).

**The problem:** an estate this uniformly aged is a **replanting cliff in slow motion.** Only
6.9% is immature. Palms are typically replanted at 25 years. With 82.7% of the area between 7
and 20 years old, a very large block hits replanting age between roughly 2030 and 2040 — and
each hectare replanted produces nothing for 3–4 years. TAPG is currently replanting at a rate
implied by Rp105,929m of additions to immature oil palm bearer plants against Rp5.76 trillion of
mature plants at cost, i.e. **under 2% a year.** To level the age curve it needs roughly 4%/yr.
The 2026 plan does list *"rejuvenating oil palm plants with superior varieties"* as one of four
strategies, which is an acknowledgement — but a Rp648bn total capex budget is not enough to both
build a new mill and step up replanting.

By June 2026 the average age had already ticked to **14.6 years** and prime mature had fallen
from 82.7% to 81.5% while old mature rose from 6.0% to 7.5%. The clock is visibly running.

## 2.4 Process — how the product is made and what it costs

### The physical process

TAPG does not describe its milling process in the annual report, so the following is the
standard Indonesian POM flow, annotated with TAPG's disclosed data:

```
1. HARVEST         FFB cut at 10–12 day rounds. 3,425k t harvested (group).
                   Tracked by Mobile eBCC barcode app at each collection point
                   (an anti-theft control, per the risk register).
                   ↓  must reach mill within 24h or free fatty acid rises and OER falls
2. RECEPTION       4,089k t processed. 74% own, 10% plasma, 16% third-party purchase.
                   ↓
3. STERILISER      Steam at ~140°C, ~3 bar. Kills lipase, loosens fruit.
                   Steam is self-generated from fibre + shell in the boiler → the mill
                   is energy self-sufficient; this is why there is no fuel line in COGS.
                   ↓
4. THRESHER        Fruit stripped from bunch. Empty bunches (EFB) → returned to field as mulch.
                   ↓
5. DIGESTER/PRESS  Mash pressed. → crude oil + press cake.
                   ↓
6. CLARIFICATION   Oil purified, dried.  →  CPO 953k t.  OER 23.3%.
                   Effluent (POME) → biogas plant at SKM → electricity.
                   ↓
7. NUT/KERNEL      Press cake → fibre (to boiler) + nuts → cracked → kernel + shell.
                   →  PK 202k t.  KER 4.9%.
                   Shell → sold, and now → ATP Bio biocoke.
                   ↓
8. KCP (SKM only)  PK crushed → PKO 22k t + PKM (kernel meal).
                   Only ~11% of PK output is crushed in-house; the rest is sold as PK.
```

**OER of 23.3% is excellent** (Indonesian industry average is roughly 20–21%) and improved
0.2ppt YoY and a further 0.7ppt in 1H2026 to 23.9%. Management attributes it to
*"end-to-end quality control from estate harvesting to mill processing."* Every 0.1ppt of OER on
4.09m tonnes of FFB is ~4,100 tonnes of extra CPO — about Rp58bn of revenue at zero incremental
cost. **OER is the highest-leverage operating variable in this business.**

## 2.5 Distribution — how the product is sold

- **Direct sales only.** No traders, no brokers, no intermediaries. Every tonne is negotiated
  directly with the buyer.
- **All sales are domestic** (Note 27: *"All sales represent the local sales"*). TAPG has **zero
  direct export exposure** and no foreign-currency revenue. It sells CPO at the mill/port gate to
  Indonesian refiners, who then export or supply the domestic biodiesel and cooking-oil programme.
- **Pricing benchmark: KPBN auction.** CPO, PK and PKO prices reference the tender results of
  **KPBN (PT Kharisma Pemasaran Bersama Nusantara)**, the Indonesian state auction platform, as
  the market reference. Prices are then negotiated per contract.
- **Payment terms are exceptionally favourable:** advance payment on contract signing, then
  progress payments. **Receivables turn in 6 days** (63× a year), and **100% of receivables are
  not yet due** — nothing in the 0–30, 31–60 or 61–90 day buckets. Average receivables just
  Rp180bn on Rp11.4tn of sales. This is close to a cash business.
- **Delivery points:** Balikpapan, Tarjun, Gresik, Kumai, Bagendang, Batam, Bontang, Bitung,
  Dumai.

## 2.6 Key variables — what actually moves the model

Ranked by sensitivity:

### 1. CPO selling price (82% of revenue)

- FY2025 realised **Rp14,220/kg**, +8% YoY. FY2024 Rp13,156/kg.
- Set off KPBN auction. As of **11–14 August 2026 the KPBN tender was Rp15,602–15,702/kg** —
  roughly **10% above TAPG's FY2025 realised average**, with Malaysian futures at
  RM4,724/tonne (a four-month high).
- **Sensitivity: every Rp100/kg on CPO ≈ Rp66bn of revenue and, since the cost base is fixed in
  the short run, ≈ Rp66bn of pre-tax profit — about 1.4% of FY2025 PBT.** A Rp1,000/kg move
  (7%) is worth ~Rp659bn, or 17% of net profit.

### 2. Palm kernel and PKO prices (17% of revenue, and the swing factor of FY2025)

- PK Rp11,967/kg (+52%), PKO Rp26,212/kg (+37%). Kernel prices are driven by the *lauric* oil
  complex (coconut oil substitution) rather than by CPO — and **2025 was a coconut supply crisis
  year**, which the AR itself flags ("weather disruptions ... affecting commodities like
  chocolate and coconut"). This is a genuinely exogenous windfall that may not repeat.
- **Sensitivity: PK+PKO contributed Rp1.94tn of revenue. A reversion of PK to FY2024 pricing
  would remove ~Rp462bn of revenue and profit — 12% of net profit.**

### 3. Fertiliser cost

- The largest controllable input. Inventory Rp384,823m at year-end (+22% YoY — they pre-bought).
- Explicitly named by management as the reason production costs fell in FY2025 and again in
  Q2 2026 ("decrease in the cost of fertiliser due to timing difference applied").
- Urea and potash prices track natural gas and the Russia/Belarus potash trade. The company's
  own risk register names **fertiliser and fuel availability and price** as a material risk and
  says it mitigates by multi-sourcing.

### 4. Purchased FFB price and volume

- 659k tonnes, 16.1% of throughput. Priced as a percentage of the CPO price, so it is a
  **natural partial hedge**: when CPO rises, this cost rises too, compressing the margin on
  bought fruit while expanding it on own fruit. Management named it as the main driver of the
  FY2025 COGS increase (+20%) and of the Q2 2026 COGS increase.

### 5. Rainfall and El Niño

- FY2025 had **above-normal rainfall** all year, which depressed harvesting but supported yield.
- **NOAA now projects a strong-to-very-strong El Niño with 97% probability for Oct–Dec 2026,
  persisting into early 2027.** TAPG's own factsheet states the impact *"will occur in the 2027
  production"* — palm has a 6–12 month lag between water stress and fruit. **This is the single
  clearest forward risk to FY2027 volumes**, and it is also price-supportive industry-wide.

### 6. Oil extraction rate

- 23.3% FY2025 → 23.9% in 1H2026. Each 0.1ppt ≈ Rp58bn of revenue at nil cost.

### 7. Government policy — biodiesel, levy, land

- **B40 in 2025 → B50 mandate commenced 1 July 2026**, full national rollout targeted October
  2026. Fastmarkets reports the mandate was scaled back to **B45 for 2026** with B50 late in the
  year. Every step up diverts CPO from export to domestic fuel, tightening supply and supporting
  the domestic price TAPG sells into.
- **Export levy and DMO** fund the biodiesel subsidy (BPDPKS) and set the wedge between the
  international and domestic price. TAPG sells domestically, so it bears the levy indirectly
  through the price its refiner customers can pay.
- **Land seizures.** Fastmarkets reports **3.3 million ha of palm land seized by the Indonesian
  government to date, 1.5m ha transferred to state-owned PT Agrinas and 1.8m ha under
  verification**, potentially affecting 2–5m tonnes of national output. TAPG's own risk register
  names the **Permentan 98/2013 group land-holding ceiling** as a "main risk that significantly
  affects business continuity" — the company cannot guarantee that its interpretation of the
  maximum permitted plantation area for a single group matches the regulator's, and an adverse
  reading could mean **revocation of IUP licences**. Combined with HGU expiries from 2036, land
  tenure is the structural risk in this name.

### 8. Interest rates and currency

## 2.7 Demand

**End-market split for palm oil (industry level, since TAPG sells only to refiners):**

- **Food** — cooking oil, margarine, confectionery fats. The largest global use.
- **Energy** — biodiesel. Indonesia's B40 (2025) → B45/B50 (2026) programme is the fastest
  growing demand block and the reason domestic CPO absorption keeps rising.
- **Oleochemicals** — soaps, surfactants, cosmetics. This is where **PK and PKO** go: lauric oils
  are not interchangeable with CPO and compete with coconut oil.
- **Animal feed** — PKM.

**What the company says:** Indonesian domestic CPO consumption is growing steadily on the
downstream food and oleochemical industries plus the strengthening national biodiesel programme.
GAPKI expects Indonesian export volumes to be *constrained* in 2026 by production stagnation,
rising domestic consumption and the B50 mandate.

**What the outside sources say (Aug 2026):**

- Global palm oil consumption continues to rise, concentrated in developing countries.
- **India remains the largest importer and the critical demand anchor.**
- **China demand is limited** — ample soybean oil stocks and elevated palm olein supply.
- The **EU Deforestation Regulation (EUDR), postponed to December 2026**, will affect palm lauric
  pricing and add compliance cost.
- The US–China tariff war is disrupting soybean oil flows; the AR's own view is that CPO can pick
  up substitution demand from soybean oil uncertainty.

**Relevance to TAPG:** near zero direct exposure to export markets or EUDR, because 100% of sales
are domestic and made to refiners who bear that risk. What TAPG gets is the *price* effect —
tighter global supply and a bigger domestic biodiesel draw both push the KPBN benchmark up.
TAPG is a pure, unhedged, levered play on the Indonesian domestic CPO price.

## 3.7 Scenario: what a mean reversion in kernel prices costs

The FY2025 result leaned unusually hard on lauric oils. Working the arithmetic explicitly:

```
FY2025 actual
  PK  revenue  = 113,364 t × Rp11,967/kg  =  Rp1,356,630m
  PKO revenue  =  22,399 t × Rp26,212/kg  =    Rp587,121m
  PK + PKO                                =  Rp1,943,751m   (17.0% of revenue)

Scenario: PK and PKO ASPs revert to FY2024 levels, volumes unchanged
  PK  at Rp7,891/kg  × 113,364 t          =    Rp894,555m
  PKO at Rp19,101/kg ×  22,399 t          =    Rp427,843m
  PK + PKO                                =  Rp1,322,398m

  Revenue impact                          =  −Rp621,353m
  Cost impact                             =  ~nil (kernel is a by-product of the same FFB)
  Pre-tax profit impact                   =  −Rp621,353m  (−13.3% of FY2025 PBT)
  After tax at 22%                        =  −Rp484,655m
  Net profit                              =  Rp3,845,347m − Rp484,655m = Rp3,360,692m  (−12.6%)
  EPS                                     =  Rp186 → Rp163
  Net margin                              =  33.7% → 31.2%
```

**Conclusion: a full reversion of kernel prices to 2024 levels costs about 13% of earnings.**
Significant but not existential. Add a simultaneous 10% CPO price fall (−Rp937bn revenue,
−Rp731bn after tax) and net profit drops to ~Rp2.63tn, EPS to ~Rp127, net margin to ~27% — still
comfortably profitable, still covering a dividend. **The business does not break in a downturn;
it just earns less.** FY2023 already proved that.

# SUMMARY VERDICT

**What TAPG is:** a 2%-share Indonesian upstream palm producer with genuinely top-quartile
operations — 24.3 t/ha FFB yield, 23.3% OER rising to 23.9%, 6-day receivables, net cash, a
single unsecured lender, no export exposure, no affiliated revenue and no royalty leakage. It
converts a Rp11.4tn revenue line into Rp3.85tn of profit and hands 99% of its operating cash flow
to shareholders.

**What TAPG is not:** a growth company. Nucleus area is shrinking, nucleus FFB fell 1% in 1H2026,
the last land acquisition was 2014, capex is below depreciation, and the only volume levers left
are OER (already near the practical ceiling), plasma, and replanting — which is running at less
than half the rate the age profile demands.

**The three things that would change the thesis:**
1. A credible, funded replanting programme at 4%+ of area per year — this is the difference
   between a 15-year asset and a perpetuity.
2. Clarity on HGU renewal and the Permentan 98/2013 land ceiling.
3. Either downstream integration (currently only a "feasibility study") or an explicit
   acknowledgement that this is a run-off cash machine and should be valued as one.

**The three things most likely to be mispriced today:**
1. **The JV.** 28% of net profit and ~20% of reported EBITDA come from an entity that is being
   paid out faster than it earns and whose book value fell 10% in a year.
2. **The kernel windfall.** 17% of revenue at PK prices up 52% in one year, driven by a coconut
   supply shock that has nothing to do with palm.
3. **The land.** Rp494bn of land on the balance sheet against Rp7.2tn of assets sitting on
   concessions that start expiring in 2036, in a country that has just seized 3.3m hectares.
