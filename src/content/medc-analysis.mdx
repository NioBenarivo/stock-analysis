## THE ONE THING TO UNDERSTAND FIRST

MEDC is two businesses stapled together, and the market prices them very differently.

**Business one — a leveraged buyer of maturing gas PSCs.** Roughly 70–80% of today's production came through acquisitions, not the drill bit (Corridor, Natuna, Oman and Thailand alone are 68% [calculated, §08.2]): South Natuna Sea B (2016, US$238m), Ophir Energy (2019, US$541m), Corridor (2022, US$1,355m), Oman Block 60 (2023, US$713m), Corridor again (2025, +24% for US$425m). Each was bought at US$6–14 per 2P barrel and, on the company's own table, paid itself back in cash within 18–36 months (§08.3). The engine that makes this work is contracted gas: 54% of gas volume sells at fixed prices (US$5.8–6.8/mmbtu) under take-or-pay contracts to PGN, PLN, Pusri and Singapore buyers, so EBITDA held at US$1.26–1.27bn in FY2024–25 even as the realised oil price fell 15%. That stability is what lets the company carry US$3.6bn of gross debt (2.4x consolidated net debt/EBITDA) and keep buying.

**Business two — a 20.92% stake in AMMN.** It contributes nothing to revenue or EBITDA (equity-accounted), but it drives the net income line: FY2025 net income fell 72% to US$101m almost entirely because AMMN's contribution fell from US$133m to US$52m during its smelter transition. At AMMN's Rp238tn market cap (June 2026) the stake is worth ~Rp50tn, more than MEDC's entire Rp29.7tn market capitalisation — i.e. the market attributes a negative equity value to the oil and gas business that generates all the cash [calculated, §08.1].

Two consequences follow. Reported net income is a poor guide to the operating business — read EBITDA, cash flow from operations (US$906m FY2025) and the debt line instead. And the portfolio has a short fuse: Corridor (38% of production) runs to 2043 but South Natuna (13%) expires in 2028, Lematang/Madura/Sampang in 2027, and the acquired fields are mature — hence the 200%+ reserve replacement has to keep coming from deals. Evidence: production by block §08.2, contract expiries §2.1, acquisition economics §08.3, financials §07.

## 1.3 History — recap

Three cycles of the same move. Buy a producing asset from a departing major (Stanvac 1995, ConocoPhillips 2016 and 2022, Ophir 2019, OQ 2023, Repsol 2025), fund it with bonds, repay from the field's own cash within two to three years, repeat. Organic exploration produced one great success (Kaji-Semoga, 1996) and a long tail of impairments (Libya, Yemen, Tunisia, Kampar, Block A). The international ventures of 2004–2010 were the one period the pattern broke, and they were written off in 2014–16. Since 2016 the company has bought only producing or near-producing assets, and each has been cash-positive on its own table.

## 1.4 Owner's character

Read from the record, not asserted:

- **They hire outsiders to run it.** Since 2016 the CEO, COO and CFO have been career oil-industry professionals (Lorato and Gunawan ex-Premier Oil), with the family holding the President Director and President Commissioner chairs. The 2026 succession (Gunawan CEO, Amri Siahaan COO, Benny Setiawan CFO, Sanjeev Bansal Chief Growth Officer) was internal and orderly.
- **They sell down rather than lose.** Having lost control to creditors in 1998–2004, the family has since kept leverage inside a stated ceiling (RG net debt/EBITDA target 3.0x, cut to 2.5x in 2021), raised equity three times (2017, 2019, 2020) and sold 21.6% to Salim in 2018 instead of over-borrowing.
- **They cut fast in downturns.** 2020: capex −39% vs budget, US$94m opex out, offices closed, cash cost held at US$9.0/boe, dividend still paid (US$35m in 2021).
- **They keep paying.** Dividends every year 2021–2025 (US$35m → US$80m), buybacks in 2024–25, total 2025 shareholder return ~US$110m — about 12% of CFO.
- **They over-claim on guidance.** FY2025 guidance included ROE >15%; actual was 5%. The FY2025 deck says "met or exceeded all operational guidance" — true only if ROE is excluded without saying so. Same in Q1 2026 (annualised ~7%).

### Distribute — government take and the PSC funds flow

Indonesian cost-recovery PSC, on US$100 of gross revenue with costs at 50% (company's own worked example, Q1 2026 Fact Book):

```
Gross revenue 100
 ├─ First Tranche Petroleum 20% ─ split govt/contractor
 ├─ Cost recovery 50 ──────────────────────────► contractor (recovers its spend)
 └─ Profit split (gas 35% contractor pre-tax-grossed to 56%) ─► taxable income 28
                                                  tax 37.6% (22% CIT + 20% branch profit) = 11
Government 33  (equity share 22 + tax 11)     Contractor 67 (50 cost recovery + 17 net profit)
```

Under gross split (Rimau, Tarakan): contractor 83% of gross before tax, 71 after costs and tax; government 29. The point: of the **profit pool** (revenue less cost), the government keeps ~66% under cost recovery and ~58% under gross split. Contractor profit-share entitlements by block: Corridor 22.3% oil / 39% gas; Senoro 35/40; Natuna 15/35; SSB 12.5/27.5; Block A 15/40; Oman Block 60 up to 35/40 with tax borne by the government. Because the government takes its share in kind, none of this appears as a cost line — it is simply absent from revenue. Volume disclosure is on a working-interest basis; revenue is on an entitlement basis, which is why revenue/boe (US$38) sits below the blended realised price (~US$44/boe [calculated]).

# 03 — DEMAND

## 3.1 Gas

Global gas is ~4,200 bcm/yr; ~30% is traded (55% pipeline, 45% LNG). Demand growth is in Asia; supply growth 2026–28 is a Qatar/US LNG wave that most forecasters expect to soften spot LNG (JKM) from 2026.

Indonesia consumes ~5.8 tbtu/day (~2% of world). By sector: industry 31%, fertiliser 12%, power 12%, domestic LNG 10%, exports 30% (LNG 22%, pipeline 8%). Domestic demand is flat-to-rising; exports fall as contracts expire and gas is redirected home. Two of MEDC's largest outlets are policy-exposed: Singapore pipeline exports (Corridor and Natuna contracts to 2028, renewal subject to government approval) and HGBT-capped industrial gas. The HGBT scheme was renewed in Feb 2025 at US$6.50 (feedstock) / US$7.00 (fuel) per mmbtu for 253 users in seven sectors; the government reviewed it in 2025 after industry complaints about price rises.

Power demand for gas is price-limited: PLN dispatches coal (US$3–4/mmbtu equivalent) before gas, so gas-to-power grows only where pipelines and PLN's RUPTL put it (Sumatra, Batam, Java peakers).

## 3.2 Oil

IEA (June 2026): 2026 demand 103.3 mb/d, down 1.1 mb/d on 2025 after the Hormuz disruption pushed prices up and Q2 demand fell ~5 mb/d YoY — the first quarterly decline since 2020; 2027 demand 105.3 vs supply 110.3 mb/d, an ~8 mb/d surplus if Gulf supply and OPEC+ targets recover. Indonesia consumes ~1.6 mb/d against ~0.6 mb/d production and imports the rest; ICP averaged ~US$86 for Jan–May 2026 (April US$117, May US$107) against a US$70 budget assumption.

## 3.3 Copper and gold

Copper demand ~31 Mt (22 Mt mined, 9 Mt recycled); consensus growth ~45% by 2035 on grid, renewables (2–5x copper intensity of thermal), EVs and data centres. Gold demand is investment-led; the price roughly doubled during 2025 (open US$2,625 → close US$4,318) — the single biggest driver of AMMN's 2026 earnings recovery.

## 8.3 Acquisition economics — the Corridor case

Corridor is 38% of production and the clearest test of the model.

```
2022  Pay US$1,355m for 54% (124 mmboe 2P → US$10.9/boe); fund with US$400m notes + US$450m loan
      Known at signing: PSC extension (signed Nov 2019 by ConocoPhillips) cuts WI to 46% from 1 Jan 2024
      and hands Pertamina 30%; extension cost US$250m signature bonus + US$250m firm work (borne by contractors)
2022–23  Produces 70–74 mboepd at MEDC's 54%; NCF US$1.8bn by Mar 2026 = 1.3x price in 4 years
2024  WI 46%: production 58 mboepd; gas sales −13%; crude purchases +148% to keep filling contracts
2025  Pay US$425m for Repsol's 24% (58 mmboe → US$7.3/boe) → 70%; +25 mboepd; NCF US$137m in 6 months
Contract tail: PGN 410 bbtud, GSPL 121, Pusri 62, PLN 20 all expire Dec 2028; PHR Rokan 105 bbtud Dec 2026
```

All-in, MEDC has paid US$1,780m for 70% of a block with ~65 mboepd of net production and a PSC to 2043, and has already received ~US$1.95bn of NCF from it. The remaining risk is commercial, not geological: five gas contracts covering ~600 bbtud reprice or renew in 2026–28, into a market where the government has capped industrial gas at US$6.50–7.00 and must decide whether Singapore exports continue past 2028.

# 10 — RED FLAG

## 10.1 Ophir — a deal that looks good only in the company's own table

The "value from acquisitions" table shows Ophir NCF of US$568m against a US$541m price. But NCF adds back D&A, deducts only current tax, and counts revenue from assets since sold. Seven years to return 1.05x on a depleting asset is a real return of roughly zero. Tanzania's contingent resources (larger than MEDC's entire 2P) remain undeveloped six years on. The table's headline "proven ability to extract value" rests on Natuna and Corridor.

## 10.2 Guidance language

"Met or exceeded all operational guidance" (FY2025 deck) while the >15% ROE target was missed by ten points, and re-issued for 2026 at the same level with Q1 annualising at ~7%. Small in itself; it shows how the decks are written.

## 10.3 Two numbers for the same thing, repeatedly

FY2024 dividends paid: US$70m (FY2024 deck) vs US$44.9m (FY2025 press release). FY2021 revenue/EBITDA restated down 5–7% between fact books, unexplained. FY2023 2P reserves 539 mmboe in one deck, a waterfall showing 481 for FY2024 in the next, and 493 in the reserves table. DD&A for 2022/2023 transposed between the FY2023 deck and every fact book. Corridor's interest dropping 54→46% with no narrative in the fact books. None of these is large; together they mean deck figures need the audited statements behind them, which were not in hand.

## 10.4 Crude purchases and the related-party channel

Cost of sales carries US$343m of crude purchases (16% of O&G revenue, +148% in 2024). The decks never say from whom or at what price. The obvious counterparties are JOB/PSC partners including Pertamina entities and the family-linked trading/service companies. Until the annual-report RPT note is read, this is the largest unexplained cost line.

## 10.5 Leverage presented on the friendlier basis

Headlines quote RG net debt/EBITDA (2.0x FY2025, 1.7x Q1 2026); consolidated is 2.4x / 2.1x, gross debt US$3.6bn, and finance cost eats 26% of EBITDA. The RG excludes Medco Power's project debt, which is non-recourse but real. Rating agencies' tolerance (Moody's: debt/EBITDA \<3.5x) is measured on the consolidated group.

## 10.6 The "Patriot Bond" contribution

The FY2025 deck lists a contribution to a "Patriot Bond" among debt-management items with no amount, counterparty or purpose. A loan to a state or group vehicle booked inside treasury activity should be explained; it is not.

## 10.7 Checked and found nothing

Auditor's opinion (unqualified per press-release references to audited FY2025 numbers, but the opinion itself not read); PSAK 38 poolings from the parent — none since the 2017 MPI buy-back on the evidence available; treasury-share and buyback mechanics — routine; ESG ratings — MSCI AAA, unusual for an upstream producer and consistent across years.
