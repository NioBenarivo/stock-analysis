A single gold mine in North Sulawesi, dressed as a group. PT Archi Indonesia Tbk owns the Toka Tindung
gold mine through two subsidiaries, MSM and TTN, which hold the two mining contracts the mine sits on.
Around that mine sit a gold-bar retail business, two service companies, a Singapore holding shell, a
quarter-share in the contractor that digs the ore, and a five-percent sliver of a geothermal power
project. Everything the company earns comes from the mine.

## THE ONE THING TO UNDERSTAND FIRST

Toka Tindung produced 261 thousand ounces of gold in 2019. In 2025 it produced 122 thousand — less
than half. Over those same six years the company's total assets grew by two thirds and the money it
owes to banks, less the cash it holds, went from about US$294 million to about US$475 million. So the
mine got smaller and the balance sheet got bigger.

FY2025 nevertheless looks like a triumph: profit went up almost tenfold. That is not the mine
improving. It is the gold price. Gold averaged US$1,403 an ounce in 2019 and US$3,528 an ounce for
what ARCI actually sold in 2025. Sell half as much metal at two and a half times the price and revenue
rises anyway. Strip the price out and what is left is a mine digging more rock every year to recover
fewer ounces from it.

The reason the mine shrank is grade — how much gold sits in each tonne of rock fed to the mill. In
2018 each tonne carried about 4.1 grams. In 2025 it carried about 1.2 grams. The mill runs at the
same size it has for four years; the ore going into it is roughly a quarter as rich. That single fact
drives everything downstream: the cost of producing an ounce, the amount of waste rock that has to be
moved to reach the ore, and how long the mine has left.

Here is the problem. The one number that would tell an outsider whether that grade decline is the ore
body running out or a temporary consequence of which pit is being mined this year is the reserve
statement — the audited estimate of how many ounces are economically recoverable, and at what grade.
**ARCI has not published one since 31 December 2020.** Five and a half years of digging, roughly 644
thousand ounces taken out of the ground, several announced discoveries, and no updated figure. Worse,
the company used to carry the 2020 reserve number — 3.9 million ounces — as a bullet on the milestone
timeline printed at the front of its annual report. It appears in the FY2021, FY2022 and FY2023
reports. It is **absent from FY2024 and FY2025**, while the older 2019 reserve bullet stays. A number
was removed, and nothing was put in its place.

That absence is not a presentational quibble. The largest single item on ARCI's balance sheet after the
mine's plant and equipment is **mine properties, US$556 million** — the accumulated cost of opening up
the ore body. It is written off against production using the reserve as the denominator: the more
ounces you believe are left, the smaller the annual charge. Nobody outside the company can check that
denominator, and the charge has swung between US$12 million and US$37 million a year without any
explanation offered in any filing. Each of those swings is larger than the company's entire head-office
cost.

So the honest way to read ARCI is this: it is a leveraged bet on the gold price attached to a mine
whose remaining life is not disclosed, run by a controlling family whose demonstrated habit across
four decades is to build businesses and then sell them. Everything else in this report is detail
hanging off that. The reserve question is picked up in **§05** and **§10**; the grade-and-cost
mechanics in **§02** and **§08**; the ownership and what the owner has actually done with the company's
cash in **§01**.

At the top sits one man. Peter Sondakh holds a token 0.01% of PT Rajawali Corpora directly and
controls the rest through PT Danaswara Utama, a private company whose own shareholders are disclosed
nowhere. Rajawali Corpora is the family's holding vehicle — a company that owns other companies rather
than trading itself — founded in 1984 and today spanning palm oil, television, hotels, property and
this gold mine.

**The mine is held twice over, and the two holders trade with each other.** MSM and TTN each hold a

each appoints the other as its *exclusive* provider of exploration, mining, supervision, geology,
processing, site support, refining and tailings services. In plain terms: essentially the whole
operation is cross-charged between two companies under identical ownership, on terms that have never
been disclosed. That is where profit gets located between the two, and an outsider cannot see the
pricing. The prospectus notes that TTN booked 91.6% of group pre-tax profit on 65.8% of revenue —
which tells you the cross-charging is not neutral.

Utama, which holds nearly all of Rajawali Corpora. PT Wijaya Anugerah Cemerlang, which held a token
share in ARCI and in KKM and pledged its KKM shares to the banks. PT Royal Raffles Capital, which owns
49% of the gold-bar business. And PT Manado Karya Anugrah and PT Global Ekuitas Nusantara, which
between them own 75% of the mining contractor. The chain that is supposed to run "down to the
individual level" stops one hop short in each case.

## 1.3 History — recap

Four things repeat.

**The mine was bought, not built, by this family.** The ore body was found and developed by a British
company. Rajawali bought into it in 2009, took it fully private in 2013, moved the pieces around
between its own companies from 2014 to 2018, and listed the result in 2021. None of those internal
transfers has a disclosed price.

**Cash goes up the chain in large lumps, not in a steady stream.** The company paid US$407 million of
dividends across the eight years covered here, and US$202 million of that landed in 2020 — the year
before listing, funded partly by drawing new bank debt. The listing itself was two thirds a sale by the
parent rather than a raise by the company (§9.5). Then nothing at all for four years. Then US$60
million for FY2025.

**Production peaked in 2019 and has not recovered.** Two pit failures at the same pit, in 2022 and
2024, took out the highest-grade ore. Guidance was missed in 2022, 2023 and 2024, and met once, in
2025.

**Expansion promises are made loudly, then quietly dropped.** In 2021 the company promised a mill of
8.0 million tonnes a year by 2026, producing more than 450 thousand ounces. The target vanished from
every deck between 2022 and 2025, and reappeared in May 2026 as a smaller 6.0 million tonnes with
construction only *starting*.

## 1.4 Owner's character

Read only from the record, not asserted.

**Rajawali builds and sells.** Television (RCTI, sold to MNC), tobacco (Bentoel, sold to British
American Tobacco), telecoms (XL, sold to Axiata), cement (Semen Gresik), taxis (Express) — four
decades of buying, building and exiting. Nothing in the filings says a sale of ARCI is contemplated.
But an investor should hold the pattern in mind, because the controlling shareholder's history is of
selling control of businesses once they are built, and the September 2025 shuffle of 11.9% between two
family vehicles is the kind of tidying that often precedes a transaction.

**The parent's executives run the company.** Four of the five directors of Rajawali Corpora also sat
on ARCI's boards at listing. Today the president commissioner is Rajawali's former managing director
for mining; the vice president commissioner and one commissioner are both currently Rajawali managing
directors; and the president director of ARCI, Rudy Suhendra, has been a **managing director of PT
Rajawali Corpora since 2025** — that is, the chief executive of the listed company also works for its
controlling shareholder. He is simultaneously president commissioner of MSM, TTN, KKM, JPP and TTG,
president director of EMAS, and a director of ARPTE.

**Governance arrived immediately before the listing.** The audit committee was formed on 10 February
2021 and "carried out no activities in 2020". The nomination and remuneration committee was formed on
7 May 2021 and "carried out no activities in 2019". The internal audit unit and its charter date from
10 February 2021. The IPO was in June.

**Independence is a label doing thin work in places.** Of the three independent commissioners today,
one — Dr Bambang Setiawan — was president commissioner of TTN from 2013 to 2019 and before that
Director General of Minerals and Coal at the energy ministry, the regulator of these very Contracts of
Work. Another, Hamid Awaluddin, was a commissioner of PT Archi Indonesia itself from 2014 to 2019.
Both are now labelled independent. The nomination and remuneration committee is chaired by an
independent commissioner but its other two members are the two Rajawali managing directors.

**Boards do not change.** From FY2022 through FY2024 the six commissioners and four directors were
identical, with each report stating explicitly that nothing changed. One director resigned in June
2025 and was not replaced, leaving three.

**Disclosure narrows over time.** Cost-of-sales detail ran to fourteen separate lines through FY2021
and collapsed to eight from FY2022, with everything else swept into "Others". General and
administrative detail went from ten lines to three. The full customer list stops after FY2023. The
reserve figure stops after 2020. The chart naming Peter Sondakh as ultimate owner disappeared from the
FY2024 report entirely, then returned in FY2025. Nothing was ever added back.

There are four separate channels through which money moves between companies under the same control,
and the accounts show only one of them.

**The contractor.** GMA does the loading and hauling. It is a quarter owned by ARCI through a dormant
subsidiary and three quarters owned by two companies nobody has to identify. Its price has never been
disclosed — the prospectus lists the contract value as "not stipulated" in both the affiliate table and
the third-party table. It is now a fifth to a quarter of everything the group spends to produce gold.

**The two mine-holders charging each other.** MSM and TTN each appoint the other as exclusive provider
of essentially every activity at the mine. No price is disclosed.

and to nobody else. Its revenue rose from about Rp36 billion to about Rp50 billion across 2018–2020.

arbitration in Singapore — an offshore forum for two Indonesian companies under identical control.

**Intercompany lending.** MSM lends to TTN under a 2015 agreement extended to 2025; ARPTE lends to MSM
under a 2011 agreement with **no term at all**, ending only when the MSM Contract of Work expires. No
principal and no interest rate is given for either.

None of these four is priced in public. Only the first appears in the related-party note.

## 2.1 Gold Mining — the Toka Tindung mine

### Product

The mine sells **doré** — rough metal bars poured on site that are roughly 30–45% gold and 55–70%
silver, with about 1–5% of other metals mixed in. Doré is not saleable to a jeweller or a bank; it has
to go to a refinery first, where it is separated and purified to 99.99% gold and 99.95% silver. Only
then does it become the product a buyer wants.

That matters for two reasons. First, Indonesian law forbids exporting gold below 99% purity, so ARCI
physically cannot sell abroad without a refiner in the chain. Second, a large share of what comes out
of the ground is silver, and **ARCI does not treat silver as revenue at all** — it nets the silver
proceeds off against costs, inside a line called other operating income. So the reported revenue line
is a gold-only line, and the silver shows up as a small credit somewhere else.

### Supply chain — the physical asset map

There is no supply chain in the ordinary sense. The company owns the rock, digs it, grinds it and pours
metal. What it buys in is contractor labour and equipment, electricity, diesel, explosives, steel
grinding balls and chemicals — and it buys **no ore from third parties at all**, which distinguishes it
from many Indonesian miners.

Two dependencies sit inside that chain and neither is under ARCI's control. Power comes from the state
utility PLN — 25 MW drawn against 60 MW available, with a 15 MW diesel generator as backup that can run
the plant at only two thirds of capacity. And refining historically ran through Antam, one of very few
Indonesian refiners on the London Bullion Market Association's accredited list. The prospectus admits
outright that a replacement would be hard to find, and shows what dependence costs: Antam closes over
the year-end holidays, so in December 2019 ARCI could not refine or sell, and a whole month's revenue
slid into January 2020.

### Purchase — what the business owns

**The concession.** Two Contracts of Work, signed in 1986 and 1997, amended together on 23 December
2015 for thirty years. MSM's expires 5 March 2041; TTN's 10 March 2041. A Contract of Work is a
contract with the state rather than a licence, which historically meant its fiscal terms were fixed at
signing. Under the 2020 mining law no new ones can be granted; on expiry a holder may apply to convert
to a licence called an IUPK, and ARCI says it is guaranteed two ten-year extensions in that form —
though the printed footnote adds "subject to fulfilment of certain requirements", and the prospectus's
own legal chapter says the extent of the adjustments required "remains uncertain".

The company does **not own the land**. The Contracts of Work grant mining rights over the ground;
surface land is bought plot by plot from villagers as mining advances. Nine plots bought in 2018–2019,
about 41 hectares, sit in **KKM — the management-services company, not either mine-holder** — and none
was certified at the time of the IPO.

### Process — how the gold is made and what it costs

Ore is drilled and blasted, loaded and hauled to the plant, crushed, then ground in a semi-autogenous
mill and two ball mills into a slurry. The slurry goes through nine leaching tanks over roughly a day,
where cyanide dissolves the gold and silver and activated carbon soaks them up. The carbon is stripped,
the metal plated out onto cathodes, and the cathodes smelted into doré bars. What is left is detoxified
and pumped to the tailings facility, where about 70% of the water is recovered and reused.

The physics of this business reduce to one equation: **ounces produced = tonnes milled × grade ×
recovery.** ARCI controls the first with capital and the third with metallurgy. It does not control the
second.

### Distribute — the government's cut, the price, and who buys

**The government takes its share before anyone else.** Indonesia charges gold miners a royalty on the
value of what they sell. Until April 2025 the published rate for gold was a flat 3.75%. Government
Regulation 19 of 2025, effective 26 April 2025, replaced it with a sliding scale tied to the government
reference gold price: 7% below US$1,800 an ounce, rising in steps to **16% above US$3,000**. Gold has
been above US$3,000 since 2025 and is above US$4,400 today, so ARCI sits in the top band and stays
there unless the metal falls by roughly a third.

**GMA** is the one that matters. It was incorporated on 2 June 2020, obtained its mining services

metre at the Toka pit, Rp36,672 at the South pit, and Rp8,212 per cubic metre per kilometre of extra

Contract of Work holder from appointing an affiliated mining service company at all except with
ministerial approval, after an advertised tender, and with a guarantee that no profit is being
transferred. The prospectus describes the rule and describes the appointment. It never says the approval
was obtained.

million at end-2024 to **US$5.7 million at end-2025**, with a US$6.6 million dividend declared and left

# 04 — SUPPLY

**Mine supply does not respond to price.** World mine production was 3,672 tonnes in 2025, up 1%, having
grown at low single digits while the price trebled since 2018. New mines take a decade from discovery
to first pour. That is why producer margins expanded so violently in 2024 and 2025: the price moved and
the supply could not.

**Costs are rising across the industry, and royalties are the reason.** The global average all-in
sustaining cost — cash costs plus royalties plus the capital needed just to keep producing at the same
rate — reached **US$1,785 an ounce in the first quarter of 2026**, up 16% on a year earlier and the 28th
consecutive annual rise. The largest single driver was royalties, up 85% year on year and now about 12%
of total cost, double their share five years ago. Indonesia's 16% top band is one instance of a
worldwide pattern.

**Indonesia** produced roughly 100 tonnes of gold in 2024, tenth in the world, and its output has been
broadly flat. ARCI is a mid-sized producer within it. On the last ranking published in its own
prospectus, for 2019:

# 05 — KEY VARIABLES

Five things move this company. Ranked by how much operating profit each moves.

**1. The gold price — every dollar of it drops straight through.** ARCI sold 123.5 thousand ounces in
FY2025. Royalty aside, the cost of producing an ounce does not change when the price changes. So a
US$100 move in the realised price is worth about **US$12.4 million of revenue**, of which roughly
US$10.4 million reaches operating profit after the 16% royalty. Against FY2025 operating profit of
US$204 million, that is 5% of the profit for a 3% move in the price. There is no hedging disclosed
since FY2021, so the exposure is unhedged as far as any reader can tell.

**2. Head grade — the variable the company does not control and no longer reports.** Ounces produced
equal tonnes milled times grade times recovery. Milling 3.7 million tonnes at 1.18 grams a tonne with
87.8% recovery gives about 123 thousand ounces, which is what FY2025 delivered. Move the grade to 1.0
grams and the same mill and the same recovery give **about 104 thousand ounces** — 14% less gold
for identical cost, which at FY2025 prices is roughly **US$60 million of revenue and almost all of it
profit**. Grade has fallen every year but one since 2018 and now sits at the reserve average, so there
is no cushion left. This is the single most important number about ARCI and it has not appeared in an
annual report since FY2023.

**3. Mill throughput — the only lever management holds.** The mill has been stuck at 4.0 million tonnes
of nameplate capacity since 2021, and the plan is 6.0 million. Each extra million tonnes at current
grade and recovery is worth about **33 thousand ounces**, or roughly US$116 million of revenue at
FY2025 prices, against a variable cost of maybe US$50 million. See the scenario in §08.

**4. The royalty band.** At 16% of gross revenue, the state's share is now about **US$550 an ounce** at
a US$3,400 gold price. If gold fell to US$2,900 the band would drop to 15%, and to US$2,400 it would be
12% — so the royalty partly cushions a falling price, and sharply amplifies the give-back on a rising
one. Between the FY2024 and FY2025 rate the royalty line alone took **US$42 million** more out of the
company.

**5. Contractor cost.** Mining contractor services are the largest single cost line, US$119 million in
FY2025, and about a fifth to a quarter of all costs goes to a related party whose price nobody outside
the company can see. A 10% move in that line is US$12 million of operating profit — the same order as a
US$100 move in the gold price.

Two more sit just below the threshold: **the strip ratio**, which determines how much waste must be
moved per tonne of ore and is no longer reported at all; and **the cost of debt**, since roughly US$500
million of borrowings at 7–8% costs about US$36 million a year of interest before any principal is
repaid.

# 08 — SECTION

## 8.1 What it actually costs ARCI to produce an ounce

The company publishes an all-in sustaining cost — cash costs plus royalties plus the capital needed
just to keep producing — in some years and not others. The two years it skipped, FY2022 and FY2023,
are the two worst ones.

| US$ per ounce | FY2018 | FY2019 | FY2020 | FY2021 | FY2022 | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **Company's all-in sustaining cost** | 613 | 684 | 823 | 1,260 | *not published* | *not published* | 1,841 | **2,224** |
| Company's cash operating cost | 389 | 449 | 505 | n/d | n/d | n/d | n/d | n/d |
| **My proxy: mining segment operating cost per ounce sold** `[calculated]` | 576 | 753 | 740 | 1,047 | **1,366** | **1,432** | 1,526 | 1,842 |
| Realised gold price | 1,271 | 1,403 | 1,807 | 1,796 | 1,810 | 1,952 | 2,401 | 3,528 |
| **Margin per ounce on the company's own cost** `[calculated]` | 658 | 719 | 984 | 536 | — | — | 560 | **1,304** |

*The proxy is gold-mining segment revenue less segment operating profit, divided by ounces sold. It
includes depreciation and the write-off of mine properties, which the company's cash-cost measure
excludes, and it excludes sustaining capital, which the all-in measure includes. The two are therefore
not the same thing, but the direction they describe is identical and the proxy is continuous across all
eight years, which the company's own figures are not.*

Three readings follow.

**The cost per ounce has more than trebled in seven years.** On any measure. That is not inflation and
it is not a one-off; it is grade, arithmetically. Half the grade means twice as much rock has to be
mined, hauled, crushed and leached for the same ounce, and almost every cost in this business scales
with tonnes moved rather than with ounces produced.

**ARCI is no longer a low-cost producer.** The IPO prospectus, quoting an industry consultant, placed
Toka Tindung "at the bottom of the first quartile" of the global cost curve in 2019, on an all-in cost
of US$684 against a global average then well above it. In the first quarter of 2026 the global average
all-in sustaining cost was **US$1,785**. ARCI's FY2025 figure was **US$2,224**. The mine has moved from
the cheapest quartile to above the global average in six years. Any note describing ARCI as low-cost
is either quoting 2019 or quoting a cash cost that leaves out the 16% royalty.

**The margin per ounce is at a record and it is entirely price.** US$1,304 an ounce in FY2025 against
US$984 at the previous best in 2020. Cost rose US$1,400 an ounce over that span and the price rose
US$1,721. The business is more profitable per ounce than it has ever been, on a cost base that would
be loss-making at the gold price of 2019.

## 8.2 What the mill expansion is actually worth

Management's plan is to take the mill from 4.0 to 6.0 million tonnes a year, construction starting in
2026. The original 2021 plan was 8.0 million tonnes producing more than 450 thousand ounces. Both
claims can be tested against the physics, because ounces produced equal tonnes milled times grade times
recovery, and that identity reproduces the actual results closely.

*Check on FY2025:* 3.7 Mt × 1.18 g/t = 4.37 tonnes of contained gold = 140.3 thousand ounces ×
87.8% recovery = **123.2 thousand ounces**. Reported: 121.7. The model works.

| Scenario | Tonnes milled | Grade | Recovery | Gold produced | vs FY2025 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| FY2025 actual | 3.7 Mt | 1.18 g/t | 87.8% | 121.7 koz | — |
| **6 Mtpa at today's grade** | 6.0 Mt | 1.18 g/t | 87.8% | **200 koz** | +64% |
| **6 Mtpa at the reserve's average grade** | 6.0 Mt | 1.20 g/t | 87.8% | **203 koz** | +67% |
| 6 Mtpa if grade slips to 1.0 g/t | 6.0 Mt | 1.00 g/t | 87.8% | **169 koz** | +39% |
| The 2021 promise: 8 Mtpa at 2.0 g/t | 8.0 Mt | 2.00 g/t | 87.8% | **452 koz** | +271% |
| 8 Mtpa at the reserve's actual grade | 8.0 Mt | 1.20 g/t | 87.8% | **271 koz** | +123% |

The arithmetic settles the argument. **A 6 million tonne mill takes ARCI back to about 200 thousand
ounces a year — the level it reached in 2016 — and no further.** The 450-thousand-ounce claim from 2021
required not only an 8 million tonne mill but ore at 2.0 grams a tonne, which is 67% above the average
grade of the company's own published reserve and 70% above what it is currently feeding. That
assumption was never justified, and the target has since disappeared.

|---|---:|
| Extra ounces at 6 Mtpa `[calculated]` | ~78 koz |

| **Extra operating profit, before the capital cost** `[calculated]` | **~US$175m** |

ᵃ From the company's own FY2025 unit costs: mining US$12/t, processing US$6/t, general and

That is a large number — it would nearly double operating profit — and it is why the expansion matters
more than anything else management controls. Two cautions. The capital cost has not been disclosed; the
2021 version of this programme was budgeted at US$84 million for a bigger expansion, so the order of
magnitude is knowable but not the number. And the gain is entirely contingent on there being enough ore
at that grade to feed a mill half as large again for long enough to pay for it — which brings the
question back to the reserve.

# 09 — IMPORTANT TRANSACTIONS

## 9.1 Rajawali's takeover of Archipelago Resources plc, 2009–2013

Toka Tindung was found and built by a British company listed on London's junior market. Rajawali bought
in progressively: a first stake in 2009, then about 35% in 2012 for roughly £120 million, and in
September 2013 the remainder at **58 pence a share, an 18% premium, valuing the whole company at
roughly US$541 million**. Archipelago was then delisted and reincorporated in Indonesia. Everything
ARCI owns today traces to that purchase. The price ARCI's own shareholders effectively paid for the
asset, through the internal transfers that followed, is nowhere disclosed.

## 9.2 The internal reorganisation, 2014–2018

Four steps moved the mine from the Archipelago structure into the entity that would be listed: ARCI
acquired ARPTE from Archipelago plc in 2014 "under a restructuring"; acquired 95% of MSM and TTN
directly in 2016 "under a further restructuring"; and reached 99.5% in 2018. **Not one of those steps
is given a deed number, a date, a price, a valuation basis or a named counterparty** in the prospectus,
which confines its formal disclosure to the last three years before the offering. Assets were moved
between companies under one man's control and the consideration is not on the record.

## 9.3 The US$202 million dividend, 2020

In the calendar year before listing, ARCI declared US$74.2 million as a final dividend and a further
US$108 million plus Rp300 billion as an interim, both by written board or shareholder resolution rather
than at a meeting. Total: **US$202,156,417 against a profit of US$123.3 million** — 164% of the year's
earnings. In the same year the bank facility was enlarged by US$81 million **and Rp300 billion**, and
one of the interim dividends is exactly Rp300 billion. (Which of the two 2020 payments was "interim" and
which "final", and their per-share amounts, are stated differently in different filings — see the note
under §7.3.) Equity fell from US$173.8 million to US$94.3
million. Nine months later the IPO raised US$62.6 million, less than a third of what had gone out.

## 9.4 The appointment of GMA, August 2020

Loading and hauling moved from an unaffiliated contractor with published rates to a company incorporated
eleven weeks earlier, 25%-held by ARCI through a dormant subsidiary and 75%-held by two undisclosed
parties, on a five-year contract whose value is recorded as "not stipulated". By FY2025 that contract
accounted for **US$61.6 million, or 21.5% of the group's entire cost of sales**. See §10.

## 9.5 The IPO, 28 June 2021 — two thirds of it was the owner selling

The offer was 3,725,250,000 shares at Rp750, 15% of the company, raising Rp2,793,937,500,000 in total.
That headline hides the structure. Of those shares, **only 1,242,500,000 were new shares issued by the
company. The other 2,482,750,000 were existing shares sold by PT Rajawali Corpora.**

of 2,482,750,000.

Of the Rp905.2 billion the company did receive, **Rp859.9 billion — 95% — repaid bank principal** and
Rp45.3 billion went to working capital. The loan agreement required a mandatory prepayment out of
primary IPO proceeds, so that was not a discretionary choice.

Put the three events together and the shape is clear. In 2020 the company paid its owner US$202
million, partly funded by new borrowing. In June 2021 the owner sold roughly **US$129 million** of
shares to the public, while the company itself raised US$62.6 million and handed 95% of it straight to
the banks that had funded the dividend. The listing moved cash to the parent and debt to the listed
company; almost none of it reached the mine.

The offer was priced at the bottom of an indicative Rp750–800 range, and one underwriter, PT UOB Kay
Hian Sekuritas, took 67% of the book.

## 9.6 The two Araren failures, January 2022 and April 2024

Covered in §06. Together they removed roughly two years of normal production and cost US$16.2 million
in directly identified remediation, on top of the lost ounces.

## 9.7 The 2023 and 2025 refinancings

August 2023: the entire 2018 facility repaid and replaced with US$365 million maturing August 2028.
November 2025: repaid again and replaced with **US$421 million plus Rp475 billion** maturing December

oddities as printed: the named lender participations sum to US$381 million against a stated US$421
million; and the Islamic tranche is stated to have been **fully drawn on 19 November, five days before
its own 24 November agreement date**.

## 9.8 The intra-family transfer, September 2025

On 19 September 2025, **PT Rajawali Kapital Emas** — described only as a Rajawali affiliate — bought
2,967,375,000 shares, about 11.9% of ARCI, from PT Rajawali Corpora and PT Wijaya Anugerah Cemerlang at
**Rp800 a share, roughly Rp2.37 trillion**. Rajawali Corpora's holding fell from 85% to 73%. No shares
reached the public: the free float was unchanged by the deal. The stock was Rp1,005 at the end of that
quarter and Rp1,620 by year end. The company describes it as internal restructuring with no change of
ultimate control, and by the AR2025 register the same buyer had continued adding. Rajawali Kapital
Emas's own owner, incorporation date and other holdings are not disclosed anywhere.

## 9.9 The MESOP exercise, November–December 2025

All **400,000,000 management and employee options were exercised at Rp750** in a six-week window
between 1 November and 12 December 2025. The stock traded as high as Rp1,715 and closed the year at
Rp1,620. Board members took roughly 128 million of the new shares. Four days after the window closed
the company paid its first dividend since listing.

## 9.10 The first dividend since listing, December 2025 and June 2026

US$30 million interim (Rp19.81 a share) declared 1 December and paid 16 December 2025, followed by a
US$30 million final (Rp20.69) approved at the 7 May 2026 meeting and paid 9 June 2026. Combined payout
about 58% of FY2025 profit — the first distribution in four and a half years as a listed company.

explanation in any year. The company also stopped disclosing head grade after FY2023, ore mined and
strip ratio after FY2021, and silver production after FY2020. And the "ore reserves increased to 3.9
Moz" bullet that appeared on the milestone page of the FY2021, FY2022 and FY2023 annual reports is
**absent from FY2024 and FY2025**, while the older 2019 bullet remains. Every physical disclosure that
would let an outsider judge the mine's remaining life has been removed, one at a time, and none has
been replaced.

## 10.2 A fifth of the cost base goes to an affiliate at a price nobody can see

GMA does the loading and hauling, is 25%-held by ARCI through a subsidiary that has never traded, and
is 75%-held by two companies with no disclosed owner. It was incorporated on 2 June 2020 and held the
group's principal mining contract by 19 August 2020. Its price is recorded as "not stipulated" in both
the affiliate table and the third-party table of the prospectus. The contractor it replaced had
published rates. Three separate rules bear on this and none is shown to have been satisfied: the **MSM
Contract of Work** requires affiliate services to be priced no higher than a non-affiliate would
charge; **ESDM Regulation 7/2020** forbids appointing an affiliated mining service company at all
except with ministerial approval after an advertised tender and a guarantee of no profit transfer; and
the FY2023 amount is stated as **US$34.7 million in the FY2023 accounts and US$49.9 million in the
FY2024 management discussion** — a US$15 million disagreement on the single largest related-party
number, with neither document acknowledging the other. Finally, GMA's net assets as carried by ARCI
fell from US$25.2 million to **US$5.7 million** during FY2025, with a US$6.6 million dividend declared
and unpaid, and its contract is described at the year end as "in the process of being extended", which
is to say expired.

## 10.3 A customer worth 12.5% of revenue looks like a related party and is booked as a third party

**PT Lotus Lingga Pratama** took US$62.0 million of FY2025 revenue. It is also one of ARCI's contracted
gold refiners. And the brand under which ARCI's own 51%-held subsidiary sells retail gold bars —
"Lotus Archi" — is a 2019 joint venture between the Lotus Lingga group and Archi, with its head office
at Rajawali Place. The 49% partner in that subsidiary, PT Royal Raffles Capital, is never described. The
accounts nevertheless record Lotus Lingga Pratama as an unrelated third party, and the related-party
note names only GMA.

Separately, **PT Suka Jadi Logam**, the largest single buyer in FY2025 at 30.9% of revenue, is a
Surabaya smelter that the city sealed in July 2024 over emissions. Two other large recent buyers —
PT Swarnim Murni Mulia and PT Indo Prosperity International — have no public footprint at all.

## 10.4 The FY2023 segment note cannot be true as printed

The gold mine reports a **US$14.9 million pre-tax loss**; a 51%-owned trading subsidiary with US$3.9
million of total assets reports **US$57.1 million of pre-tax profit**; internal sales eliminated between
them quadrupled to US$62.5 million; and the outside 49% of that subsidiary is credited with
**US$189,385** of profit and shown holding **US$95,085** of equity. Either the segment allocation or
the minority interest is wrong. The transfer price between the two is described only as "set in a manner
similar to transactions with third parties". If the allocation is right, ARCI has been moving mine
profit into a partly-owned entity without compensating the minority; if the minority interest is right,
the segment note misstates where the group earns its money.

## 10.5 The options: a discount, no charge, and no diluted earnings figure

Four hundred million options at Rp750 exercised into a Rp1,620 stock in six weeks, with roughly 128
million shares going to insiders — a spread of about Rp870 a share, or roughly **Rp348 billion of
value transferred from existing shareholders**. No share-based payment expense appears in the FY2025
income statement; the small option reserve was simply reclassified into share premium. **No diluted
earnings per share is presented in any year**, despite 240 to 400 million options being outstanding
from FY2021 onwards.

## 10.6 US$30 million placed in an unnamed instrument at a below-market coupon

On 21 October 2025 the company put **Rp500 billion into a private debt issue** at a **2% fixed coupon**
— below its own 2.25% rupiah deposit rate and far below its roughly 7–8% cost of borrowing — with
limited transferability and an issuer right to repurchase at nominal. The issuer is not named and no
related-party disclosure is made. The balance sheet carries it at the gross US$29,912,668 while the
note's own working ends at **US$24,789,914** after a US$5.1 million day-one loss that appears nowhere
in the profit statement, the comprehensive income statement or the equity statement.

## 10.7 Loan terms are disclosed to lenders, to the market in 2021, and to nobody since

Every annual report gives the interest margin as "a certain margin" and the covenants as "certain
financial ratios", in all eight years, on a facility that reached US$449 million with a cross-default.
The IPO prospectus disclosed the margin as **3.7%–4.5% over LIBOR** and the FY2021 investor deck
disclosed it as **4.00%** with covenants of total debt to EBITDA below 3.0x, debt service cover above
1.1x and a **reserve tail ratio above 20%**. Net debt to EBITDA reached **5.22x in FY2024**. Whether
that breached the covenants then in force is unknowable, because they stopped being published — and one
of them depends on a reserve figure that also stopped being published.

## 10.8 The tailings dam

It sits in a valley in an active earthquake zone in northern Sulawesi, and by the technical expert's own
description "**the processing plant is immediately downstream of the dam, as is the Toka open pit**". It
is rated High-A consequence under the Australian dam guidelines, the second-highest category. Roughly
**25 hectares of the concession used for the tailings facility sits inside limited production forest
without the required forest-use approval**, and at the time of the IPO the company was "calculating the
administrative fine payable" — a conceded breach with no amount attached, and no subsequent filing says
how it was resolved. The dewatering effluent at Araren is separately described as unsuitable for direct
discharge because of high salinity, temperature, arsenic and boron.

## 10.9 The royalty rate stepped up twice before the law changed

Royalty as a share of the mine's gross revenue went 5.0% (FY2020–FY2022) → **8.4% (FY2023)** →
**10.0% (FY2024)** → 14.9% (FY2025). Only the last step is explained by PP 19/2025. The two earlier
steps, which together cost about US$12 million a year, are unexplained in every filing. Either the line
carries levies beyond royalty, or the Contract of Work terms changed, or the calculation base is not
what it appears. The accounts do not say.

## 10.10 The free float has less than one and a half points of cushion, and the family is consolidating

The Indonesia Stock Exchange raised the minimum public float from 7.5% to 15% on 31 March 2026. ARCI
stands at **16.35%**, and it only got there because 400 million option shares were issued into the
public count in December 2025 and a 6.17% holder, PT Basis Utama Prima, moved into "public". The
September 2025 transfer of 11.9% moved shares between two family vehicles and created no float at all.
A controlling group that has spent 2025 consolidating its holding, in a company one and a third
percentage points above a hard regulatory floor, is worth watching.

**Checked and found clean.** The auditor has been EY Indonesia in every year of the period. The
prospectus names Indrajuwana Komala Widjaja as the signing partner for FY2018–FY2020 with an unmodified
opinion carrying an other-matter paragraph; the FY2022 report's own fee table names Dede Rusli for
FY2021 and FY2022; the FY2023 and FY2024 reports name him again, with an unmodified opinion in FY2023;
and FY2025 is signed by Edward Dharmadi with an unmodified opinion. The firm changed its name from
Purwantono, Sungkoro & Surja to Purwanto, Susanti & Surja in 2025, which is why the FY2025 report
describes the engagement as a first-year assignment without mentioning that the firm is the same one.
The audit fee ran Rp6.27 billion in 2020 (the IPO year), then Rp600m, Rp620m, Rp655m, Rp655m and
Rp700m for FY2021 to FY2025. Realised gold prices track the London benchmark with no persistent
discount. Physical volumes reconcile to segment revenue within 0.3% in every year the two overlap. No
covenant breach, waiver or distressed restructuring is disclosed anywhere. No administrative sanction
was received in 2025. The only litigation disclosed is a civil claim **brought by** the company against
an insurer over an unpaid claim.

# MISSING DATA AND OPEN ITEMS

Ranked by how much each would change the analysis.

**1. A current ore reserve and mineral resource statement.** The last one is dated 31 December 2020.
Without it, the remaining mine life, the depletion charge on US$556 million of mine properties, the
reserve-tail loan covenant and the whole expansion case are untestable. *Where it likely exists:* an
internal JORC or KCMI update almost certainly exists, since the banks require reserve reports and mine
plans under the loan agreement. *What it would decide:* whether the grade decline is pit sequencing or
depletion — the single question this report cannot answer.

**2. The price GMA charges.** Never disclosed, in any document, for a contract that is now a fifth of
all costs and a fifth of the group's cost of sales, awarded to an affiliate under a Contract of Work
clause that caps affiliate pricing. *Where it likely exists:* the contract itself, the ministerial
approval file under ESDM 7/2020, and GMA's own filed accounts. *What it would decide:* whether roughly
US$60 million a year of value is leaving the listed company.

**3. Ore mined, waste mined, strip ratio and tonnes milled, FY2022 onwards.** Not disclosed in any
filing after FY2021. Without the strip ratio there is no way to see whether the cost increase is grade,
haulage distance or contractor pricing.

**4. Which FY2023 related-party number is right** — US$34.7 million or US$49.9 million. A 44% swing on
the largest related-party line, which also flips the year-on-year direction.

**5. The reconciliation of the FY2023 segment note to the non-controlling interest.** As printed the two
cannot both be right.

**6. The interest margin and the covenant thresholds** on a US$500 million debt book. Disclosed in 2021,
withheld since.

**7. A cost per tonne series, and an all-in sustaining cost for FY2022 and FY2023.** The two years the
company skipped are the two worst.

**8. The identity of PT Danaswara Utama's owners, PT Royal Raffles Capital, PT Manado Karya Anugrah,
PT Global Ekuitas Nusantara and PT Rajawali Kapital Emas.** Five entities inside or adjacent to the
control chain with no disclosed ownership. *Where it likely exists:* the Indonesian company registry.

**9. The issuer of the Rp500 billion, 2% instrument** bought in October 2025, and the accounting for the
US$5.1 million difference between the balance-sheet figure and the note.

**10. Whether MSM and TTN are contractually shielded from PP 19/2025.** A Contract of Work traditionally
freezes fiscal terms; ARCI's royalty line suggests it is not shielded, but no filing says so. At a 16%
top band this is worth roughly US$70 million a year.

**11. Any hedging position.** Hedging contracts are pledged as security in every year and hedging costs
appear in finance costs through FY2021 — after which there is no derivative asset, liability, gain,
loss or notional disclosed anywhere. Whether the company is hedged into 2026 cannot be determined.

**12. Which year the 2019 and 2020 dividends belong to, and their per-share amounts.** The audited
accounts and the management discussion disagree, and the FY2021 and FY2022 reports label the same
US$108 million payment differently. The total is not in doubt; the attribution is.

**13. The reclamation and mine-closure cost estimate.** Only the provision (US$13.7 million) and the
guarantees posted are given. No total liability estimate for a 39,817-hectare concession.

**14. The capital cost of the 4→6 Mtpa mill expansion.** Announced in May 2026 with no budget.

**15. Sources never supplied.** No quarterly or half-year financial statements were available for any
year, so the FY2026 picture rests on press coverage of the Q1 result. No FY2019 or FY2020 annual report
exists as a filing (the company listed in 2021), so those years come only from the prospectus. No
sustainability reports were supplied.

**16. Documents worth fetching next.** The Independent Technical Assessment Report on Toka Tindung is
public and was read for this analysis; a post-2020 update, if one exists, is not. BankTrack maintains a
campaign page covering objections to the mine from 2003 to 2012 — permit sequencing, cyanide and
groundwater, tailings in a seismic zone, and a 2007 mud flood affecting about 400 villagers, which led
Credit Suisse to withdraw a US$48 million facility in 2008 — but the record there stops in 2015. A
February 2026 allegation by a local NGO about turbidity in the Marawuwung river is unverified, carries
no company response, and is repeated here only as an attributed allegation, not a fact.
