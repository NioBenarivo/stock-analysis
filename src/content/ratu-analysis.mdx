**What it does:** RATU is a Jakarta-based holding company with eleven employees. It owns minority, non-operated stakes in three Indonesian oil and gas blocks and runs none of them. It is the upstream arm of **PT Rukun Raharja Tbk (RAJA)**, which owns about 68.7% of it.

- **Jabung (Jambi).** RATU owns 8% of the block through its subsidiary PT Raharja Energi Tanjung Jabung (RETJ, 99%). The block produces oil, gas and LPG and is operated by PetroChina. It is consolidated and is the **only source of reported revenue**.
- **Cepu / Banyu Urip (East Java).** RATU owns 49% of PT Petrogas Jatim Utama Cendana (PJUC), which holds a 2.2423% interest in the block. ExxonMobil operates it. PJUC is equity-accounted, so Cepu never appears in revenue.
- **Madura Strait (East Java, gas).** Since June 2026 RATU has held this through PT Raharja Energi Madura (REM), which it owns 51%. REM owns 100% of SMS Development Ltd, which owns 20% of Husky-CNOOC Madura Ltd (HCML), the 100% holder of the PSC.

RATU reports in US dollars. Everything is in Indonesia.

**Market:** Rp3,980 per share (Ajaib, 29-Sep-2026) × 2,715,053,800 shares = **Rp10.81tn ≈ US$606m** market cap [calculated at Rp17,850/US$, the rate implied by the Company's own 1H26 profit translation].
- Trailing P/E is 27.6x and P/BV 10.7x (Ajaib).
- The 52-week range is Rp3,620–12,900. The IPO price was Rp1,150 on 8-Jan-2025.
- A 10% private placement (271.5m new shares) was approved on 8-Sep-2026 and has not yet been executed.

## THE ONE THING TO UNDERSTAND FIRST

**RATU is a small, cash-generative, non-operated oil and gas portfolio. For 2025 it earned about US$15m. The market prices it at roughly 40x that year's earnings and 10–11x book. Since the IPO it has turned itself into a leveraged acquisition vehicle, and the price the market pays is for the acquisitions, not for the assets it already had.**

Five facts, each evidenced later:

1. **The IPO was mostly a sale by RAJA, not a capital raise.**
   - Of the 543.0m shares offered at Rp1,150, only **190.1m (35%) were new**, raising Rp218.6bn (~US$13.4m) for RATU.
   - **352.96m (65%) were RAJA's own shares, and RAJA took Rp405.9bn.**
   - RAJA's stake then fell from 80.0% after the IPO to ~69.9% by August 2025. That is roughly **274m more shares sold in the first seven months of trading**, when the stock ran between Rp1,435 and Rp9,900.
   - Neither annual report says who bought them or at what price (§1.2).
   - *(This also corrects the RAJA teardown in this project, which assumed the IPO issued 20% new shares.)*

2. **RATU's stated interests overstate what it owns.**
   - **Cepu:** the "2.2423% PI" belongs to PJUC. RATU owns 49% of PJUC, so its **look-through interest is ~1.10%** [calculated].
   - **Madura:** the "20% PI" is held by REM, which RATU owns 51%. A partner the 2025 annual report does not name paid in US$7.36m for the other 49%. RATU's **look-through is ~10.2%** [calculated].
   - Every headline percentage is roughly double the economics that reach a RATU shareholder.

3. **The legacy business is shrinking; earnings rose only on costs and other income.**
   - FY2025 revenue fell 14.6% to US$49.3m on lower Jabung volumes and a lower ICP (Indonesia's official crude price).
   - Net profit rose 9% to US$15.2m only because Jabung's lifting costs fell 31%.
   - Cepu's gross output was ~151k bopd in 2025. It fell to **~127–130k bopd by May–June 2026**, against a 148.5k target (§2.2).
   - Management's own 2026 plan (RKAP) guides Jabung volumes down 8–13%.

4. **2026 growth is an acquisition plus an accounting gain, funded with debt.**
   - 1H26 net profit doubled to US$15.5m on revenue up only 2%. Management names a **bargain-purchase gain on Madura** as a driver; the amount is not disclosed in anything read.
   - Total assets tripled from US$76m to US$232m, and liabilities rose from US$19m to US$167m (DER 2.42x).
   - **The FY2026 net-profit target of US$26m implies only ~US$10.5m in 2H26**, below 1H's pace, with a full half of Madura included (§3.7).

5. **On a normalised basis the valuation is aggressive** (§3.6) [calculated].
   - At about **US$22 per boe of look-through 2P reserves** and **~US$80k per flowing boe/d**, RATU is priced several times above what non-operated Indonesian barrels usually change hands for.
   - Normalised attributable earnings are about **US$16–20m**, against a US$606m market cap.
   - Listed peers: MEDC trades at 6.3x P/E and 0.9x book; ENRG at 20x and 2.1x.

The investment question is therefore not whether RATU's assets are good. Jabung and Cepu are good, cheap-to-hold barrels, and the Company converts profit to cash very well. The question is **whether management can keep buying assets like Madura at bargain prices fast enough to grow into a roughly 40x valuation, while its legacy production declines and its balance sheet goes from net cash to leveraged.**

**Cross-collateralisation runs both ways.**
- RETJ's Mandiri loan is secured by RETJ receivables, **RAJA's Thamrin Residence and Patal Senayan office buildings** (Rp146.8bn), and corporate guarantees plus cash-deficit undertakings from RAJA, Triguna and Heksa (both RAJA subsidiaries).
- In the other direction, RATU's receivables are fiduciary collateral for a Mandiri facility to RAJA, which is why RAJA now pays RATU a guarantee fee.
- For a minority RATU shareholder, the group's credit is one pool.

**The IPO proceeds that did not stay deployed.**
- RATU reports net IPO proceeds of Rp212.2bn as "100% realised": Rp157.3bn lent to RETJ and Rp34.9bn to PJUC for cash calls, plus Rp19.9bn of working capital.
- At year-end no such loans are outstanding. Instead the parent holds **Rp100bn of Danantara "Patriot Bonds" at a 2% coupon** and Rp10bn in money-market funds.
- Meanwhile RETJ still pays 7.5% on its Mandiri loan.
- The loans were made, repaid inside the year, and the cash was parked in a 2% bond issued by the state investment agency. That is a policy-flavoured use of IPO money, and it is not explained.

## 2.7 Demand and 2.8 Supply

- **Demand.** Indonesia is a net oil importer with a national lifting target of ~605–610k bopd; all crude finds a buyer at ICP. East Java gas demand exceeds supply, and Madura is ~30% of regional supply, so the gas has a market. RATU is a pure price- and volume-taker.
- **Supply and competition.** RATU's competition is for **acquisitions**, not customers. It is bidding for non-operated interests against MEDC, Pertamina, regional players and funds. Its stated plan is non-operated interests in large PSCs for years 1–3, operating smaller PSCs in years 3–5, and operating larger PSCs in years 6–10. It has **11 employees** and no operating capability today.

# SUMMARY VERDICT

**What it is.** A 20-year-old shell that held 49% of a Cepu partner company until 2023. Since then RAJA has turned it into an upstream acquisition vehicle: first Jabung (2023, debt-funded), then an IPO in which RAJA sold roughly twice as many shares as RATU issued, then Madura (2026, debt-funded, bought with a 49% partner). It owns good, cheap-to-hold non-operated barrels and converts profit into cash exceptionally well. It has 11 staff, operates nothing, and its legacy production is declining.

**What it earns through the cycle.** About US$14–15m a year at US$70–80 Brent on the legacy assets. With Madura net of acquisition interest and minorities, roughly **US$10–16m attributable in a normal year**, before any further deals [calculated, §3.7].

**What the price requires.** At ~US$606m (~40x FY2025 earnings, ~10.7x book, ~US$22 per look-through 2P barrel), the market is paying for the **roll-up**. RATU must keep buying producing barrels at ~US$5–10/boe, fund them with debt and expensive equity, and keep the equity expensive. The Company has done exactly this once (Madura), with a bargain-purchase gain to show for it.

**What would break it.**
- Cepu decline running into lower oil.
- No extension for the Madura PSC.
- A re-rating of RATU's thinly traded, institution-heavy float.
- An acquisition bought from, or alongside, related parties on terms that suit the controller more than the minority.

**The record so far gives minorities reasons for caution.** The controller monetised about 23 points of RATU (13pp in the IPO plus ~10pp afterwards) while the stock ran 3–8x above the IPO price, and the annual reports never disclosed the post-IPO sales. The IPO money was parked in a 2% state bond. Board pay tripled. Two Barito-group executives sit on the board unexplained. The only partner in the flagship deal is undisclosed.

**Position.** RATU is a well-run cash harvester wrapped in an expensive acquisition story. On the documents in hand it looks **significantly overvalued on normalised earnings**. The upside depends on deal flow and on the share price staying high enough to fund that deal flow. For a RAJA holder the risk is concentrated: half of RAJA's value sits on RATU's multiple.

*This is an analytical view of the business from filings and press coverage, not investment advice. I'm not a financial advisor.*
