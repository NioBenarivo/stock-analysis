**What it does:** Indonesia's second-largest private fixed-line network owner (by its own claim), with 4.62 million homes passed (HP) across ~50 cities and regencies, 33,861 km of fibre and 16,346 km of legacy coaxial cable at end-2025. Until September 2024 it also ran the First Media consumer brand, selling broadband and pay-TV to ~750,000 households. It sold that retail business to its sister company PT XL Axiata (now PT XLSMART Telecom Sejahtera Tbk) and now operates as three wholesale units:

- **FiberCo:** leases its network to XLSMART and to about 12–15 regional ISPs.
- **EnterpriseCo:** provides corporate connectivity and ICT to ~2,359 companies.
- **MediaCo:** wholesales pay-TV content, mostly to XLSMART.

All operations and revenue are in Indonesia, and the company reports in Rupiah.

**Why this report exists:** since the 2024 carve-out, one related party (XLSMART) supplies 70.9% of revenue. The company lost Rp1.45tn in 2025 and Rp852bn more in 1H2026. Every rupiah of its Rp7.0tn bank debt fell due in 2026. Its controlling shareholder, Axiata Group Berhad (Malaysia), has been trying to sell it since 2024. This report asks what a minority shareholder actually owns here and on whose terms that value is set.

**Market:** Rp1,660/share at the 30-Sep-2026 close (IDNFinancials) × ~2,750m shares outstanding (ex-treasury) = **Rp4.57tn market cap**. Net debt at 31-Dec-2025 was Rp6.64tn, giving **EV ≈ Rp11.2tn**, or **13.0x FY2025 EBITDA** [calculated]. The company is loss-making, so it has no P/E. The share price is set by a free float of only ~7.5% and has swung between Rp935 and Rp5,300 over the past year, with two IDX trading suspensions in 2025 for abnormal price rises.

**Period covered:** FY2022–FY2025 audited (three annual reports, each with a prior-year comparative), FY2020–2021 from comparative and highlights tables, 1H2026 from press coverage of the interim filing, plus four public-expose decks (Nov-2022 to Dec-2025) and the Dec-2025 expose Q&A.

**Conventions:** Rp billion (bn) unless stated; the filings print Rp million. `[calculated]` marks every derived figure. "Continuing" means excluding the residential/B2C business sold in September 2024. That business is shown as a discontinued operation in FY2024 and in the restated FY2023 comparatives. EBITDA is computed as revenue minus all operating costs except depreciation and amortisation, and includes receivable impairment. On this basis it matches the "operating income" the company prints in its 2025 targets table.

## THE ONE THING TO UNDERSTAND FIRST

**LINK sold its customers to its sister company, now rents its network back to that same company at undisclosed prices, and that one relationship now sets both LINK's revenue and its solvency.**

The sequence, all inside the Axiata group, runs as follows:

1. **June 2022:** Axiata and XL Axiata buy 66% of LINK from Lippo/First Media and CVC (Asia Link Dewa) at Rp4,800/share. The mandatory tender offer that followed takes the Axiata group to 95.6% and leaves a public float of 0.47% [AR2023 p.467; web].
2. **Immediately after:** LINK pays a Rp531bn dividend (FY2021, 60% payout, resolved 14-Nov-2022, six weeks after the tender offer settled), ~99.5% of which goes to Axiata and XL.
3. **May–September 2024:** LINK sells its entire residential business (~750k customers, the First Media brand now rebranded "XL SATU", and staff) to XL for **Rp1,875bn**. That price is **~2.5x the segment's FY2023 pre-tax profit of Rp735.5bn** [AR2024 Note 10; calculated]. The carrying amount of what was sold was just **Rp8.5bn**: the network, set-top boxes and modems stayed with LINK. The deal was approved at an independent-shareholder meeting where only ~42m shares (1.5% of the company) were eligible to vote. 30.4m of them voted in favour [AR2024 p.143, 174]. Because the buyer was under common control, the Rp1,866.5bn gain went straight to equity (APIC) and never touched the P&L. The tax on it **did** hit the P&L. The gain was fully taxable: Rp1,869bn of "other taxable income" created a Rp411bn swing in the tax reconciliation and turned a loss-making year into Rp182.6bn of current tax, paid in 2025 [AR2024 Note 15; AR2025 cash flow].
4. **From September 2024:** LINK leases the network (HFC and FTTH) back to XL under a 10+5-year Master Service Agreement. It also wholesales its pay-TV content to XL under a Content Reseller Agreement (CRA), and XL keeps a margin. **None of the prices is disclosed**, in any filing or deck. A 4-Dec-2024 side letter amended the "minimum commitment rate" to an undisclosed level. A 31-Oct-2025 amendment ended XLSMART's exclusivity in several regions [AR2025 p.359–360].

The results are visible in three numbers:

| | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Related-party revenue, % of revenue | **0.36%** | 26.3% of continuing [calculated] | **70.9%** |
| Group EBITDA (Rp bn) | 1,611 (total, incl. B2C) | 642 (continuing) | 859 |
| Net profit (Rp bn) | (533) | (1,185) | (1,449) |

Between 2023 and 2025 homes passed rose 30%, from 3.55m to 4.62m, while group EBITDA fell 47% [calculated]. The retail margin that used to sit inside LINK now sits inside XLSMART. Axiata owns 34.7% of XLSMART and 69.4% of LINK, so for Axiata this is a transfer between two pockets. For the ~7.5% public float of LINK, it is not.

What remains is a network business that **does not cover its own depreciation**. FY2025 EBITDA of Rp859bn compares with depreciation and amortisation of Rp1,758bn and finance costs of Rp514bn. **Per average home passed, LINK earned ~Rp198k of EBITDA and spent ~Rp405k on D&A and ~Rp118k on interest** [calculated, §3.5].

At 31-Dec-2025 all Rp7.0tn of bank debt was current, maturing June–November 2026. Cash was Rp361bn, the current ratio 13.2%, and lenders had waived "any potential or actual" breach of financial covenants [AR2025 p.339–340]. The replacement funding is a Rp5.81tn, 9-year IFC and ADB facility signed 18-Dec-2025 and undrawn at year-end. It is Rp1.19tn short of the maturities.

PwC's unmodified opinion (12-Feb-2026) carries **no material-uncertainty paragraph on going concern**. It does, however, flag as a key audit matter that **"indicators of impairment were identified"** on the Rp11.6tn of non-financial assets. Management tested them against a 10-year revenue-growth assumption of **2.72%–21.52% a year** and booked no impairment [AR2025 PDF p.276; Note 3]. In 1H2026 revenue then fell 13.6%.

So the investment question is not whether FiberCo is a good business model; wholesale fibre is a respectable model elsewhere. The question is **whether LINK's minority shareholders get paid for the network at a market price**. Three things decide that, and a minority holder controls none of them:

- the price XLSMART pays for the lease;
- the terms on which the 2026 debt was refinanced;
- the price at which Axiata eventually sells. Surge (WIFI) and I Squared were reported bidders in 2025 at an EV of >US$1bn, and talks were reported "delayed" in August 2026.

The rest of this report quantifies each.

**The conflict is structural.** XLSMART is simultaneously LINK's 19.22% shareholder, its anchor tenant, its content reseller, its largest debtor (Rp453bn of receivables) and its largest creditor outside the banks (Rp1,350bn "other payables"). Axiata controls LINK outright but owns only about a third of XLSMART. Every rupiah of lease price paid by XLSMART to LINK is therefore worth ~69% to Axiata if it lands in LINK, and ~35% to Axiata if it stays in XLSMART, before Sinar Mas's interest is considered [calculated]. On a pure look-through, Axiata's incentive is for LINK to be paid well. After the April 2025 merger, however, XLSMART has its own board, its own 34.8% Sinar Mas shareholder and its own minorities, and it negotiates as an independent listed company. Management told the Dec-2025 public expose that XLSMART gets "no preferential rates... all the agreements rates are done on arm's length commercial terms" [PE2025 Q&A Q5]. The Dec-2024 deck said the opposite, pitching "preferential rates offered only to anchor tenants" [PE2024 p.10], and XLSMART is the anchor tenant. Neither document gives a price.

**Free float history:** 29.9% (2018) → 0.47% (end-2022, post-tender) → 1.46% (end-2024) → 7.50% (end-2025). IDX placed LINK on its Special Monitoring Board from 31-Jan-2025 for free-float shortfall [web, IDNFinancials; single-sourced]. Its status after the board's 28-Sep-2026 overhaul is not confirmed. The Axiata sell-downs that restored the float were made on-market during two price spikes (Rp3,060 block in August 2025; December 2025) that ran the stock to a Rp5,300 high. The shares then fell back to ~Rp1,500–1,660 by September 2026 [AR2025 p.18; web].

- **Turnover.** Board churn has been unusual since the change of control. Between 2023 and 2025 there were:

**The Rp1,350bn "other payable" to XLSMART is not explained in any note.** It more than doubled in 2025, and it lines up with an unexplained Rp2,545bn "other cash receipts" line in the 2025 cash flow, where "receipts from customers" collapsed to Rp869bn against Rp3,082bn of revenue [AR2025 p.290]. Two readings are possible. Neither is confirmed by the company:

- **(a) Customer collections held for XLSMART.** LINK may still be collecting from the transferred residential customers on XLSMART's behalf, under the Transitional Services Agreement or the CRA, and passing the money on with a lag. In that case the payable is a pass-through float that should shrink once the TSA ends. The TSA was a 12-month agreement plus a 3-month option from September 2024, so it should have ended by late 2025. Instead the payable grew.
- **(b) De facto funding from XLSMART.** Whatever its label, XLSMART is financing ~Rp1.0tn of LINK's balance sheet net, at undisclosed cost.

Either way, **2025 operating cash flow of Rp1,355bn includes the Rp767bn increase in this payable. Excluding it, operating cash flow was ~Rp588bn** [calculated], below 2025 interest paid plus capex.

Five years of capex exceeded operating cash flow by **Rp4.1tn**. Indonesian company law does not allow dividends while retained earnings are negative, so **no dividend is possible until cumulative profits refill that hole**.

**Reading it.** At the operating level LINK is a single-customer business (variables 1, 3 and 6). At the equity level it is a leverage business: variable 2 and the refinancing itself decide whether the equity is worth anything. Variable 3, winning third-party tenants on the existing footprint, is the **only** lever that can move earnings enough. Adding Rp5k/month of revenue per home passed across the base would lift EBITDA by a third. The 1H2026 flat network rental says that has not happened yet.

## 2.7 Demand

- **Fixed-broadband penetration is low and policy-backed.** About 21% of Indonesian households have fixed broadband (Komdigi), or 28% by APJII's survey measure. The government targets **50% household penetration at ≥100 Mbps by 2029** and has floated a Rp100k/100 Mbps "affordable" plan. Internet penetration overall reached 81.7% in APJII's 2026 survey [web: detik, intimedia, Antara]. The long-run demand case for fibre to the home is not in question.
- **Demand for LINK's specific product, wholesale access, depends on ISPs' retail success.** The anchor tenant, XLSMART, is post-merger and loss-making (1H26 net loss ~Rp994bn, single-sourced) and focused on integrating Smartfren. Its fixed-broadband push under the "XL SATU" brand is the main source of LINK's volume. The regional ISPs signing B2S deals are small, and two of the 12 contracts are only MoUs.
- **Enterprise demand** is steady but not growing: corporate customer numbers have been flat at 2,200–2,400 since 2020, and enterprise broadband revenue fell 4% in 2025 and 14% in 1H2026.

## 2.8 Supply

The supply side is where the thesis is most exposed. Fibre to the home is being over-built by well-funded players at falling retail prices:

| Player | Scale | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Telkom / IndiHome → InfraNexia** | ~9.4m IndiHome subscribers (3Q24); InfraNexia wholesale fibre spin-off (Stage 2 effective 7-Aug-2026; ~112k km of fibre) | A >99% Telkom-owned **open wholesale FiberCo**, directly competing for the same ISP tenants [web: Telkom] |
| **MyRepublic** (Sinar Mas / DSS) | 6.41m HP at end-2024 (+91% YoY) | Sinar Mas is XLSMART's co-controller |
| **Surge / WIFI** (Solusi Sinergi Digital) | 1.85m FTTH connections + 513k FWA (mid-2026); ARPU **~Rp70k**; 8.0x FY26F EV/EBITDA | A bidder for LINK in 2025 and a LINK tenant (WEAVE) |
| **Indosat** | Booked ~Rp1.0tn FiberCo monetisation gain in 1H26 | A precedent for telco FiberCo carve-outs |
| Biznet, Iconnet (PLN Icon+), CBN, Oxygen, FiberStar, Moratelindo, Remala | Not quantified | Retail entry prices: Rp100k/200 Mbps (Surge), Rp116k/500 Mbps (Remala), Rp175k/65 Mbps (Biznet); 67% of fixed-broadband users pay Rp100–300k [web: Bisnis] |

**LINK's 4.07m HP was ~7% of national homes passed at end-2024** [AR2024 p.117]. With Telkom now selling wholesale fibre openly, and retail prices collapsing to Rp100–175k/month, the wholesale price LINK can charge any ISP other than its captive anchor tenant is under structural pressure. The Sinar Mas angle deserves a note. XLSMART's co-controller also owns MyRepublic, a 6.4m-HP fibre network that competes with LINK for XLSMART's fixed-broadband volume.

**What break-even requires.** Net break-even needs EBITDA equal to D&A plus interest, about Rp2.27tn, against Rp0.86tn today. At a ~90% incremental margin that means **+Rp1.57tn of revenue, or +Rp28k per home passed per month** on the current footprint [calculated]. That is more than double today's network-lease take of ~Rp22k/HP/month.

The two levers are tenancy (more ISPs per home) and price (what XLSMART pays). With retail FTTH now selling for Rp100–175k a month (§2.8), an ISP paying LINK an extra Rp28k per home passed would need to be converting a large share of those homes into subscribers. The pre-transfer penetration of 18.5% implies that is a long way off.

**The new-build economics look better than the legacy base.** A new B2S home at Rp1.66m of capex, a 10.5% WACC (the company's own impairment-test rate) and a 20-year life needs ~Rp200k a year of EBITDA, about Rp17k/month, to earn its cost of capital [calculated: capital-recovery factor 0.1208]. That is in line with today's ~Rp16.5k EBITDA per home, so new builds roughly clear the hurdle at current tenancy. The problem is the **legacy balance sheet**. The 2022–24 build cost Rp3.6–9.7m per home added, the HFC plant still needs migrating, and CPE left behind after the B2C sale still depreciates. Together these drag depreciation far above what the network earns.

## 3.7 Scenario: what is the equity worth, and to whom?

The business model cannot tell you what LINK is worth on its own. Its revenue is a related-party transfer price and its solvency is a refinancing decision. So the question is framed as four ways to value the Rp11.2tn of enterprise value the market currently assigns, each netting off debt.

**Assumptions.**

- Net debt at mid-2026 of **Rp7.2–7.6tn**. That is the Dec-2025 figure of Rp6.64tn plus ~Rp0.6–1.0tn of 1H2026 cash burn, consistent with the Rp1.12tn rise in total liabilities. It is an assumption, because the 1H2026 debt figure was not found.
- ~2,750m shares.
- FY2025 EBITDA of Rp859bn.
- A Rupiah range of Rp16,500–17,900/US$ for US-dollar references.

### Case A — Axiata sells at the valuation reported in 2025

- **EV:** "more than US$1bn" (Surge and I Squared as front-runners, Aug–Oct 2025) = **Rp16.5–17.9tn**.
- **Less net debt:** Rp7.2–7.6tn, leaving **equity of Rp8.9–10.7tn**.
- **Per share: Rp3,240–3,890** [calculated], or **+95% to +134%** vs Rp1,660.
- **Cross-check:** CIMB (14-Oct-2025) put implied equity at ~US$609m. Axiata's own Aug-2025 block traded at Rp3,060.

**Would minorities get it?** Only if the buyer acquires LINK shares and triggers a mandatory tender offer. Indonesia's takeover rule (POJK 9/2018) generally sets the MTO price at no less than the higher of the 90-day average of daily highs and the takeover price. The 2022 MTO was done at exactly the acquisition price, Rp4,800.

**The risk to minorities is structure.** Axiata could instead sell **network assets** out of LINK. Two dormant shells, PT Linknet Fiber Indonesia (est. 2024) and PT Axentec Fiber Indonesia, sit ready as carve-out vehicles (inference). An asset sale would need an independent-shareholder vote as a material affiliated transaction, but the 2024 B2C vote shows how small that constituency is. It would also trigger no tender offer.

### Case B — no sale; value at digital-infrastructure multiples

| EV/EBITDA applied to Rp859bn | EV (Rp tn) | Equity (Rp tn) | Per share (Rp) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 8x (WIFI / MTEL) | 6.87 | (0.3)–(0.7) | **nil** |
| 11x (TBIG) | 9.45 | 1.85–2.25 | **670–820** |
| 13x (current) | 11.2 | 3.6–4.0 | 1,300–1,440 |

At fibre-tower multiples on reported EBITDA, **the equity is worth between nothing and about half the current price** [calculated].

### Case C — replacement cost

- **Replacement cost:** ~Rp1.66–2.0m per home for FTTH (2025 build cost, plus a margin for CPE and backbone), × 4.62m HP = **Rp7.7–9.2tn**.
- **Less net debt:** equity **Rp0.1–2.0tn = Rp25–740/share** [calculated].
- **Caveats:** this understates the value of the enterprise business, the Java backbone and the two data centres. It overstates the value of the ~35% of homes still on HFC, which will need re-building.

### Case D — no sale, recapitalisation

At the 1H2026 loss run-rate of ~Rp1.7tn a year, book equity (~Rp2.73tn at June 2026) falls to ~Rp1.9tn by end-2026 and to ~Rp0.2tn by end-2027 [calculated].

Suppose lenders want net debt/EBITDA of 4x, a typical Indonesian bank covenant level and an assumption here, since LINK's own covenant thresholds are undisclosed. On today's EBITDA that means net debt of ~Rp3.4tn. That implies **~Rp4tn of new equity**:

- at Rp1,660, about **2.4bn new shares, ~87% dilution** [calculated];
- at a typical rights-issue discount, more.

Axiata and XLSMART (~92% of the shares between them) would take up most of it. The public float's holdings would shrink unless they subscribed.

### What the price implies

Blend the midpoints of Case A (Rp3,565) and Case B at 11x (Rp745): **Rp1,660 prices a ~32% probability of an Axiata exit at 2025-reported values, with minorities included** [calculated: (1,660 − 745) ÷ (3,565 − 745)]. The August-2026 report that monetisation is "delayed" and that talks are "largely ongoing with domestic parties" is the most recent evidence on that probability. The share price's fall from Rp4,070 at end-2025 to ~Rp1,500–1,660 suggests the market has already marked it down.

# SUMMARY VERDICT

**What it is.** A 4.6m-home fixed network, ~65% FTTH and ~35% legacy HFC, built on borrowed money since 2022. Its retail business was sold in 2024 to a sister company, and it now rents that network, mostly, to the same sister company at prices nobody outside the Axiata group can see. The operating business is a wholesale landlord with one tenant providing 70.9% of revenue. It also has a small, ageing enterprise book and a content pass-through that flatters revenue by ~Rp540bn.

**What it earns.** EBITDA of ~Rp0.86tn against depreciation of ~Rp1.76tn and interest of ~Rp0.5tn gives a net loss of ~Rp1.4–1.7tn a year. That has held for two years, and 1H2026 was worse, with revenue down 13.6%. There is no "through the cycle" profit to speak of. Unless the tenant pays more or more tenants arrive, the business earns roughly **negative Rp330k per home passed per year** after depreciation and interest [calculated, §3.5]. New builds at 2025 costs roughly earn their cost of capital. The legacy base does not.

**What has to be true for it to work.**

- **Wholesale revenue per home passed has to roughly double.** Network lease revenue would need to rise from ~Rp22k to ~Rp50k per home a month, through third-party ISP tenancy on the 4.6m-home footprint or a higher XLSMART price. That has to happen while Telkom's InfraNexia sells wholesale fibre openly and retail FTTH prices sit at Rp100–175k a month.
- **The 2026 debt wall has to be refinanced at long tenor without a heavily dilutive equity raise.**
- **For a public shareholder specifically, Axiata's exit has to take the form of a share sale that triggers a tender offer**, not a carve-out of the network assets.

**What would break it.** Any one of the following:

- an asset-level sale of FiberCo that leaves listed LINK as a shell with the enterprise book and the debt;
- a rights issue priced to recapitalise at a deep discount;
- XLSMART shifting fixed-broadband volume to its other co-controller's network (MyRepublic) as exclusivity lapses;
- an impairment that pushes book equity through a covenant.

**Position.** On the filings, **LINK is not an investable business for a minority shareholder on its fundamentals. It is an event-driven option on Axiata's exit, and the terms of that option are set entirely by the controlling group.** At Rp1,660 the stock trades at 13x trailing EBITDA, the richest multiple in its peer set for the weakest standalone economics. That price is only justified if a buyer pays something close to the >US$1bn EV reported in 2025 **and** does it through LINK shares.

The track record inside the group points the other way. The Lippo/CVC exit in 2022 went at Rp4,800 and minorities were included through the tender offer. Since then:

- the one material transaction, the 2024 B2C sale, was priced at ~2.5x pre-tax earnings and routed its gain around the P&L;
- the transfer prices that now set LINK's revenue are undisclosed;
- the float has been restored by on-market sell-downs into price spikes.

I would treat LINK as a small, sized speculation on a share-level takeover, not as a fibre-infrastructure holding. The base case, absent a deal, is a recapitalisation at some point in the next 12–18 months that dilutes the public heavily. **The asymmetry favours waiting for the transaction structure to be announced rather than paying today's premium for it.**

*This is an analytical view of the business from its filings, not investment advice. I'm not a financial advisor.*
