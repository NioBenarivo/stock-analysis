**What it does:** Indonesia's largest taxi operator. The listed company runs about **26,000 vehicles** in 18–22 cities under five brands: Bluebird (regular taxi), Silverbird (executive taxi, Jakarta only), Goldenbird (car rental with or without driver), Bigbird (bus charter, plus a Transjakarta medium-bus contract since May 2025) and Cititrans (intercity and airport shuttle). Around the fleet sit a vehicle auction house (Caready, 51% with MUFG), a used-car platform (BirdMobil), a delivery service (Bluebird Kirim) and the MyBluebird app, which is also plugged into Gojek (since 2016) and Grab (since Oct-2024). Taxi is 70% of revenue, non-taxi 30%. The group dates its origins to 1972; the listed entity was set up in 2001 and floated in Nov-2014 at Rp6,500 a share.

**Market:** Rp1,570/share (25-Sep-2026) × 2,502,100,000 shares = **Rp3,928bn market cap** [calculated]. P/E 6.2x FY2025, P/BV 0.63x, dividend yield 10.6% on the Rp166 FY2025 dividend, EV/EBITDA 3.7x [all calculated, §3.6]. The stock is 76% below its IPO price.

**Period covered:** FY2022–FY2025 audited (FY2021 from the AR2023 three-year highlights; FY2019–20 headline figures from the public expose decks), plus 1Q26 and 1H26 from press coverage.

**Sources in hand:** 3 annual and sustainability reports (FY2023, FY2024, FY2025; 410–446 pages each) and 3 public expose decks (Mar-2024, Mar-2025, Mar-2026). Web research filled in the market, 2026 results and industry context. The full source table is at the end.

**Conventions:** Rp bn unless stated (the filings are in Rp millions). "Attrib." means attributable to owners of the parent. `[calculated]` marks every figure derived here rather than printed in a filing. FY2022 figures come from the comparative column in AR2023. Where AR2023 and AR2024 disagree on FY2023, both are shown.

## THE ONE THING TO UNDERSTAND FIRST

**The listed company owns the cars. The family owns the name, most of the pools and the shared back office, and since July 2023 it charges for the name.**

Three filing details, read together, set the frame:

1. **The "Blue Bird", "Silver Bird", "Golden Bird", "Big Bird" and "Pusaka" trademarks belong to PT Pusaka Citra Djokosoetono (PCD),** the family holding company that owns 28.4% of BIRD. BIRD uses them under a non-exclusive licence signed on 25-Jul-2013, a year before the IPO. The licence was free for its first ten years. From the tenth year (Jul-2023) BIRD pays PCD **2% of annual net revenue**, and the rate "may only be reassessed from the 15th year" (Jul-2028). The licence rolls over automatically for another ten years unless both parties agree to end it (AR2025 Note 28).
2. **About 59–68% of BIRD's rental expense goes to family companies** (PCD, PT Pusaka Bumi Mutiara, PT Blue Bird Taxi, PT Golden Bird Bali, PT Golden Bird Metro, PT Pusaka Buana Utama). These are the land leases for taxi pools and offices, running to 2027–2033 (Note 24, Note 28).
3. **Under a 2013 "Joint Operational Management" agreement, BIRD shares its call centre, training centre, customer service, payment systems, IT and even spare-parts buying with family-owned companies that are not part of the listed group**, including PT Blue Bird Taxi, PT Surabaya Taksi Utama, PT Golden Bird Metro and PT Golden Bird Bali. Costs are "shared for efficiency". The allocation basis is not disclosed (Note 28).

Four things follow:

1. **The trademark fee is not in the related-party note.** Note 24 lists PCD's transactions as "trade receivables, other receivables, other payables, rental expense and lease liabilities". The Rp98.4bn royalty appears only as a G&A line called "Trademark" and, separately, as a contract description in Note 28. A reader of the RPT table alone would put related-party costs at 1.6% of revenue. The true measurable figure is about twice that.
2. **The royalty explains part of the margin stall.** The fee went from zero to Rp98.4bn in two and a half years. That is 1.7% of revenue arriving just as the post-COVID recovery matured. Operating margin was 12.2% in FY2023, 12.6% in FY2024 and 12.0% in FY2025. Without the fee, FY2025 operating margin would have been 13.8% [calculated]. The first FY2023 figure (Rp36.0bn for ~5 months) and the implied base in FY2024–25 (Rp4.48tn and Rp4.92tn, 86–89% of consolidated revenue) fit a 2% rate on the revenue of the entities that use the brands [calculated; the base is not disclosed].
3. **The next step is a rate review in 2028, and PCD sits on both sides.** Every point on the royalty rate is about Rp49bn a year before tax, or 6.0% of attrib. profit [calculated, §3.7]. The independent commissioners (4 of 9) are the only check, and the filings show no fairness opinion or benchmark for the 2% rate.
4. **None of this makes BIRD a bad business.** It earns a 12% operating margin, carries little debt and has doubled profit since 2019 on a smaller fleet (§3.2). But the listco is a fleet operator renting the family's brand and land, not the owner of the Bluebird franchise. Value that accrues to the brand can be taken out through the royalty, and the land it would need to control its own network is mostly leased.

**The second thing to know is that the dividend is paid with borrowed money while the fleet grows.** Over FY2022–FY2025, operating cash flow minus capex, plus used-car sale proceeds, minus interest and lease payments, came to **−Rp158bn** in total. Dividends to parent shareholders were Rp858bn over the same years. Bank loans rose from Rp678bn to Rp2,264bn [all calculated, §1.5]. That is fine while the growth capex earns its keep. It also means the 10.6% yield is a policy choice (a 65% payout in FY2025, up from ~50%) rather than a measure of spare cash.

**Adding it up:** the family controls 75.1% directly and through three holding companies. The FS says BIRD has **no ultimate parent** because ownership is spread among several individuals (Note 1). PCD's own shareholders are not disclosed. The two small holdcos are named "Purnomo" and "Chandra", which match the names of two founder-generation family members referred to in press coverage of the 2013 family dispute (web notes). That points to two family branches holding through separate vehicles. **This is our inference; the filings do not describe the family tree.**

**The control block has crept up** from 73.7% (FY2022) to 75.1% (FY2025) through small purchases (Purnomo Prawiro +0.7pp in 2024, PT Purnomo Investama +0.3pp in 2025, Adrianto +0.2pp in 2023). The free float is 24.9%, close to the level at which IDX float rules start to bite.

**Family disputes are part of the record.** In Jul-2022 a third party, Elliana Wibowo, sued BIRD, some of its shareholders and affiliates in South Jakarta, alleging her shareholder rights in affiliated companies were obstructed. BIRD won in May-2023; her Supreme Court review was withdrawn in 2024 (Note 29, AR2023–AR2024). Press coverage also records a 2013 split between founder-generation siblings over the company and the logo, and an Aug-2023 claim by "a founder's child" for BIRD shares (CNBC Indonesia headline; not verified).

**Governance flags:**

- **The controlling group holds the top job on both boards and six of the twelve board seats** (four commissioners and two directors: Bayu, Noni, Sri Adriyani, Kresna, Adrianto, Sigit; all six are described in the AR as affiliated with the controlling shareholders). **Reading it:**

- **Family rent more than doubled in three years** (Rp46.0bn → Rp91.5bn, +99%) while group revenue rose 59%. PCD's rent nearly tripled (Rp8.4bn → Rp23.6bn). The pricing basis for the leases is "market price" in AR2023 and "to be further determined by the parties" for relocations. No independent valuation is disclosed.
- **The trademark fee is the largest single related-party cost** and it is missing from the RPT table. Management's statement that there were "no conflict-of-interest transactions" in 2025 is formally true (the licence pre-dates the IPO), but the economic flow to the controlling shareholder is ~Rp98bn a year and rising with revenue.
- **Rp53bn sat with PBM as an "advance for property and equipment" from at least FY2022 to FY2024** before dropping to Rp31bn in FY2025. That is money paid to a family company for assets not delivered for three-plus years. What was being bought, and whether the Rp22bn reduction was delivery or refund, is not disclosed.
- **Cost sharing under the joint-ops agreement is invisible.** BIRD shares its call centre, IT, training centre and payment systems with family taxi companies. The receivables and payables flowing both ways show that money moves, but Note 24 does not quantify revenue from, or costs recharged to, these companies. "Administration and support fees" in G&A (Rp107.0bn in FY2025) may include some of it; the counterparties are not named.
- **AR2025 adds, for the first time, an "Affiliate Transaction Policy"** requiring arm's-length pricing checked against unaffiliated terms, with Audit Committee review on request. The disclosure is an improvement; none of the numbers above is benchmarked against anything.

## 2.4 Process — how the service is made and what it costs

### The physical process

```
TAXI:    Pool (58 depots; many leased from family) ─► partner driver checks out car (daily shift)
         ─► trips from street hail / outlet / MyBluebird / Gojek / Grab / corporate account
         ─► IoT taximeter records fare; cash or cashless ─► driver deposits fares ("setoran")
         ─► BIRD pays driver's share, fuel, maintenance, KIR licences; driver bears losses he causes
         ─► car returns to pool; maintenance in-house; retired at end of life ─► auction
RENTAL:  corporate contract ─► vehicle (± driver) assigned for days/months ─► monthly invoice
BUS/SHUTTLE: charter or scheduled route ─► per-trip or per-seat; Transjakarta per-km/service fee
```

**The driver model matters.** Taxi drivers are partners, not employees (Note 29). They post a security deposit, remit all fares, bear the cost of damage they cause, and get paid a share. BIRD, not the driver, pays for fuel, which makes fuel BIRD's second-largest cost. This is the opposite of ride-hailing, where the driver owns the car and buys the fuel.

## 2.5 Distribution — how the service is sold

- **Street hail, pools and outlets.** 58 pools and 1,300 "exclusive outlets" (hotel, mall, airport and office queues). Airport taxi and shuttle concessions at Soekarno-Hatta and other airports.
- **MyBluebird app.** BIRD's own booking app, with a subscription plan since 2024, OVO and other e-wallets (2025), and cross-booking between Bluebird and Goldenbird. No booking share is disclosed.
- **Ride-hailing platforms.** Gojek since 2016 (GoBluebird, integrated with GoPay since 2020) and **Grab since Oct-2024**. The platforms, once the threat, are now distribution partners. What BIRD pays them per booking is not disclosed. The 8% ride-hailing commission cap from 1-Jul-2026 applies to the platforms' own driver partners; whether it changes the terms for Bluebird bookings is unknown.
- **Corporate accounts.** Taxi vouchers, Goldenbird long-term rental, Bigbird charters, "Corporate Solutions". Trade receivables are only Rp233bn (15 days of revenue [calculated]), so most revenue is cash or near-cash.
- **Government contracts.** Transjakarta (from May-2025) and Medan BRT (2024).
- **Third-party channels.** Traveloka sells Cititrans tickets and Bigbird/Goldenbird airport transfers.

### Pricing

Taxi fares are set by BIRD within tariff bands set by the Ministry of Transport and regional governments. Press in Apr-2026 quotes Bluebird's regular Jakarta tariff at a **Rp9,000 flag fall and Rp5,000–5,200 per km**, and its EV taxi at Rp10,000 plus Rp6,300 per km. Newcomer Xanh SM charges Rp9,600 plus Rp5,800 per km for EVs (Suara.com, 29-Apr-2026). BIRD does not disclose tariff changes, average fare or trip length in any filing. Rental and bus prices are contract-by-contract.

### Customer concentration

None. No customer above 10% of revenue in any year. Related-party revenue is not separately disclosed, but related-party receivables are only Rp7.0bn.

**How to read this:** BIRD is most exposed to **what a car earns in a day and what it burns in fuel**, then to used-car prices and the royalty. It is not very sensitive to rates. Fuel and tariffs are politically linked: when the government raises subsidised fuel prices, tariff bands usually follow, but with a lag. Used-car prices have turned into a real profit driver. FY2025's Rp137bn gain is five times FY2023's, and 16.6% of pre-tax profit comes from selling old cars above book value. That gain is also the proof that the fleet is depreciated conservatively (residuals of 20–50%), so it is recurring as long as used-car prices hold.

## 2.8 Supply

- **Conventional taxis:** BIRD has won the consolidation. Its old rival Express Transindo (TAXI) is a shell (Rp18 a share, 29 employees). The long tail of regional operators remains, and BIRD's "Kawan Bluebird" programme partners with some of them.
- **Ride-hailing cars (GoCar, GrabCar, Maxim, inDrive):** the main competition on price. Driver numbers are rising in 2026 as laid-off workers take up gig driving (Bisnis.com, Apr-2026), which pushes per-driver earnings down and keeps prices low. BIRD now sells through Gojek and Grab, so it competes with and distributes through the same platforms.
- **EV taxis:** Xanh SM (VinFast-backed) launched in Jakarta in Dec-2024 with ~500 VinFast cars and undercuts Bluebird's EV tariff by Rp400–500 per km. It is Jakarta-only so far. This is the first well-funded, fleet-owning competitor since Express.
- **Car rental:** TRAC (Astra), ASSA (~30,000 units), MPM Rent and many smaller firms compete with Goldenbird. BIRD's rental fleet was 5,903 units in FY2023, the last year disclosed.
- **Consolidation of the platforms:** a Grab–GoTo merger was still unresolved in the sources found (Bloomberg, Jan-2026). A merged platform would be BIRD's partner for both app channels at once, with more bargaining power.

## 3.7 Scenario: the dividend, the royalty review and a fuel shock

| **= steady-state FCF (Rp450m case)** | **592.2** | **418.6** |
| Dividend declared for the year | 300.3 | **415.3** |

**Steady-state free cash flow is roughly Rp130–590bn a year, and the FY2025 dividend (Rp415bn) sits in the middle of that range.** The 65% payout therefore uses about all of the cash the business would throw off without growth. Any growth (2,000–3,000 net units are guided for 2026) is financed by debt, which is what has happened. That is sustainable at 0.7x net debt/EBITDA; it is not sustainable indefinitely. At FY2025's pace (net debt up Rp722bn in the year), net debt/EBITDA would reach ~1.2x by end-2026 and ~1.8x by end-2027 [calculated, holding EBITDA flat at Rp1,339bn].

### B. Earnings shocks

Base: FY2025 attrib. profit Rp635.8bn, EPS Rp254, price Rp1,570, 22% tax.

| Step | A: Royalty 2% → 3% (2028 review) | B: Fuel +Rp1,000/litre, no tariff change | C: Fleet-sale gain back to FY23 level | D: A + B + C | E: Tariff +5%, fuel flat |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pre-tax change | −49.2 | −105.0 | −110.7 | −264.9 | +205.5 |
| After tax (× 0.78) | −38.4 | −81.9 | −86.3 | −206.6 | +160.3 |

*Assumptions: royalty base Rp4,918bn (FY2025 implied); fuel 105.0m litres; fleet-sale gain falls from Rp137.2bn to Rp26.5bn; tariff +5% on all revenue with driver share (~28%) moving and fuel unchanged (5 × Rp41.1bn). NCI ignored (1.2% of profit).*

Two points follow:

- **Even the combined bad case (D) leaves BIRD at 9x earnings and a 7% yield.** The downside in earnings is cushioned by the price.
- **The single most asymmetric item is the royalty,** because it is a decision by the controlling shareholder, not a market variable. Fuel and used-car prices move both ways and tariffs can follow fuel. The royalty can only be raised against BIRD. At 4% it would take Rp77bn of profit a year (12%) [calculated].

## 3.8 Roadmap and what to watch

1. **The royalty (Jul-2028 review).** Any change to the 2% rate, any renegotiation, any independent valuation of the licence. The best outcome for minorities would be BIRD buying the trademarks from PCD at a fair, independently valued price. Nothing in the filings suggests that is planned.
2. **Quarterly profit vs revenue.** Two quarters of falling profit on 11% revenue growth. Watch 9M26 depreciation and finance charges, and whether disposal gains hold.
3. **Non-taxi returns.** Segment operating margin fell from 19.1% to 13.7% in three years. Watch whether the Transjakarta buses and Cititrans routes lift segment profit in FY2026 or keep diluting it.
4. **Capex vs guidance.** 2026 guidance is Rp1.5–1.9tn and 2,000–3,000 net units. Net new borrowing above ~Rp1tn a year would mean leverage is rising faster than the business needs.
5. **Payout policy.** A 65% payout was declared in 2026. Whether it holds in 2027 with profit falling and capex high tells you whether the dividend is policy or residual.
6. **Fuel and tariffs.** Any Pertalite price change (unchanged since Sep-2022) and how fast tariff bands follow.
7. **Competition.** Xanh SM's fleet and city count outside Jakarta; the Grab–GoTo merger and what it means for BIRD's two app channels; the effect of the 8% commission cap on ride-hailing prices.
8. **Disclosure fixes to ask for:**
   - fleet by type, utilisation, trips and revenue per car per day
   - the share of bookings via MyBluebird, Gojek and Grab, and what BIRD pays the platforms
   - the royalty base and the counterparties behind "administration and support fees"
   - what the Rp53bn advance to PT Pusaka Bumi Mutiara was for
   - the reason for the undisclosed FY2023 segment reallocation
   - the cities count (18 or 22)
9. **Possible catalyst:** a transfer of the trademarks or family-owned pool land into BIRD, or a float increase. None has been announced.

# SUMMARY VERDICT

**What it is.** BIRD is Indonesia's surviving large taxi fleet, now 30% rental, bus and shuttle. It owns about 26,000 cars and Rp2.1tn of land, carries little debt, and turns a 12% operating margin into an 11% net margin. It is not the whole Bluebird franchise. The brand belongs to the controlling family's holding company, which has charged 2% of revenue for it since July 2023. Most pool and office space is rented from family companies, and the call centre and IT are shared with family taxi companies outside the listed group.

**What it earns through the cycle.** On FY2022–25 evidence: revenue per car rises with tariffs and mix, gross margin holds near 32%, and operating profit is 12–13% of revenue (about 1.7 points lower since the royalty). Attributable profit runs at Rp600–650bn today. About Rp140bn of pre-tax profit comes from selling retired cars above book, which depends on used-car prices. Steady-state free cash flow, if growth stopped, is probably Rp130–590bn a year; the dividend takes most of it.

**What has to be true for it to work.**

- Revenue per car keeps rising faster than fuel, driver pay and depreciation, so the gross margin holds.
- Taxi demand holds up against ride-hailing, mass transit and Xanh SM, helped by using Gojek and Grab as channels.
- The growth capex in non-taxi earns more than its 5.7% cost of debt. It still does, at 7.5% on segment assets, but that figure is falling.
- The royalty stays at 2% after the 2028 review.

If those hold, BIRD at 6x earnings, 0.63x book, 3.7x EBITDA and a 10.6% yield is cheap for a business growing revenue 11–13% a year with 0.7x net debt/EBITDA.

**What would break it.**

1. The royalty is raised in 2028, or another related-party cost appears. That transfers value to the family at no cost to them.
2. A fuel price rise without a matching tariff rise. Each Rp1,000 a litre is about 13% of profit.
3. A used-car price fall combined with EV residual write-downs. That removes the disposal gain and adds impairments at the same time.
4. Borrowing to fund both 2,000–3,000 net new cars a year and a 65% payout, until leverage forces a choice between them.

**Position.** On the filings, **BIRD is undervalued as a business and fairly valued as a minority position.** The operating numbers are better than the multiple implies: the enterprise trades at the book value of its cars, with the land and network thrown in. The discount is not irrational, though. The largest intangible asset, the brand, sits with the controlling shareholder, who can re-price it from 2028. Related-party payments already take 30% of attributable profit. The disclosure of operating data has got worse as the business recovered. And the 10.6% yield is being partly funded with debt. A reader who buys BIRD is buying a well-run fleet at a low price, with a family landlord and licensor on the other side of about 3% of revenue. The yield is the compensation for that. The two things to watch are whether the payout survives a year of flat profit and heavy capex, and what happens to the royalty in 2028.

*This is an analytical view of the business from its filings, not investment advice. I'm not a financial advisor.*
