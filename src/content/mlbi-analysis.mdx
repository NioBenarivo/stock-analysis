## THE ONE THING TO UNDERSTAND FIRST

**MLBI is a near-monopoly Indonesian beer franchise that Heineken runs as a cash pipe. The listed company keeps almost nothing: equity is Rp1.36tn against Rp1.18tn of annual profit. Every rupiah of profit goes out as dividend, and a growing slice goes out before profit as Heineken group fees.**

Five facts carry the whole report:

1. **The business needs almost no capital.**
   - Distributors pre-fund it through container deposits (Rp153bn). Advertisers and trade partners fund it through Rp659bn of accrued A&P. The state funds it through deferred excise (Rp161bn).
   - The result is **operating working capital of about −Rp1.0tn**. Operating invested capital is only ~Rp0.5tn against Rp1.53tn of operating profit, so ROIC is well above 200% [calculated, §3.1].
   - ROE is 88% only because the balance sheet has been emptied, not because of leverage: debt/equity is 0.06x.

2. **Payout is exactly 100% of EPS, by design.**

   | FY | DPS (Rp) | EPS (Rp) |
   |---|---|---|
   | 2023 | 110 + 396 = **506** | 506 |
   | 2024 | 190 + 352 = **542** | 542 |
   | 2025 | 190 + 371 = **561** | 562 |

   Heineken collected **~Rp1.06tn** of the FY2025 dividend [calculated].

3. **The fee line is rising fast. This is the change most investors will miss.**
   - Royalties, management, procurement, expatriate and "other" charges paid to Heineken entities were **Rp122bn (3.6% of sales) in FY2024 and Rp208bn (5.9%) in FY2025**, up 70% [calculated, §1.4].
   - A new Global Knowledge & Service Agreement took **1.7% of revenue from 1 Jul 2025**, so only half a year is in FY2025.
   - Undescribed "other charges" from Heineken International **doubled to Rp113bn**.
   - The Tiger royalty steps up from 1.25% to 5% by 2028.
   - This is a slow transfer of value from the 10.68% minority to the 89.32% owner, and it takes place *above* the dividend line.

4. **The franchise has pricing power that its peer lacks.**
   - Beer excise rose 10% in January 2024. MLBI's FY2024 sales still grew 1.9% and operating profit grew 2.2%. DLTA's sales fell 12% and its operating profit fell 29% in the same year.
   - MLBI brews ~1.6m hl a year [calculated from sustainability data, §2.2]. That is **~5x DLTA's volume**, so the market is effectively Bintang versus everyone else.

5. **2026 is a breakout year after six flat ones.**
   - FY2025 sales (Rp3.54tn) and net profit (Rp1.18tn) were *still below 2019* (Rp3.71tn / Rp1.21tn).
   - H1 2026 sales rose **16.8%** and net profit **24.3%**. Q2 alone was +20% and +30% [calculated].
   - Heineken says Indonesia beer volume grew "in the teens". The drivers are a November 2025 price increase, a 250ml affordability pack, and Bali tourism.

**The resulting profile:**
- At Rp6,850 the stock is on **11.2x trailing earnings** with an ~8–9% cash yield. Earnings are growing double digits for the first time since the post-COVID rebound.
- Against that sit:
  - a rising Heineken fee toll;
  - two distributors taking **39% of sales** (PT Bintang Bali Indah alone takes 28%);
  - a recurring tax-dispute pattern;
  - rupiah weakness against imported inputs;
  - a new **15% free-float rule** that MLBI (10.68% float) must meet by around 2029.

Evidence is in §1.2, §1.4, §2.5, §2.6, §3.1 and §3.2.

**Where the story sits.** The company has belonged to Heineken for 90 years with two interruptions (1949–65 and 2010–13). ### Heineken has been *buying*, not selling, and the new free-float rule points the other way

**What the rule means for MLBI** [calculated]:
- To reach 15%, Heineken (or the company) must put **~91m more shares (4.32%) into public hands. That is ~Rp624bn at Rp6,850**, about 40% more stock than the current float.
- The daily traded value is roughly Rp1.6bn [calculated from ~239k shares/day], so a placement of that size is ~390 trading days of normal volume. It would have to be a marketed block, probably at a discount.
- The alternatives are:
  - A tender offer and voluntary delisting, which is the opposite direction. Heineken paid Rp9,000 in 2022 and the stock is now 24% lower.
  - Seeking an exemption.
- **Either way this is the largest shareholder-structure event on the horizon.** Nothing has been announced (web check, Sep 2026).

**Reading the table.**

1. **The toll almost doubled in one year.** Two lines drive it: the new GKSA and the unexplained "others". Neither appears in the Public Expose decks.

2. **The 2026 run-rate is higher still** [calculated]:

   | Change | Effect |
   |---|---|
   | GKSA on a full year: 1.7% × ~Rp3.9–4.0tn of 2026 sales | ≈ **Rp66–68bn**, +Rp31bn vs 2025 |
   | Know-How ends | −Rp6bn |
   | Tiger steps to 2.5% | Small |
   | "Others" held flat | — |
   | **Total** | **≈ Rp235bn, ~6% of sales** |

   Every 1% of revenue charged by Heineken costs minorities ~Rp3.8bn of dividends a year: 10.68% × Rp35bn × (1 − 22% tax).

3. **The 0% Bintang royalty is the offset, and it is the lever.**
   - At the pre-2022 2.5% rate, the Bintang royalty would cost roughly Rp70–80bn a year. That assumes Bintang-branded revenue of ~Rp3.0tn; the split is not disclosed.
   - Heineken cut it to zero in 2022. From July 2025 it now charges 1.7% on *all* revenue through the GKSA.
   - **Net, the group has moved from a brand royalty to a group-services fee of similar size.** The group-services fee is harder for the tax office to challenge as a brand payment, and it is outside the minority's control.

4. **Tax authorities have been fighting these deductions.** That is an inference, not a disclosure: the notes never name transfer pricing. See §3.8: every fiscal year from 2016 to 2022 had a contested assessment. The FY2022 CIT (Rp22.2bn) and **Art. 26 withholding tax** (Rp8.2bn) assessments were issued in December 2025. Art. 26 is the tax on payments to *foreign* parties such as royalties and fees.

5. **Contract brewing for Diageo's channel is not a related-party transaction.**
   - The Guinness/Kilkenny Brewing & Distribution Agreement generated **Rp232.5bn of revenue in 2025 (6.6% of sales) and Rp246.9bn in 2024** [Note 32d].
   - The counterparty, LKJ then PT Gitaswara Indonesia from Jan 2025, is a third party.
   - Guinness is a Diageo brand, so this is Heineken's Indonesian plant filling a competitor-brand's volume.

## 2.1 Product

**The business in one paragraph.**
- MLBI brews lager and flavoured beer at ≤5% ABV (excise Category A) in two breweries, and makes Bintang 0.0 and Green Sands at a non-alcoholic plant.
- It sells ex-brewery through its distribution subsidiary MBIN to independent distributors. Two of them take 39% of sales. From the distributors the product goes to traditional trade, on-premise outlets (Bali above all) and supermarkets. Minimarkets have been closed to beer since 2015.
- Net sales are reported after VAT, excise and **trade discounts and rebates**. That last deduction matters for any peer comparison (§2.4).
- MLBI does **not own the Bintang trademark**, which it licenses from Heineken Asia Pacific at a 0% royalty. It pays 7.2% on Heineken®, CHF1.79/hl on Green Sands and a rising rate on Tiger. It also earns a fee-like margin contract-brewing Guinness and Kilkenny for a third party.

- **Modern off-premise:** supermarkets and hypermarkets only; minimarkets have been banned since 2015.
- **E-commerce** is "a relatively small portion of revenue, growing faster than category".
- **Exports:** 0.3% of sales. Rp4.0bn to third parties (US, South Korea) and Rp6.7bn to Heineken affiliates (Timor-Leste, Australia, Netherlands, Singapore).
- **Affordability:** the BINTANG Pilsener 250ml pack (2025) and the "Bintang Cool Pack" regional rollout (2026). This is the "addressing affordability" pillar of EverGreen 2030. It is a price-point tool to keep the 2024 excise shock from pushing drinkers to spirits or illicit alcohol.

- **The two variables minorities cannot influence, Heineken fees and excise, are now of similar size to the input-cost variables.**

**Market size.** This is the same third-party estimate used in the DLTA report (Ken Research 2025); treat it as order-of-magnitude:

**Share** [calculated, rough]:
- MLBI's ~1.6m hl includes Green Sands (~65k hl), Bintang 0.0 and the Guinness contract volume.
- Own-brand beer is therefore perhaps ~1.3–1.4m hl, **~60–65% of a 2.2m hl market**. DLTA has ~14% and Bali Hai plus others the rest.
- The only published share figure is **61% in 2012** (Wikipedia). The AR says only that share "improved" in 2025.
- By listed-company sales, MLBI is **84%** of MLBI + DLTA combined [calculated: 3,542.5 / (3,542.5 + 674.5)].

**Demand drivers:**

1. **Tourism.**
   - National foreign arrivals were **11.41m in 2025 (+14.4%)**, against a 2026 target of 17.6m (AR25).
   - Bali direct foreign arrivals were **6.95m in 2025 (+9.7%)**. Australia is the largest source market at 23%.
   - MLBI's single largest customer is the Bali distributor.
2. **Mass-market purchasing power.**
   - The AR25 MD&A: "active outlet count declined", with the "sharpest volume declines among upper-middle-income households" and the beer market growing "more moderately than expected".
   - The 250ml pack and the "affordability" pillar respond to this.
3. **Substitution** toward spirits, flavoured alcohol and illicit *miras oplosan* after excise hikes.
   - MLBI's flavoured Bintang variants (Anggur Merah = red wine, Arak Jeruk & Madu = arak) are a direct attempt to capture spirit-drinking occasions **inside the lower-excise beer category**. Category A beer pays Rp16,500/L, against Rp42,500 for B and Rp101,000 for C.
   - This is a clever piece of excise arbitrage.
4. **Regulation.**
   - Minimarket ban (2015); regional bans; licensing under Permendag 20/2014; PP 28/2024 health rules.
   - The draft alcohol-ban bill has been dormant since 2021.
   - **PMK 89/2025** tightens control of excisable-goods movement against illegal alcohol, a positive for legal brewers (AR25 p65).
   - None of these appears in MLBI's risk section, which lists only "business and market" and "regulatory" risk in generic terms.

**2026 read.** Demand is surging:
- MLBI Q1 volume **+12.5%**, H1 sales **+16.8%**.
- Heineken's HY26 release: Indonesia "beer volume growth in the teens, led by local power brand Bintang and Heineken®".
- DLTA H1 sales +13%.
- **The whole category is recovering, and MLBI is taking more than its share of the profit**: net profit +24% against DLTA's −4%.

## 2.8 Supply

- **Entry is limited by licensing and excise registration, not capital.** MLBI and DLTA have both run below capacity for years. Bali Hai and small local and contract brewers form the tail.
- **MLBI also supplies a competitor brand:** Guinness and Kilkenny are brewed at MLBI for Diageo's Indonesian channel. This turns a potential competitor's volume into MLBI revenue. It also means the premium-stout segment effectively runs through an MLBI brewery.
- **Excise is the supply-side price floor:**
  - Category A Rp16,500/L since Jan 2024.
  - Total alcohol-beverage excise was Rp8.86tn (2024) and Rp8.92tn (2025).
  - MLBI's own excise bill is not disclosed. At ~1.4–1.5m hl of alcoholic product × Rp1.65m/hl it is **~Rp2.3–2.5tn a year, ~26–28% of all national alcohol-beverage excise and ~70% of its own net sales** [calculated, rough]. The Rp161bn accrued excise at year-end (≈ 0.8 months) is consistent.
- **Imports:** licensed imported beers pay higher Category A import rates and are a premium niche, not a volume threat.

**A. FY2026 dividend (DPS = EPS).** The starting point is H1 2026 net profit of Rp529.1bn; H2 2025 net profit was Rp757.6bn (FY25 1,183.3 − H1'25 425.8).

| | Bear | Base | Bull |
|---|---|---|---|
| H2 2026 vs H2 2025 | 0%: Q4'25's +21% profit is a tough base; rupiah >18,000; volume normalises | +10%: sales +10–12%, full GKSA drag | +18%: teens volume continues, Nov-26 price rise |
| H2 2026 net profit | 757.6 | 833.4 | 894.0 |
| **FY2026 net profit** (529.1 + H2) | **1,286.7** | **1,362.5** | **1,423.1** |
| YoY | +8.7% | +15.1% | +20.3% |
| EPS = DPS (÷ 2,107m) | **Rp611** | **Rp647** | **Rp675** |
| Yield at Rp6,850 | 8.9% | 9.4% | 9.9% |
| Final dividend (Jul 2027), after a Rp190 interim | Rp421 | Rp457 | Rp485 |

Rp bn except per-share figures. All [calculated]. The interim may well rise above Rp190 in Nov 2026, given H1 EPS of Rp251.

**B. What the Heineken fee creep costs minorities** [calculated]:

| Step | Value |
|---|---|
| FY2025 fees | Rp207.7bn → after 22% tax Rp162.0bn → **Rp76.9 per share = 13.7% of EPS** |
| At the FY2024 fee level (Rp122.4bn) | EPS would have been Rp31.6 higher (+5.6%) |
| FY2026E (≈ Rp235bn, §1.4) | ≈ Rp87 per share |
| **Present value of +Rp31.6/share a year at a 12% required return** | **≈ Rp263 per share ≈ 3.8% of the price** |

So the fee step-up has already quietly cost minorities about 4% of the stock's value. It would cost more if "other charges" keep doubling.

**C. The 15% free-float requirement** [calculated]:

| Step | Value |
|---|---|
| Shares needed in public hands | 15% × 2,107m = 316.1m, vs 225.0m now → **91.0m shares** |
| Value at Rp6,850 | Rp624bn |
| At a 10% placement discount (Rp6,165) | Rp561bn raised by Heineken (or the company, if new shares: 4.3% dilution) |
| Normal liquidity (~239k shares/day) | ≈ 380 days of volume |
| Deadline | Three-year phased transition from Apr 2026, first checkpoint Mar 2027 |

**Outcomes:**
- A secondary placement by Heineken is most likely and non-dilutive. It would be a one-off overhang, but it also **adds MSCI/FTSE free-float weight and liquidity**.
- A tender and delisting would crystallise value for minorities. Heineken paid Rp9,000 in 2022, so it has shown it will pay up.
- A regulatory exemption leaves the status quo.

# SUMMARY VERDICT

**What it is.**
- Indonesia's dominant beer franchise: Bintang, ~83% of revenue and ~60–65% of market volume by estimate. It is brewed in two old, fully depreciated breweries and sold through distributors who pre-fund the business.
- It earns a **43% operating margin and a ~226% ROIC**, pays out 100% of profit, and carries no net debt.
- Heineken owns 89.32%. It licenses the main brand to the company at 0%, and since mid-2025 charges it 1.7% of revenue for "global knowledge and services" on top of other rising charges.

**What it earns through the cycle.**

| Period | Net profit |
|---|---|
| 2019 (peak) | Rp1.21tn |
| 2020 (COVID) | Rp0.29tn |
| 2023 | Rp1.07tn |
| 2024 (excise-hike year) | Rp1.14tn |
| 2025 | Rp1.18tn |
| TTM Jun 2026 | **Rp1.29tn** (new high) |
| 2026, likely | **~Rp1.29–1.42tn** |

Mid-cycle is now ~Rp1.3tn, ~Rp620 per share. That is a franchise that has proven it can pass through excise, something its only listed peer cannot.

**What has to be true for it to work as an investment:**

1. Volume growth holds at mid-single digits or better after 2026's teens-level surge. Tourism and affordability packs are the drivers.
2. Excise stays at Rp16,500/L in 2027, or any hike is passed through as in 2024.
3. **Heineken fees stabilise near ~6% of sales.** The GKSA's first full year, 2026, is the test; watch whether the Rp113bn of "other charges" keeps growing.
4. The free-float fix is a market placement or a fair-priced tender, not a prolonged overhang.

If all four hold, a buyer at Rp6,850 collects **~9% a year in dividends that grow high-single-digit**. That is roughly **15–18% annual total return with no multiple change**, from one of the highest-returning consumer franchises on the IDX, at the same multiple as ex-growth regional peers.

**What would break it:**
- A double-digit excise hike combined with weak consumers, so that pass-through fails.
- A Bali tourism shock (28% of sales through one distributor).
- Continued fee creep: every +1pp of revenue to Heineken is −2.3% of operating profit, and the minority has no vote on it.
- Rupiah above ~19,000 with malt costs rising.
- A regulatory escalation: regional bans, Health-Law rules on advertising or sales, or revival of the alcohol-ban bill.

**Position.**
- **Accumulate as a growing-income position.** MLBI is preferred over DLTA for total return: DLTA is a cash box with a shrinking business; MLBI is a compounding franchise at a similar ~9% yield.
- **Sizing is constrained** by ~Rp1.6bn a day of liquidity. The float may widen before 2029, and a Heineken placement would be the moment to add size.
- Before sizing up, get:
  - the Q3 2026 results (the laps are easy);
  - an answer from investor relations on the **Rp113bn of Heineken "other charges"** and the **Rp30.9bn receivable from Heineken International**;
  - any statement on how MLBI will meet the 15% free-float rule.

*This is an analytical teardown, not investment advice.*
