**What it is:** an East Kalimantan crude palm oil producer — 28,026 planted hectares (72% nucleus / 28% plasma), two mills totalling 150 t FFB/hr in Kutai Timur, selling CPO and palm kernel 100% domestically. Listed 25 November 2019 at Rp105. **Market cap Rp3,581.5bn** [calculated: 18,850,000,000 shares × Rp190, 19 Aug 2026], trailing P/E 8.1× [calculated], FY2025-profit dividend Rp6/share (yield 3.2% at Rp190), 52-week range Rp160–290, beta 0.24. Controlled 80.03% by two family holding companies whose disclosed ultimate beneficial owner is **Sohat Chairil**; public float 11.48% and shrinking.

**Period covered:** FY2016 – FY2025 audited, plus Q1 2026 (public expose, May 2026). **Sources in hand:** 2019 IPO prospectus (470 pp), annual reports FY2019–FY2025 (287–340 pp each, all with audited FS and clean text layers), seven public-expose decks FY2019–FY2025+Q1'26, and a dated web pass (19 Aug 2026). ## THE ONE THING TO UNDERSTAND FIRST

**PSGO's two mills process far more fruit than its estate grows, and the filings are constructed so that you cannot see exactly how much.** Purchased third-party FFB was **Rp865.2bn in FY2025 = 49.1% of total COGS and 61.4% of all fruit cost** (both figures straight from AR2025 Note 28; the 61.4% vs 54.4% in FY2024 is the company's own arithmetic). Yet since FY2021 the company has disclosed bought-in fruit **only in Rupiah, never in tonnes**, has never once stated its oil extraction rate in any filing since founding, and stopped publishing mill utilisation after FY2021. The result (worked in §2.4 and §3.5) is that the mill mass balance cannot be closed: at plausible East Kalimantan FFB prices the implied OER is a sub-standard ~18–19%; at plausible OERs the implied purchase price makes the bought fruit near-breakeven or loss-making — the same unresolvable triangle as CSRA, but with one more variable deleted from the disclosure. Roughly a third of this "plantation company" is an un-auditable-from-outside fruit-buying and milling business.

That is the economic reframe. The governance reframe arrived in eleven days of early 2026: on **24 February 2026** the company disclosed that President Director **Budiono Tanbun — the only executive shareholder outside the Chairil family (8.49%), President Director of the parent and of every subsidiary since before the IPO — is under law-enforcement legal proceedings**, described only as "a private matter"; VP Director Elisabeth Priska Chairil (Jalinan shareholder, 38, 50% board-meeting attendance in FY2025) became Acting President Director the same day. Nine days later, on **5 March 2026**, in one day and before one notary: the group **repaid its entire Rp847.8bn of long-term Bank Mandiri debt**, the two borrowing subsidiaries received matching capital injections (+Rp210bn AE, +Rp175bn NUSA), and **8 of 9 subsidiary boards were reshuffled**, installing a person named **Heri Gunawan Lindu** — who appears nowhere else in seven years of filings — as director across all eight. The payoff extinguished the UBO's personal Letter of Undertaking and released Jalinan's pledge of AE shares. The filing presents these as unconnected subsequent events. Group cash fell from Rp1,145.6bn to Rp441.8bn in the quarter.

So the reader should hold both facts at once: a decent, young-estate, now debt-free CPO producer trading at ~8× — and a business where the biggest cost line is untraceable in physical terms, the controlling family has been quietly buying back the float, and the operating head of eighteen years is in criminal process while the corporate structure was rearranged around him in a single day. Everything below is detail on those two sentences.

Two things in this history do the most work. First, **the float bought almost nothing**: the IPO raised Rp299bn gross against Rp1.6tn of pre-IPO paid-in capital — a listing exercise, not a fundraise — and the proceeds were consumed in eighteen months. Second, **the perimeter of the listed group is a family choice, exercised twice**: nine subsidiaries were moved out sideways at par before listing (some to the family's own BBK), and two more were sold in 2023 at a Rp399.7bn gain that manufactured the retained-earnings position from which dividends became legal. What is inside PSGO is what the family elected to list; what it earns includes what the family elected to sell.

Three register mechanics worth naming:

1. **The float is being retired by the controller, not the company.** No corporate buyback exists. Jalinan personally bought 400m shares in Dec-2023 (Rp130/Rp134) and 286.55m in Dec-2024 (Rp165) — ~Rp99.7bn total [calculated] — taking the float from 15.12% to 11.48%. Every purchase was above the Rp105 IPO price and below today's Rp190. The same pattern (controller absorbing float) ran at CSRA before its float hit 6.7%.
2. **The "Sohat/Sohut" ambiguity.** The disclosed UBO is *Sohat* Chairil. PSGO's first President Commissioner (2019 – 30 Jun 2021) is printed *Sohut* Chairil, and subsidiary AKM's 1.00% minority holder is "Tn. Sohut Chairil" in AR2025. Five filings across seven years never clarify whether these are one person spelled two ways or two people. Recorded, not concluded.
3. **Two coexisting "ultimate parent" statements.** The narrative names the individual (Sohat Chairil); FS Note 1a names PT Jalinankasih Sesama as "entitas induk dan entitas induk terakhir." Both are internally consistent, but nothing above Jalinan's shareholder list is ever audited or shown.

### Land title

HGU 35-yr / HGB 20-yr leasehold, expiries 2030–2059, "renewable" per management. **No consolidated certificated-hectare schedule has ever been published** — only collateral fragments (AE ~9,867 ha across 24 SHGU parcels; NUSA 19 parcels; GPM 4,483.89 ha). The IPO land bank of 80,900 ha (62,168 nucleus + 18,733 plasma) is now mostly gone or unusable: the divested and carved-out entities took land with them, and the 2026 deck's "land scarcity" admission confirms the remaining bank is not plantable at scale. On forestry exposure: PSGO's filings are clean of *kawasan hutan*/Satgas PKH language (keyword-swept in AR2024 and AR2025), **but the IDX put Palma Serasih on its 13 Oct 2025 letter to 19 palm issuers about plantation land inside forest areas** (Perpres 5/2025 regime, fines up to Rp25m/ha/yr); the company answered that it "does not own plantation land in forest areas," and no 2026 resolution is on the record [web]. The group's own prospectus history (CHBE's Rp60.2bn land-permit loss, and plasma agreements that state some plasma land lies *outside* the subsidiaries' HGU) says the exposure class is not hypothetical here. Unresolved; on the watch list (§3.8).

Take FY2025 as printed: CPO 140,179 t; own FFB 480,802 t; purchased FFB cost Rp865,201m; purchased tonnage undisclosed; OER undisclosed. Then for any assumed OER, purchased tonnage and implied purchase price follow mechanically [all calculated]:

And the corresponding KER (PK 21,579 t ÷ throughput) runs only 2.8–3.4% against a normal 4–5% — every branch of the tree has at least one implausible leaf. Meanwhile the *disclosed* 2019–2020 throughput implies OER ~24.5–24.9%, beyond the ceiling in the other direction. **Conclusion, stated plainly: PSGO's published operating data are not internally consistent enough to verify what its mills actually do**, and the three numbers that would fix it — purchased tonnage, OER, utilisation — were all discontinued or never started. The economics in §3.5 therefore carry ranges, not points. (CSRA had the same gap on one variable; PSGO has it on three.)

What can be said with confidence, because it needs only Rupiah lines: at FY2025 prices, a tonne of CPO made from *purchased* fruit at ~Rp3,100–3,600/kg with ~19% OER costs ~Rp16.3–19.0m before PK credit, ~Rp14.5–17.3m after [calculated], against a realised ~Rp15.7m ASP — i.e. **the bought-in half of the business runs between small-profit and small-loss and exists mainly to absorb mill fixed costs**, while virtually all true profit comes from the 480kt of own fruit. That is the same conclusion CSRA's segment note proves explicitly; PSGO publishes no segment split to prove or disprove it.

## 2.6 Key variables — what actually moves the model

1. **CPO price (≈90% of revenue).** Every Rp1,000/kg (Rp1m/t) on ~147kt of CPO sales ≈ ±Rp147bn revenue ≈ ±Rp115bn PBT after tax-free pass-through of purchased-fruit cost linkage is ignored — but fruit prices co-move, so the *net* sensitivity is closer to ±Rp75–90bn PBT [calculated: own-fruit tonnage bears full price delta; bought-in margin roughly price-neutral]. Against FY2025 PBT of Rp555bn, a 10% CPO move is ~±15% of pre-tax profit.
2. **Purchased-fruit share and spread (49.1% of COGS).** The bought half earns a processing spread that oscillates around zero (§2.4). When local FFB tightens (as in 2021–22 and again 2025: +24.6% bought-in reliance per AR2025 narrative), reported gross margin compresses even in good CPO years. This is the margin-quality variable, and it is unobservable in tonnes.
3. **Own FFB volume (the entire profit engine).** 480–490kt, flat, 99% mature, La Niña-sensitive. The FY2026 target (+20.6% to 579,926t) would require yield jumping 17.4 → ~20.6 t/ha — management has missed every FFB target since 2021 by 13–23%.
4. **Kernel complex (9.8% of revenue).** FY2025 PK ASP +53% [calculated] on the lauric shock; full reversion to FY2024 PK prices costs ~Rp85bn revenue ≈ ~15% of PBT [calculated: (12.11 − 7.93)m/t × 20.7kt].
5. **Policy wedge: B50 + levy.** B50 in force 1 Jul 2026 (full rollout 1 Oct 2026), funded by the 12.5% export levy (PMK 9/2026); Aug-2026 reference price US$996.5/t with US$272.6/t of combined duty+levy. PSGO sells 100% domestic: the levy *depresses* its netback via the domestic-price discount to world prices, while B50 *supports* domestic offtake volume — it is on the right side of the demand shift but the wrong side of the funding mechanism, and (unlike TAPG) its current customer book is no longer concentrated in allocation holders.
6. **Governance/control events.** Non-financial but priced: resolution of the President Director's case, the FY2026 RUPS board decisions, any further float absorption by Jalinan, and any move by the family on the remaining 11.48% float. On a Rp3.6tn cap with Rp3.3tn EV, the float is worth ~Rp411bn — one year of dividends plus change.

## 2.7 Demand

Domestic-heavy and policy-made. Indonesian biodiesel absorbs the marginal tonne: B40 ran through 1H2026, **B50 took force 1 Jul 2026** (Kepmen ESDM 257.K/EK.01/MEM.E/2026; 2026 allocation lifted to ~17.6m kL, +12.5%), pulling an incremental ~6–7m t of CPO into FAME at full run-rate — while GAPKI data showed food-sector domestic consumption −3.6% in 2025 against biodiesel +11%. The HIP biodiesel price formula is cost-plus (KPB CPO + US$85/t margin), so allocation-holding refiners are structurally price-insensitive feedstock buyers — a volume underwrite for upstream sellers like PSGO even when, as now, its direct sales route runs through local aggregators rather than the allocation holders themselves. Export demand is the release valve PSGO does not use (0% export), so its realised price is the domestic KPB-linked price: benchmark context, FCPO RM4,873/t on 19 Aug 2026 (+8.4% y/y; all-time high RM7,268 Mar-2022), Indonesian reference price US$996.5/t for Aug-2026 — third straight monthly decline and first sub-$1,000 print of the streak. Q1'26 already shows the price rollover: revenue −8.7% YoY on FFB +13%.

## 2.8 Supply

Indonesia produces ~46–48m t of CPO against slowing area growth; 2025 output recovered modestly, and the 2026 policy year opened with El Niño risk flagged for late-2026 (NOAA probabilities were running high per the TAPG pass — production impact would land in 2027). East Kalimantan specifically is a fruit-deficit milling region in stretches — which is why PSGO can buy 200–280kt of third-party fruit a year, and also why that fruit is not cheap. Company-level supply is the §2.2 story: 99% mature, 1–2% immature, no land pipeline, replanting not yet due — a production plateau of ~480–550kt own FFB for the rest of the decade, with weather as the residual variable.

7. **Dividend cadence post-degearing.** With zero debt and ~Rp350bn of mid-cycle earnings, a 40%+ payout (Rp7.5+/sh, ~4%) is easily fundable; the FY2025 cut to Rp6 despite +26% profit says the family prioritised the loan payoff. What they do with FY2026 cash — dividend, capex on the Rp450bn plan, or something across the perimeter — is the single best tell of intent.

# SUMMARY VERDICT

**What the business is:** a low-cost, prime-age, 28,026-ha East Kalimantan CPO estate (fruit at Rp1,132/kg — cheaper than any peer in this series) bolted to 150 t/hr of milling capacity it cannot fill, run under near-absolute family control (80.03% via two holdcos, float 11.48%) with an operating perimeter the family has redrawn twice at its own prices. Roughly all true profit is the rent on its own mature hectares; the ~Rp865bn/yr bought-in fruit leg is a near-zero-margin throughput business whose physical size the company has declined to disclose since 2021 — the mill mass balance cannot be closed from public filings, and that opacity is a choice.

**What it earns through the cycle:** ~Rp350bn net at mid-cycle prices [calculated three ways: scenario arithmetic, FY2024 actual, management's own FY2026 guide], against Rp442.8bn printed in FY2025 on a CPO+kernel price year. Cash conversion is excellent (OCF 1.7× earnings cumulatively), the balance sheet went from 3.4× geared (2018) to debt-free (Mar-2026), and dividends started in 2024 at a 25–43% payout.

**What has to be true for it to work at Rp190 (8.1× trailing, ~10× mid-cycle, Rp117.5m EV/ha):** only that prices hold near mid-cycle, the estate keeps yielding ~17 t/ha, and the family keeps distributing. Any yield progress toward the 20+ t/ha its tree age supports is uncosted upside. The stock is not expensive; on EV/ha it is the cheapest quality-adjusted estate in the peer table after CSRA.

**What would break it:** governance, not agronomy. The unresolved criminal proceeding against the eighteen-year operating head; a one-day, unexplained restructuring of every subsidiary board around an unknown new director; a controller with a demonstrated habit of perimeter transactions (nine entities out at par pre-IPO, the growth subsidiary out at 3.3× book in 2023) and of buying in the float; a Satgas PKH question mark the company has answered only with a denial; and three disclosure deletions (bought-in tonnage, OER, utilisation) exactly where the model is most sensitive. None of these costs anything today; all of them price the minority's claim on that Rp350bn.

**Position:** as a business, better than its reputation-by-obscurity — genuinely cheap fruit, young trees, no debt, real cash. As a security, this is a captive minority stub in a family vehicle mid-way through some corporate manoeuvre it has not explained, with CSRA-style float erosion already underway. It earns a place on the watch list at a price, not a core position: the discount demanded should be to *governance*, and 8× trailing / 10× mid-cycle is fair value, not a margin of safety. The two triggers that would change that: disclosure that closes the mill mass balance (would prove the profit pool is bigger and cleaner than assumed), or resolution of the February–March 2026 events in a way that shows minorities were not the residual. Watch item #7 — where the post-debt cash goes — answers most of this within a year.
