**What it does:** It is a branded chilled-and-frozen food manufacturer in Indonesia. There are two segments:
- **Premium Dairy** (Cimory brand): yogurt drinks, squeeze and stick yogurt, UHT and fresh milk, Eat Milk.
- **Premium Consumer Foods** (Kanzler sausages, nuggets and meatballs, Mamayo mayonnaise, Euro Gourmet sauces).

Production runs in six plants across Bogor/Sentul, Semarang, Pasuruan and Tangerang. The group sells through its own cold-chain distributor (PT Macrosentra Niagaboga) into modern trade, general trade and its own door-to-door channel, Miss Cimory. It owns no cows.

**Headline numbers:**
- **FY2025:** net sales Rp10,724bn (+18.8%), operating profit Rp2,268bn (21.1%), net profit Rp2,033bn (19.0%).
- **1H26:** sales Rp6,461bn (+25.5%), net profit Rp1,172bn (+17.9%).
- **Market:** share price Rp4,500 (18-Sep-2026) × 7,934.7m shares = **market cap ≈ Rp35.7tn**, with **net cash Rp3.66tn** at 30-Jun-2026 and zero bank debt.
- **Period covered:** FY2018–1H2026.

**Sources in hand:**

- Annual reports FY2023, FY2024 and FY2025, each with audited financial statements and a comparative column.

## THE ONE THING TO UNDERSTAND FIRST

**Cimory is sold as a dairy company, but since 2023 it has been a sausage-and-nugget company. A stalled yogurt franchise pays for it, and a large cash pile sits on top.**

Four facts carry that statement:

1. **Consumer foods (Kanzler) went from 35% of sales in FY21 to 62% in FY25.** It added Rp5.2tn of revenue over the period, while dairy added Rp1.4tn. Across FY22–FY25, **dairy compounded at 4.9% a year and consumer foods at 32.7%** `[calculated]`. Dairy grew only +3.4%, +6.0% and +5.3% in FY23–FY25, and its segment profit *fell* in FY23 (§2.1, §3.3). The yogurt business had 53% share of Indonesia's yogurt market at IPO, and it saturated its core channel around the time of the IPO.

2. **The 2026 dairy rebound (+54% in 1H26) is largely a government programme, not a recovery of the franchise.** Cimory began supplying 125ml UHT milk to *Makan Bergizi Gratis* (MBG), the national free-meals programme, in January 2026 from existing capacity. Management and analysts say it earns **below commercial margins** (web). Gross margin fell from 44.9% to 43.3% in 1H26 while sales rose 25%. The company has disclosed no MBG revenue figure. MBG is policy-dependent volume: large, low-margin, and awarded on terms Cimory does not set (§2.6, §3.7).

3. **About 43% of total assets is cash and bonds, not plant.** At end-FY25 cash plus marketable securities was Rp3,780bn, or 43.2% of assets and 55.7% of equity. **77% of the securities book is USD government bonds**, about US$130m, and the book is unhedged. This pile is why reported ROE is 30% while the operating business earns about 60% on the capital it actually uses `[calculated, §3.5]`. It also produces 10–12% of pre-tax profit as interest and FX gains, which move with the rupiah rather than with yogurt.

4. **The company is still controlled like a family firm.**
   - The founder, Bambang Sutantio, owns 53.56% directly. Three family members, all stated as "related" to him (the prospectus says they are his sons), hold another 21.5%. The insider bloc is **75.1%**, and the real free float is about 19%.
   - Every related-party counterparty is a company Bambang chairs.
   - An audit-committee member is CFO of the founder's hospitality group.
   - Related-party trading is small in value (0.6% of sales; 2.2% of COGS in FY25), but it swings unpredictably. Purchases from the founder's PT Macrotama Binasantika went from Rp176bn (FY22) to Rp10bn (FY23) to **Rp253bn (FY24)** to Rp110bn (FY25). The goods bought are never described (§1.4).

**Why it matters:** The margin, cash and valuation story rests on consumer foods continuing to compound at 25–30%. Dairy cannot carry the group at commercial margins, and MBG volume is cheap growth of uncertain quality. The cash pile both lowers measured returns and supports the dividend: FY25 cash dividends were 158% of free cash flow.

- **The 2015 restructuring is the origin of today's group.** Before 2015 the Macroprima (Kanzler) and Macrosentra (distribution) businesses were separate founder companies. **What the long run shows:**

1. **Sales to related parties are shrinking into irrelevance**, from 5% to 0.6% of revenue. The founder's shops and restaurants are a legacy, not a channel.
2. **Purchases are not trending; they are lurching.** Macrotama purchases went 176 → 10 → 253 → 110 in consecutive years. In FY23 and FY24, **the entire "purchase of merchandise inventories" line in COGS equals the related-party purchase total to the rupiah** (100,197 and 324,531). In FY25 it again equals 127,274. Every finished good the group buys in rather than makes comes from a founder company.
   - Nothing in any filing says what the goods are, how they are priced, or why volume swung 25× in a year. The prospectus identifies Macrotama as a supplier of **sausage casings**, but a Rp253bn year is far larger than casings for a Rp5tn meat business would need `[inference]`.
3. **The accounting policy note says the opposite of the governance section.** FS Note 2i reads: RP transactions "are made based on terms agreed by the parties, **which may not be the same** as those with unrelated parties." The AR governance section and the audit committee (which includes a founder-group CFO) certify arm's-length pricing. No independent appraisal is reported for any year.
4. **Payable days to Macrotama ran 120–196 days** against 30-day terms before the IPO (PROSP). FY23 payable of Rp15.4bn exceeded that year's Rp10.1bn purchases. Pre-IPO, the founder's supplier was financing the listed group, and some stale balance persisted after.
5. **IP sits with the founder personally.** The mascot copyright and bottle design are licensed for Rp1m in total and renew yearly. The word marks Cimory and Kanzler are group-registered, but the visual identity is not.

**Materiality verdict:** The flows are too small to move group earnings, at 2% of COGS in FY25. They are large enough and erratic enough that a year like FY24 (Rp324bn, 6.6% of COGS) could carry margin effects the reader cannot see. It is the least-disclosed line in otherwise clean accounts.

# 02 — MODEL

## 2.1 Product

**What is sold.** The group sells chilled and frozen branded packaged food to Indonesian consumers through modern-trade chains, general-trade stores and its own agents. Pricing is premium: at IPO, Cimory Pouch cost Rp83,000/kg retail against UHT at Rp25,000/kg (PROSP).

The inputs are:
- mostly imported milk powder (whole milk powder and skim milk powder, 100% imported);
- local fresh milk from cooperatives;
- imported beef;
- local chicken;
- sugar;
- imported specialty packaging (Tetra Pak, pouches).

The value is in formulation, brand, cold chain and shelf access, not in owning the raw material.

## 2.3 Purchase — what the business buys and owns

### Raw materials: self-supply ratio ≈ 0%

Cimory owns **no farms and no cows** (PROSP; no herd in any AR). The only farm link is founder-owned farms supplying "\<1% of net sales" worth of milk before the IPO.
- **Local fresh milk:** from cooperatives representing 18,458 smallholder farmers (FY25; 15,784 in FY24), priced on quality tests. Indonesia's national farmgate reference is about Rp7,600–8,000/L (Batu, East Java, 2026).
- **Milk powder:** 100% imported (NZ, Australia, Europe, US; suppliers at IPO included Synlait and Anta Tirta Kirana).
- **Beef:** imported (NZ/Australia).
- **Chicken:** bought weekly from integrators (a Japfa unit appeared in the IPO supplier list).

**Why this matters:** Unlike a planter, Cimory cannot gain from owning its input. It is a **price-taker on global dairy (GDT) and on USD/IDR**. Its only defences are:
- a 6-month forward buy (up from 3 months pre-COVID), which *lags* rather than removes cost moves;
- premium pricing;
- mix.

The FY22 episode shows the defence is imperfect. The FY21 deck said the company was "well positioned to weather inflation thanks to premium price points". FY22 gross margin then fell **6.4pts** (48.2% → 41.8%).

## 2.6 Key variables — what actually moves the model

Anchor: **FY25 sales Rp10,724bn; COGS Rp5,870bn; operating profit Rp2,268bn.**

### 1. Consumer-foods volume growth (62% of sales)
Every 5pts of consumer-foods growth equals Rp332bn of sales. At about a 45% gross margin that is Rp150bn of gross profit, **6.6% of operating profit** `[calculated]`. Consumer-foods growth slowed from +29% (FY25) to +14.8% (1Q26) and about +6% (2Q26). This is the most important number in the model.

### 2. Imported dairy input prices (WMP/SMP) and beef, in USD
Raw and packaging materials were Rp4,892bn (45.6% of sales). The split by ingredient is not disclosed. At IPO, raw materials were about 57–60% of COGS and packaging 18–20%.

Illustrative sensitivity `[assumption: ~50% of RM&P is USD-linked]`: a 10% move in USD input cost = Rp245bn = **2.3pts of gross margin = 10.8% of operating profit**.

Path of inputs:
- WMP (GDT) ~US$4,600/t at the Mar-22 peak; ~3,200 in FY23; ~3,900–3,960 in FY24–25; ~3,560 in Sep-26.
- **SMP rose from US$2,615/t (Jan-26) to US$3,689/t (Sep-26), +41%.** Cimory's SMP cost was +17.6% y/y in 1H26 (web).
- Beef was US$4,300–5,766/t over FY19–FY25 (company data).

### 3. USD/IDR
Two opposing exposures:
- **Cost side:** imported milk powder, beef and packaging.
- **Balance sheet:** net USD monetary assets were **Rp2,275bn** (mainly ~US$130m of USD government bonds).

Per the company, a 5% IDR move = ±Rp113.7bn of PBT from translation. A 10% rupiah depreciation therefore adds about **Rp227bn of non-operating FX gain** against about **Rp245bn of extra input cost** `[calculated, same assumption as above]`.

**The USD bond book is a de facto hedge of the import bill**, but in different lines of the P&L and with different timing. The FX gain is immediate and non-cash; the cost hit lags six months and is permanent. Explicit hedging: none. The rupiah was ~16,240 at Dec-24, ~16,840 at Feb-26 and ~17,990 at 28-Sep-26.

### 4. Promotion intensity (A&P % of sales)
A&P ranged 11.0–14.3% of sales over FY22–FY25. **Each 1pt = Rp107bn = 4.7% of operating profit.** Historically this is the swing variable management uses to defend volume, as in FY24.

### 5. MBG volume and pricing
Not disclosed. The 1H26 dairy increment was Rp976bn y/y. Suppose half of it is MBG at, say, a 10pt lower gross margin than commercial dairy. That costs about Rp49bn of gross profit per half year `[illustrative only; the company gives no split]`. MBG's national budget and procurement terms are political.

### 6. Treasury yield on Rp3.7tn cash and bonds
FY25 interest income was Rp184.7bn (≈4.6% yield) plus Rp76.8bn of FX. It is **10–12% of PBT**. A 100bp move in yields equals about Rp37bn of PBT.

### 7. Channel mix (Miss Cimory and general trade versus modern trade)
Channel margins are not disclosed. The 1H26 distribution and salary cost growth of +43–44% suggests the newer channels carry higher cost-to-serve.

## 2.8 Supply

- **Domestic fresh milk** is about 1mn t a year against demand of about 4.7mn t: **~20% self-sufficiency**, with ~3.7mn t (milk equivalent) imported at about Rp25tn a year. The herd is about 540k cows at 12.5–16 L/cow/day, and 85% of farmers keep fewer than 50 head.
- **Policy:** Kementan targets +600k dairy cows by 2029 (the earlier plan was 1mn). Actual 2025 imports were about 15k head against a 200k plan. Supply-side policy has under-delivered. Local milk will stay scarce, so **imported powder remains the marginal supply and GDT prices remain the marginal cost** for all Indonesian dairy processors.
- **Competitive supply shock (NEW, Sep-2026): FrieslandCampina is taking control of Ultrajaya (ULTJ).** The deal is a Rp14.6tn transaction (rights issue at Rp2,150 plus a contribution of Frisian Flag Indonesia), with an EGM on 27-Oct-2026.
  - It combines about **52% of Indonesian UHT volume** (ULTJ 36% + Frisian Flag 16%) under a global dairy major with its own milk pool.
  - This lands exactly in the category (UHT) and channel (MBG) where Cimory's 2026 dairy growth is coming from.
- **Processed meat competitors:** So Good (Japfa), Champ (Madusari), Fiesta (Charoen Pokphand), Belfoods. Owners are integrated poultry producers with their own chicken. Kanzler buys chicken on the market. This is the structural cost disadvantage in consumer foods that Kanzler's brand premium has so far outrun.
- **Regulation:**
  - The sugar-sweetened-beverage excise (MBDK) was not implemented in 2026. The 2027 target is ~Rp1.6tn, and dairy coverage is unclear.

# SUMMARY VERDICT

**What the business is.** CMRY is a branded chilled-and-frozen food platform with a cold-chain distribution system and a direct-sales army. It has two brands that each lead or nearly lead their niche:
- **Cimory yogurt**, with >50% of Indonesian yogurt;
- **Kanzler**, the fastest-growing premium processed-meat brand.

It owns no farms, buys most of its inputs in dollars, and turns them into 45% gross margins through brand, format innovation and shelf presence. The operating business earns **40–60% on the capital it uses** and converts about 94% of profit into cash. Governance is family-controlled, but the accounts are clean: unqualified EY audits, no debt, no receivable games, and related-party flows that are small (the one exception being the undisclosed nature of the Macrotama purchases).

**What it earns through the cycle.**
- FY22 (input shock): operating margin 19.7%.
- FY23 (dairy stall): 18.4%.
- FY24 (promotion spike): 18.5%.
- FY25 (clean year): 21.1%.

A reasonable through-cycle view is **~19–20% operating margin and ~17–18% net margin including treasury income**. On a Rp12–13tn sales base that is **Rp2.1–2.3tn of net profit**, or roughly Rp265–290 EPS. The stock at Rp4,500 is **15.5–17× through-cycle earnings, about 14× ex-cash**, with a 4.4% dividend yield. It is not expensive for a 50%-ROIC franchise, and far cheaper than the 49× it listed at.

**What has to be true for it to work:**
1. **Kanzler keeps compounding at ≥15%.** It is 62% of sales and the only engine that has worked since 2022. The Bogor plant and the Sep-26 MP injection imply management sees capacity, not demand, as the constraint. 2Q26's +6% says otherwise, or reflects the fire. This is the single variable to underwrite.
2. **Gross margin holds ≥42% through the 2026 SMP and rupiah spike**, i.e. pricing power still exists. The trade-down pack launches suggest it is limited.
3. **MBG proves accretive in absolute profit**, even if dilutive in percentage, and is not taken by the Frisian Flag–Ultrajaya combination.
4. **The cash is deployed or returned.** At Rp200 DPS it is being returned, slowly.

**What would break it:**
- Consumer-foods growth falling to mid-single digits for several quarters. The integrated poultry groups (Japfa's So Good, CP's Fiesta) own their chicken and could compete on price in a stretched consumer market.
- Dairy stuck at +5% ex-MBG, with MBG margins set by the state.
- A gross margin back at 41–42% (the FY22–23 level) *combined with* S&M at 24% (the FY24 level), which would put operating margin near 15%.
- A governance event in the related-party channel.

**Position:** A high-quality franchise at a fair-to-cheap multiple, **with a deteriorating near-term trajectory that the headline 1H26 numbers disguise**:
- Operating profit ex-fire is +17%, reported +9%, and 2Q26 reported is down.
- Cash flow is flat.
- Consumer foods is decelerating.
- About half of net-profit growth is FX.

Base-case FY26 EPS growth is about 9%, with a real risk of a down year if gross margin lands in the bottom half of guidance. **This is an accumulate-on-evidence name, not a buy-on-price name.** The evidence to wait for is consumer-foods growth back above 15% in 3Q26/4Q26 with gross margin ≥43%. If both show, the 16× multiple and 4.4% yield on a 50%-ROIC business are attractive. If consumer foods prints another single-digit quarter, the 18× bear-case multiple on falling EPS is where the stock goes to find support.
