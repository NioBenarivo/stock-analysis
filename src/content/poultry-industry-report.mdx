## 00. Executive summary

**The three together are the industry.** Net sales Rp144.1tn in FY2025 (CPIN 70.7, JPFA 60.7, MAIN 12.7) against a national feed market of roughly 36m tonnes (GPMT outlook 35–40m t) and ~3.9m tonnes of chicken demand (BPS 2025, via the JPFA teardown); by the companies' own market maps they hold ~62% of feed capacity and ~69% of day-old-chick output. This is the opposite of palm oil, where the thirteen listed names were 12% of the crop: here two firms set the price of chicks and feed for every independent farmer in the country, and the state regulates them because of it.

**It is one business model in three sizes, and the size decides everything.** All three run the same chain — imported grandparent stock → breeding farms → hatcheries → day-old chicks → broiler farms → live bird → slaughter and processing — around a feed mill. The economics are identical in shape: the feed mill never loses money (every year disclosed: CPIN FY2021–25, JPFA FY2022–25, MAIN FY2020–25), the bird segments swing with the government-managed live-bird price, and consolidated profit is the sum. What differs is scale and integration depth. CPIN and JPFA sell 60% of their feed and 61–64% of their chicks to their own farms; MAIN sells 27% and 22%. That single ratio explains why FY2025 net margins were 8.0% / 6.6% / 3.1% and why, in the 2023 trough, CPIN and JPFA earned Rp2.3tn and Rp0.9tn while MAIN earned Rp63bn.

**Six findings worth carrying:**

1. **Raw material is ~80–90% of manufacturing cost and it is locally priced by decree.** Corn imports have been banned for feed since 2017; the government sets a floor (HPP Rp5,500/kg) and a ceiling (HET Rp7,000/kg) and subsidises corn to independent farmers, not integrators. Raw materials are 68% of CPIN's revenue, 62.5% of JPFA's, 82% of MAIN's. Pass-through lag is the feed margin: 5.5–8.7% in FY2025, 0.9% for MAIN in Q2 2026 when corn hit Rp6,600/kg.
2. **The bird segments are one price.** Broiler/commercial-farm segment results moved together in every year: FY2023 −Rp420bn / −611bn / −218bn; FY2025 +3,350bn / ~+1,797bn / +53bn. Government culling and hatching-egg-cutting circulars (2019, 2021, five in 2023, 2025) are the mechanism; nobody in the peer group controls its own selling price for a live bird.
3. **Related-party cost is a bigger differentiator than operating cost.** CPIN pays a ~Rp715bn/yr "Nugen" royalty (1.0% of sales, basis undisclosed since 2009) plus Rp3.3tn of affiliate purchases; JPFA buys Rp7.0tn of raw material (14.8% of COGS; 15.0% for all affiliates) through the parent's private Singapore trading arm on an undefined "max 5%" margin; MAIN buys Rp1.77tn (15.5% of COGS) from two Leong Hup affiliates. In the FY2023 trough, CPIN's royalty alone was 21.5% of pre-tax profit.
4. **Balance sheets diverged in FY2024–25 and are re-converging in 2026.** Net debt/equity at end-2025: CPIN 0.05x, MAIN 0.26x (0.44x with the off-balance-sheet syirkah), JPFA 0.41x. By June 2026 JPFA was at 0.64x with Rp4.4tn of new debt the filings do not explain and a US$350m bond that fell due in March without a word of disclosure; MAIN was back to 0.34x building a feed mill into an oversupplied market.
5. **FY2025–1H2026 earnings are policy-inflated.** The free-meals programme (MBG: 63m beneficiaries, Rp101tn spent in 1H2026) is the demand event; 1H2026 net profit +95% / +100% / +267%. The budget was cut in August 2026, farm-gate prices crashed below cost in June, the 2026 GPS import quota was raised 38% (that is 2027's chick supply), and Permentan 10/2024 forces 50% of chicks to independent farmers from 2027. The two leaders are exposed to all four at once.
6. **Nobody discloses a volume.** Not one of the three has ever printed feed tonnes, chicks hatched, or kilograms slaughtered in an annual report or deck. Every unit-economics comparison in this report is an inference, and the market-share maps all three reproduce cite a 2021 consultant study.

Two things stand out before any operating comparison. The market prices CPIN and JPFA on the same book multiple (1.47x) with JPFA 2.1 P/E points cheaper — a discount that maps onto the leverage and disclosure gap in §4.6, not onto operating quality. And MAIN trades at half book and 3.8x earnings, which is what the market pays for a third-tier integrator with 78% of its chicks sold to the farmers who bear the cycle first.

### Three consolidation traps you must know before comparing anything

**1. Product-basis sales are not segment sales, and neither is "revenue".** All three report external net sales by product (broiler, feed, DOC, processed) *and* a segment note with inter-segment sales. The bird segments book the feed and DOC they sell to partner farmers as their own external sales, so "broiler revenue" overstates bird sales. Gross segment sales exceed net sales by 72% (CPIN, FY2025: 121.5 vs 70.7tn), 53% (JPFA: ~92.9 vs 60.7tn, from the MD&A segment totals; the FS segment note for FY2025 is OCR-illegible) and ~22% (MAIN: ~15.5 vs 12.7tn) [calc]. The deck pie charts switch between bases without footnotes (JPFA from FY2023; MAIN's segment mix is on the gross basis). This report uses segment-basis totals for margins and product-basis for the revenue mix, and says which.

**2. "Operating profit" means three different things.** CPIN's is the FS line (Rp8,133bn). JPFA's decks print GP − selling − G&A (Rp6,150bn), which excludes Rp134bn of FY2024 write-offs and the bio-asset line; the FS figure is Rp6,184bn. MAIN's decks use "EBIT" including FX. All three teardowns rebuilt to the audited FS operating line; that is what appears here.

**3. What sits outside the consolidated perimeter.** MAIN carries Rp518bn of sharia "syirkah" bank financing presented as neither liability nor equity and excluded from its published gearing (0.26x → 0.44x including it). JPFA consolidates a 50/50 Sumatra subsidiary (Indojaya Agrinusa) whose unnamed partner takes Rp360bn of FY2025 profit through minorities, and keeps a 49% poultry JV (Griya Mitra) financed by JPFA trade credit off the balance sheet. CPIN's FY2025 eliminations exceed the printed inter-segment rows by ~Rp6tn of unlabelled "others". Net-debt and margin comparisons below use the fullest basis available and flag where it is not the company's.

### What the internal ratios say

| FY2025 (segment basis) | CPIN | JPFA | MAIN |
|---|---|---|---|
| Feed sold internally | 59.8% | ~58% (57.3% FY2024) | 27% |
| DOC sold internally | 63.7% | ~61% (FY2024) | 22% |

Two-thirds of CPIN's and JPFA's chick output and three-fifths of their feed never meet a market price: they are transferred at internal prices to farms the group owns or contracts. That does two things. It gives the feed and DOC segments a captive floor in a downturn — CPIN's feed result was Rp4.56tn in the FY2023 trough, its best feed year — and it means the group captures the whole chain's margin when live-bird prices are high (FY2025 broiler result Rp3.35tn). MAIN sells 73% of its feed and 78% of its chicks to independent farmers: it earns the integrator's input margin but not the bird margin, and its customers are the people the cycle bankrupts. Its FY2025 broiler result was Rp53bn on Rp5.0tn of segment sales.

### The finding that should change how you read "growth"

CPIN's external feed sales rose 27.6% in FY2025 (Rp16.4tn → 21.0tn) while its internal feed fell 8.9%, and its internal DOC share dropped from 73% to 64% in a year. JPFA's external feed grew 7.6%, internal ~9%. Read against Permentan 10/2024 (≥50% of chicks to independents from January 2027, §06), CPIN is already re-externalising; JPFA is not. The regulation converts integration margin into input-seller margin — higher-quality earnings (feed has not lost money in any disclosed year) at lower capture. CPIN's FY2025 pivot is the first evidence of an integrator pricing the rule in; JPFA's filings do not mention it.

## 06. The managed cycle — who is exposed

### The mechanism

Indonesian broiler supply is quota-managed at the top and rationed by instruction at the bottom. All genetics enter as imported grandparent stock under an annual quota (578,000 birds in 2025, raised to 800,000 for 2026 — +38%, linked publicly to MBG demand); 15–18 months later that is chick supply. When live-bird prices fall below the reference (HAP Rp18,000/kg from June 2025; Rp19,500 from 15 July 2026), Ditjen PKH orders early culling of parent stock and destruction of hatching eggs — 2019, 2021, five circulars in 2023 (~10m PS culled, ~105m eggs cut), April 2025. Corn is priced by decree at both ends (HPP Rp5,500 floor, HET Rp7,000 ceiling; feed-corn imports banned since 2017; SPHP subsidised corn to independents only). The structural condition is ~10% DOC oversupply (3.5bn vs 3.2bn chicks, 2025).

### What each down-cycle did

| Episode | CPIN | JPFA | MAIN |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2020 COVID | NP Rp3.85tn (record year then) | sales −5%, NP Rp1.22tn, capex halved | net loss Rp39bn (only loss year) |
| 2021–22 input inflation + oversupply | GM 15.7% → 14.3%; broiler −Rp1.27tn (2021), −316bn (2022); NP −19%; FY2022 dividend skipped | farm −Rp744bn; FCF −656bn; ND/E 81%; bond at 80 cents; S&P cut to B+ | farms and food −Rp324bn; interest cover 0.8x; NP Rp26bn |
| 2023 trough | GM 13.4%; broiler and DOC both lost (−Rp722bn); NP Rp2.32tn; capex halved | breeding −79%, farm −611bn; NP Rp0.93tn; interest cover 2.2x; no dividend | Q1 net loss Rp173bn; year +Rp63bn |
| 2024–25 recovery | NP 3.71 → 5.64tn; net debt → 1.8tn | OP ×2.3; net debt −2.0tn; dividend 140 | NP ×7.7 then −19%; two Q2 losses |
| Q2 2026 | still profitable (~Rp1.1tn [calc]) | OP halved from Q1 (2.6 → 1.1tn) | net loss Rp24bn; feed margin 0.9% |

Amplitude on the FS operating line, trough-to-peak FY2023 → FY2025: CPIN Rp3.65tn → 8.13tn (2.2x); JPFA 2.21 → 6.18tn (2.8x); MAIN 0.31 → 0.76tn → 0.57tn (2.4x then −25%). Same cycle, same timing (trough Q1–Q2, harvest Q4), three amplitudes.

### Exposure to what comes next

Four things arrive together in 2027, and they cut differently:

| Risk | Mechanism | CPIN | JPFA | MAIN |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **2026 GPS quota +38%** | chick supply up from ~2027, into a market already 10% oversupplied | most exposed in absolute terms (38% of DOC) but 64% internal | 25% of DOC, 61% internal; DOC is its best-margin segment (15%) | 78% of DOC sold to independents who will be the first not to restock |
| **Permentan 10/2024** — ≥50% of chicks to independents from 1 Jan 2027 | forces disintegration of the chick flow; revision under way (draft DOC reference price Rp4,800) | 36% external → must move ~Rp1.3tn of DOC sales outside, or grow DOC (which the FY2025 capex does) | 39% external → ~Rp0.9tn; no mention in any filing | already compliant (78% external) — the only one the rule helps |
| **MBG budget cut** (§07) | demand pulse withdrawn | processing 15.5% of sales; RPA network is the MBG supplier | processing 17.5%; 1H2026 +28.7% is the most MBG-shaped growth of the three | Q1 2026 explicitly credited to MBG; 3% processing |
| **Corn** (HET Rp7,000; imports contested; Rp6,600–6,935/kg in 1H2026) | raw material 62–82% of sales; pass-through lag | 10% corn ≈ Rp2.4tn COGS ≈ 31% of PBT [teardown calc] | Rp500/kg ≈ Rp2tn COGS vs Rp3.1tn feed result | Rp500/kg ≈ Rp240bn vs Rp440bn feed result; 0.9% margin already seen |

The second-order effect that inverts the ranking: **the deeper the integration, the more of the 2027 chick surplus the company eats itself.** MAIN's independent-farmer customers absorb the price, and MAIN loses volume; CPIN's and JPFA's own farms absorb the price, and they lose margin on two-thirds of their chick output. The regulation then requires the leaders to sell more chicks to the very farmers the quota expansion will be bankrupting. What the filings do not say: which company's GPS allocation is which — the 800,000 birds are split across 27 breeders and no company discloses its share.

## 07. MBG — who benefits, and how

### What it is, and where it stands

Makan Bergizi Gratis is the state free-meals programme: launched 2025, ~Rp900bn a day by late 2025, >20,000 kitchens, 63.13m active recipients and Rp101.1tn disbursed in 1H2026 (target had been 82.9m before duplicate-ID cleanup). It is the demand event of the period for exactly the products the three sell — chicken, eggs, processed portions — and the government linked the 2026 GPS quota expansion to it explicitly. Status at 25 August 2026: the 2026 budget was reported cut to Rp268tn (7 Aug; BGN disputes the framing), recipients trimmed to ~63m with 57.1m projected for 2027, and Bapanas used MBG kitchens as the offtake channel to stop the June 2026 price crash (proposed chicken/egg 3–5 times a week). No filing from any of the three quantifies MBG volumes; CPIN's annual reports do not mention it at all.

### The benefit map

| | CPIN | JPFA | MAIN |
|---|---|---|---|
| Direct channel to kitchens | 30 RPA + 2,482 stores + processed brands — the natural supplier of portions | 15 RPA + So Good/JFI plants + cold storage — the same, at 60% of the scale | 1 RPHU, 9,000 t/yr plant — negligible |
| Indirect (price) | broiler 48% of sales; +Rp1,000/kg on own-farm volume is the largest single sensitivity | commercial farm 40% of sales; ±Rp1,000/kg ≈ ±Rp1.0tn [teardown calc] | broiler 22% of sales; ±Rp140bn |
| Feed to independents restocking | 40% external feed | 42% | 73% — the most MBG-leveraged feed book |
| What the 1H2026 numbers show | revenue +19.2%, GP +47% | revenue +28.7% (vs +8.8% FY2025) | Q1 revenue +17%, profit credited to MBG; Q2 loss |
| What a budget cut does | removes a floor under processing and live bird | same, plus the re-levered balance sheet has less room | independent farmers stop restocking → feed volume, not just price |

Who is a direct beneficiary: the two leaders, through slaughter and processing capacity that independent farmers do not have. Who benefits indirectly: all three, through the live-bird price, in proportion to bird sales. Who is most exposed to the cut: MAIN, because its customers are the marginal restockers, and JPFA, because its 1H2026 growth is the most MBG-shaped and its balance sheet has just been re-levered against it. The counter-signal: the June 2026 crash happened *with* MBG running — a 10% chick surplus overwhelms a demand programme that feeds 63m people a portion a day.

## 08. Synthesis — three kinds of company

### CPIN — the scale leader, priced as the scale leader, with a fee on top

The largest feed base (Rp21tn external), the deepest downstream (30 RPA, 2,482 stores, 15.5% processing), the cleanest balance sheet (0.05x), 93% cash conversion, a dividend every year but one, and the best trough margin in the group (3.8% net, FY2023). It is also the company that has never explained a Rp715bn annual royalty to its parent, paid a Rp1.64tn dividend with no declared source, lets its store and farmer counts fall without comment, and is already re-externalising feed and DOC ahead of a rule it never mentions. At 8.9x and 1.47x book it is priced as the sector's quality name; the question the filings leave is whether 1.0% of sales off the top, every year, is the price of the brand or of the family.

### JPFA — the integrated challenger, cheaper for reasons the filings hand you

The same model at ~70% of CPIN's feed scale (segment basis) and 95% of its processing scale, with the best DOC franchise (15–17% margin), a real aquaculture business, vaccines, and the higher operating leverage that made FY2023–25 a 2.8x swing in operating profit — the most of the three. Priced at 6.8x, one book-multiple with CPIN, for three visible reasons: 15% of cost of goods routed through the parent's private trader on an undefined margin; a USD bond that matured in March 2026 with the 1H2026 debt up Rp4.4tn and no explanation; and a third-generation board resident in Singapore that took the parent private last year and now sits on both sides of every related-party line. It has the most to lose from a MBG cut and a 2027 chick surplus, and the least disclosure about either.

### MAIN — the price-taker at a trough multiple

A feed mill with farms attached, at 8% of national feed and 6% of chicks, selling 73% of feed and 78% of chicks to the independent farmers the cycle hits first: net margin −0.6% to 3.9% through FY2020–25, a downstream that has lost money in every disclosed year (FY2020–25), Rp518bn of bank money outside the reported gearing, 2024 key-management pay worth 38% of net profit, and a 300,000 t feed mill (+23%) completing into a Q2 in which its feed margin was 0.9%. It is the only one of the three that Permentan 10/2024 helps. At 0.52x book and 3.8x a non-trough year, the market has priced the model, not the moment.

### The three questions that separate them

1. **What share of the chain does the company own, and will the regulator let it keep it?** Internal feed 60/58/27%, internal DOC 64/61/22%. The leaders' margin premium is this ratio; the 2027 rule attacks it directly.
2. **How much of the profit leaves through the parent before minorities see it?** Royalty 1.0% of sales (CPIN); 15% of COGS through Annona (JPFA); 15.5% of COGS through SCI/LHJ plus 39% of NP in board pay (MAIN). None is benchmarked.
3. **What does the balance sheet look like at the next trough?** CPIN unlevered; MAIN 0.34x and rising into new capacity; JPFA 0.64x with a refinancing the filings have not described. The last trough (2023) arrived with JPFA at 0.80x and interest cover 2.2x.
