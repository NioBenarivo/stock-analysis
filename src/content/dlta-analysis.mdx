## THE ONE THING TO UNDERSTAND FIRST

**DLTA is a local mass-market beer brand, Anker, sitting on a cash pile. San Miguel harvests it for dividends rather than growth. The "San Miguel" in the story is mostly the owner, not the product.**

Four facts carry the whole report:

1. **The product is Anker, not San Miguel.** Royalties are paid at 6% of Carlsberg sales and 3% of San Miguel-brand sales. Back-solving from them, Carlsberg is about **10% of net sales** and San Miguel brands about **3.5%**. That leaves **~87% as the company's own Anker/Kuda Putih family** [calculated, §2.1]. A lapse in the San Miguel trademark licence (expired 15 Mar 2026, renewal "in process") matters far less than it sounds.
   - `REVISED v2:` v1 called the licence expiry a major risk. It is a minor one.

2. **Most of the balance sheet is cash, and the cash is shrinking by design.**
   - Cash was Rp646bn at FY2025 and Rp737bn at 30 Jun 2026, with zero borrowings.
   - FY2022–25 dividends were **Rp861bn against free cash flow of Rp691bn** [calculated]. The Rp171bn gap is almost exactly the fall in cash since 2021 (Rp813bn → Rp646bn).
   - The payout is now ~97% of EPS, so the dividend moves one-for-one with profit.

3. **Volume is the swing factor, and it has been falling since 2019.**
   - Net sales were Rp827bn in FY2019 and Rp675bn in FY2025.
   - Production fell 17% in FY2024, from 362k hl to 301k hl, when excise rose 10% and cheap entrants discounted.
   - Each 1% of volume moves operating profit by ~3% [calculated, §2.6].

4. **2026 shows a recovery in sales being spent on marketing.**
   - H1 2026 net sales rose **13%**, and Q2 alone rose 18%.
   - Selling expense rose **27%**, so operating profit fell 17% and net profit fell 3.6%.
   - Peer Multi Bintang grew sales by the same 13% in Q1 2026 with profit up 18%. DLTA is buying back share; MLBI is not.

**The resulting profile:** a ~10% dividend yield and 42% of the market cap in net cash, at about 6.4x core earnings excluding cash. On the other side sit a structurally declining volume base, one distributor (PT Jangkar Sejati) taking 22–28% of sales with its ownership never disclosed, and a thin float. Evidence is in §1.3–1.5, §2.1, §2.5 and §3.2–3.7.

**What a sale would mean:**
- It needs a regional regulation (Perda) and DPRD approval.
- The natural buyer is San Miguel, which would take it to ~84.6%. The alternative is a block placement into a market where DLTA trades only ~Rp100–150m a day.
- San Miguel already controls DLTA, so buying DKI out would not normally trigger a mandatory tender offer to minorities. It would, however, leave a ~15% float that could invite a voluntary tender or delisting later. A placement to the market would be an overhang on the price.
- Either way it is a **shareholder-structure event, not an operating one**. AR2023 does not mention the divestment at all.

# 02 — MODEL

## 2.1 Product

**The business in one paragraph.**
- DLTA brews lager and ales at ≤5% ABV. The largest format is returnable 620ml glass; it also sells 330ml bottles and cans.
- It sells ex-brewery, net of excise, to ~55 independent regional distributors. The distributors carry it into traditional trade (warungs, liquor shops, cafés), modern on-premise (hotels, bars, beach clubs) and modern off-premise (supermarkets; minimarkets have been banned since 2015).
- The consumer pays roughly **Rp2.2m/hl to Delta, Rp1.65m/hl to the state as excise, and 11–12% VAT on top.** Excise is ~43% of the ex-VAT price [calculated, §3.5].
- Delta owns the Anker, Kuda Putih and Batavia brands outright and licenses Carlsberg (6% royalty) and San Miguel (3%).

```
malt + adjunct ─► milling ─► mashing ─► lautering ─► wort boiling (+hops) ─► cooling
      ─► fermentation (yeast) ─► maturation/lagering ─► filtration ─► packaging
         (returnable 620ml/330ml glass: washed & sterilised; cans; one-way)
      ─► warehouse ─► excise stamp & release ─► distributors
 waste: WWTP (IPAL) sludge + dry leaves ─► compost; broken glass ─► cullet sold to glass makers
```

**PT Jangkar Sejati is the single most important and least explained counterparty in the company:**
- It takes **a fifth to a quarter of all sales**, every year. In the FY2024 volume collapse its share *rose* to 28% even as the rupiah amount barely moved.
- It is disclosed only as a "third party". It is **never listed in the related-party note** (Note 28) in AR2023, AR2024 or AR2025.
- "Jangkar" is Indonesian for *anchor*, which is Anker. The name shares that root with the company's own subsidiary **PT Jangkar Delta Indonesia** and with a payables counterparty, **PT Jangkar Pusat Perkasa**. A third, **PT Delta Niaga Sentosa**, also appears in other payables.
- It does not appear among the large receivable groups (LSH and WEP are the named debtor groups). That implies it pays quickly, on cash or deposit terms.
- Web searches found no corporate footprint: no website and no news. One LinkedIn profile names a "General Manager, PT Jangkar Sejati", and that page could not be read.

**What this could be:**
- A legacy Anker master distributor from the BUMD era (the naming fits).
- A vehicle linked to the old subsidiary's management after its 2019 wind-down. The timing also fits.

**What it means:**
- If it is unrelated, it is a **customer-concentration risk**: losing it would remove ~Rp150–180bn of sales overnight, though its outlets would likely be re-served by other distributors.
- If it is related to an insider, the related-party disclosure is deficient.

## 2.7 Demand

**Market size.** These are third-party estimates (Ken Research 2025); treat them as order-of-magnitude:

**DLTA's share.** 305k hl against ~2.2m hl is **~14%** [calculated on third-party data]. Multi Bintang (Heineken) is dominant. Its FY2025 sales of Rp3.54tn are 5.2x DLTA's, which puts MLBI at perhaps 60–70% of volume. That figure is inferred, not disclosed. Bali Hai, Lovina/Prost, Beverindo and a tail of cheap local brands make up the rest.

**Demand drivers:**

1. **Tourism.** Bali foreign arrivals were 5.8m in Jan–Oct 2025, 1.4m in Q1 2026 and 3.2m in H1 2026. New hubs include Labuan Bajo, Mandalika and Bintan. On-premise premium beer follows arrivals.
2. **Middle and lower-middle-class spending power.** It is weak in 2024–25, which is when beer becomes discretionary (AR25 p18). This hits traditional trade, Delta's core channel.
3. **Substitution.** Spirits and illicit alcohol offer more alcohol per rupiah, especially after excise hikes.
4. **Regulation.** There are regional bans (perda), the 2015 minimarket ban, and licensing under Permendag 20/2014. PP 28/2024 (the Health Law implementing rules) targets "addictive substances". The draft national alcohol-ban bill (RUU Larangan Minol) has shown no activity since 2021.

**2026 read.** Industry demand is recovering: MLBI's Q1 2026 sales rose 13%, and DLTA's H1 2026 sales rose 13%.

## 2.8 Supply

- **What limits supply is licensing, not capacity.** New breweries need permits, and excise registration and the distribution licensing regime keep entry slow. Both incumbents have spare capacity.
- **The competitive threat is cheap entrants and imported or contract-brewed value brands** discounting in traditional trade. They discounted aggressively in 2024; AR25 says they "gradually scaled back" in 2025 as the promotions proved unsustainable. Delta's stance is "reasonably aggressive": it did not match every discount.
- **Excise is the supply-side price floor.** Class A (≤5% ABV) is Rp16,500/L; there was no change in 2025 or 2026 (web check; PMK 160/2023 still in force). Total alcohol-beverage excise revenue was Rp8.86tn in 2024 and Rp8.92tn in 2025, so the state earns ~Rp9tn a year from the category. DLTA's ~Rp500bn of excise is ~5.6% of it [calc].

**What the cash is worth.** The pro forma cash is Rp592bn, or Rp739 per share. A one-off special dividend of even half of that would be **Rp370 per share, about 21% of the price**. There is no sign of one:
- San Miguel has preferred the steady over-100% payout, which drew the cash down slowly.
- DKI politics (a dividend to a province that says it wants out) cut both ways.

**The DKI exit.** A sale of the 26.25% stake (~Rp374bn at market) is binary for the share price:
- If San Miguel buys it at a premium, a later tender or delisting becomes plausible. That is upside.
- If it is dumped into the market in a single ~210m-share block, against a normal daily volume of ~50–100k shares, it is a multi-month overhang.

# SUMMARY VERDICT

**What it is.** A 94-year-old local beer brand, Anker, which is ~87% of sales. It is brewed in one old, fully depreciated brewery and sold through ~55 distributors, one of which takes a quarter of the volume. San Miguel runs it for cash: a Filipino management team, a light royalty toll (1.1% of sales) and a ~100% payout. It has no debt, negative working capital, and 42% of its market cap in cash.

**What it earns through the cycle.**

| Period | Net profit |
|---|---|
| 2019 (peak) | Rp318bn |
| 2020 (COVID) | Rp124bn |
| 2022 | Rp230bn |
| 2024 (trough after the excise hike) | Rp136bn |
| 2025 | Rp150bn |
| 2026, likely | ~Rp135–155bn |

The mid-cycle level now is ~Rp150bn, down from ~Rp200–230bn, because volume stepped down ~17% in 2024 and has not come back. Excise, not beer demand, is the structural ceiling. So is the Heineken-owned competitor's scale: 5x the sales and a 33% net margin.

**What has to be true for it to work as an investment:**

1. Volume stops falling. FY2025 at +1% and H1 2026 sales at +13% say it has.
2. The 2026 promotion surge is an investment phase, not a permanently higher cost base.
3. Excise stays at Rp16,500/L in 2027, or rises only modestly.
4. PT Jangkar Sejati remains a stable, independent distributor.

If all four hold, the shareholder collects **~10% a year in cash**, with **~Rp740 per share (42% of the price) sitting in the bank**. The operating business is priced at ~6x earnings. That is a very cheap price for a monopoly-like, cash-generative franchise, even a slowly shrinking one.

**What would break it:**
- Another double-digit excise increase. The FY2024 playbook of volume −17% and operating profit −29% would repeat.
- A promotion war that turns the H1 2026 pattern (sales up, profit down) into the norm.
- Loss or disruption of the Jangkar Sejati channel.
- Regulatory tightening: regional bans, or distribution-licence changes under the Health Law.
- A disorderly DKI block sale.

**Position.**
- **Hold or accumulate as an income position, not a growth position.**
- At Rp1,700–1,800, the cash plus a ~10% yield give real downside support: even the bear case pays ~9%.
- Liquidity of about Rp100–150m a day limits position size severely.
- The upside triggers are a special dividend from the cash pile or a San Miguel buy-out of DKI.
- Before sizing up, get the Q3 2026 numbers on selling expense and an answer on who owns PT Jangkar Sejati.

*This is an analytical teardown, not investment advice.*
