**What it does:** PBSA is a Jakarta-based industrial construction contractor. It does civil, structural, mechanical-electrical, tank, piping, jetty and land-preparation work, almost entirely for **palm-oil refineries, oleochemical plants, bulking stations and storage terminals** in Sumatra, Kalimantan and Java. The customers are, year after year, a handful of entities that (to the best of my knowledge, as the filings never say so) belong to the **Sinar Mas agribusiness group**: PT SMART Tbk, Soci Mas, Ivomas Tunggal, Binasawit Abadipratama, Sumber Indah Perkasa, Energi Sejahtera Mas and Sinarmas Bio Energy. From 2025 the list widens to Sinar Mas's pulp arm (Indah Kiat, OKI). It also has a small materials-trading subsidiary (PT Paramita Multi Sarana), owns its office building (Plaza Paramita, Central Jakarta), and runs a **Rp200–330bn portfolio of mutual funds and managed accounts**.

**Market:** Rp787/share (6-Oct-2026, aggregator range Rp785–790) × 2,984,886,300 shares outstanding excluding treasury = **Rp2,349bn market cap** [calculated]. Trailing P/E ~9.4x (TTM), 7.4x on FY2025 reported EPS, **11.6x on FY2025 earnings excluding investment gains** [calculated, §3.6]. FY2025 dividend Rp60 = 7.6% yield. **A 1:2 stock split takes effect 14-Oct-2026**: shares go from 3.0bn to 6.0bn and the price roughly halves to ~Rp395. Every per-share figure in this report is on the **pre-split** basis.

**Period covered:** FY2011–FY2015 (prospectus), FY2022–FY2025 (three annual reports, each with a comparative year), 9M2025 (incidental public expose, Jan-2026), 1Q2026 (interim FS via web). **FY2016–FY2021 is a gap**: no filing for those years was in hand, and only FY2021 revenue/profit and some dividend totals are recoverable from the decks.

**Sources in hand:** IPO prospectus (2016, 192pp), AR2023 (260pp), AR2024 (303pp), AR2025 (261pp), four public expose decks (Jun-2024, Jun-2025, Jan-2026 incidental, Jun-2026). Web research for market data, 1Q26, industry and peers. Source table at the end.

**Conventions:** Rp bn unless stated. "Core" profit = reported profit minus fair-value and realised gains on securities and dividend income, plus back the 22% tax paid on the one-off 2023 EcoOils gain. `[calculated]` marks every derived figure. "To my knowledge" marks outside knowledge the filings do not state.

## THE ONE THING TO UNDERSTAND FIRST

**PBSA is two things glued together: a captive contractor whose revenue is one conglomerate's capex budget, and a securities book managed in insider-linked funds whose marks now swing the bottom line more than the construction business does.**

**1. The revenue is Sinar Mas capex.** Customers above 10% of revenue have taken 74–97% of revenue in every year where the split is disclosed:

| | 2013 | 2014 | 2015 | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Customers >10% of revenue, % of revenue | 91.8% | 97.4% | 86.6% | 85.9% | 74.3% | 84.8% | 5 names, % not given |
| of which Sinar Mas-group names (to my knowledge) | 91.8% | 97.4% | 86.6% | 72.3% | 42.6% | 84.8% | 5 of 5 |
| Palm oil share of trade receivables | — | — | — | 96% | 70% | 99.5% | 92.2% |

The one non-Sinar-Mas year (2023, Unilever Oleochemical at 31.6%) is also the year revenue fell 22%. When Sinar Mas spends, PBSA doubles (FY2024 +101%, FY2025 +38%). When it doesn't, PBSA shrinks (2014 −22%, 2023 −22%, and a deeper trough in 2019–2021, when revenue fell to Rp279bn). The filings label every one of these customers a "third party". The IPO underwriter (Sinarmas Sekuritas, 98% of the book), the IPO-era lender (Bank Sinarmas) and the share registrar (Sinartama Gunita, in Sinar Mas Land Plaza) sit in the same orbit. No ownership link between PBSA's owners and Sinar Mas is disclosed, and none was found.

**2. The profit increasingly comes from a fund book.** PBSA's surplus cash is not in deposits. It sits in a discretionary managed account ("KPD Nusadana"), three funds (two Nusadana-branded, one "Juara Capital") and a few listed shares. **The President Commissioner and the Finance Director both have careers at Nusadana entities**, and an "independent" audit committee member has been a VP at "Ascend Capital" since 2014 (the controlling shareholder is PT Ascend Bangun Persada). None of this is disclosed as related-party.

| Rp bn | FY2022 | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | 1Q2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Securities book at period end | 17.6 | 101.8 | 88.9 | **327.8** | 207.9 |
| Fair-value + realised gains/(losses) on securities | (2.5) | (49.6)¹ | 46.4 | **117.2** | **(69.9)** |
| Reported profit | 134.0 | 192.7 | 215.0 | 320.1 | 1.5 |
| Securities result as % of reported profit [calculated] | n.m. | n.m. | 21.6% | **36.6%** | n.m. (−4,700%) |
| Core profit (ex-investment) [calculated] | 136.5 | 102.0 | 168.3 | 202.9 | ~71 |

¹ 2023 excludes the Rp176bn gain on selling the 10% EcoOils stake, which is shown separately (§1.1). The Rp(49.6)bn = Rp(38.3)bn fair-value loss plus ~Rp(11.3)bn realised trading loss that the FS nets inside the EcoOils gain line [calculated, §1.5].

So the headline story — profit up 66% from FY2023 to FY2025 — is really core profit roughly doubling from a depressed FY2023 base (good), plus a fund mark of Rp117bn in FY2025 that **reversed by Rp70bn in the very next quarter** (bad). Mutual-fund gains are, to my understanding, outside the Indonesian income-tax base, while construction pays 2.65% final tax on gross revenue and deposits pay 20% final tax on interest, so the fund book is tax-efficient. That is the charitable explanation for holding it. The uncharitable reading is that a contractor with a 7–16% free float lets insiders' former firms run a quarter of its balance sheet with no disclosed mandate, holdings or fee.

**What this means for the reader:** value PBSA on **core profit (~Rp200bn in FY2025)**, treat the securities book as cash with an equity-market beta and a governance discount, and treat the order book as a call on Sinar Mas's downstream capex (biodiesel B40→B50, refinery/fractionation expansion, now pulp). Everything else in the report is evidence for these two points: customer concentration in §2.5, the fund book and its links in §1.4 and §3.1, and the earnings split in §3.2.

# 02 — MODEL

## 2.1 Product

PBSA sells **construction capacity for agro-industrial plants on a percentage-of-completion basis**. It does not design processes or supply proprietary equipment. The owner (usually a Sinar Mas refinery or bulking company) fixes the scope; PBSA builds the civil works, steel structures, tanks, pipe racks, piping, jetties and the electrical/mechanical installation around the owner's process equipment. Revenue is recognised on physical progress (output method), billed in progress certificates, with a retention held back until handover.

```
 Owner's capex decision (refinery, fractionation, biodiesel, bulking terminal, oleochemical, pulp mill)
          │
          ▼
 ┌──────────────── PBSA SCOPE ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
 │ Land grading / cut & fill ─► Piling & foundations ─► Civil & structure (concrete, steel)    │
 │ ─► Storage tanks (300–5,000 MT; substructure + upperstructure) ─► Pipe racks & piping       │
 │ ─► Jetty / trestle ─► Mechanical & electrical (ISBL/OSBL, E&I) ─► Buildings / warehouses     │
 └──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

## 2.7 Demand

PBSA's demand is not Indonesian construction GDP; it is **downstream palm-oil (and now pulp) capex by Sinar Mas**. The drivers:

**Biodiesel mandates.** Indonesia moved from B35 to B40 in 2025 and **formally started B50 on 1-Jul-2026** (GAPKI, Jul-2026; F&L Asia), after months of doubt (S&P Global, Dec-2025, reported the 2026 biodiesel quota was kept flat, raising questions over B50; Sep-2025 reports had floated B45 as a fallback). Each step requires more FAME (biodiesel) capacity, more refining/fractionation of CPO, and more storage and bulking — exactly the tanks, pipe racks, refineries and jetties PBSA builds. The FY2024–25 tank/bulking wave at Lubuk Gaung, Lampung, Bagendang, Tarjun and Marunda fits this cycle. **The risk is the other side of the same coin:** once capacity for B50 is in place, the next leg needs B60 or export demand; without it, Sinar Mas's tank and refinery programme slows and PBSA's revenue falls as it did in 2019–21 and 2023.

**Downstreaming (hilirisasi) and oleochemicals.** Sinar Mas's oleochemical and biodiesel complexes (Energi Sejahtera Mas, Dumai/Lubuk Gaung) were PBSA's 2015–16 growth engine. EcoOils (now Shell-controlled) is building "Mentari" at Sei Mangkei.

**Pulp & paper.** PE2026 presents OKI (Rp450bn) and Indah Kiat Karawang (Rp377bn) as "new industry expansion". These are, to my knowledge, Asia Pulp & Paper (Sinar Mas) mills. This is a genuine widening of the end-market but not of the customer group.

**General construction backdrop (web):** Indonesian GDP +5.11% in 2025, +5.29% YoY in 2Q26 (BPS). Construction ≈9.8% of GDP; construction growth ~3.8–3.9% in 2025 (quarters 2.2% / 5.0% / 4.2%). Cement consumption +9.5% YoY Jan–Jul 2026 from a weak 2025. These are only loosely relevant — PBSA has no government, residential or office exposure.

## 2.8 Supply

- **Competitors:** none are named in any filing, including the prospectus. In industrial agro-processing construction PBSA competes with general contractors (Total Bangun Persada, Nusa Raya Cipta, the state-owned Karya subsidiaries), specialist tank/EPC firms, and Chinese EPC contractors (PBSA itself subcontracted 66% of the 2015 oleochemical plant to a CNCEC unit).
- **State-owned contractors are distressed:** Danantara is merging PTPP with ADHI and WIKA with Waskita; WIKA lost Rp9.75tn in 2025; WEGE and PPRE are loss-making (web, Oct-2026). That reduces competition for private industrial work at the margin.
- **The real "supply" constraint is the relationship.** PBSA's moat is 17 years of repeat work inside one group's sites, not technology or scale. That moat holds as long as the group keeps choosing PBSA; it would not survive a change of procurement policy at Sinar Mas, and PBSA has never shown it can win comparable volume elsewhere (2023, when Unilever filled the gap, revenue fell 22%).
- **Licences:** final tax at 2.65% implies a non-small SBUJK qualification since May-2022 (the prospectus showed K1/K2 small grades while executing a US$59m contract). ISO 9001:2015 and ISO 37001 from 2025; SMK3 audit score 92.17% (2024). Safety: zero fatalities and zero LTIs 2023–25.

**Return on a rupiah of capital:** ~Rp13.5 of net profit per Rp100 of revenue, on roughly Rp30–35 of operating capital per Rp100 of revenue → **~40% after-tax return on operating capital** [calculated]. That is excellent, and it is the reward for being an asset-light, prepaid, captive supplier. The flip side is that the economics are entirely set by the customer: there is no plant, licence or technology that would keep that return if the customer took the work elsewhere.

**Revenue per unit of capacity:** Rp2.24bn per employee; Rp59 of construction revenue per Rp1 of machinery book value. Neither is a constraint — capacity is rented in.

**Mid-cycle check:** across FY2011–15 and FY2021–25 (the years in hand), average revenue was ~Rp800bn and the core margin averaged ~20% in the 2010s but ~15% in FY2022–25. A mid-cycle year today might look like **Rp800–1,000bn revenue × 15–16% core margin ≈ Rp120–160bn core profit** [calculated, judgement]. That — not FY2025's Rp203bn — is the earnings level a through-cycle valuation should use.

## 3.8 Roadmap and what to watch

1. **Order intake.** Ask for (and watch for) a backlog figure. Without it, track contract liabilities (customer advances: Rp183.8bn at FY25) and the Note "contracts in progress" list — and whether AR2026 restores contract values.
2. **1H/9M2026 results:** construction gross margin (23% in 1Q26 vs 19.7% FY25) and whether revenue tracks the Rp1.7tn guidance.
3. **The fund book:** quarter-end value, fair-value line, and any disclosure of manager, holdings and mandate. A shift into deposits/government bonds would remove the noise; further switching between equity sleeves would confirm it is an active trading book.
4. **Customer mix:** the share of OKI/Indah Kiat and EcoOils in revenue. Revenue growth *without* SMART-group names is the single best evidence that the business is broadening.
5. **B50 implementation** (from 1-Jul-2026) and any B60/export policy; Sinar Mas refinery, fractionation and biodiesel capex announcements.
6. **Cash conversion:** 1Q26 CFO was −Rp106bn. FY2026 dividends (~90% of core profit) need CFO to recover in 2H26; otherwise they come from selling the fund book.
7. **Ownership:** further Sigma/ABP selling, any new nominee holder above 5%, and the "Sinar Mas acquisition" rumour. A takeover by the customer group would crystallise value; a further insider sell-down would signal the opposite.
8. **Governance fixes to ask for:** a third commissioner, an audit committee without Ascend Capital staff, an investment policy, per-person pay, IPO use-of-proceeds breakdown, and audited FS without "FINAL DRAFT" stamps.
9. **Mechanics:** the 1:2 split on 14-Oct-2026 doubles the share count to 5.97bn ex-treasury; all per-share figures here halve.

# SUMMARY VERDICT

**What it is.** PBSA is an asset-light, debt-free, very high-return contractor that builds the tanks, refineries, jetties and plant buildings of one conglomerate's agro-industrial expansion. For 17 years that one customer group has supplied ~75–97% of revenue. Bolted onto it is a securities book — now Rp200–330bn, up to a quarter of the balance sheet — run in funds and a discretionary account carrying the Nusadana name, which the President Commissioner and Finance Director both worked for, with no related-party disclosure and no disclosed mandate.

**What it earns through the cycle.** Core (ex-fund) profit was Rp102bn in FY2023, Rp168bn in FY2024 and **Rp203bn in FY2025**, on a margin that has fallen from 35% (2013) to 13%. A mid-cycle year is more like **Rp120–160bn**. At Rp787 the stock trades on **11.6x peak core earnings and ~15–20x mid-cycle**, at ~3x book, with ~Rp31/share of net cash left after the July dividend. The 7.6% yield is real but depends on peak volumes: it was 3–4% equivalent at FY2022–23 activity.

**What has to be true for it to work.**
- Sinar Mas keeps spending at the FY2024–25 pace (B50 build-out, refinery/fractionation expansion, pulp) — or PBSA shows it can replace that spend with genuinely new customers.
- Construction margin stabilises near 19–23% instead of continuing to slide.
- The fund book stops being a source of losses, and the cash keeps coming back to minorities as dividends.

**What would break it.**
1. **A pause in the customer's capex.** This has happened at least three times (2014, 2016–21, 2023). Revenue fell 22–70% each time, and PBSA has no other pillar.
2. **The fund book.** A large drawdown in an undisclosed, insider-linked, equity-like portfolio — 1Q26 already showed −Rp70bn in a quarter.
3. **A governance event:** related-party leakage through the funds, or controllers continuing to sell into rallies while the company buys back (as in 2025–26).

**Position.** On the filings, **PBSA is a good business priced for its peak**. The construction engine is real — it earns ~40% on the capital it uses, it is debt-free, and the 1Q26 operating numbers are strong. But at Rp787 the price already assumes FY2025-level Sinar Mas spending continues, gives no discount for a single-customer franchise with no published order book, and ignores a securities book whose governance would justify a discount on its own. The asymmetry is unfavourable: scenario A (guidance met) adds ~10%; a return to FY2022–23 activity takes ~45–50% off. **I would want to own it only nearer mid-cycle value (roughly Rp450–550 pre-split, Rp225–275 post-split) or with hard evidence of diversification** — a published backlog and a year of growth outside the SMART group. For an income holder already in, the dividend is well covered by FY2026 guidance; the risk is the year the capex cycle turns, which the filings give no early warning of.

*This is an analytical view of the business from its filings, not investment advice. I'm not a financial advisor.*
