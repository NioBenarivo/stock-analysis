**What it does:** develops and sells land plots, houses, shophouses and commercial land inside PIK2, a township on the north coast of Tangerang Regency, Banten, immediately west of Jakarta. Everything it owns sits in one place.

**Who owns it:** the Kusuma family (Agung Sedayu Group, founder Sugianto Kusuma, known as "Aguan") and the Salim group, together, through a 50/50 holding company.

**Scale:** market capitalisation Rp109.6 triliun (4 Sep 2026) — the 20th largest listed company in Indonesia, and the largest listed property developer by market value. (IDNFinancials ranks it second of 92 in its broader real-estate category, which includes names outside the developer peer set.) FY2025 revenue Rp4.32 triliun, net profit to shareholders Rp1.15 triliun. Land bank about 1,838 hectares carried at Rp37.5 triliun.

**Period covered:** FY2020 through FY2025, with H1 2026 and market data to early September 2026.

**Sources in hand:** annual reports FY2022, FY2023, FY2024 and FY2025; the PMHMETD III rights-issue prospectus (December 2025); public expose decks for 2024, 2025 and 2026; plus web research on market data, peers, the PSN designation and the sea-fence affair. Full source table at the end.

## THE ONE THING TO UNDERSTAND FIRST

PANI is not a company that grew. It is a company that was assembled, and it was assembled using money raised from the public market.

In October 2021 a holding company owned equally by the Kusuma family and the Salim group bought 80% of a small listed can-and-frozen-fish business called PT Pratama Abadi Nusa Industri, for Rp54 miliar. That shell had total assets of about Rp98 miliar.

Over the next four years it issued new shares six times and collected roughly **Rp39.6 triliun** from investors. Every one of those raises was spent buying, or paying for, assets that already belonged to the same two families. The land, the project companies, and finally the biggest subsidiary itself were transferred from the family side of the fence to the listed side, and the listed company's shareholders paid for the transfer.

| Where the money came from | Rp triliun |
|---|---:|
| Six share issues, 2022–2025 | 39.6 |

| Where it went — to parties controlled by the same families | Rp triliun |
|---|---:|
| 2022: repaid bonds and loans the family had lent the project companies, net | 7.2 |
| 2023: repaid the remaining family loans | 8.6 |
| 2024–25: net further repayments to family entities | 1.1 |
| 2025: bought a 41% slice of the biggest subsidiary directly from the two family holding companies | 15.1 |
| **Total that reached the family side** | **~32.0** |

The rest went into land bought from a small group of companies that are also under the same control — those affiliated vendors supplied 68% of all the land the group bought in 2023 and 55% in 2024.

None of this is hidden. It is disclosed, appraised, given fairness opinions, and approved. The point is not concealment; it is that this is *what the business does*. A conventional developer buys land from strangers, builds on it, and sells to the public. PANI buys land and companies from its own controlling shareholders using money from the public, and then builds and sells. The second half is a real, high-margin, well-run operation. The first half is the mechanism that made it possible, and it is where the money has actually moved.

Two consequences run through everything below.

**The reported profit is real but small relative to the price.** FY2025 revenue was Rp4.3 triliun against a market value of Rp110 triliun — about twenty-five times sales, where the two largest conventional Indonesian developers trade at roughly one times sales. On 2025 pre-sales PANI ranks fourth among listed Indonesian developers; on market value it ranks first, by a factor of eight. That gap is a judgement about what the 1,838-hectare land bank is worth, and it is the whole investment case in either direction.

**The company has almost no debt because it does not need any.** Buyers pay in instalments before their unit is built. At the end of 2025 customers had handed over **Rp17.0 triliun** for units not yet delivered — roughly four years of revenue, collected in advance and carried as a liability. Bank borrowings were Rp550 miliar, about 1% of assets. The land bank is funded by shareholders and customers, not lenders. That makes the company unusually safe in a downturn and unusually dependent on buyers continuing to pre-pay.

Three things about this picture matter.

**The two groups are equal partners, not majority and minority.** PT Multi Artha Pratama is owned half by PT Agung Sedayu (the Kusuma family) and half by PT Tunas Mekar Jaya. Tunas Mekar Jaya is the Salim connection: it is 99.9995% owned by an individual named Budiono, and press reporting identifies Hindarto Budiono as indirectly controlling half of MAP and as co-shareholder with Anthoni Salim's son Axton in another vehicle. PIK2 is an Aguan–Salim joint venture at the ownership level, even though Agung Sedayu's name is on the office tower.

**The Salim side is invisible in the filings.** No Salim-named individual or company appears anywhere in the ownership chart. The company's narrative repeatedly credits "Agung Sedayu Group and Salim Group" as sponsors but never maps that onto a shareholding. A reader working only from the filings could not establish it.

**The company names three ultimate owners in one document and four in another.** The FY2025 annual report names Susanto Kusumo, Richard Halim Kusuma and Alexander Halim Kusuma. The rights-issue prospectus, filed weeks earlier, names those three plus Hindarto Budiono — and states explicitly that they are *not* an "organised group" acting in concert, a classification that would trigger extra disclosure. That sits awkwardly beside four people jointly controlling the company through two vehicles that split a third 50/50.

**The float is shrinking on the measure that counts.** MAP held 83.75% at the end of August 2026 and the public 16.25%. But the exchange's own free-float measure, which strips out holdings it judges connected, was reported at **8.24%** in May 2026, down from 15.91% at the end of 2025. The company attributes the fall to a reclassification by the exchange, regulator and depository; that has not been independently confirmed. The consequence is concrete: PANI is in no major index — not LQ45, IDX30, IDX80 or MSCI — despite being the twentieth largest listed company in the country, and the exchange added a "high shareholding concentration" screen in May 2026 that works against it specifically.

Two structural features matter.

**Every operating company has a family stake left in it.** PT Agung Sedayu and PT Tunas Mekar Jaya hold minority positions directly in almost every subsidiary. ## 1.3 History — recap

The pattern is one move repeated six times: raise money from the market, use it to move an asset from the family's private companies into the listed company, repeat.

What changed between repetitions is the price. In 2022 the market paid Rp500 a share. In 2023, Rp5,000. In 2024, Rp5,200. In 2025, Rp12,975. Each raise was priced against a share price that the previous raise had helped create, because each raise brought in another tranche of land and another step up in reported scale. The share count went from 410 juta to 18.1 miliar in three and a half years, and the price went up nearly thirty-fold at the same time. That combination — more shares and a higher price per share — is what turned a Rp98 miliar shell into a Rp330 triliun company at the peak.

The operating business grew too, and genuinely. Revenue went from Rp578 miliar in 2022 to Rp4.3 triliun in 2025, and gross margin from 50% to 60%. But the operating business grew because the assets were injected, not the other way round.

## 1.4 Owner's character

What the record shows, taken from what the owners actually did rather than what they say.

**They fund with equity, never debt.** Bank borrowings have never exceeded Rp770 miliar in a company holding Rp37.5 triliun of land. Where a normal Indonesian developer would gear up against the land bank, this group has gone back to the market six times instead. No lender can force it to do anything — a real strength in a sector where several Indonesian developers were broken by dollar bonds.

**They keep a slice of everything outside the listed company.** Minority stakes in nearly every subsidiary; Kukuh Mandiri Lestari, wholly outside PANI, owning the convention-centre land and holding permits. The 2026 deck added a new category to its own land map — "group development area, not consolidated" — acknowledging family land next to PIK2 that is not inside the listed company. None of that is improper. It does mean shareholders own a slice of the project, not the project.

**They take almost nothing out through pay or dividends.** Total remuneration for all commissioners and directors was Rp29.8 miliar in 2024, for a board including the founder and a Rp50 triliun balance sheet. Dividends began only in 2024, six years after listing: Rp2 a share for 2023, Rp4 for 2024, Rp5 for 2025, with the payout ratio falling each year from 11.4% to 7.4%. Cash has not left through the income statement. It has left through the balance sheet (§01.5, §09).

**Family in the seats that matter, professionals in the rest.** The president director is the founder, Sugianto Kusuma, 74. The president commissioner is his brother; two sons and a nephew sit on the boards. The three independent commissioners are credible career figures who hold the same posts across half the Salim-group listed companies. Finance is run by Agung Sedayu Group's own controller and its own internal audit head, both holding the same jobs at the parent. Below board level, one slate of six or seven names sits on every land-holding subsidiary.

**They sold down only when the exchange made them.** MAP went from 89.93% to 84.09% in late 2025 through three October tranches described as raising the float, plus the December rights issue. At CBDK, the family companies sold in the open market in February and March 2026 — after the exchange's 15% float rule bit, not before.

**They exit cleanly and cheaply.** The can business was closed in October 2022 in one sentence. The fish business, fifteen years old with Rp294 miliar of revenue in its last full year, was sold in 2023 to four individuals for about Rp28 juta in total. One buyer, Hendra Hasan Kustardjo, appears in the FY2022 accounts as a lender to that same subsidiary.

## 1.5 RPT — dealings with companies owned by the same families

These are deals between PANI and companies the same two families own privately. The two sides are not negotiating against each other; whatever price is agreed, the same people are on both ends of it. Five channels, different in kind.

Two years in three, most of the land this company bought came from its own controlling shareholders. No filing gives a pricing basis for these purchases — no independent valuation, no per-square-metre benchmark, no comparison to third-party land in the same villages. The FY2024 annual report says only that the transactions were at arm's length and were reviewed by the audit committee, and names no appraiser. The 2025 fall to 9% is real. Total land purchases that year were about Rp3.8 triliun `[calculated]` against roughly Rp9–12 triliun in 2024 — land buying fell by more than half and shifted almost entirely away from affiliated vendors, because the year's capital raise went into CBDK shares instead.

Alongside it sits a balance that has been on the books for years. PANI is owed **Rp1.57 triliun** by PT Cakra Karya Semesta — 3% of everything the company owns. PANI advanced the money so Cakra Karya Semesta could acquire land, and the debt will be settled by handing that land over "whenever Cakra Karya Semesta and the landowner reach agreement." No interest, no security, no repayment date. It has been outstanding since at least 2022 and stood at Rp2.66 triliun at the end of 2023.

### Channel 2 — the family as lender, then as seller

Before the listed vehicle existed, the family financed the PIK2 land companies itself. At the end of 2021 the group owed **Rp4.14 triliun** of bonds to seventeen family companies at 2.5% a year, plus **Rp3.05 triliun** of ordinary loans to family companies. That was 56% of everything the group owed.

The 2022 and 2023 rights issues paid all of it off.

### Channel 3 — buying the subsidiary from the family

In December 2025 PANI bought 2,344,961,240 shares of its own listed subsidiary CBDK from PT Agung Sedayu and PT Tunas Mekar Jaya for **Rp15.13 triliun**, at Rp6,450 a share. This is the single largest transaction in the company's history and it is dealt with in full in §09 and §10.

### Channel 4 — the family bank

PT Bank Artha Graha Internasional, where Sugianto Kusuma is deputy president commissioner, is simultaneously the group's largest bank lender, a holder of its deposits, and the recipient of most of its interest payments.

|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Group cash and deposits held at Artha Graha, Rp bn | 233 | 311 | 934 |

The amounts are small against a Rp50 triliun balance sheet. The pattern is not: for a group with almost no debt, a third of what little interest it pays goes to a bank the chairman helps run, and the deposit balance there jumped seven-fold in 2025.

### Channel 5 — sales and services back and forth

Smaller, but they run in both directions. PT Agung Sedayu Permai, a family construction company, is paid for building work — Rp45 miliar owed to it at the end of 2025. And family companies buy property from PANI: in 2024, sales to PT Sedayu Sejahtera Abadi and PT Citra Abadi Mandiri together were **13.3% of the group's revenue**. In 2025 that fell to zero, which is itself a change worth noting — a revenue line worth Rp377 miliar simply stopped. Three family entities also hold Rp721 miliar of advances for property they have bought but not yet received, including a Buddhist foundation of which the founder is vice chairman.

**Where this leaves the reader.** The related-party network is not a footnote here; it is the supply chain, the former financier, the current banker, part of the customer base, and the counterparty to the largest transaction the company has ever done. Every leg is disclosed. None is priced against an observable outside benchmark — with the single exception of the CBDK purchase, where a market price did exist and where the price paid differed from it by a wide margin in both directions depending on the date (§10.3).

# 02 — MODEL

PANI does one thing: it turns raw coastal land in one regency into serviced plots and buildings, and sells them to Indonesian upper-middle-income buyers. There is a second, much smaller activity starting — renting out a convention centre and, from 2027, a hotel. Both are described below.

## 2.1 Property development at PIK2

### Product — what is actually sold

Three categories, and they behave quite differently.

**Commercial land plots.** A bare, serviced plot inside the PIK2 central business district. The buyer builds on it themselves. This is the most profitable thing the company sells, by a distance: the gross margin on commercial land was 76% in 2025. There is almost nothing to build, so almost the whole price above the land cost falls through. In 2025 this was Rp1.58 triliun of revenue, 37% of the total.

**Residential houses.** Landed houses in named clusters — Rumah Milenial at Rp1.5–5.5 miliar a unit, Permata Hijau Residences at Rp6–16 miliar, Pantai Bukit Villa at Rp8–25 miliar. Margin around 48%. Rp1.56 triliun of revenue in 2025, 36% of the total.

**Commercial products.** Shophouses (ruko), shop-offices (rukan) and small office-and-home units branded SOHO, plus warehousing at Bizpark. Prices from about Rp3 miliar to Rp18 miliar a unit. Margin 54%. This category grew 200% in 2025 as several projects reached handover. Rp1.06 triliun, 25% of the total.

| Segment | Revenue 2025, Rp bn | Revenue 2024, Rp bn | Change | Gross margin 2025 | Gross margin 2024 |

| Residential houses | 1,557 | 1,093 | +42% | 48.0% | 46.8% |

The single most important line in that table is the jump in commercial-land margin, from 61% to 76%. The explanation given is that more of the plots handed over in 2025 were in the CBD, where the land cost base is lower relative to price. That is plausible — the CBD plots sit on land acquired years earlier — but it also means the group's headline margin now depends heavily on a mix that may not repeat.

Everything is in one place: a strip of former fishponds, mangrove and eroded coastline running about 20 kilometres along the north coast of Tangerang Regency, in the sub-districts of Kosambi, Teluknaga and Pakuhaji. PIK2 as the family describes it covers roughly 6,000 hectares; PANI's consolidated share is about **1,838 hectares**.

### Purchase — the land bank

Two observations. At roughly **Rp2 juta per square metre** of book cost, the land bank is carried at about Rp20 miliar a hectare — whether that is cheap depends entirely on what PIK2 land is worth, which is what the share price turns on. And **the land bank stopped growing in 2025**: total area is flat and undeveloped land actually fell, because the year's capital went into CBDK shares instead.

At Rp1.72 triliun of cost of revenue against Rp37.5 triliun of inventory, the land bank represents about **twenty-two years** of supply at the current rate of consumption. Even doubling the pace leaves more than a decade. This company does not need to buy land; it has been buying land far ahead of its ability to sell it. That is deliberate — the family has been assembling this coastline for years — but it explains the cash flow through 2024 (§07.4).

### Purchase — how land actually enters the company

The family's private companies do the assembly. Buying coastal land in Indonesia means negotiating with hundreds or thousands of village landholders, many with informal title, over years — slow, politically delicate work, done outside the listed vehicle by companies like Wahana Utama Karya and Cakra Karya Semesta.

Once a block is assembled and titled, it is sold into a PANI subsidiary. The listed company pays cash it has just raised from investors. The price sits in inventory at cost — no revaluation, no goodwill, no independent appraisal disclosed. The subsidiary then puts in fill, roads and utilities, obtains a spatial-planning approval (PKKPR) and building permits, and starts selling.

This is why the group has almost no debt but keeps issuing shares. The related-party land purchase is not an occasional event; it is step one of the production process.

### Process — turning land into a saleable plot

The physical work is filling and levelling former fishpond and mangrove land, building main roads, and connecting drainage, water and power. Capital expenditure was **Rp6.22 triliun in 2025** and Rp4.8 triliun in 2024, described only as "infrastructure development." No breakdown is given anywhere — a significant gap, since this is the largest use of cash after land.

Above the land a contractor builds the unit. Construction runs roughly 36 months for houses and shophouses and 48 for apartments, under the timelines written into the bank mortgage agreements. At the end of 2025 there were **3,505 units under construction covering 373,533 m²** — 1,418 residential, 2,087 commercial.

### Process — what it costs

The cost of a sold unit is land cost plus construction plus capitalised infrastructure. It is not disclosed separately, and the accounts give only one line: cost of revenue, Rp1.72 triliun in 2025, of which Rp1.68 triliun is real estate and Rp46 miliar is the new convention business.

### Distribute — how a unit is sold and paid for

This part of the model explains the balance sheet.

PANI sells **indent**: the unit is sold before it is built. A buyer signs, pays a deposit, then pays in instalments over the construction period, or takes a mortgage from one of the nine banks the group has agreements with (BCA, BNI, Danamon, Permata, Panin, Maybank, UOB, OCBC NISP, Artha Graha). Around 70% of Indonesian new-home purchases are mortgage-financed.

Because the money arrives before delivery, none of it is revenue yet — revenue is recognised only at handover. Everything collected before then sits as a liability called **advances from customers**: money owed to the buyer in the form of a house not yet delivered. Three consequences follow.

**The company has no trade receivables at all.** It states this explicitly. It is never owed money by a customer, because customers pay first. There is therefore no bad-debt risk and no collection cycle — a structural difference from almost any other business.

**The pre-sale float funds the business.** At the end of 2025 advances from customers were **Rp17.0 triliun**, against total liabilities of Rp18.9 triliun. Ninety percent of what this company owes, it owes to its own customers, in houses. That float is why bank debt is 1% of assets.

## 2.2 Recurring income — the second business, just starting

Until 2025 this company sold things and never rented anything. Two assets change that.

# 03 — DEMAND

**The national shortage is real but it is not this company's market.** Indonesia's statistics agency counts 9.29 juta households without a home of their own in 2026 — 12.4% of households, down from 12.75 juta in 2020 — plus 18.0 juta substandard homes. That is a mass-market shortage. PANI sells houses from Rp1.5 miliar to Rp25 miliar in a country where the government's own tax break for new homes is capped at Rp5 miliar. The backlog is context, not demand.

**The actual demand pool is the Greater Jakarta upper-middle household, and the draw is location plus completeness.** PIK2 is about seven minutes from Soekarno-Hatta airport on the new toll road, fifteen from Pondok Indah, thirty from the central business district. The pitch is that it is a finished city rather than a housing estate: schools (St Johannes Berchmans, Chevalier, Hope Academy, Universitas Prasetiya Mulya, UPH), a hospital, a golf course, malls, a beach club, a convention centre. Search-traffic data the company cites puts Tangerang Regency at 16.8% of national housing search demand, the highest of any area, ahead of South Jakarta at 11.4%.

**Interest rates move volumes, and they have turned.** About 70% of primary-market home purchases in Indonesia are mortgage-financed, so the policy rate transmits almost directly into pre-sales. Bank Indonesia cut 125 basis points through 2025, from 6.00% to 4.75%, and 2025 was a decent year for the sector. From May 2026 it reversed and has since raised 100 basis points, to 5.75%. Teaser mortgage rates roughly doubled between mid-2025 and mid-2026; floating rates now sit at 9–11.5%, and the full effect on new lending is expected to land only in late 2026.

**The VAT break expires at the end of 2026.** Under the PPN DTP scheme the state pays the value-added tax on qualifying new homes priced up to Rp5 miliar, covering the first Rp2 miliar of price. It has been extended repeatedly and currently runs to **31 December 2026**. It matters less to PANI than to peers — much of its range sits above the cap — but the mass-market clusters like Rumah Milenial and Ilona (Rp1.4–2.3 miliar) are squarely inside it. Pakuwon, for contrast, derived 73% of its 2025 pre-sales from eligible product.

**Conditions in 2026 are weak.** Bank Indonesia's residential price index rose 0.69% year on year in the second quarter of 2026 — below inflation, so house prices are falling in real terms. Primary residential sales fell 25.7% year on year in the first quarter and were still down 2.4% in the second. Against that, PANI's H1 2026 pre-sales rose 66%, off a weak base.

# 04 — SUPPLY

**Competing supply is abundant and cheap.** Indonesia's listed developers hold tens of thousands of hectares of Greater Jakarta land bank and the market values almost all of it below book — Bumi Serpong Damai trades at 0.26 times book, and most Indonesian property stocks trade below one times. The sector is demand-constrained, not supply-constrained.

**PANI's own supply is enormous relative to its sales rate.** The 1,838-hectare land bank represents roughly twenty-two years of sales. There is no scarcity inside this company; the constraint on revenue is how fast it can sell and hand over.

**What changed is that the land bank can no longer easily be extended.** Two reasons, both dating from the last eighteen months.

*The National Strategic Project designation is gone.* In March 2024 President Joko Widodo designated "Tropical Coastland" — 1,755 hectares in the Pantura corridor of Tangerang Regency, a stated Rp65 triliun investment — as a National Strategic Project, item 226 in the tourism sector. That status eases permitting and, critically, opens access to the state's compulsory land-acquisition machinery. In September–October 2025, under President Prabowo Subianto, it was **deleted from the list**, the agrarian minister citing spatial plans that did not align and the absence of a detailed plan. The company says there is no impact and development continues; the coordinating minister for economic affairs stated separately that PIK2 is not a National Strategic Project. What is lost is the easier route to assembling more land.

Notably, **PSN status is not mentioned anywhere in any of PANI's own filings** — not in four annual reports, not in the prospectus, not in three public expose decks. A designation that generated national controversy for eighteen months does not appear in the company's own account of itself in either direction.

*The legal position on coastal land is now contested.* A bamboo fence roughly 30 kilometres long appeared in shallow waters off Tangerang during 2024. Investigation established that 263–280 land certificates had been issued over sea areas, which Indonesian law does not permit; where such a certificate is under five years old the minister can cancel it administratively without going to court. About 50 were cancelled in January 2025 and the minister stated all beyond the coastline would follow. Four individuals — a village head, a village secretary, a lawyer and a journalist — were convicted of falsifying records in January 2026 and sentenced to three and a half years each; no company and no Agung Sedayu executive was charged. The certificates and PANI's own exposure to them are dealt with in §10.5; what matters here is the supply consequence.

**Net effect on supply.** PIK2's expansion has always depended on converting coastal and reclaimed land into titled, buildable inventory. That route is narrower than it was in 2024: no strategic-project status, an unresolved question about titles over former sea, and a government that has shown it will withdraw a designation its predecessor granted. The existing 1,838 hectares are unaffected and are enough for two decades. Anything beyond them is harder than it looked.

# 05 — KEY VARIABLES

In order of how much they matter.

**1. What PIK2 land is worth per square metre.** The land bank is carried at about Rp2 juta per square metre; the market values the company at roughly three times the book value of that land. Everything in the valuation debate reduces to this number, and there is no published, verifiable series for Greater Jakarta land prices — which is why the debate never resolves.

**2. Pre-sales.** They lead revenue by one to three years and are the only forward-looking operating number given. They fell 28% in 2025 and the 2026 target is flat. If pre-sales stay near Rp4.3 triliun, revenue converges there and the reported growth stops.

**3. The Bank Indonesia policy rate.** With 70% of purchases mortgage-financed, this is the transmission channel into demand. It has risen 100 basis points since May 2026 after falling 125 through 2025. The direction has reversed.

**4. Advances from customers.** Rp17.0 triliun already collected against undelivered units. Growing means pre-sales exceed handovers and revenue growth is banked; flat, as in 2025, means the two are in balance; falling means revenue will follow it down.

**5. The share of commercial land plots in the mix.** Commercial land carries a 76% gross margin against 48% for houses. The mix shifted to residential in 2025 (47% of pre-sales against 33% in 2024) and the 2026 target puts residential at 59%. That pulls the blended margin down mechanically.

**6. Related-party land pricing.** The cost base is set by what the group pays affiliated vendors, and both sides of that table are the same people. It is the largest single input cost and the least externally verifiable number in the accounts.

**7. The PPN DTP VAT incentive.** Expires 31 December 2026; relevant to the sub-Rp5-miliar clusters.

**8. Free float and index inclusion.** Not an operating variable, but it moves the price more than several of the above. The float on the exchange's measure is around 8%, PANI is in no major index, and a new concentration screen works against it.

# 06 — VICISSITUDES

**The share price has already had its crash.** From Rp19,550 on 11 December 2024 to Rp6,025 on 4 September 2026 — a **69% fall** in the share price, 61% of it in the last twelve months. Market value fell less, by about two-thirds, from roughly Rp330 triliun to Rp110 triliun, because the share count grew 7% in between. The company's own decks record it going from Rp263 triliun in September 2025 to Rp133 triliun in May 2026. This is not a risk to anticipate; it has happened, and it happened while revenue grew 52% and profit grew 84%. What fell was the multiple, not the business. The sector de-rated at the same time but less violently — the Salim group's own aggregate listed value, per PANI's decks, fell about 40% over the same period. PANI fell further because it started higher.

**A reputational shock arrived and produced no financial damage.** The sea-fence affair ran from late 2024 to the January 2026 convictions. The Banten Ombudsman found maladministration in December 2024, including misuse of the strategic-project designation, blocked fishing access and estimated annual losses to fishers of Rp8 miliar; Mongabay documented families evicted for PIK2 whose relocation site then flooded; the strategic-project status was revoked. Through all of it, PANI's own operating land titles were untouched, no corporate defendant was charged, no regulator opened a case against PANI, and the company raised Rp15.7 triliun from the market in December 2025. The lesson from this cycle is that reputational and political shocks have not translated into financial ones — so far.

**One very large contingent claim is outstanding.** In December 2024 twenty plaintiffs including nine retired army colonels filed suit in the Central Jakarta District Court (754/Pdt.G/2024) against Sugianto Kusuma, Anthoni Salim, **PT Pantai Indah Kapuk Dua Tbk**, PT Kukuh Mandiri Lestari, former President Joko Widodo and Coordinating Minister Airlangga Hartarto, seeking **Rp612 triliun** payable to the state over the Tropical Coastland designation. No reporting on a ruling, dismissal or settlement was found. The rights-issue prospectus, dated 28 November 2025, declares that no group company or board member is party to any proceeding anywhere. Those two facts sit uncomfortably together (§10.4).

**A downturn would hurt this company less than most.** With Rp550 miliar of debt, Rp3.8 triliun of cash and Rp17 triliun of customer money already collected, there is no lender who can force a sale and no refinancing wall. Several Indonesian developers were badly damaged in past cycles by dollar borrowings; this one has none. A repeat of the 2015–16 downturn would look like pre-sales falling to perhaps Rp3 triliun, the float drawing down as handovers continue, revenue holding for two years on backlog and then falling, and margins compressing as the mix shifts to whatever will sell. Profit would fall; the company would not be threatened. The share price is a separate question, because the price does not rest on current profit.

**2023 and 2024: operations consumed cash on a very large scale.** Minus Rp938 miliar, then minus Rp4.57 triliun. In 2024 the group collected Rp6.75 triliun from customers and paid out Rp11.04 triliun to suppliers, because the money raised from shareholders was being spent on land — and for a developer, land is inventory, so it runs through the operating section. Reported profit that year was Rp1.27 triliun. The Rp5.8 triliun gap between profit and operating cash flow was entirely land.

**2025: operating cash flow turned positive, but only just** — plus Rp4 miliar, essentially breakeven. Receipts from customers fell from Rp6.75 triliun to Rp4.57 triliun and payments to suppliers fell from Rp11.04 triliun to Rp4.35 triliun. Both halves shrank. The company explains this as development running to plan; the simpler reading is that land buying more than halved.

**2025 investing: minus Rp17.3 triliun,** of which Rp15.13 triliun is the single line "purchases of shares of subsidiaries from non-controlling interests" — the CBDK purchase — plus Rp1.15 triliun on the convention centre and Rp850 miliar on the Danantara bonds.

**Financing has funded everything, every year.** Rp16.87 triliun in 2025, of which Rp16.03 triliun was the rights issue and Rp2.30 triliun CBDK's own IPO. Over four years financing has brought in about Rp25.6 triliun while operations produced roughly minus Rp5 triliun. **Free cash flow — cash produced after paying for the assets the business needs — has never been positive.** Every rupiah spent on land, infrastructure and buying its own subsidiaries has come from shareholders. That will not change until land buying stops permanently; 2025 was the first year it came close.

**The tax line is unusual.** Indonesian property sales carry a **final tax** — a flat charge on sale value paid at the transaction, instead of ordinary corporate income tax on profit. This is how Indonesian property is taxed, not an arrangement. It also means most subsidiaries recognise no deferred tax at all.

# 09 — IMPORTANT TRANSACTIONS

The money table is §08.1. This section adds only the mechanism of each deal and the detail that table cannot show.

## 9.1 October 2021 — the backdoor listing

MAP bought 80% of PT Pratama Abadi Nusa Industri at Rp165, then tendered at Rp255: about **Rp54 miliar** for a company with Rp98 miliar of assets making metal cans and freezing fish. What was bought was the listing.

The two directors appointed in August 2021, two months *before* the purchase, are the tell. Surya Pranoto Budihardjo had been at the Salim group since 1973 and a director of PT Kapuk Naga Indah — a well-known Agung Sedayu PIK vehicle — since 1990. Ipeng Widjoyo held directorships across roughly twenty-five "Citra Kirana" and "Karya Sedayu" entities dating from 2014. The network was in place before the shares changed hands.

## 9.2 August 2022 — 51% of BKS, and the family loan repayment

PANI subscribed for new BKS shares for **Rp6.50 triliun**. Because BKS was deemed to have come under common control with PANI in October 2021 — ten months before any money moved — the deal was accounted for by pooling of interests: assets in at book value, no goodwill, no independent valuation required, and the whole FY2021 comparative column restated as though BKS had always been in the group. The Rp4.71 triliun gap between price and book value was charged directly against equity.

Within the same month BKS used the money to redeem **Rp4.14 triliun** of bonds — every one held by a company under the same family control, seventeen of them named in the FY2022 accounts, unsecured, at 2.5% a year. PANI separately repaid Rp3.75 triliun of family loans. The market put in Rp6.56 triliun; Rp7.89 triliun went out to family entities within months.

## 9.3 December 2023 — seven land companies in one transaction

Rights issue II raised Rp10.49 triliun, of which Rp9.48 triliun subscribed for new shares in seven existing family-owned land companies: CISN (Rp4,160bn, 99.33%), KUS (Rp1,656bn, 99.15%), JIS (Rp1,273bn, 93.06%), BMW (Rp1,268bn, 94.07%), KKU (Rp461bn, 90.14%), SHM (Rp340bn, 90.91%) and SCU (Rp328bn, 90.91%), plus Rp990bn into PET.

The mechanism matters: this was a **subscription for new shares**, not a purchase from the family, so the cash went into the companies. But those companies' loans from the family were then repaid, which is where the Rp16.87 triliun of related-party repayments in 2023 came from. Some of these companies were decades old — Sharindo Matratama dates from 1988. Under the same common-control rule, FY2022 total assets were restated from Rp15.9 triliun to Rp28.0 triliun. Also in 2023: the frozen-fish subsidiary was sold to four individuals for a combined **Rp27.7 juta**, and the company changed its name.

## 9.4 August 2024 — two placements, and the land they bought

Two capital increases *without* pre-emptive rights, three days apart at Rp5,200, raising Rp4.10 triliun and Rp2.44 triliun — so existing public shareholders had no right to participate. The money went into PET, which bought **2,323,789 m²** in Tangerang Regency: 1,475,551 m² from **PT Wahana Utama Karya** and 848,238 m² from **PT Karya Indah Raya**, both under common control with PANI. The FY2024 annual report discloses these as affiliated transactions at arm's length, and names **no appraiser, no fairness opinion and no shareholder vote** for either.

## 9.5 January 2025 — CBDK's own listing

BKS sold 566,894,500 new shares — 10% of itself — at Rp4,060, raising **Rp2.30 triliun**. It listed on 13 January 2025, closed limit-up on day one, and hit **Rp11,450** within a week. PANI's direct stake fell to 45.90%, and control never left the family, because the other 44.10% sat directly with PT Agung Sedayu and PT Tunas Mekar Jaya.

# 10 — RED FLAG

Six things deserve suspicion. For each, the evidence and where the money goes.

## 10.1 The listed company is a buyer of last resort for its own controllers' assets

Six capital raises in four years, Rp39.6 triliun, every one funding the acquisition of — or repayment of debt owed on — assets the controlling families already owned. About Rp32 triliun ended up on the family side (§01.5, §08.1).

Nothing about that is unlawful, and Indonesian rules require disclosure, appraisal and, for material transactions, an independent shareholder vote — all of which happened for the largest deal. But the structure has an unavoidable feature: the family sets both the timing and the price of every injection, and the public provides the cash. The disclosure tells you what happened, not whether the price was right.

The specific thing that cannot be checked: **Rp8.4 triliun of land bought from PT Wahana Utama Karya, PT Karya Indah Raya and PT Cakra Karya Semesta across 2023–2025 has no disclosed pricing basis at all** — no appraiser named, no per-square-metre benchmark, no comparison to third-party land in the same villages. The FY2024 annual report says only that the transactions were at arm's length and were reviewed by the audit committee.

## 10.2 An interest-free, open-ended Rp1.6 triliun loan to a family company

PANI is owed **Rp1,570,234,575,000** by PT Cakra Karya Semesta, to be settled by handing land over "whenever Cakra Karya Semesta and the landowner reach agreement," with **no interest, no security and no repayment period**. That is 3.1% of the company's total assets, sitting with a private company owned by the same controllers, on terms no third party would accept. If the land is never delivered there is nothing to enforce and no date on which the debt falls due. If it is delivered, the price at which it is credited will be set by the same people on both sides.

## 10.3 The largest deal in the company's history was priced against a stale number

The Rp6,450 CBDK price was fixed against a 30 June 2025 valuation, contracted on 29 August 2025, and executed on 16–23 December 2025. Over that window CBDK traded between Rp6,025 and Rp8,850. The price was never re-set.

This deserves suspicion not because the direction favoured the family — on the day of execution it did not — but because a transaction of this size in a *listed* subsidiary should reference the observable market price at execution, in either direction, and this one referenced a discounted-cash-flow model six months old. The appraiser and the fairness-opinion provider were the same firm. On one analyst's reading, the rally that opened the gap was caused by the rights issue that funded the purchase.

The second-order effect: because PANI paid roughly four times CBDK's book value, **Rp11.4 triliun was deducted directly from group equity** without ever passing through the income statement. Reported profit was unaffected, and the pro-forma income statement in the prospectus shows literally zero adjustment on every line.

## 10.4 The prospectus says there is no litigation; a Rp612 triliun suit names the company

The PMHMETD III prospectus carries a signed declaration dated 28 November 2025, from the company, every subsidiary, and every commissioner and director, stating that none of them is involved in or party to any civil, criminal, labour, commercial, competition, tax or administrative proceeding, in any court, mediation body or arbitration body, in Indonesia or abroad.

Case 754/Pdt.G/2024/PN Jkt.Pst was filed at the Central Jakarta District Court on 8 December 2024 — almost a year earlier. **PT Pantai Indah Kapuk Dua Tbk is named as the third defendant**, alongside Sugianto Kusuma, Anthoni Salim, PT Kukuh Mandiri Lestari, Joko Widodo and Airlangga Hartarto. The claim is Rp612 triliun, payable to the state.

There are innocent explanations — the suit may have been dismissed on formal grounds before November 2025, or never properly served, or the company may take the view it does not meet the disclosure threshold. I could not find any reporting on its outcome, so I cannot say which. But a prospectus for a Rp15.7 triliun raise declaring no litigation, while a widely reported suit naming the issuer sits on a court docket, is a discrepancy a reader is entitled to have explained. The FY2025 accounts likewise contain no litigation or contingency note of any kind.

## 10.5 One of the group's own subsidiaries held land certificates over the sea

Of the 263–280 certificates issued over sea off Tangerang that the agrarian minister found "defective in procedure and substance," **20 were held by PT Cahaya Inti Sentosa** — a direct PANI subsidiary, 99.90% owned, holding 234 hectares and developing the Permata Golf clusters. On the agrarian ministry's own count of 263, another 234 were held by PT Intan Agung Makmur, an Agung Sedayu company **outside** PANI, and 9 by individuals. About 50 certificates were cancelled in January 2025 and the minister stated all beyond the coastline would follow; I could not establish that the process is complete, and no revocation of PANI's or CBDK's own operating titles has been reported.

A second connection: Rp375 miliar of the December 2025 rights-issue proceeds was injected into **PT Cahaya Inti Sentosa**, the same entity.

The company's own risk factors, annual reports and decks contain no mention of land title risk, reclamation, the sea-fence affair, or the loss of strategic-project status. The prospectus names one main risk — "dependency on subsidiaries" — then eight business risks and five general ones, and states that management believes **all** risks have been disclosed. For a company whose entire asset base is coastal land in the exact regency where a titling scandal produced criminal convictions and ministerial cancellations, the complete absence of a land-title risk factor is a disclosure choice, not an oversight.

- **The auditor's opinion is clean.** KAP Johan Malonda Mustika & Rekan, a Baker Tilly member firm, signing partner Putu Astika. Unmodified opinions on FY2024, on H1 2025 and on FY2025 (4 March 2026). No emphasis of matter.
- **No regulatory action against PANI.** No enforcement action by the financial regulator, no exchange query, no unusual-market-activity notice, no trading suspension and no tax dispute involving PANI itself surfaced in searching. A subsidiary settled a Rp9 miliar tax assessment for 2022 within days of receiving it.
- **Board pay is modest** — Rp29.8 miliar for all commissioners and directors combined — and **no director or commissioner holds PANI shares directly**; their interest is entirely through the holding chain.

One item sits between the two lists. The auditor was paid Rp175 juta for the FY2025 audit and Rp165 juta for the half-year review, and separately Rp50 juta for reviewing pro-forma figures and **Rp85 juta for "Rights Issue III preparation services."** Deal-preparation work by the audit firm on the transaction it audits around is not prohibited and the amounts are trivial, but it is worth noting.

# MISSING DATA AND OPEN ITEMS

Ranked by how much each would change the analysis.

**1. What PIK2 land actually sells for, per square metre, to third parties.** The single number the whole valuation rests on, and nothing in the filings gives it — no units sold, no units handed over, no price per square metre. Most likely recoverable from quarterly filings if they break out unit volumes, or from a broker model with primary-market survey data. It decides whether the Rp2 juta per square metre book cost is a third of realisable value or most of it.

**2. The pricing basis for land bought from related parties.** Rp8.4 triliun across 2023–2025 with no appraiser named and no benchmark given. Most likely in the information-disclosure filings (keterbukaan informasi) lodged with the exchange at the time of each purchase, which were not in the document set. It decides whether the 60% gross margin is a real economic margin or a transfer-pricing artefact.

**3. The outcome of case 754/Pdt.G/2024** — the Rp612 triliun civil suit naming PANI as third defendant. No ruling, dismissal or settlement was found. It decides whether the prospectus's no-litigation declaration is explicable, and it is the largest contingent item anywhere near this company.

**4. The final status of the cancelled sea certificates, including CISN's twenty.** About 50 of roughly 265–280 were cancelled in January 2025; completion is not established and no administrative-court ruling on any challenge was found. Most likely recoverable from ATR/BPN announcements. It decides whether any of PANI's own titled land is at risk, which the filings assert it is not.

**5. The free-float reconciliation.** Public shareholding 16.25%; the exchange's measure about 8%. Which holders were reclassified as connected, and why, is the crux of the index-inclusion argument underpinning much of the bull case. Probably obtainable from KSEI register detail or an IDX methodology note.

**6. Capital expenditure detail.** Rp6.22 triliun in 2025 and Rp4.8 triliun in 2024, described only as "infrastructure development," with no breakdown and no plan-versus-actual. This is the second-largest use of cash in the business and it is one unexplained line. Probably only obtainable from management.

**7. The 2025 pre-sales target.** Rp5.3 triliun announced in January 2025 and printed in the company's own September 2025 deck; Rp4.3 triliun reported as 100% of target. No formal revision found. A direct question would settle it.

**8. The Rp850 miliar Danantara "Patriot Bond" purchase.** CBDK bought Series A and B at 2% a year in October 2025, maturing 2030 and 2032, with no rationale given anywhere. At 2% against deposit rates of 2.5–6.75%, this is a below-market placement into a state vehicle. The reason matters more than the amount.

**9. The variable rent on the NICE lease.** IPN pays PT Kukuh Mandiri Lestari — a family company outside PANI — a rent that varies with IPN's own profit or loss, on 18.8 hectares for twenty years renewable. The formula is in Article 6 of the agreement, which is not reproduced. It determines how much of the convention centre's economics reaches shareholders. NICE's and the Hilton's operating metrics — occupancy, rate, revenue target — are likewise entirely undisclosed.

**10. Land bank reconciliation.** Four different group totals across three documents in one year (1,809 / 1,825 / 1,838 hectares), with per-subsidiary figures moving in both directions between decks. No authoritative figure exists in the sources at hand.

**Sources never supplied.** The FY2021 and FY2020 annual reports, so the pre-transformation history rests on comparative columns inside FY2022. The FY2023 annual report contains no financial statements at all, so every FY2023 figure comes from the FY2024 comparative column. No quarterly filings for 2026 beyond press summaries. And no FY2026 half-year accounts, which would show whether the pre-sale float has started to draw down.
