Indonesia's largest cigarette maker. Nine factories and 43 contract hand-rolling partners on Java,
about 78 billion cigarettes a year, roughly 30% of the legal Indonesian market. 92.44% owned by
PT Philip Morris Indonesia, itself wholly owned by Philip Morris International. Listed on the IDX
since August 1990.

**Market capitalisation Rp85.5 trillion at Rp735 a share (28 August 2026).** Down about 87% from
the January 2018 peak of Rp5,500 intraday (Rp5,200 on a closing basis), when it was the largest
company on the exchange.
Price/earnings about 10×, dividend yield about 7.7%.

**Period covered: FY2020 – FY2025**, plus events to August 2026.

**Filings in hand:** five annual reports (FY2021–FY2025), nine public-expose presentations
(2019–November 2025). No FY2019 or FY2020 annual report, so FY2020 figures come from the comparative
column of the FY2021 report rather than from the original filing. Full source table at the end.

## THE ONE THING TO UNDERSTAND FIRST

**In Indonesia, cigarette tax is set by how big you are, not by what you charge. Sampoerna is the
biggest, so it pays the most — per cigarette, permanently, by law.**

Every cigarette sold legally in Indonesia carries a fixed excise stamp, a tax paid per stick rather
than as a percentage of price. The rate depends on two things: how the cigarette is made, and how
many the maker produces in a year. Machine-made cigarettes are sorted at a line of three billion
sticks a year *per category*. Produce more than three billion and you are in the top band —
*Golongan I* — and pay Rp1,231 on every machine-made clove cigarette. Produce less and you pay
Rp746. Same product, same shop, same shelf; a difference of Rp485 a stick, or Rp9,700 on a
twenty-pack, decided entirely by the size of the company that made it.

Sampoerna produces around 78 billion cigarettes a year. It has never been anywhere near the line and
never will be. Its scale — the thing that in any ordinary manufacturer buys lower unit costs — is
here a fixed, unavoidable tax penalty against precisely the competitors that are taking its
customers. A small producer in Kudus or Malang starts every year with a cost advantage of roughly
Rp485 a stick that no amount of Sampoerna's efficiency can close.

For most of the last decade this did not matter much, because Indonesian smokers were willing to pay
for brands. Then the government raised the tax hard — 24% in 2020, 14% in 2021, 13% in 2022 — and
retail prices rose with it. At that point the gap stopped being a technicality and became the market.
Cigarettes made by sub-three-billion producers went from 18% of everything smoked in Indonesia in
2017 to 44% by the middle of 2024. Sampoerna's share of the market fell from 33% to 27% over the
same stretch. It was not out-marketed. It was out-taxed.

Three consequences run through everything that follows.

**First, the reported accounts do not show a cigarette business.** Sampoerna books revenue with the
excise stamp inside it, so about 60% of the Rp112 trillion "revenue" line is tax that never belonged
to the company. The reported gross margin of 18% is arithmetic on a number that is mostly government
money. Strip the tax out and the real gross margin is nearer 47%. Every margin, every ratio and every
comparison in this report is rebuilt on revenue net of excise, and the rebuilt series behaves
completely differently from the filed one (§07).

**Second, the company has no lever on the thing that decides its year.** The tax rate is set by
ministerial decree each December. In 2025 it was frozen for the first time since 2019, and in 2026 it
was frozen again along with the minimum retail price. Those two decisions, not anything management
did, are why FY2025 gross profit rose 11% on revenue that fell 5%, and why brokers expect FY2026
profit up more than 20%. The single most important input to this business is a political decision
taken annually by someone else (§05, §08.1).

**Third, the listed company exists for one shareholder, and the exchange is about to change that.**
Philip Morris owns 92.44%; the public owns 7.56%, a number engineered to the old regulatory minimum
in a 2015 rights issue and not moved since. Essentially every rupiah of profit is paid out as
dividend — the payout ratio has been between 98% and 103% every single year — and much of what was
left sat as a loan to Philip Morris's Swiss treasury company rather than in Sampoerna's own bank
accounts. In March 2026 the Indonesia Stock Exchange raised the minimum public float from 7.5% to
15%. Sampoerna is on the published non-compliant list. Meeting the rule means putting roughly 8.7
billion more shares — about Rp6.4 trillion, close to a full year's profit — into public hands by
March 2028, and neither the company nor Philip Morris has said how (§08.3).

# 01 — OWNER

## 1.1 Group structure

Sampoerna sits at the bottom of a short, entirely wholly-owned chain. There is no complexity here to
untangle and no hidden minority: the chain exists to hold one asset.


Two features of this chart are worth pausing on.

**The controlling shareholder is also a competitor and a supplier.** PT Philip Morris Indonesia is
not a holding shell. It manufactures Marlboro at its own plant in Bekasi, West Java, and has done
since 2006. Sampoerna does not make Marlboro; it buys finished Marlboro from PMID and distributes it,
and it also rents PMID land and buildings at Karawang and Sukorejo. So the parent sells to its own
subsidiary, buys distribution from it, and rents property from it, all inside the same group. Those
flows are set out in §1.4.

**The Vietnamese joint venture is drawn inside Sampoerna's structure chart but is not really
Sampoerna's.** Vinataba–Philip Morris Limited is 51% owned by Vietnam's state tobacco corporation and
49% by the Philip Morris side, and Sampoerna carries that 49% as an associate worth Rp94.8 billion.
It contributed Rp9.7 billion of profit in 2025 — a rounding error against Rp6.6 trillion. It is
mentioned here only so that a reader who sees it on the chart knows it is not material.

commercial model for the cheap end of the Indonesian market and Sampoerna began acting as a "brand
management and fulfilment services agent" for volume it no longer owns. The entity's assets went from
Rp235 million to Rp23.6 billion to Rp82.3 billion in two years. No filing connects the two events, and
the connection is inference, not disclosure — but it is the obvious place to look (§08.4).

**The recap.** This is a 113-year-old family kretek maker that was sold to a multinational at the top
of its powers, kept its brands and its factories, and has since spent twenty years as the Indonesian
manufacturing and distribution arm of Philip Morris. Every strategic decision that matters — pricing,
product, capital, board — is made inside a global group for which Indonesia is one market among many.
The listed shares are a 7.5% window onto that arrangement.

## 1.3 The owner's character

There are two owners here in different senses: Philip Morris, which controls the company, and the
public shareholders, who own a sliver of it. What the controller does with cash tells you most of
what you need to know.

**It takes everything out.** In six years Sampoerna earned Rp43.4 trillion and paid out Rp50.7
trillion in dividends. The payout ratio against the prior year's profit has been 98.7%, 103.2%,
100.6%, 99.6% and 98.4% in the five years to FY2024, and about 99% again for FY2025. In two of
those years the dividend exceeded the profit it was paid out of. Shareholders' equity, which should
grow with retained profit, instead fell from Rp30.2 trillion to Rp28.4 trillion. Nothing is being
reinvested that is not needed for the next twelve months.

That is not, on its own, a criticism. A mature cigarette business with no growth and no debt has
little reason to hoard cash. But it does define what the minority is buying: a claim on a dividend,
not on a compounding asset.

**What it does not pay out, it lends to itself.** In 2022 Sampoerna liquidated Rp12.8 trillion of
rupiah time deposits and lent US$800 million to **Philip Morris Finance SA**, the group's Swiss
treasury company. That single receivable was 22.8% of everything Sampoerna owned. Its cash balance
collapsed from Rp17.8 trillion to Rp3.3 trillion in one year. The loan has since been wound down —
US$735 million, then US$445 million, then US$240 million at the end of 2025 — but for four years a
fifth of the balance sheet was money sitting inside the parent group rather than inside the listed
company.

The terms of that facility are asymmetric in a way worth stating plainly. Sampoerna may **lend** the
treasury company up to 100% of its own net income. Sampoerna may **borrow** from it only up to 50% of
its own equity — a limit that was halved from 100% in July 2022. It has never borrowed a rupiah. The
facility runs to June 2033.

**It charges for its services, and the charges are growing far faster than the profit.** Philip
Morris entities bill Sampoerna for information technology, management services, technical support and
brand royalties. Those charges have risen from Rp1.6 trillion in 2020 to Rp3.0 trillion in 2025, an
88% increase, over a period in which Sampoerna's pre-tax profit *fell* 16%. Measured against pre-tax
profit, group charges have gone from about a seventh to about a third:

The largest single line is not the trademark royalty. It is information technology: **Philip Morris
International IT Service Center SARL** billed Rp1.15 trillion in 2025, up from Rp445 billion in 2020,
and now charges Sampoerna more than three times the trademark royalty. Neither the royalty rate as a
percentage of sales nor the mark-up on services is disclosed in any year, in any filing. The
accounting policy note says only "a percentage of net sales" and "cost plus a mark-up". Those two
undisclosed percentages set the price on roughly Rp11 trillion a year of transactions.

**The boards are Philip Morris.** Three of the four commissioners and, by the company's own account,
every one of the eight directors has a Philip Morris career history. Three of the
commissioners who served between 2021 and 2026 are former Presidents Director of the company they now
supervise — John Gledhill (2009–12), Paul Janelle (2012–16) and Mindaugas Trumpaitis (2016–22).
Trumpaitis holds the post of Vice President Commissioner while simultaneously running Philip Morris's
global combustibles business from Switzerland. The annual report explicitly states that board
succession runs through "PMI's global talent review process, which involves assessing talent from
other overseas affiliates, or former or retired employees of PMI."

The "independent" commissioners deserve a sentence each. **Justin Mayall** is classified as
independent and states no affiliate relationship with the controlling shareholder. He also spent 27
years in Philip Morris's law department and was the lead lawyer on the acquisition of Sampoerna
itself. **Luthfi Mardiansyah**, a medical doctor who ran Pfizer and Novartis in Indonesia, is the only
person on either board with no Philip Morris history at all.

much.

2012 and subsequent financial years until determined otherwise," and has not been back to a

Four things stand out.

**Sampoerna is becoming an export factory for the group.** Exports to Philip Morris affiliates have
gone from Rp219 billion to Rp2.16 trillion in five years, a tenfold increase, while domestic volume
shrank. Nearly all of it goes to **Philip Morris Products SA** in Switzerland, with Korea and the
Philippines behind it. This is the Karawang smoke-free plant working: the company says combined
exports with PMID reached about US$300 million in 2025, of which roughly US$100 million was smoke-free
product. It is genuine business, and it is the one part of the model that is growing.

**The purchase side is shrinking as the service side grows.** Buying finished Marlboro from the parent
has fallen from Rp8.8 trillion to Rp5.5 trillion, and from 12% of cost of goods sold to 6%. Over the
same period, what the group *charges* Sampoerna for services nearly doubled. The transaction that
transfers most value to the parent has quietly shifted from goods to fees.

**Nothing is priced transparently.** The transfer-pricing note says the royalty is "calculated on net
sales of the related products" and that supply and service transactions are "cost plus a mark-up." In
six annual reports neither percentage is ever given. The FY2021 report also names the methods used —
comparable uncontrolled price, transactional net margin — and later reports drop even that.

**The distribution deal was rewritten and the new price was never disclosed.** Sampoerna had been
PMID's sole distributor in Indonesia since 2010, under an agreement extended in 2015 to run to
February 2025. It was terminated with effect from **1 January 2023**, twenty-six months early, and
replaced with a five-year rolling agreement under which Sampoerna is paid "a defined percentage of
earnings before tax." What percentage, and whose earnings, is not stated anywhere. The front matter of
the FY2023 annual report does not mention the change at all; it describes the old arrangement in the
present tense.

Alongside this, Sampoerna is PMID's landlord. It leases land and buildings at Karawang and Sukorejo to
the parent — every one of its investment properties is let to PMID — for about Rp60 billion a year
under a 2021 lease, cut to about Rp52 billion by a 2022 amendment, then re-cut in June 2024 to two
leases totalling about Rp5 billion a year. Those properties are carried at Rp316 billion and were
independently appraised at **Rp877 billion** at February 2026, on an income approach built on rents
paid by the controlling shareholder.

# 02 — MODEL

## 2.1 Product

Sampoerna sells cigarettes in five shapes, and Indonesian tax law — not marketing — defines the
categories. Learning the four abbreviations is unavoidable, because every price, tax rate and market
statistic in this industry is quoted by category.

| Code | What it means in plain words | Sampoerna's brands |
|---|---|---|
| **SKM** | *Sigaret kretek mesin* — a clove cigarette made by machine. Tobacco blended with crushed clove buds, the standard Indonesian smoke. Split in the market into "high tar" (full flavour) and "low tar" (mild) | **Sampoerna A** (A Mild, Avolution, A Splash), **Dji Sam Soe Magnum**, **Marlboro Filter Black**, **Marlboro Advance**, U Mild |

**The company has been retreating into hand-rolled cigarettes.** Machine-made clove, its historic
core, has fallen from two-thirds of revenue to a bit over half. Hand-rolled has gone from under a
quarter to a third. This is not a marketing decision; it is a tax decision. Hand-rolled cigarettes are
taxed at roughly a third of the machine-made rate — Rp378 to Rp483 a stick against Rp1,231 — because
the government protects the jobs of the women who roll them. When machine-made prices rose with the
tax and hand-rolled prices did not, smokers moved, and Sampoerna moved with them. Hand-rolled's share
of the whole Indonesian market went from 17% in 2019 to 28% in 2023.

The catch is that hand-rolled cigarettes cost more to make in every respect except tax. The FY2023
report contains the single most useful unit-economics sentence in six years of filings: **a hand-rolled
cigarette uses twice as much tobacco and clove as a machine-made one.** It also needs a person to roll
it. So the mix shift that saved volume raised raw-material intensity and labour intensity at the same
time — which is why raw materials in the cost line rose from Rp7.7 trillion to Rp12.1 trillion between
2020 and 2025 while volume fell.

**Marlboro is not Sampoerna's.** The white-cigarette business is made by the parent at Bekasi and
bought in for distribution. That is why "cost of merchandise inventory sold" appears in the cost
statement at all, and it explains a large and unexplained movement in 2025: purchases of merchandise
fell from Rp14.4 trillion to Rp6.1 trillion and the cost of merchandise sold from Rp9.6 trillion to
Rp1.9 trillion. The FY2025 report attributes higher operating expenses partly to a *higher* volume of
Marlboro distributed. The two statements do not sit comfortably together, and no filing reconciles
them (§08.4).

**Smoke-free is real but tiny.** Rp2.4 trillion of revenue in 2025, 2.2% of the total, growing 44% a
year. Around 1.5 billion heated-tobacco sticks, against roughly 78 billion cigarettes. In its one
disclosed test market — West, Central and South Jakarta, about 1.5 million adult nicotine users —
smoke-free products reached 7.7% share by the end of 2025, from 3.5% two years earlier. That is a
genuine trend line, and it is confined to one wealthy urban pocket.

## 2.5 Distribute — how it reaches the smoker

Sampoerna does not use wholesalers to reach the market. It runs its own distribution company,
**PT Panamas**, with more than 5,000 staff, seven regional branch offices and about 110 sales offices
and distribution centres reaching more than 90% of Indonesian districts. Roughly 1.5 million retail
outlets are served.

Sitting on top of that is the more interesting asset. The **Sampoerna Retail Community** is a network
of independent corner shops — *warung* — that agree to work with Sampoerna in exchange for shop
fittings, training, a wholesale app and a branded identity. It started with 57 shops in Medan in 2008.
There are now more than 250,000, organised into over 10,000 local associations, transacting through
the **AYO SRC** app at a rate the company puts above US$1 billion a year with about 90% adoption.

Sampoerna's own claim is that these shops turn over about Rp263 trillion a year — roughly 11% of
Indonesia's entire retail economy. That figure should be read carefully: it is the shops' total
turnover of everything they sell, not Sampoerna's revenue through them. But the strategic point holds.
In a market where the competitive threat comes from small, cheap, locally-made brands that reach
smokers through exactly these shops, owning the relationship with a quarter of a million of them is
the most durable moat Sampoerna has. It is also entirely undisclosed as a financial matter: PT SRC
Indonesia Sembilan has Rp109 billion of assets and no separate revenue or profit is ever given.

**Customer concentration is nil.** In every year the accounts state that no single customer exceeded
10% of revenue. There is one segment for reporting purposes — cigarettes — so no product-level margin
is available anywhere.

Twenty-five third-party "exclusive zonified distributors" were disclosed in the FY2021 report. They
vanish from every report thereafter with no comment.

# 03 — DEMAND

Indonesia has roughly 60 million adult smokers and one of the highest smoking rates among men in the
world. Demand for nicotine is not the variable; demand for *legal, taxed, branded* nicotine is.

**The market is shrinking, and the measurement of it is contested.** Philip Morris's own estimate of
total Indonesian cigarette consumption ran 303.6 billion sticks in 2018, 305.7 billion in 2019 as later restated, then
276.3 billion in 2020 and 258.7 billion in 2025. Official production data from the customs directorate
gives a different and higher series — 318.1 billion sticks in 2023, the lowest in a decade — because
it counts everything with a tax stamp, including exports.

There is a serious break in the share numbers that any reader of this company must know about. Between
its 2024 and 2025 results, Philip Morris **cut its estimate of the total Indonesian market for 2024
from 295.5 billion sticks to 265.2 billion** — a 10% downward revision — without changing its own
shipment figure of 80.8 billion. Reported market share for 2024 therefore jumped from 27.4% to 30.5%
on identical volume. Sampoerna's own annual report adopted the new basis, restating 2023 from 28.6% to
31.2%. **The apparent stabilisation in market share after 2024 is a change of denominator, not a
change of performance.** The same company had already restated its share upward once before, in the
FY2023 report, without comment.

**Where the volume went: downtrading.** The industry's own shorthand splits the market into "Volume
Tier 1" — cigarettes made by companies above the three-billion-stick line, paying the top tax — and
"Below Volume Tier 1", everything made by smaller producers at the lower rate. The split has moved
like this:

| Share of the Indonesian market | 2017 | 2019 | 2020 | 2021 | 2022 | H1-2023 | H1-2024 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|

In seven years the cheap tier went from less than a fifth of the market to nearly half. Sampoerna sits
entirely in the expensive tier. In fact it was *gaining* share inside Tier 1 through this period —
38.2% of Tier 1 in 2020, 39.8% in 2021 — while losing overall. It was winning its own race and losing
the market.

**And below even that: illegal cigarettes.** Untaxed product went from 4.9% of consumption in 2020 to
6.9% in 2023 and, on the customs directorate's own 2025 estimate quoted by Sampoerna, **13.9%** — a
doubling in two years. A private research house, Indodata, puts it at 46%, which is roughly ten times
the official estimate; that figure should be treated as an outlier, but the direction is not disputed
by anyone. Seizures rose from 254 million sticks in 2023 to 1.4 billion in 2025. Sampoerna's FY2025
report says its volume decline was "primarily attributable to the erosion of legal industry volume by
illicit products as opposed to competitive share loss."

**Smoke-free is the only growing demand pool**, and it is small: 1.5 billion heated-tobacco sticks in
2025, up 23%, against 77.9 billion cigarettes.

# 04 — SUPPLY

Supply, in this industry, means two things: the raw crops, and the number of factories allowed to
compete.

**Crops.** Sampoerna contracts with more than 22,500 tobacco and clove farmers (the November 2025
presentation says more than 19,500 — the two numbers conflict) under agricultural-practice standards
with guaranteed purchase. Leaf is also procured through **PT Sadhana**, a third party under a 2008
contract, against advances that have run between Rp0.5 trillion and Rp1.4 trillion and are secured by
a standby letter of credit whose issuer is never named.

Clove prices at the farm gate have risen from about Rp77,000 a kilogram in 2021 to roughly
Rp100,000–110,000 in mid-2026, an increase on the order of 40%, though the market is intensely regional
and seasonal — East Java quotes near Rp150,000 in the same period that Banten quotes Rp100,000. No
clean national series exists after 2021. Tobacco has no published national price at all; what exists is
a district reference floor negotiated each season, and it is worth noting that **the cigarette makers
sit at that negotiating table**, so Sampoerna is a party to setting its own input floor rather than a
pure price-taker. In Pamekasan, Madura, the 2026 floor for premium mountain-grown leaf was Rp66,547 a
kilogram, up 4% on 2025.

Sampoerna discloses no tonnage purchased and no purchase price for either crop, in any year.

**Factories.** The supply of competitors is itself a policy variable. Industry bodies report that the
number of cigarette factories in Indonesia has fallen from around 5,000 to around 1,000 over the past
decade — roughly 4,000 closures — driven by regulation and enforcement. That consolidation cuts both
ways for Sampoerna: fewer small producers means fewer cheap-tier rivals, but the ones that survive are
the effective ones, and the illegal segment absorbs the demand that the closures displace.

# 05 — KEY VARIABLES

Six things move this business. They are listed in order of how much profit each one swings, and each
is tied to a number in the accounts. Pre-tax profit in FY2025 was Rp9.43 trillion; every sensitivity
below is measured against that.

### 1. The annual excise tariff decision — the whole game

Excise charged on cigarettes sold was **Rp67.9 trillion in 2025**, seven times pre-tax profit. If the
tariff rose 10% and Sampoerna could not pass any of it on, roughly **Rp6.8 trillion of cost would land
on a Rp9.4 trillion profit** — about 72% of it gone. In practice the company passes most of it on over
a year or two, but not immediately, and the pass-through itself destroys volume.

The evidence that this is the dominant variable is in the record. Between 2019 and 2022 the tariff
rose 24%, 14% and 13%. Sampoerna's own words in the FY2022 report: net pricing finally turned positive
against excise in the third quarter of 2022 — **"for the first time since 2019."** Three full years
during which the company could not raise price fast enough to cover the tax. Own revenue per cigarette
(revenue after excise, divided by sticks sold) tells the same story:

| Per cigarette, full rupiah [all calculated] | 2020 | 2021 | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Revenue as reported | 1,163 | 1,194 | 1,281 | 1,381 | 1,459 | 1,413 |
| less excise | (714) | (786) | (859) | (823) | (861) | (855) |
| **= Sampoerna's own take** | **449** | **409** | **422** | **558** | **598** | **557** |
| Production cost | (191) | (180) | (178) | (218) | (251) | (270) |
| Net profit | 108 | 86 | 73 | 96 | 82 | 83 |

The company's own take per cigarette fell in 2021 and 2022 — the tax was rising faster than it could
price — then recovered sharply in 2023–24 as the increases slowed to 11% and prices caught up.

Then the tariff was **frozen for 2025 and frozen again for 2026**, with only the minimum retail price
moved up in 2025. That freeze is the entire explanation for FY2025: revenue fell 4.8% but gross profit
rose 11.2%, because the company kept taking price into a flat tax. It is also why a broker forecast in
August 2026 puts FY2026 profit up 23%. Nothing about the business changed; the decree did.

The freeze is not a policy commitment. No statement exists on 2027.

Measured on the like-for-like decree rates in the second row, the gap has gone from Rp195 in 2017 to

the company loses roughly 3 billion sticks of volume annually before any competitive effect.

Note the freeze does not fix this. Freezing both tiers preserves the gap in absolute rupiah; it stops
it widening but does not close it.

### 3. Mix — machine-made versus hand-rolled

Hand-rolled cigarettes carry a tax of Rp378–483 against Rp1,231, so they are the escape hatch, and
Sampoerna has used it: hand-rolled went from 23% of revenue in 2020 to 34% in 2024. But the escape has
a price. Hand-rolled uses twice the leaf and clove and needs a person to roll it, which is why
production cost per cigarette rose 41% over six years while volume fell. The mix shift protects
volume and erodes unit margin at the same time.

There is also a ceiling: hand-rolled has its own three-tier structure with the top band starting at
two billion sticks a year. Sampoerna is far above it, so hand-rolled buys it a lower rate, not the
lowest rate.

### 4. Illicit trade — the tax rate that is zero

On the customs directorate's own estimate, cited by Sampoerna, illegal cigarettes went from 4.9% of
consumption in 2020 to **13.9% in 2025**. Each point of the market that goes illegal removes roughly
2.6 billion sticks of legal demand — and Sampoerna's share of what is left cannot recover it, because
the buyer has left the taxed market entirely.

This variable is uniquely unhelpful to model because the estimates disagree by an order of magnitude
(4.9–13.9% official versus 28–46% from a private research house). What is not in dispute: government
tobacco excise receipts **fell** in both 2024 and 2025 — to about Rp212 trillion on one published
series and Rp222 trillion on another, both showing the same 2.1% decline — even though the tariff rose
about 12% for Sampoerna's own tier in 2024. Tax revenue falling while the tax rate rises is the signature of a base moving out
of reach.

### 5. Leaf and clove prices

Raw materials were Rp12.1 trillion in 2025, about 13% of cost of goods sold and 27% of revenue net of
excise. Clove at the farm gate is up roughly 40% since 2021. A 10% move in raw materials is about
**Rp1.2 trillion, or 13% of pre-tax profit** — the second-largest sensitivity after tax, and rising in
importance as hand-rolled grows.

The company discloses no tonnage, no purchase price and no hedging, so this cannot be modelled from
the filings. It also sits at the table where the district reference price is set, which limits how
much of a price-taker it really is.

### 6. What Philip Morris charges

Group service charges and royalties were **Rp3.05 trillion in 2025 — 32% of pre-tax profit**, up from
15% in 2020. A 10% increase in those charges costs Rp305 billion, or 3.2% of profit. That is smaller
than the others in a single year, but it is the only one of the six that has moved in the same
direction every single year regardless of what the business did, and the rate at which it is set is
never disclosed.

The threshold applies to "the company **and its affiliates**", which is why a large group cannot simply
split production across subsidiaries to get the lower rate.

**The rest of the tax stack.** Excise is not the only levy. On top of it sits the *pajak rokok*, a
regional cigarette tax equal to **10% of the excise**, and value-added tax charged on a deemed basis at
an effective **9.9%** of the retail price since April 2022. On a 2026 Golongan I machine-made clove
cigarette sold at the legal minimum of Rp2,375: excise Rp1,231 + regional tax Rp123 + VAT about Rp235
= roughly **Rp1,589, or 67% of the price floor**. Sampoerna's own presentations reach the same
neighbourhood from the other direction: tax payments were about 69% of net revenue in both the first
half of 2021 and the first half of 2024.

**What the freeze actually did.** For 2025 the tariff was held and the *minimum retail price* raised
instead — machine-made clove Golongan I from Rp2,260 to Rp2,375 per stick, up 5.1%. That is a
regulator lifting the floor under prices without taking more tax: it hands the increase to the
manufacturer rather than the treasury, and it squeezes the cheap tier harder than the expensive one
(hand-rolled Golongan III's floor rose 18.6% against 5.1% for machine-made Golongan I). For 2026 both
the tariff and the floor were frozen outright, announced by the finance minister in October 2025 on
the stated grounds that another increase would cause layoffs and push more volume into the illegal
market.

This is the most favourable policy setting Sampoerna has faced since 2019, and it is why the shares
are up 40% on the year. It is also entirely revocable, annually, with no roadmap. The company has been
asking for a **multi-year excise policy tied to inflation** in its public presentations since 2021 and
has not got one.

## 8.2 Rp5.8 trillion the tax office is holding

The fourth-largest asset on Sampoerna's balance sheet is money it has already paid to the Indonesian
tax authorities and is trying to get back.

| Rp million | 2020 | 2021 | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Claims for tax refunds under objection or appeal | 880,263 | **4,713,793** | 4,627,898 | 4,716,944 | 5,216,682 | **5,757,106** |
| As % of total assets [calculated] | 1.8% | 8.9% | 8.4% | 8.5% | 9.6% | **11.2%** |
| As % of shareholders' equity [calculated] | 2.9% | 16.1% | 16.4% | 15.8% | 18.4% | **20.3%** |
| Additional tax expense accepted in the year | — | 1,800 | 54,400 | 66,900 | 214,000 | *not quantified* |

The balance is 87% of a full year's profit. Not one rupiah of it is provided against.

**How it got there.** Indonesian tax practice lets the authority assess back years within five years.
The FY2021 report — the only one that gives any case detail — lays out a decade of it: assessments for
2011, 2014, 2015 and 2016 fought through the objection process, the Tax Court and the Supreme Court,
with mixed results. Then, in **October 2021, the tax office assessed 2017, 2018 and 2019 all at once**,
for roughly Rp3.8 trillion across corporate income tax, VAT and withholding tax. Sampoerna paid the lot
in November 2021 — under Indonesian procedure you generally pay first and argue afterwards — and filed
objections in January 2022. Five years later, none of the three years is resolved.

**And then the disclosure stopped.** From the FY2022 report onward, every annual report reduces the
whole subject to one sentence: the Group "received a number of tax assessment letters for various
fiscal years; it received tax refunds and accepted some of the corrections." No years, no issues, no
amounts, no court stage. The governance chapter of the FY2025 report states there were no legal
disputes with a significant financial or operational impact, and the accounts state in every year that
there are **no significant contingent liabilities** — including the year in which a subsidiary had just
lost a Rp50 billion excise objection and appealed it.

**What the disputes are about is not disclosed anywhere, and could not be found.** Indonesian tax-court
decisions are published anonymised, so a name search cannot work. The generic pattern in Indonesian
practice for a multinational subsidiary of this type is a fight over the deductibility of royalties and
service fees paid to foreign affiliates — which is exactly what Sampoerna pays, at rates it does not
disclose, in amounts that have grown from Rp1.6 trillion to Rp3.0 trillion a year. That is a hypothesis
consistent with the facts, not a finding, and it is flagged as such.

**Two live signals.** In FY2025 the effective tax rate jumped from 23.5% to 29.9%, entirely on a
**Rp645 billion prior-period adjustment** — five times the prior year's. The FY2025 accounts say
additional tax expense from assessments was recorded but never say how much. And a brand-new permanent
difference labelled "**transfer within the Group**", Rp134 billion, appears in the FY2025 tax
reconciliation for the first time, unexplained.

For the first time in the period, Rp917 billion of the claims were reclassified as recoverable within
twelve months, and Rp429 billion of refunds were actually received in cash in 2025. Something is
moving. What, is not said.

## 8.3 The 7.5% float and the March 2028 deadline

**The register.** Philip Morris Indonesia holds 92.44%; the public holds 7.56%, spread over about
69,000 holders of whom 68,667 are individuals. No shareholder other than the parent has ever held more
than 5%. No director or commissioner owns a single share. There is no employee share scheme.

That 7.5% is not an accident. In October 2015 Sampoerna raised about US$1.4 billion in a rights issue
at Rp77,000 a share for the sole stated purpose of meeting the exchange's then-new minimum public float
of 7.5% by January 2016. The mechanism is worth noting because it is the template for what happens
next: **Philip Morris did not sell shares and take the cash.** Sampoerna issued new shares; the parent
sold its subscription rights to institutions and the proceeds went to **Sampoerna, not to Philip
Morris**. The parent's holding was diluted from 98.18% to exactly 92.50% — to the regulatory minimum and
not one share further. The only movement since was 71 million shares transferred in 2024, taking the
float from 7.50% to 7.56%.

**The rule has changed.** On 31 March 2026 the Indonesia Stock Exchange raised the minimum public float
from 7.5% to **15%**, with a phased deadline for existing issuers:

| Current float | First deadline | Final deadline |
|---|---|---|
| Below 12.5% (Sampoerna) | **12.5% by 31 March 2027** | **15% by 31 March 2028** |

As at 31 March 2026, 667 issuers were compliant and **281 were not. Sampoerna is on the published
non-compliant list**, at 7.5%.

**The arithmetic.** Going from 7.5% to 15% means moving about **8.7 billion shares** into public hands
— roughly **Rp6.4 trillion** at Rp735, which is close to a full year's net profit, in a stock whose
entire public float is currently worth about Rp6.5 trillion. The company would have to roughly double
the amount of its stock the market holds. The intermediate step to 12.5% by March 2027 is about 5.8
billion shares, or Rp4.3 trillion. Market-wide, the exchange's rule requires roughly 307 issuers to
restructure ownership, needing an estimated Rp227 trillion of cash to absorb.

**Nobody has said what will happen.** Neither the FY2024 nor the FY2025 annual report mentions the
free-float rules at all. Philip Morris's own filings contain no statement about its Sampoerna
shareholding or the Indonesian rule. Precedent exists for a dispensation — PT Adira Dinamika Multi
Finance was permitted 12.5% — so an exemption request is a live possibility. The mechanism most
expected across the market is a rights issue in which the controlling shareholder deliberately declines
to take up its rights, diluting itself into compliance.

**Why this matters to the minority.** Sampoerna already fell foul of the exchange's board rules once
on this issue: it was **demoted from the Main Board to the Development Board in May 2024** and only
promoted back in May 2025 — the exchange published no reason, but its board test for a company this
size is a 10% float against Sampoerna's 7.5%, so free float is the near-certain cause — a full year on the second-tier board that neither the FY2024 nor the
FY2025 annual report mentions anywhere. If the company again fails a listing requirement, that is the
kind of consequence. If instead the parent dilutes, the float doubles and the stock becomes eligible
for index funds and mandates it is currently too small and too illiquid to attract. The share price is
up 40% over the last year and daily volume is up about seven-fold. Some of the market appears to be taking a
view on which way this goes.

## 8.4 The 8.7 billion sticks that stopped being Sampoerna's

Buried in a footnote to a volume table is the most consequential accounting change of the period.

Philip Morris's 2025 results carry this sentence, and Sampoerna's annual report carries its own version
of it: *"2025 includes 8.7 billion units and 2024 includes 0.6 billion units of cigarette shipment
volume under an arrangement where PMI acts as brand management and fulfilment services agent."*

Alongside it: *"A change in our commercial model for the below tier-one cigarette segment in Indonesia
in the fourth quarter of 2024 resulted in lower net revenue growth, with no meaningful impact on
operating income."*

**What that means in plain words.** For part of its cheap-cigarette business, Sampoerna stopped being
the party that makes and sells the cigarettes and became a service provider to whoever now does. It
manages the brand and handles fulfilment for a fee. It no longer books the revenue, the cost, or the
excise on those cigarettes — but it still counts them in the volume it reports. In 2025 that was
**8.7 billion sticks, about 11% of reported volume**.

Three things follow, and the reader should hold all three.

**The volume decline is much worse than it looks.** Reported volume fell 1.8% in 2025. Strip out units
Sampoerna no longer sells as principal and the underlying decline is roughly **12%**.

**It probably explains the collapse in the merchandise lines.** Purchases of bought-in merchandise fell
from Rp14.4 trillion to Rp6.1 trillion and the cost of merchandise sold from Rp9.6 trillion to Rp1.9
trillion in 2025 — an Rp8 trillion swing, unexplained anywhere in the accounts. That is the size and
the timing one would expect if a bought-and-resold cigarette business became an agency business. The
connection is inference; no filing makes it.

**The most likely reason is the tax line.** A cigarette made by a company producing more than three
billion sticks a year pays Rp1,231. One made by a smaller, unaffiliated producer pays Rp746. A group
of Sampoerna's size cannot access the cheap tier on cigarettes it manufactures and sells itself,
because the threshold explicitly counts affiliates. It can, however, lend its brands and its
distribution to somebody who is under the line. Philip Morris's own words — "no meaningful impact on
operating income" — say the units were earning nothing as principal, which is consistent with a
Golongan I producer trying to compete in a Golongan II segment.

**Nobody names the counterparty.** Not Philip Morris, not Sampoerna. The one suggestive fact in the
filings is that in August 2024, three months before the change, a dormant Sampoerna subsidiary called
PT Harapan Maju Sentosa was renamed **PT Harapan Karya Sembilan** and re-registered from cigarette
manufacturing to **management consulting, advertising and brand management services**; its assets went
from Rp235 million to Rp82 billion over the following eighteen months. That is where to look. It is not
where any filing points.

# 09 — IMPORTANT TRANSACTIONS

**The 2015 rights issue (October 2015).** 269,723,076 new shares at Rp77,000, raising about US$1.4
billion, to meet the then-new 7.5% minimum public float. The parent sold its rights rather than
subscribing and diluted itself from 98.18% to exactly 92.50%. The Rp20.78 trillion rights-issue component of
additional paid-in capital on the balance sheet today is this transaction, and nothing else. It is the template for
whatever the company does about the 15% rule.

**The 25-for-1 stock split (June 2016).** Par value cut from Rp100 to Rp4, share count to
116,318,076,900. Unchanged since; there has been no corporate action on shares and no trading
suspension in any year 2021–2025.

**The loan to Philip Morris Finance SA (2022–2026).** In 2022 Sampoerna liquidated Rp12.8 trillion of
rupiah time deposits earning 2.25–5.00% and lent US$800 million to the group's Swiss treasury company
at LIBOR plus 27–52 basis points, realising 3.78–4.84%. At its peak this was 22.8% of total assets and
generated 81% of all the company's finance income in 2023. It has since been repaid down to US$240
million. Three features:

- The facility is **asymmetric** — Sampoerna may lend up to 100% of its net income, but may borrow only
  up to 50% of its equity, a cap halved in July 2022. It has never borrowed.
- The hedging has been **persistently short of the exposure**. Foreign-exchange swaps covered US$750
  million against an US$800 million loan in 2022, US$610 million against US$735 million in 2023, and
  only **US$215 million against US$445 million in 2024** — leaving US$230 million unhedged in a year
  the rupiah weakened.
- The pricing basis has been amended three times as benchmark rates were retired (LIBOR to BSBY in
  2023, BSBY to SOFR in 2024), and the facility now runs to **30 June 2033**. The FY2025 restatement
  contains a bilingual discrepancy: the Indonesian text reads "4 basis points" where the English reads
  "40 basis points" for twelve-month advances.

**The distribution agreement rewrite (1 January 2023).** The 2009 sole-distribution agreement with the
parent, extended in 2015 to run to February 2025, was terminated 26 months early and replaced with a
five-year auto-renewing agreement under which Sampoerna is paid "a defined percentage of earnings
before tax". The percentage is not disclosed. The FY2023 annual report's front matter describes the
old arrangement in the present tense and never mentions the change.

**The Taman Dayu disposal (27 December 2023).** Sampoerna sold PT Taman Dayu and PT Golf Taman Dayu —
a property development and golf course in Pasuruan, a legacy of the family era — to two unaffiliated
buyers for **Rp723 billion**, booking a **Rp477 billion gain**. That gain is most of the FY2023 jump in
other income from Rp258 billion to Rp674 billion, and therefore a meaningful part of what made FY2023
the best year of the period. The company's front matter gives no price, no buyer and no gain figure;
all three come from the notes.

**The Karawang smoke-free plant (2021–2022).** Announced at US$166.1 million in November 2021, delivered
at "more than US$186 million", and now described as carrying more than US$330 million of cumulative
investment. Philip Morris's first smoke-free facility in Southeast Asia and one of only two of its
advanced research laboratories worldwide. It is the reason capital spending quadrupled in 2022 and the
reason exports to group companies rose tenfold.

**The FY2024 dividend (May 2024).** Rp8.06 trillion, Rp69.3 a share, paid out of FY2023 earnings —
**156% of the operating cash flow Sampoerna generated that year.** The company funded it partly by
calling in Rp4.2 trillion of the loan to its parent's treasury.

**The commercial-model change (Q4 2024).** Covered in §8.4.

# 10 — RED FLAGS

Ranked by how much each would change a view of the company.

**1. The company discloses no volumes, no market share, no tariff, no capacity — nothing operational —
in its audited accounts.** In six years of financial statements there is not one cigarette, not one
tariff, not one retail price, not one factory capacity. The excise note, covering the line that is
two-thirds of costs, is a single sentence in all six years. Every operating number in this report comes
from presentation slides, the parent's US filings, or government decrees. A shareholder relying on the
audited accounts alone cannot tell whether the business sold more or fewer cigarettes.

**2. The share-of-market series has been restated twice, both times upward, both times without
explanation.** The most recent restatement cut the estimated size of the Indonesian market by 10%,
which lifted reported share from 27.4% to 30.5% on identical volume. The company adopted the new basis
and restated prior years. Anyone comparing "share was 33% in 2018, 30.7% in 2025" is comparing two
different measurements. Volume is the honest series, and volume is down 22%.

**3. Reported volume includes 8.7 billion cigarettes that Sampoerna does not sell.** Underlying volume
fell roughly 12% in 2025, not 1.8%. The counterparty is not named by anyone.

**4. Rp5.8 trillion of tax is in dispute — 20% of equity — with no case detail since 2021 and no
provision.** In the same reports the company states there are no significant contingent liabilities and
no legal disputes with significant impact. The FY2025 tax charge contains a Rp645 billion prior-period
adjustment and an unquantified assessment expense.

**5. Every governance committee contains people who report to the board it oversees.** The head of tax
sits on the audit committee and was previously a director of the controlling shareholder; the head of
HR sits on the remuneration committee; the head of internal audit sits on the risk committee. The
"independent" commissioner spent 27 years as a Philip Morris lawyer and led the acquisition of
Sampoerna. Three commissioners are former Presidents Director of the company. Board succession runs
explicitly through the parent's global talent process.

**6. What the parent charges is growing much faster than what the company earns, and the rate is
secret.** Group service charges and royalties have gone from 15% to 32% of pre-tax profit in five
years. Neither the royalty percentage nor the cost-plus mark-up is disclosed in any year. These are the
two numbers that determine how much of an Indonesian business's profit stays in Indonesia, and they are
the two numbers most likely to be at issue in the Rp5.8 trillion tax dispute.

**7. Directors' pay has not been to a shareholder vote since 2012.** The 2012 annual meeting delegated
it to the commissioners "for 2012 and subsequent financial years until determined otherwise". Rp153
billion was paid to eight directors in FY2025 — about Rp19 billion each — with no individual figure and
no bonus formula. The commissioners who set it are, with one exception, career Philip Morris people.

**8. The exchange's board demotion is not mentioned in any annual report.** Sampoerna spent a year on
the Development Board, from May 2024 to May 2025, almost certainly on free float. Neither the FY2024
nor the FY2025 report acknowledges it, and neither mentions the new 15% rule that it is currently in
breach of.

**9. Four unexplained pension credits, one of them very large.** A negative past-service cost of
**Rp538 billion** turned FY2021's staff-benefit charge into a credit — the largest single non-operating
support to that year's profit — and further credits appear in 2022, 2024 and 2025. None is explained in
any filing. The actuary was changed in the same year as the large one.

**10. Recurring restatements that are never flagged as restatements.** The FY2022 report silently
changed FY2021's gross profit by Rp106 billion and operating income by Rp217 billion; the FY2023 report
re-cut FY2022's cost lines; the FY2022 shareholder table appears to be the FY2021 table copied over,
contradicting its own facing page by 349 million shares; the FY2024 share register totals 210 shares
short. Individually trivial; together they say the reports are not carefully checked.

**11. Two disclosures that appeared once and vanished.** The FY2021 report gave a full share-by-category
breakdown of the market and a full ten-year tax-dispute history. Neither appears in any later report.
Both disappeared in the years the underlying position deteriorated.

**12. A Rp550 billion bond investment with no named issuer.** New in 2025, maturing 2030 and 2032,
valued using unobservable inputs, in a company that has never held a bond. And a new balance-sheet line,
"other non-current liabilities" of Rp241 billion, appearing from nil with no note reference at all.

# MISSING DATA AND OPEN ITEMS

Ranked by how much each would change the analysis if it surfaced.

**1. The royalty rate and the service mark-up.** Two percentages set the price on roughly Rp11 trillion
a year of transactions with the parent and determine how much Indonesian profit stays in Indonesia.
Never disclosed in any year. They would most likely appear in a Tax Court decision, or in an OJK
related-party disclosure if one were required. They would decide whether the Rp5.8 trillion tax dispute
is a timing issue or a structural one.

**2. What the tax disputes are actually about.** Rp5.8 trillion, 20% of equity, no case detail since
the FY2021 report. Indonesian tax-court rulings are published anonymised, so a name search cannot find
them; the realistic sources are the FY2021 report's own history extended forward, an OJK material-
information disclosure, or the company answering the question at a public expose.

**3. Volume by category, in sticks.** Never disclosed by anyone, in any year, in any document. Without
it, no per-category margin, no price-versus-volume decomposition and no proper mix analysis is
possible. It would most likely come from a Nielsen subscription or from the customs directorate's
production data by tariff category.

**4. The counterparty and economics of the 8.7 billion agency sticks.** Neither Philip Morris nor
Sampoerna names it. This decides whether 11% of reported volume is a clever tax structure, a disposal,
or an accounting reclassification. Likely sources: the FY2026 related-party note, an OJK disclosure, or
Indonesian trade press.

**5. What Sampoerna intends to do about the 15% free-float rule.** No statement from the company, the
parent, or the exchange about this specific issuer. It is a Rp6.4 trillion question with a March 2027
first deadline. It would come from an OJK disclosure, an extraordinary general meeting notice, or the
2027 annual meeting materials.

**6. The percentage of pre-tax earnings paid under the post-2023 distribution agreement.** The entire
economics of distributing Marlboro. Not disclosed. Would appear only if the agreement itself were filed.

**7. Factory capacity and utilisation.** Not disclosed for any of the nine plants in any year, including
the US$330 million smoke-free facility. Without it there is no way to judge whether the two new
hand-rolling plants of 2024 were needed or whether the machine-made plants are running half empty.

**8. The identity, terms and volumes of the 43 contract hand-rolling partners.** They employ more than
50,000 people making a third of the company's revenue, and are paid about Rp3.7 trillion a year. Not one
is named. This is the single largest undocumented dependency in the business.

**9. The composition of "other income" and "other expenses".** No note supports either line. Other
expenses quadrupled to Rp604 billion in 2025 with no explanation, in a year when net profit was flat.

**10. Explanation of the pension past-service credits**, particularly the Rp538 billion in FY2021.

**11. FY2019 and FY2020 annual reports.** Not in hand. FY2020 figures here come from the FY2021 report's
comparative column, and FY2019 not at all — so the pre-Covid baseline, the FY2019 dividend and the
share-price history for 2018–2019 are all second-hand or absent.

**12. Philip Morris's Form 10-K management discussion and its goodwill note.** The narrative sections
could not be retrieved in full. They would carry PMI's own account of the Indonesian business, the
carrying value of the 2005 acquisition, and whether it has ever been impairment-tested.

**13. Board remuneration for FY2021–FY2024.** Only FY2025 is disclosed in the material read, and only as
board-level aggregates.

**14. National tobacco and clove production and price series after 2021.** The official Ministry of
Agriculture outlooks stop there; only scattered regional farmgate quotes exist since.

**15. Manufacturer-level market share for Indonesia.** No public source gives Sampoerna against Gudang
Garam, Djarum, Nojorono and Bentoel for any recent year. Nielsen holds it behind a subscription.
