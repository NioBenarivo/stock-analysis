Indonesia's largest poultry integrator: poultry feed, day-old chicks (DOC), live broiler,
and processed chicken, sold through ~10 feed mills, 140 breeding farms, 56 hatcheries,
30 slaughterhouses and a >12,500-farmer contract network across 22 provinces. IDX: CPIN.

Thailand). Period covered: FY2021–FY2025 audited, 1H2026 from press; 1991 IPO prospectus
for pre-history. Sources in hand: five annual reports (FY2021–FY2025), the Feb-1991 IPO
prospectus (OCR), two public-expose decks (2023, 2025), web research. Figures in Rp

## THE ONE THING TO UNDERSTAND FIRST

CPIN is really two companies wearing one income statement. The first is a feed and
genetics oligopolist: the Feed segment sold Rp52.2tn gross in FY2025, earned Rp3.3–4.6tn
in every year of the cycle, and never once lost money — but only Rp21.0tn of
that feed ever left the group. The second is a giant customer for the first: the Broiler
segment, which buys most of the feed and most of the DOC internally, sells commodity live
birds into a chronically oversupplied market, and swings from a Rp1.27tn loss (FY2021) to
a Rp3.35tn profit (FY2025) as farmgate prices move. In FY2025, 41.8% of gross segment
sales [calculated] were eliminated on consolidation; 59.8% of feed output and 63.7% of
DOC output stayed inside the group [calculated]. The internal transfer price decides
which segment shows the profit, so segment lines are bookkeeping, not businesses — the
real unit is the integrated bird, and the real earnings driver is the spread between
(corn + soybean meal) and (live-bird + processed-chicken prices), levered by whatever
share of the downstream loss the government's supply interventions remove.

Three structural facts sit on top of that machine, all evidenced below. First, the
company pays a royalty to Nugen Bioscience International Pte. Ltd. (Singapore), an entity
under common CP-family control, that has run at 1.01–1.11% of net sales every year
(FY2025: Rp715bn, ~31% of G&A; cumulative FY2021–25: Rp3.25tn ≈ 17.9% of the period's
net profit [calculated]) — and no filing since at least 2009 has disclosed the rate; the
1991 prospectus shows the ancestor fee was an explicit 0.5% of net sales (§8.2). Second,
CPIN discloses no volumes, no prices, no capacities and no utilisation, in any segment,
in any year since the 1991 prospectus — every physical number in this report comes from
expose decks, the prospectus, or outside sources (§8.3). Third, Permentan 10/2024 forces
integrators to allocate ≥50% of broiler DOC production to independent farmers from
1 January 2027; CPIN currently keeps 63.7% internally, so the rule attacks the exact
mechanism — internal DOC absorption — that the vertical model runs on (§8.4).

## 1.3 History — recap

The company has never diversified away from the bird; it has integrated along it. Feed
only at IPO (with 30% national share already), then hatching and farming through CPJF,
then processing (Golden Fiesta/Fiesta/Champ/Okey brands), then retail (Prima Freshmart),
with the only sideways moves being small: packaging, animal pharma, and the Singa Mas
beverage line. Two patterns repeat across 50 years. Capacity and businesses are built or
bought *inside the family perimeter first* and moved into the listed entity later, at
the family's price (CPJF 1990, the 2016 breeding flock, the 2021 brand pushdowns, the
2023 slaughterhouse consolidation). And the IP/technical layer has always sat offshore
with the family, charging the listed company a fee that survived every restructuring —
Bangkok Feedmill 1988 → CP Holding 1990 → CP International Group 2009 → Nugen Bioscience
Singapore 2017.

## 1.4 Owner's character

Facts a reader can weigh; the judgement is left to the reader.

- Disclosure minimalism is a 30-year policy, not an accident: the 1991 prospectus printed
  capacity, tonnage, market share and even affiliate-sales percentages; no annual report
  since discloses a single volume, price or capacity number (§8.3). Expose decks give
  facility counts only.
- The board is small, old, long-tenured and unpaid in shares: 6 directors unchanged
  2018–2025, commissioners 3→4 (2023); every officer holds zero shares; remuneration is
  modest against the size of the company (directors Rp85.7bn total FY2025) — and against
  the Rp715bn royalty leaving for Singapore the same year.
- Nobody trains: every governance body reports "no training attended" in 2021, 2022 and
  2023 — a verbatim recurring line. The Head of Internal Audit holds no internal-audit
  certification (stated as fact in the AR).
- The Audit Committee met 43 times in 2023 (35 in 2022) — an extraordinary cadence that
  coincides with the transfer-pricing documentation engagement and the committee's full
  membership turnover (§10.5). All three 2023-appointed members are CPIN retirees
  (VP Financial Controller 1976–2008; AVP Chain Restaurant 1977–2009; Tax Department
  Manager 1983–2008) — "independent" chairs with 30-year house careers.
- Family fingerprints on the board are Tjoe, not Jiaravanon: President Commissioner Hadi
  Gunawan Tjoe and Director Ferdiansyah Gunawan Tjoe are disclosed as affiliated with
  each other. No Jiaravanon sits on either board; the family governs through the 55.53%
  holdco and the offshore IP chain.
- President Director Tjiu Thomas Effendy is concurrently President Commissioner of PT
  BISI International Tbk — the sister company whose corn "farmer partnership" the
  Commissioners' report credits for feed-procurement efficiency, while the RPT note
  records only Rp5–8bn/yr of direct BISI purchases. The economic content of that
  partnership is not visible in the filings.
- HSBC's bilateral facility requires the Jiaravanon family to remain ≥51% shareholder —
  the banks price the family, not the board.

## 1.5 RPT

The related-party system has three tiers, in descending size:

**Tier 1 — the royalty (Rp715bn in FY2025).** To Nugen Bioscience International Pte.
Ltd., Singapore, under common control. Full series and lineage in §8.2. It is the
largest single disclosed related-party flow, every year, and its pricing basis has never
been disclosed.

**Tier 2 — recurring trade.** Purchases from related parties run Rp2.1–3.3tn/yr
(FY2025: Rp3,280,856m ≈ 5.6% of COGS [calculated]); the largest counterparty every year
is PT SHS International, followed by PT Satria Multi Sukses, PT Indovetraco Makmur Abadi
and PT Nugen Bioscience *Indonesia* (a local trading entity, distinct from the Singapore
royalty payee). Related-party **sales** are trivial — 0.20–0.32% of revenue — the mirror
image of 1991, when 24–38% of sales went to affiliates. The modern group buys from the
family perimeter; it no longer sells into it.

## 2.3 Supply chain and purchase — what the business buys and owns

**Inputs.** Feed raw materials are the base of the whole cost pyramid: corn (bought
domestically — 367,000 tons from 36,000 ha of partner farmers in FY2024, the only
purchased-volume number in any filing; the BISI partnership supplies seed to those
farmers) and imported soybean meal, plus additives. Corn imports have been effectively
closed to feed millers for most of the period (§04), so domestic corn price is the
binding input. Genetics enter as imported grandparent stock under government quota;
CPIN's Arbor Acres Indonesia subsidiary ties it to the Aviagen breed line.

**Fixed assets: Rp17,362,597m net = 37.9% of total assets (FY2025).** The asset base is
already built: net fixed assets have been flat around Rp16.9–17.7tn since FY2022 while
sales grew 24%. Capex tells the cycle story:

FY2021–22 expansion, FY2023–24 retrenchment ("invest only as needed", AR2023), then a
FY2025 re-acceleration aimed at feed and DOC — not broiler, not processing.
Construction-in-progress confirms the direction: feed-mill buildings under construction
Rp449,945m (2023) → 978,468 (2024) → **1,809,770 (2025)** — the South Sulawesi mill —
while henhouse CIP ran down 1,286,654 → 303,651. The reinvestment is going into the
segment that never loses money and into DOC capacity (parent-stock housing, 2025 plan),
positioning for MBG-era feed and chick demand rather than more own-farm live-bird
exposure.

**Working capital is the other asset.** Inventories Rp11,406,228m + biological assets
Rp5,242,332m = 36.3% of total assets (FY2025). Inventory days ~71 [calculated] — corn
and SBM stocks are the buffer against the import-policy lottery; the FY2025 inventory
jump (+21.7%) against only +2.2% COGS growth reads as deliberate raw-material
stockpiling.

## 2.5 Distribute

Feed's external 40% goes to independent farmers through poultry shops and the plasma
network; DOC's external 36% to independent broiler growers; live birds into the daily
wet market via brokers (price-taker, no contracts disclosed); processed chicken through
modern trade, >2,400 own Prima Freshmart stores, and food service. No customer exceeds
10% of revenue in any year (no concentration disclosure at all — [not disclosed]); no
offtake contracts, no export franchise. ASP versus any benchmark cannot be computed —
no volumes (§8.3).

Two distribution facts move opposite to the growth narrative and are never explained
(§10.4): retail stores 3,616 (early-2024 deck) → 2,482 (FY2024 deck) and plasma farmers
>15,000 → >12,500 over the same interval; CPIN's own printed processed-chicken market
share fell 57% (FY2022) → 51% (FY2023) → 47% (FY2024) → 47% (FY2025).

regulated utility inside the group. Broiler is
the cycle: −1.27tn → −316bn → −420bn → +2.03tn → +3.35tn — a Rp4.62tn swing across the

recovery in group operating profit over those two years [calculated]. But since ~60% of feed and ~64–73% of

"feed profit" and "broiler loss" is a management choice. In the loss years, feed's
steady margin was partly a transfer-priced claim on its own downstream segment; a
different transfer price would have shown a smaller feed profit and a smaller broiler
loss with identical group results. Segment results are where profit is *booked*, not
where it is *made* — the group P&L is the only economically real statement, and it is
priced off external corn, SBM, live-bird, DOC and processed-chicken prices.

Two second-order signals in the FY2025 note: feed's internal share fell 67.5% → 59.8%
and external feed sales grew 27.6% — CPIN selling more feed to the independent farmers
whose share Permentan 10/2024 will enlarge (§8.4); and processed chicken swung from a
Rp82bn loss to a Rp862bn profit on *falling* revenue, an admission that FY2024's +19%
processed growth was bought with unprofitable volume (§10.4).

Demand grows structurally (urbanisation, protein substitution — chicken is the cheapest

**MBG (Makan Bergizi Gratis) is the demand event of the period.** The free
nutritious-meal programme reached 63.13m beneficiaries with Rp101.1tn disbursed in
1H2026 (target cut from 82.9m on duplicate-ID cleanup; headline budget ~Rp228tn). An
industry (HKTI) proposal — advocacy, not policy — would have MBG absorb 905,268 t of
chicken and eggs worth Rp31.68tn in H2 2026 alone. Even without the proposal, a daily
protein meal for ~63m schoolchildren is a state-funded floor under exactly the products
CPIN sells (eggs, chicken, processed portions), and the government explicitly linked
the 2026 GPS import-quota expansion to MBG demand. CPIN's filings never mention MBG —
not once in the FY2024 or FY2025 annual report (§8.3) — so the read-through is entirely
external: the 1H2026 revenue acceleration (+19.2% YoY vs +4.8% in FY2025) is the first
result period with MBG at scale.

Feed demand is derived demand: every extra bird is ~4 kg of feed [industry standard,
not company-disclosed], and MBG-driven layer/broiler expansion by independent farmers
lands on the feed segment CPIN is now deliberately re-externalising (§2.6, §8.4).

# 04 — SUPPLY

The supply side is administered, and that administration is the industry's real margin
mechanism.

**The structural condition is oversupply.** National DOC production ≈3.5bn chicks vs
≈3.2bn demand (2025) — a ~300m chick structural surplus. The state manages it downward
with blunt tools: mandated culling of parent stock and hatching-egg destruction
(recurring since 2019; a mid-2025 GP/PS culling round is what tightened supply into the
FY2025 margin recovery), and moral-suasion price floors. When the tools lag, prices
crash below cost: June 2026 farmgate live-bird ran Rp15,000–16,000/kg against
Rp21,000–22,000/kg production cost — farmers losing Rp5–7k per bird, the classic
signature of the cycle. Every such crash transfers losses to independent farmers and,
through CPIN's own broiler segment, to integrators — but integrators recover on feed
sold to whoever restocks. CPIN's broiler-loss years (FY2021–23) are the oversupply
years; the FY2024–25 profit years are the managed-supply years.

**GPS import quota is the throttle valve.** All broiler genetics enter as imported
grandparent stock under annual quota: 578,000 birds (2025) → **800,000 (2026, +38%)**
across 27 breeding companies — the government expanding the pipeline for MBG. Inside
that sits a US-Indonesia trade-deal commitment to buy 580,000 GPS from the US
(~US$17–20m; the exact overlap with the 800k national quota is unreconciled in public
sources). Quota up = 15–18 months later, chick supply up: today's demand policy is
2027's oversupply risk, arriving in the same year as Permentan 10/2024 (§8.4).

**Corn policy sets the cost floor.** Feed millers' corn imports have been banned or
near-zero through the period; domestic corn ran Rp6,935/kg vs the Rp5,800 HAP ceiling
in Feb 2026 (+19.6%), with a reported full-year 2026 import ban (unverified lead) and
national production ~16.1–16.6m t (2025, two conflicting official figures). Integrators
with dryers, silos and partner-farm networks (CPIN: 10 mills + 3 standalone dryer/
storage sites, 36,000 ha partner farmers) hold structural advantage over small millers
in a closed corn market.

**Competitive supply structure.** CPIN is the largest integrator, JPFA the clear
second, then a long tail:

| FY2025 | Revenue | Net income | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| CPIN | Rp70.70tn | Rp5.64tn | feed share ~31–32% (single undated source) |
| JPFA | Rp60.71tn (+8.8%) | Rp4.00tn (+32.6%) | broiler Rp24.5tn, feed Rp15.8tn, processing Rp10.7tn, DOC Rp3.5tn |

The two leaders together are roughly half the modern sector; the fringe is thousands of
independent farmers who bear the price cycle first. Danantara's reported Rp20tn
state poultry plan (18,000 GPS initial phase, 30 provinces) would, if real, add a
state-owned supplier to the same pool — a lead, not yet a verified programme.
# 05 — KEY VARIABLES

Ranked by earnings leverage. Weights restate their anchors; sensitivities are shown
line-by-line, all [calculated] unless sourced.

**1. Domestic corn price.** Raw materials are Rp48,226,002m = 68.2% of FY2025 revenue.
Corn is roughly half of a broiler-feed formula [industry standard — not disclosed by
CPIN]. If corn ≈ 50% of raw-material spend, then a 10% corn move ≈ Rp2.4tn of COGS ≈
31% of FY2025 pre-tax profit before any pass-through. Feed selling prices do
re-price with a lag (the FY2023 GM trough followed by FY2024–25 recovery is exactly
that lag), so the *net* sensitivity is a one-to-three-quarter margin squeeze, not a
permanent loss — but in the squeeze quarters the broiler segment eats it twice, as
feed cost and as weak bird prices. Range over the period: ~Rp4,000/kg (2021 normal) to
~Rp8,000+/kg (2023 El Niño) to Rp6,935/kg vs Rp5,800 HAP (Feb 2026).

**2. Farmgate live-bird price vs cost.** Broiler is 48% of external sales and 100% of
the earnings swing: segment result −Rp420,300m (FY2023) → +Rp3,349,806m (FY2025), a
Rp3.77tn delta on a roughly flat revenue base — pure price/spread. Reference points:
production cost Rp21–22k/kg vs farmgate Rp15–16k/kg (June 2026 crash); the government
reference band and culling response are what pull it back. There is no hedge; CPIN is
long the spread on every bird.

**3. SBM and the rupiah.** The second feed input is imported soybean meal, paid in USD.
CPIN's disclosed net monetary FX position is small (net asset Rp526,921m FY2024) and
debt is mostly rupiah, so the FX exposure is a cost-of-goods exposure, not a
balance-sheet one — it arrives as feed-margin pressure with the same lagged
pass-through as corn. Commodity futures via Phillip Futures (Singapore) exist but are
small and not hedge-accounted (trading balance Rp355,147m FY2022).

**4. DOC price.** DOC external sales are only 4.9% of revenue but the segment result
swung −Rp301,560m → +Rp648,594m (FY2023→FY2025), and DOC price is the *first* place
supply management shows up (culling acts on eggs and parent stock). The +38.1% DOC
revenue growth in FY2025 at rising margin is the cleanest evidence of the 2025
supply-side tightening.

**5. Government supply management (culling orders, GPS quota).** Not a price but the
regime that sets prices 2 and 4. Quota series: 578,000 GPS (2025) → 800,000 (2026).
The 15–18 month biological lag [industry standard] means the 2026 quota expansion is
the 2027–28 supply. Watch quota decisions and culling instructions the way an oil
analyst watches OPEC.

**6. MBG offtake.** 63m beneficiaries, Rp101tn disbursed 1H2026; the first
demand-side floor the industry has ever had. Not in any CPIN filing; visible only in
the 1H2026 acceleration (+19.2% revenue, +95% net income YoY).

**7. Permentan 10/2024 (from 1 Jan 2027).** ≥50% of broiler DOC to independents vs
CPIN's current 63.7% internal retention — a forced re-plumbing of the model's core
flow, quantified in §8.4.

**8. The royalty.** A fixed ~1.01–1.11%-of-sales related-party drag, every year,
uncorrelated with profit: Rp715,039m in FY2025 = 9.3% of pre-tax profit; in the FY2023
trough it was 21.5% of pre-tax profit [both calculated]. It functions as a senior
claim on revenue ahead of minority shareholders (§8.2).

# 06 — VICISSITUDES

The company's own 50-year record says the business is structurally thin and cyclically
violent, and that the family's response to every squeeze is the same: integrate
further, keep capacity, let the cycle kill the fringe.

- **1988–1990 (prospectus era):** gross margin 16–17%, net margin 0.4–0.6% in
  1988–89 jumping to 4.9% in 1H1990 — even pre-IPO, profitability was a
  feed-conversion spread, not a brand margin.
- **FY2020–FY2021 (COVID + input inflation):** revenue +21.6% (FY2021) but gross
  margin fell to 15.7% and the broiler segment swung to a Rp1.27tn loss as demand
  (hotels, restaurants, catering) collapsed while corn and SBM rose. DOC profited
  (Rp1.56tn) on restocking. Group net profit held at Rp3.62tn because feed and DOC
  offset broiler.
- **FY2022 (Ukraine):** SBM spiked; GM 14.3%; broiler lost Rp316bn; net profit fell
  19% to Rp2.93tn. Net debt peaked at Rp6.9tn [calculated] as working capital and
  capex were debt-funded; FY2022 dividend was skipped outright (§10.2).
- **FY2023 (El Niño):** domestic corn ~Rp8,000/kg; GM bottomed at 13.4%; broiler
  −Rp420bn and DOC −Rp302bn simultaneously — the only year both bird segments lost
  money; net profit troughed at Rp2.32tn (net margin 3.8%). Capex was cut in half
  and the language turned to "invest only as needed."
- **FY2024–FY2025 (managed recovery):** culling tightened supply, corn eased, MBG
  arrived; GM 15.4% → 17.6%; broiler +Rp2.03tn → +Rp3.35tn; net profit Rp3.71tn →
  a record Rp5.64tn; net debt run down to Rp1.8tn [calculated]. Processed chicken
  took its turn as the problem child (−Rp82bn in FY2024) — cycle rotation, not cycle
  escape.
- **1H2026:** revenue +19.2% YoY (Rp36.4tn or Rp39.4tn — two press figures,
  unreconciled), net income ≈Rp3.7tn, +95% — on track for the best year in company
  history, at the same moment farmgate prices crashed below independent farmers'
  cost (June 2026). The integrator wins both halves of the cycle: margin when supply
  is managed, share when it is not.

The through-cycle constants: feed never lost money; the dividend was paid in every
year except FY2022's skip; the royalty was paid in full in all of them.

## 8.4 Permentan 10/2024 — the arithmetic of forced disintegration

The regulation (fetched in full from the ministry's legal database): from 1 January
2027, broiler-DOC producers must allocate a **minimum 50% of DOC final-stock
production** to independent farmers, cooperatives and smallholders (Pasal 23); layer
DOC carries an 88% minimum (Pasal 24).

CPIN's current flow (FY2025, Note 36): DOC total segment sales Rp9,521,649m, of which
internal Rp6,066,271m = 63.7% — external allocation 36.3%, against a 50% floor. On
FY2025 proportions, compliance means shifting ~Rp1.3tn of DOC sales [calculated:
(0.50−0.363) × 9,521,649] from internal supply to external customers — roughly 14
points of DOC output. The own-broiler operation then either shrinks by the same chick
count, buys DOC from competitors (paying away the integration margin), or the group
grows total DOC capacity so that 50% external still covers internal needs — which is
exactly what the FY2025 capex mix (DOC capex ×4.4 YoY to Rp610,632m; parent-stock
housing plan) and the doubled GPS quota make possible. The FY2025 pivot to external
feed sales (+27.6%) reads the same way: if the rule stands, the future CPIN sells more
inputs to independents and owns relatively less of the bird — higher-quality earnings
(feed never loses money), lower integration capture.

Open questions the filings ignore (CPIN has never mentioned the regulation): whether
"production" is measured per entity or per group (CPIN's DOC sits in multiple
subsidiaries), enforcement mechanics, and whether the 2027 date slips — Indonesian
agri-regulation deadlines often do. Watch item, not yet a booked cost.

# 09 — IMPORTANT TRANSACTIONS

## 9.1 The 1990–91 pre-IPO package

Everything about the modern structure was set in the 18 months before listing: 80% of
CPJF (the farming leg) swapped in; paid-in capital lifted Rp3.65bn → Rp50bn partly by
capitalising a property revaluation; the par value un-pegged from US$100; the
technical fee converted from fixed US$1.5m/yr to 0.5% of net sales — a change that
initially *cut* the fee (0.5% of 1990's annualised revenue ≈ US$0.5–0.6m vs the fixed
US$1.5m [calculated]) but scaled with every rupiah of growth thereafter.
The float itself was 4.76% new money at Rp5,100.

## 9.2 The 2016 breeding-flock takeover (common control)

CPIN took over the *ayam pembibit* (breeding-flock) business from parent PT Charoen
Pokphand Indonesia Group, 30 June 2016. Consideration exceeded the net carrying value
by Rp169,560m, booked as a debit in additional paid-in capital under PSAK 38 — the
listed company paid the family a premium for assets the family controlled on both
sides of the table, and the premium bypassed the P&L entirely. It is the largest
single item in the negative APIC balance (§7.3).

## 9.3 The 2017 royalty novation

CP International Group → Nugen Bioscience International (Singapore), 1 Jan 2017,
"terms remain effective" — terms never shown. See §8.2.

## 9.4 The 2021 pushdowns

September 2021: land, buildings, machinery and **12 brands** transferred into PT
Primafood International, and 3 brands into PT Singa Mas Indonesia — both consolidated
subsidiaries, so no P&L effect, but no fairness opinion was obtained for either
(contrast the same year's Rp183,229m land purchase from PT Sumber Budi Asih — outside
the consolidation perimeter, fairness opinion obtained). The asymmetry is the tell for
how the group treats intra-perimeter vs perimeter-crossing deals.

## 9.5 The 2023 land purchase from the President Commissioner

8 May 2023: CPIN bought 82,922 m² in Sukabumi from **Hadi Gunawan Tjoe personally** —
its own President Commissioner — for Rp45,607.1m (≈Rp550k/m² [calculated]). KJPP Toto
Suharto fairness opinion: fair; OJK-disclosed under POJK 42/2020. Procedurally clean;
still a listed company buying land from the individual who chairs its supervisory
board.

## 9.6 The 2023 slaughterhouse consolidation

31 Jul 2023 (English text says 1 July — the filing's own discrepancy): poultry
slaughterhouses in 10 locations across Kalimantan, Sulawesi, Sumatra and Java,
Rp374,255.6m, bought from six subsidiaries described as "indirectly owned 100%" (the
subsidiary table shows 99.94–99.99%). Intra-group tidying of the RPHU network into the
parent — but unlike 9.5, **no independent appraiser is named** for a deal 8× larger.
Same year, audit fees quintupled and included transfer-pricing documentation (§10.5).

## 9.7 Dividends as a family cash channel — the FY2022–FY2023 sequence

Skip (AGM May 2023: 100% retained) → unexplained Rp100/share in November 2023 →
retroactive reframing as an FY2023 interim totalling a 91.9% payout. Detail in §10.2.
At 55.53%, every Rp1tn of dividend moves Rp555bn to the family holdco — across
FY2021–25 plus the 2026 payment, Rp5.81tn of the Rp10.46tn paid [calculated].

## 9.8 The 2025 perimeter cleanup

~15 dormant farming shells (mostly layer-farming and trading entities with \<Rp10bn
assets) liquidated during FY2025 — the first visible simplification of a 60-entity
structure that PSAK consolidation had made costless to maintain. Routine, but it
shrinks the surface area through which perimeter deals (9.4, 9.6) can move.

# 10 — RED FLAG

## 10.1 A ~Rp715bn/yr related-party fee with no disclosed basis

The Nugen royalty (§8.2): 1.0–1.1% of net sales, every year, to a family entity, rate
undisclosed since 2009, implied rate double the last disclosed one (0.5%, 1990),
never mentioned in the MD&A in absolute terms. For scale: FY2023 royalty = 21.5% of
pre-tax profit [calculated]. Nothing else in the filings comes close as a governance
issue.

## 10.2 The November 2023 dividend that has no declared source

The 22 May 2023 AGM resolved to retain 100% of FY2022 profit — zero dividend — five
months after which Rp1,639,800m (Rp100/share) was paid (29 Nov 2023). AR2024 then
described FY2023's payout as "Rp130/share, 91.94%" by folding the November payment in
as an interim. Possible legitimate mechanics exist (an interim declared by directors
against FY2023 running profit), but no filing in hand states the resolution, date or
legal basis of the November declaration — a Rp1.6tn distribution with a retroactive
explanation, paid in the trough year while capex was being cut and ST debt peaked at
Rp7.4tn.

## 10.3 Comparatives that move between reports

AR2023's restated FY2022 comparatives disagree with AR2022's own audited FY2022
figures — current assets Rp17.76tn vs 18.03tn, trade receivables 1.55tn vs 1.82tn,
non-current assets 22.08tn vs 21.82tn — with no restatement note anywhere. The FY2019
and FY2020 highlights columns are likewise flagged "restated," unexplained, in AR2021
and AR2022. Individually small; as a pattern, comparatives at this company are soft,
and any multi-year series should anchor on each year's own audited statements (as this
report's §07 does).

## 10.4 The downstream is quietly shrinking while the narrative says growth

Between the two expose decks (early-2024 → FY2024): retail stores 3,616 → 2,482
(−31%), plasma farmers >15,000 → >12,500, three retail brands dropped. Same period:
processed-chicken share 57% → 47% over three reports, FY2024 processed segment loss of
Rp81,829m on +19% revenue, then FY2025 processed revenue −8.5%. The consistent story
underneath: the FY2022–23 downstream push (stores, volume, share defense) lost money
and is being unwound — but no annual report acknowledges any of it; the store count
simply stops being mentioned and the share number steps down without comment.

## 10.5 The audit-fee spike and the 43-meeting audit committee

Audit + non-audit fees: Rp4.98bn (FY2022, audit only) → Rp28.33bn (FY2023) → 26.04 →
30.69, the increment being "Agreed Upon Procedures and **Transfer Pricing
Documentation**" — engaged the same year (2023) the Audit Committee was wholly
replaced with three CPIN retirees, its meeting count hit 43, and the two §9.5/9.6
affiliated transactions ran. A 5.7× fee jump for TP documentation at a company whose
defining RPT has no disclosed pricing basis is either the tax authority forcing
documentation (DJP TP audits of royalty payers were intensifying industry-wide) or
pre-emptive papering of the royalty; the filings say nothing either way. Related:
CPIN lost its 19% preferential tax rate in FY2024, reverting to 22%, "no longer
meeting the requirements" — unexplained, and the free-float condition (≥40%) was
failed in *both* years, so the stated trigger doesn't parse (§7.5's 29.4% effective
rate is partly this).

## 10.6 Frozen affiliate receivables

Rp202,462m allowance against related-party receivables (~78% of gross RP receivables,
largely PT Central Proteina Prima group — the family's shrimp listco), identical to
the rupiah in FY2024 and FY2025: dead affiliate exposure neither collected, written
off, nor explained.

## 10.7 Smaller items, recorded

- Effective tax rate 17.2% (FY2022) → 29.4% (FY2024) with three moving parts
  (preferential-rate loss, Rp578bn unrecognised DTAs at loss-making subsidiaries
  tripling YoY, subsidiary loss pooling) — subsidiaries as a group were pre-tax
  **loss-making** in FY2021 (−Rp508bn) and FY2022 (−Rp2.88tn): the parent entity
  carries the group.
- A DJBC customs-duty dispute at the Supreme Court (FY2022 notes) — the only
  litigation disclosed in five years; outcome not traced in later notes.
- The same 2023 AoA amendment carries two different ministry decree numbers between
  the profile section and FS Note 1, in both AR2024 and AR2025; the FY2025 auditor's
  report is signed "KAP Purwanto Susanti dan Surja" while the narrative says
  "Purwantono, Sungkoro & Surja" (both EY network — likely a rebrand, unreconciled).
- English MD&A translation errors that reverse the Indonesian meaning (AR2021, gross
  profit "increased"/decreased; tax "decreased"/increased) — read the Indonesian.
- Governance hygiene: no training for any organ, three years running; no
  individually-named remuneration; Head of Internal Audit uncertified.

## 10.8 Checked and found nothing

Audit opinions unmodified all five years (single KAM: biological-asset valuation —
routine). Covenants compliant throughout at trivial leverage. No qualifications, no
going-concern language, no sanctions, no whistleblowing reports, no material lawsuits
disclosed (beyond 10.7's customs case), no pledged shares disclosed at the controller
level, and the segment note's five lines cross-foot to the printed totals in both
detail years. The FY2021–22 OCR figures were verified against their own subtotals.
