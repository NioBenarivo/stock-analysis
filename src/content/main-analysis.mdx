Controlled 57.27% by Dragon Amity Pte Ltd, a wholly-owned vehicle of Leong Hup International Bhd (KLSE: LHI), itself controlled by the Lau family of Malaysia through Emerging Glory Sdn Bhd.

## THE ONE THING TO UNDERSTAND FIRST

MAIN is a feed mill with farms attached, and the two halves do opposite things to the P&L.

**The feed mill is the business.** The Feedmill segment earned a positive segment result in every one of the six years in hand — Rp451bn (2020), 221, 456, 775, 648, 440 (2025) — on total segment sales of Rp7.9–9.2tn, a 2.8–8.4% margin. Roughly a quarter of its output (Rp2.1–2.5tn a year, 25–32% of feed sales) never leaves the group: it is sold to the breeder and broiler segments at transfer prices, so the feed mill has a captive floor and the farms carry a captive cost. Feed is 58% of external sales and 90% of its cost is raw material (corn, soybean meal), 15–17% of which is now bought from two Leong Hup-affiliated companies (§1.5).

**The farms decide the year.** Breeder (DOC), Broiler and Food Processing together lost Rp257bn (2020), Rp7bn profit (2021), −324, −465, +108, +129 (2025). Consolidated segment result is simply feed profit plus that farm swing: 2023's Rp313bn and 2024's Rp759bn differ by Rp446bn, of which the feed mill contributed *minus* Rp127bn and the farms *plus* Rp573bn. The farm result is the Indonesian live-bird/DOC price cycle — a government-managed oversupply cycle (cutting of hatching eggs, GPS import quotas, farm-gate reference prices, §04) that MAIN does not control — arriving in the accounts through a Level-3 biological-asset fair value that the auditor flags as the key audit matter. FY2025's Q2 was loss-making; so was Q2 2026 (−Rp24bn after +Rp123bn in Q1), when feed segment margin fell to 0.9% because raw-material inflation was not passed through (§06, §8.4).

**Consequences.** Read the feed segment for the franchise and the farm segments for the cycle; the consolidated net margin (0.2–3.9%) is the difference between two large numbers. The balance sheet has been repaired — net debt Rp2.2tn (2021) → Rp0.74tn (2025), first dividend ever in 2025 — but a further Rp518bn of Islamic "syirkah" financing sits outside both liabilities and equity and outside the company's gearing ratio (§7.2). And the group has a parent with its own feed-additive business on the other side of the purchase ledger. Evidence: segment table §8.2, RPT §1.5, price cycle §04–§06, financials §07.

## 1.3 History — recap

Built in three layers from the mill outward: feed (1997–2008), then breeding and broiler farms bought from or built beside the parent's own (2008–2019), then a thin downstream (processed food 2013, QSR 2021, slaughterhouse 2022) that is still loss-making after twelve years. Capital came in twice from the market (2014–15, Rp873bn at Rp1,200–3,500 a share, 1.8–5.2x today's price) and was not returned until 2025. The physical footprint has barely changed since 2015 — five mills, the same 335m-chick breeding capacity every year since 2022 — so growth has been price, not volume; the Lampung mill is the first capacity step in a decade.

## 1.4 Owner's character

Read from the record:

- **Family-run, professionally accounted.** Five Laus across the two boards (President Commissioner Lau Chia Nguang, President Director Tan Sri Lau Tuang Nguang, directors Lau Joo Hwa, Lau Joo Keat, Lau Joo Kiang); finance under Rudy Hartono Husin (ex-EY, since 2004) and feed/DOC marketing under Rewin Hanrahan (ex-Gold Coin, since 2008). No board change since Dato' Abdul Azim joined in 2022; all reappointed 2024–2029. The President Commissioner attended 4 of 6 meetings in 2024.
- **They did not pay for eighteen years, then started.** No dividend on FY2006–FY2023 earnings (some of those years were losses). The FY2024 and FY2025 payouts are both ~29.5% of net profit [calculated] — a policy without a stated ratio.
- **They were slow to fix the downstream.** Food Processing has lost Rp29–52bn every year since 2020 (§8.2); Sunny'Chick missed its 80-store target by a third and the store count then disappeared from disclosure for three years.
- **They restate capacity quietly.** Feed capacity 1.65m t (FY2021–22 decks) became 1.3m t (AR2023 on); broiler capacity 90,000 t (2022) → 50,000 t (AR2023 profile) → 120,000 t (AR2024) → 161,000 t (AR2025); processed food 7,000 t (AR2021) → 9,000 t (AR2022/23/25 MD&A) while every deck and AR2024 say 6,000 t. None is explained.
- **They lean on the parent's supply chain.** Purchases from Leong Hup-affiliated PT Sehat Cerah Indonesia and PT Leong Hup Jayaindo rose from 10.5% of purchases (2020) to 17.1% (2025) while the arm's-length statement stayed one sentence (§1.5).
- **Management pay tracks the good year.** Key-management compensation Rp69bn (2023) → Rp186bn (2024, 38% of net profit) → Rp152bn (2025, 39%) — see §10.2.

Product-basis feed sales exceed segment external sales by Rp1.2–1.5tn because feed sold to plasma farmers is booked inside the Broiler segment. Feed sales have fallen two years running (−4.1%, −2.2%) with no volume explanation given; the FY2025 deck attributes revenue growth to "DOC and live-bird prices", i.e. feed did not grow.

### Supply chain
Corn is the binding input. MAIN has built corn dryers (Makassar 2017/18/19, Gresik 2017, Sumbawa 2024) and silos (Grobogan 2020) to buy wet local corn at harvest, and reports "partnerships with local corn farmers"; 790 local suppliers are 92% of the supplier base by count. Soybean meal is imported (USD; net USD monetary position +Rp205bn at FY2025, so a weaker rupiah raises input cost without a matching liability). Premix, additives and animal health come partly through SCI/LHJ (§1.5). The local-vs-import corn split, tonnes bought and price paid are not disclosed in any year.

### Purchase — what it owns
Fixed assets Rp2,387bn = 42% of total assets (FY2025); gross cost Rp5.2tn at FY2024, 30% of it fully depreciated but in use (Rp1.55tn); capex/depreciation was 0.64x in 2024 and 1.48x in 2025 as Lampung began [calculated]. Lampung: 300,000 t/yr, capex guided Rp700–800bn (LHI's own deck carries it at MYR74m), completed Q2 2026, operations Q3 2026 — a 23% capacity addition into a market the company itself describes as oversupplied.

Raw material is 90% of cost in every year — this is a pass-through business whose margin is the lag between corn/SBM and feed list prices. Note the FY2025 line "biological assets and inventory movements": Rp119bn of cost was parked into a larger livestock and finished-goods balance (biological assets +Rp87bn, hatching eggs +Rp58bn), which flattered COGS.

### Distribute
Feed is sold direct and through distributors, cash-in-advance for main products to some customers; receivable days 20. Pricing is list price, adjusted with input cost — but the Q2 2026 feed margin of 0.9% (a research note's reading of the quarterly segment data) shows the pass-through is not automatic when live-bird prices are weak and farmers cannot pay more. The government's 2026 corn measures (HPP Rp5,500/kg, HET Rp7,000/kg, subsidised SPHP corn to *independent* farmers at Rp5,000–5,500) cap what integrators can charge in practice. Selling expense Rp268,590m = 2.1% of revenue, 58% of it distribution.

## 2.2 Breeder / DOC — 15% of external sales

### Product and supply chain
Grandparent stock is imported under government quota (national broiler GPS quota raised to 800,000 head; MAIN's allocation not disclosed) to the Majalengka GPS farm (3.1m GPS-DOC/yr), multiplied through parent-stock farms into 335m final-stock DOC/yr (capacity unchanged since 2022), sold by the chick to independent farmers (78%) and to the group's own broiler farms (22%). Hatching-egg and DOC output is the lever the government pulls to fix oversupply: cutting of hatching eggs and early culling of parent stock (2019, 2021, Q2 2023, 2025), which hits this segment first.

Own farms (27 at seven sites) plus a plasma scheme (436 partner farmers in 2022, the last count given) that receives DOC, feed and vaccines from the group and sells the live bird back. The segment therefore books the plasma farmers' feed and DOC as its own external revenue, which is why the segment (Rp4,970bn total) is nearly double product-basis broiler sales (Rp2,739bn).

## 3.1 Chicken meat, Indonesia

| | 2019 | 2020 | 2021 | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |

| National chicken meat production (m t) | 3.5 | 3.2 | 3.2 | 3.8 | 4.0 | 3.9 | 4.1 |

Indonesia eats 7.5 kg of chicken per head — the company's own regional chart puts it at 10 kg on a carcass basis against 40+ kg in Malaysia — and poultry is the protein for ~90% of a non-pork-eating population, so the structural case is real but slow: demand growth 2–4% a year, tracking income. The 2025 surplus was 591,300 t (Bapanas, to Mar 2026), i.e. ~14% of production, which is the demand-side fact that matters for prices.

Drivers to watch: (i) Makan Bergizi Gratis — the free-meals programme restarted July 2026 and lifted farm-gate broiler prices 4.1% in a week; CPIN and JPFA are sized to supply ~1.1m t into it; its budget was reported cut in August 2026. (ii) Purchasing power — the company's own reason for missing its ≥10% growth target in FY2024 and FY2025. (iii) Processed and QSR — growing but tiny for MAIN (5% of sales).

## 3.2 Feed

National feed demand grows with the bird population: GPMT reports 6.7% industry growth in 2025 and targets 6% in 2026 (the latest tonnage found is 18.9m t produced on 26.7m t capacity in 2017 — stale; no 2024–25 series located). Poultry is ~85–90% of Indonesian compound feed. MAIN's feed sales have fallen two years in a row against that growth — a share loss the company does not discuss.

## 3.3 DOC

Demand for DOC is demand from independent farmers, which collapses whenever the live-bird price is below cost (Rp16,900–17,600/kg HPP, June 2025) for more than a few weeks. Permentan 10/2024 obliges integrators to allocate at least 50% of DOC to independent farmers — MAIN's 78% external DOC share sits comfortably inside that.

## 4.2 The managed cycle

Indonesian broiler supply is quota-managed at the top (GPS imports; national broiler quota raised to 800,000 head) and rationed at the bottom by government instruction: cutting of hatching eggs and early culling of parent stock whenever the live-bird price falls below the reference band. Reference prices in force: Permendag 7/2020 Rp19,000–21,000/kg; Bapanas Perbadan 5/2022 Rp21,000–23,000/kg; Kementan HAP floor Rp18,000/kg from 19 June 2025 (market then Rp15,000–17,000, below the Rp16,935–17,646 cost); a farmer-agreed ceiling of Rp22,000 (June 2026); new HAP Rp19,500/kg from 15 July 2026, with market prices still below it in August. Farm-gate prices in the trough: Rp14,500/kg Central Java (July 2024), Rp15,000-ish (Aug 2024), Rp15,000–17,000 (June 2025). DOC Rp7,500–8,500/chick (July 2024).

Read together with §3.1's 14% surplus: this is an industry that structurally overproduces and is periodically told to destroy hatching eggs. The integrators with the lowest feed cost and the most captive downstream (CPIN, JPFA) ride it; a third-tier integrator with 78% external DOC and 73% external feed sells into the farmers who get squeezed.

## 4.3 Inputs

Corn: Indonesia is nominally self-sufficient, imports are quota-restricted, and the price is politically managed at both ends — HPP Rp5,500/kg (Feb 2025, Bulog to absorb 1m t) and HET Rp7,000/kg (Nov 2025) after feed-corn hit Rp6,600/kg in May 2026; SPHP subsidised corn goes to independent farmers, not integrators. Soybean meal is 100% imported (USD). Feed is therefore squeezed from both sides: corn floor-priced for farmers, feed effectively ceiling-priced by the farmer's ability to pay.

# 09 — IMPORTANT TRANSACTIONS

## 9.1 Leong Ayamsatu Primadona (2008)
Acquisition of the parent group's original Indonesian farm company under common control; the Rp137,266m negative APIC entry is the difference between price paid and book value and has sat in equity ever since. LAP is now the largest subsidiary (Rp708bn assets) and the entity whose FY2019–20 tax appeals were won in 2025.

## 9.2 Equity raisings 2014–2015
Rp336bn placement at Rp3,500 (2014) and Rp537bn rights issue at Rp1,200 (2015) — Rp873bn, all still in APIC (Rp334bn + Rp525bn). Funded Makassar and the breeder build-out. At Rp670 today, the 2015 rights price has not been seen again on a sustained basis.

## 9.3 Downstream: MFD (2013), Sunny'Chick (2021), RPHU (2022)
Twelve years of Food & Processing losses totalling Rp257bn over 2020–2025 alone [calculated]; the segment holds Rp301bn of assets. The RPHU exists partly to give the broiler segment an internal outlet when live-bird prices collapse (2022 disclosure). No impairment test of MFD is discussed.

## 9.4 Syirkah refinancing (2020–2024)
Rp100bn (2020), Rp300bn (2021), Rp500bn drawn and Rp521bn repaid (2022), Rp100bn converted from a BCA term loan (2023), Rp300bn new (Dec 2024): sharia-compliant bank financing that moved Rp0.5–0.6tn of debt out of "liabilities" and out of the company's gearing ratio. Terms (nisbah 99.7%/0.3%, maturities 2027–28) are disclosed; the profit-share cost is not.

## 9.5 First dividend and first buyback (2025–2026)
Rp65/share (Rp144.8bn, Jun 2025) on FY2024; Rp52/share (Rp116.0bn, Jun 2026) on FY2025; buyback 10.75m shares at Rp752 average, 2.5m resold at Rp834. Combined 2025 capital return Rp151bn = 21% of CFO [calculated]. Dragon Amity received ~Rp83bn and ~Rp66bn of the two dividends [calculated].

## 9.6 Leong Hup Jayaindo buys 2% (Jul 2025)
44.6m shares at Rp800 (~Rp35.6bn), a premium to the Rp685–710 market, "for investment". LHJ is the parent of SCI and the counterparty to Rp1.77tn of MAIN's purchases; it is now also a shareholder alongside Dragon Amity. Not disclosed in MAIN's FY2025 AR as far as the notes read.

## 9.7 Lampung feed mill (2025–2026)
300,000 t/yr, Rp700–800bn, ~30% spent by May 2026, completed mid-2026, operating Q3 2026. First capacity addition since Makassar (2015); adds 23% to feed capacity while feed sales have fallen two years running and Q2 2026 feed margin was 0.9%. Funded from cash and the Rp165bn of new short-term borrowing in 2025; net debt rose Rp247bn in 1H 2026.

## 9.8 Auditor entity change (FY2024)
KAP Tanudiredja, Wibisana, Rintis & Rekan → KAP Rintis, Jumadi, Rianto & Rekan, both PwC network, same signing partner (Buntoro Rianto) FY2022–FY2024 — a rebranding, not a rotation. Fee Rp2.6bn. Unmodified opinions throughout; KAM on biological-asset valuation from FY2025.

# 10 — RED FLAG

## 10.1 The parent on the other side of the purchase ledger
Rp1.77tn of FY2025 purchases (17.1% of purchases, 15.5% of COGS) from SCI and LHJ — entities controlled by the same family, one of which is now a 2% shareholder — with the ratio rising from 10.5% in 2020 and the pricing basis disclosed in one sentence. Nobody outside the group can tell whether MAIN's feed cost disadvantage against CPIN/JPFA (§4.4) is scale or transfer price. Money flow: Rp0.25tn of payables outstanding to affiliates at every year-end since 2023, i.e. the affiliates also finance MAIN. What would settle it: the additive/premix price paid versus a market quote, and SCI's own margin.

## 10.2 Key-management pay in the good year
KMP compensation Rp69bn (2023) → Rp186bn (2024) → Rp152bn (2025): 38–39% of net profit in both years, against 110% of net profit in 2023 (Rp69bn on Rp63bn). Accrued salaries Rp155bn at end-2024 (Rp56bn a year earlier), G&A salaries +70%. The AR gives no BoC/BoD split, no individual figures, no policy — the NRC "recommended tantiem for FY2023 and salary increase for FY2024". A bonus pool sized on the cyclical peak, paid to a board on which five of twelve seats are family.

## 10.3 Leverage shown on the friendlier basis
Net gearing 0.26x (deck) excludes Rp518bn of syirkah bank financing that is secured on the same fixed assets and repaid on schedule. Including it, net debt is 70% higher (Rp1.26tn) and net debt/EBITDA 1.5x rather than 0.9x. The FY2024 deck also relabelled "total" to "net" interest-bearing debt between two years without saying so (Dec-24 shown as 1,180 then 851).

## 10.4 Biological-asset valuation inputs changed in a good year
In 2024 the projected selling price and feed cost for broilers and hatching eggs moved from a 3-year to a 1-month history — mechanically raising fair value when spot prices are high — and the FV charge fell from Rp60bn to Rp37bn. The effect is not quantified; the auditor made it the key audit matter a year later. Hatching-egg carrying value then rose 64% in 2025 (Rp91bn → Rp149bn).

## 10.5 Feed capacity added into an oversupplied market, as prices turn
Lampung adds 23% to feed capacity (Rp700–800bn, the largest single investment in the company's history) at the moment feed sales have fallen two years running, the company itself calls the market oversupplied, the Q2 2026 feed margin was 0.9% and net debt has started rising again (+Rp247bn in 1H 2026). The parent's Indonesia segment EBITDA fell 22% in FY2025. Either MAIN has a customer base for 300,000 t that it has not described, or the mill will be filled by cutting price. No utilisation, customer or ramp guidance has been given (MISSING DATA item 10).

## 10.6 Numbers that do not agree with themselves
FY2021 gross profit Rp558bn (audited) vs Rp440bn (next year's deck comparative); feed capacity 1.65m vs 1.3m t; broiler capacity 50,000 / 90,000 / 120,000 / 161,000 t across four documents; processed-food 6,000 vs 9,000 t; listing date 10 Feb vs 10 Jun 2006; shareholder-class percentages that sum to 114%; current ratio 1.58 vs 1.54 in the same report; MBP 99.84% vs 99.89%; insurance cover printed as "Rp 8 billion" on Rp2.4tn of fixed assets and "Rp 10 million" on Rp608bn of livestock in the FY2025 notes, where the FY2024 note had Rp6.93 trillion — the units were dropped and the auditor let it through; an "Independent" commissioner (Koh Bock Swi) who declares an affiliation with the controlling shareholder, an audit-committee "independent" who directs a Leong Hup farm company, and a director (Lau Joo Keat) whose Indonesian bio says he is affiliated and whose English bio says he is not. None is large; together they say the filings are not read closely before they go out.

## 10.7 Checked and found nothing
No litigation, sanctions or whistle-blower cases in any year; no related-party guarantees of bank debt; no going-concern language; no restatements; no qualified opinions; no pledged shares disclosed; no contingent liabilities (note 24/25 nil each year); the FY2017 tax case was the only dispute lost (Rp5.1bn).
