**What it is:** Maker of **INACO**, Indonesia's best-known mini-jelly and nata de coco brand. It also makes pudding, aloe vera, gummies, jelly drinks and RTD coconut drinks. There are four plants: Bekasi (HQ, the core plant), Pandaan in East Java (subsidiary NPS), Pontianak (aloe vera) and Sukabumi (subsidiary SNU, nata de coco). It has 150+ SKUs and 251 distribution points, and exports are about 2% of sales.

**Listing:** IPO on 7 Jul 2026. 266m new shares at **Rp900**, the bottom of the Rp900–1,120 range, raising Rp239.4bn. Sole lead underwriter was Sucor Sekuritas. Post-IPO share count is 1,266m.

**Market:** About **Rp470/share (22 Sep 2026)**, giving a market cap of **≈Rp595bn** [calculated: 1,266m × Rp470]. The 52-week range is Rp456–2,190.

**Period covered:** Audited FY2023–FY2025. FY2023 and FY2024 are **restated**. No post-IPO interim filing was in hand.

**Sources in hand:** Preliminary prospectus (Prospektus Awal) dated 15 Jun 2026, 392 pages, plus web research. See the Sources table at the end.

All figures are IDR. "bn" means Rp billion (miliar). Derived figures are marked `[calculated]`.

## THE ONE THING TO UNDERSTAND FIRST

**The falling revenue line comes from a trading business being wound down, not from the brand shrinking. The profit recovery is real but only one year old, and it has not yet turned into cash.**

Reported sales fell for two years: Rp838.9bn → Rp788.4bn → Rp753.1bn (−10.2% cumulative). Management calls it "dropping low-margin SKUs". The cost-of-sales note shows what happened physically:

| | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Finished goods **bought in** for resale (COGS note) | 115.9bn | 33.1bn | **0** |
| KDU (distribution subsidiary) standalone revenue | 212.5bn | 92.0bn | **0** (now "inactive") |
| Consolidated sales | 838.9bn | 788.4bn | 753.1bn |
| Gross margin | 29.4% | 32.9% | **38.6%** |

In 2023, INACO was still acting as a wholesaler of other people's products. It bought Rp115.9bn of finished goods, roughly 20% of COGS, and pushed them through its distribution arm KDU as "locomotive" products to open General Trade doors. That layer is now gone. Suppose the bought-in goods resold at a 5–10% markup. Then **own-manufactured revenue was roughly Rp715bn → Rp752bn → Rp753bn**: up ~5% and then flat, not down 10% [calculated, estimate]. Gross margin gained 9.2 points partly because the trading layer went (mix) and partly because of lower sugar prices and price increases from late 2024.

Three things temper the good news:

1. **One-year profit.** FY2025 net profit of Rp39.0bn is **75% of the three-year total** of Rp52.3bn [calculated]. FY2023 profit was Rp1.7bn, FY2024 Rp11.6bn.
2. **Poor cash conversion in the good year.** FY2025 operating cash flow was Rp20.5bn against Rp39.0bn of profit. Trade receivables rose 67% to Rp174.1bn, and days sales outstanding (DSO) went from 48 to 84 days [calculated]. Management blames Eid 2026 pre-shipments into Nov–Dec 2025. That is plausible, but it has to reverse in 1H26.
3. **The same gross margin as YUPI, a third of the operating margin.** YUPI (Yupi Indo Jelly Gum, a listed peer that also contract-makes product for INACO) runs 38.8% gross / 27.5% operating. JELI runs 38.6% / 8.8% [calculated]. The gap is almost all **selling expense at 22.5% of revenue**, of which promotion alone is 12.3% and rising. JELI's problem is distribution cost, not factory cost.

Behind all of this sits a closely held family group. The **controlling family's own houses and shophouses** secure the main bank lines. The family lends money to the subsidiaries, and a family property company is **landlord to two of the four plants**. Evidence: §1.4 and §3.1.

At Rp470 the stock trades at ~15x FY2025 earnings and ~4.8x EV/EBITDA [calculated, pro forma for IPO cash]. The IPO was priced at 29x, and the peak implied 71x. The whole equity story now rests on **whether FY2025 margins hold and receivables collect in 1H26**. One data aggregator implies trailing EPS of only ~Rp15.9. If that is right, 1H26 was weak. I could not verify it; see §3.8.

**C. Free use of equipment.** JELI lends 41 machines to NPS **free of charge** (pinjam pakai, Nov 2022, 5 years). The effect is to move margin from parent to subsidiary within the consolidated group. Neutral for JELI shareholders now that NPS is 99.99% owned.

**D. Family collateral and guarantees behind the bank debt**

- JELI's Bank Mandiri working-capital and investment lines are secured by fiduciary liens over receivables (Rp70bn) and inventory (Rp50bn), by fixed assets (**Rp242.7bn pledged**), **and by about 11 personal properties** in Jakarta, Bogor, Cimahi, Bandung, Sukabumi and Bekasi. Ten are registered to Ham Pak Japyusuf Hamdani and one to Indrijati Suryani. The mortgage values listed add up to roughly Rp90–100bn. On top of that there is a **personal guarantee plus a cash-deficit guarantee** from Hamdani.
- NPS's Mandiri investment loan is secured by the **Mitra Cahaya Properti** Pandaan land, a JELI corporate guarantee, and a Hamdani personal and cash-deficit guarantee.
- Nothing in the prospectus says these personal guarantees will be released after the IPO. **In practice the group's credit rests on an 85-year-old individual's personal balance sheet.**

**E. Other**

- Cash of Rp123m is held at **PT BPR Supra Artapersada**, a Hamdani-chaired rural bank (trivial).
- A "temporary loan" from PT Supra Sentral Investama appears in the relationship table, with no balance shown.
- Commercial guarantee: JELI guarantees NPS's payment and performance obligations to **Tarami Corporation** (a Japanese jelly maker, via Kawasho Foods). Tarami supplies the machines and NPS manufactures for Tarami for three years from 1 Mar 2025. Tarami paid a Rp944m machine-installation claim to JELI in FY2025.

Fully depreciated assets still in use rose from Rp80.5bn to **Rp160.4bn** of original cost. The asset base is being run down. That is the reason the IPO money goes to machines.

## 2.4 Process — how the product is made and what it costs

### The physical process

- **Jelly:** sugar syrup + gelling agent (carrageenan/konjac "jelly powder") + flavour/colour + optional nata pieces → hot-fill into PP cups → seal → pasteurise → cool → multipack into bags.
- **Nata de coco:** coconut water + sugar + *Acetobacter xylinum* → ~1–2 week static fermentation into cellulose sheets → cut → wash (the Buntaro cleaning machine is in the capex list) → cook in syrup → cup or bag → retort. The retort line added in 2025–26 doubles bag output per machine, from 480 to 960 bags.
- **Aloe vera:** leaf → fillet → dice → syrup → cup/pouch (Pontianak).

## 2.7 Demand

- **Category:** INACO claims **49.6% of modern-retail jelly**, **19.9% of nata de coco**, and **16.0% of pudding** (2025, company-cited modern-retail data, source unnamed). The main nata de coco competitor is **Wong Coco** (PT Keong Nusantara Abadi). Jelly drinks compete with **Okky** (Suntory Garuda) and others. YUPI competes for the gummy share of stomach, and is also INACO's contract gummy manufacturer.
- **Macro backdrop cited:** GDP growth 5.11% (2025), inflation 2.92%, household consumption 53.9% of GDP, F&B industry 7.13% of GDP, food industry growth 6.38%. Global "healthy low-fat dessert" market around USD 5bn (2025) growing ~7% CAGR (Fortune Business Insights). An Indonesia-specific jelly or nata market size in IDR was **not found** in the prospectus or on the web.
- **Demand character:** low-ticket, children-oriented, impulse, sold through the warung/General Trade channel. It is resilient but price-sensitive. Seasonal peaks come at Eid and Christmas, so shipment timing shifts working capital across year-end (the FY2025 receivables jump).
- **Growth claims vs. evidence:** management targets **+26% revenue growth in 2026** and net profit of **Rp49.2bn**. Own-manufactured revenue grew ~0–5% a year over FY2023–25 [calculated]. A 26% year needs new categories (gummies arrive in late 2027 at best), distribution expansion, or price. **Treat the 2026 guidance as aspirational until 1H26 is seen.**

## 2.8 Supply

- **Sugar:** Indonesia will **not import consumption-grade sugar in 2026**. Industrial raw-sugar quotas are 3.12m t plus 0.51m t under the KITE bonded-export scheme. Food makers like INACO buy **refined sugar (GKR)** from quota-holding refiners (Angel Products, Sentra Usahatama Jaya). As of Sep 2026 there is an active parliamentary fight over GKR "leaking" into consumer markets, with proposals to **cut allocations to "misbehaving" industrial users**. That is a supply and price risk for the second half of 2026 and 2027.
- **Coconut:** raw coconut prices spiked in 2025 on Chinese demand for coconut water and milk. The industry ministry proposed a 6-month export halt; the government **refused**. An export levy has been floated and repeatedly postponed. Nata needs coconut water, a by-product of copra and desiccated coconut, so higher coconut prices flow into SNU's cost. That is why SNU's gross margin was only ~20.6% [calculated]. Bringing SNU in-house secures supply but **not price**.
- **Packaging resin:** Indonesian plastic resin rose ~20% in a single month in early 2026 (PE +26%, PP +19–20% m/m globally) on crude prices. Packaging is at least a quarter of purchases. This is the most immediate 2026 margin threat.
- **Carrageenan / jelly powder:** Indonesia is a large raw-seaweed producer but has limited domestic carrageenan refining. No price index was found. JELI's jelly-powder supplier (Galic Bina Mada) is 10.6% of purchases, and Galic's share of JELI's purchases has doubled since FY2023.
- **Policy overlay:** the MBDK (sugar-sweetened beverage) excise was **delayed for 2026** by Finance Minister Purbaya; it returns once GDP growth exceeds 6%. The budget had targeted Rp7tn of revenue. Whether cup jelly (a food) would be caught is unclear. RTD Jelly Drink and I'm Coco likely would be.

## 3.7 Scenario: what FY2026 earnings does Rp470 price?

Common assumptions [calculated]:

**B. FY2025 repeats.**

Gross profit = 753.1 × 34.0% = 256.1
Operating profit = 256.1 − 169.8 − 54.8 = **31.5**

Extra sugar cost = 66.6 × 10% = 6.7

# SUMMARY VERDICT

**What the business is.** A 35-year-old, category-defining kids' dessert brand. INACO has ~50% of modern-retail jelly. It runs on old, largely depreciated lines and sells through a General Trade distributor network. Between FY2023 and FY2025 it stopped acting as a wholesaler of bought-in goods, raised prices, and benefited from cheaper sugar. Gross margin went from 29% to 39% and ROIC from ~2% to ~20%. The brand is real. Consolidated revenue ex-trading has been **flat at ~Rp750bn for two years**.

**What it earns through the cycle.** Not FY2025's Rp39bn. That year combined peak gross margin, a lower promotion accrual and year-end loading. Not FY2023's Rp2bn either, which carried a low-margin trading layer and 79% tax. My mid-cycle estimate is **~Rp25–35bn of net profit** (post-IPO, with lower interest cost) [calculated, judgement]. That is **EPS Rp20–28**, so **P/E ~17–24x at Rp470**. That is a fair price, not a bargain, for a single-bank, family-collateralised, one-good-year company with a rising top-customer concentration.

**What would have to be true for it to work.**

1. The FY2025 gross margin (~38%) holds through the 2026 resin spike and sugar-quota politics.
2. Promotion stops outgrowing sales.
3. Receivables normalise. The FY2025 OCF of Rp20.5bn has to become Rp50bn+.
4. The gummy/extruder capex, the real growth leg, gets built despite the ~Rp150bn funding shortfall and finds customers (Tarami, export).

If all four hold, the stock is ~12x earnings with net cash, and the market has over-punished a post-IPO squeeze.

**What would break it.** A 1H26 print showing gross margin back in the low-30s. That is scenario C, ~29x, and the aggregator trailing EPS hints at it. Also: loss or downgrade of a top distributor, a sugar-allocation cut, or a finding that PT Supralita Mandiri is family-linked.

The governance overlay is heavy. An 85-year-old controller personally guarantees the bank lines. Family property companies own two plants. The family lent at 12%, cut to 7% on the eve of the IPO. A related-party asset (SNU) was bought at 2.9x book two weeks before the audit cut-off. The accounts were restated and reissued twice. None of this is illegal or hidden, and all of it is disclosed. But it caps the multiple the market will pay.

**Position: Watchlist, not a buy yet.** The post-IPO collapse has removed the valuation objection that applied at Rp900 (29x) and Rp2,190 (71x). The earnings-quality objection is still open. The decision point is the **1H26 filing**. Buy below Rp500 **only if** 1H26 shows gross margin ≥36%, DSO ≤65 days and half-year net profit ≥Rp15bn. Otherwise the business is probably worth nearer Rp350–400 on scenario C–D earnings at 20x [calculated]. At that level I would own it for the brand and the net cash. I would not own it for the growth guidance.
