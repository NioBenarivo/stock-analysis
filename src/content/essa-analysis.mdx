**What it does:** Two plants. (1) A 700,000 t/yr ammonia plant at Banggai, Central Sulawesi, held through 70%-owned PT Panca Amara Utama (PAU) — 87% of revenue. (2) An LPG + condensate refinery at Lematang, South Sumatra, held directly — 13% of revenue. Both are single-feedstock (natural gas), single-customer (Genesis Corporation for ammonia, Pertamina for LPG) operations. Reporting currency US$.

**Market:** ~17,226,975,700 shares; price Rp570–710 across Aug-2026 source snapshots; market cap ≈ Rp9.4–12.2tn ≈ US$580–750m. 52-wk range Rp505–995. P/E ~11–13x on trailing owner-attributable earnings. Formerly PT Surya Esa Perkasa Tbk (renamed Oct 2023). IPO Feb 2012.

**Period covered:** FY2020–FY2025 audited (FY2020 via FY2021 comparatives), quarterly 2024–2025 and 1H2026 from public-expose decks and press releases. Sources: five annual reports (FY2021–FY2025, each with audited consolidated FS), six pubex decks (2021–2025/Q3-25), four results press releases (FY2023–1H2026), plus web research for industry, prices and news. ## THE ONE THING TO UNDERSTAND FIRST

ESSA is a single world-scale ammonia plant wrapped in a set of related-party contracts that were all signed around the plant's 2012–2015 financing — and **every one of them expires in 2027**:

| Contract | Counterparty | Terms | Expires |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ammonia offtake — 100% of output | Genesis Corporation (Japan), "under common control" | FOB, Fertecon-indexed (re-indexed 26 Jan 2023) | **3 Dec 2027** |
| Feed gas — the plant's only input | JOB Pertamina-Medco Tomori Sulawesi | DCQ 62 MMSCFD (signed 2014, amended 2018) | **2027** |
| Management/marketing fee | PT Akraya International (Laroya-run) | 4% of PAU EBITDA | **3 Dec 2027** |
| Gas/CSR/liaison services fee | PT Mega Consultindo Perdana | 2% of PAU EBITDA | **3 Dec 2027** |
| Syndicated term loan (repaid Jul 2025) | Mandiri + BNI | — | was 25 Dec 2027 |

The revenue side of this stack is the sharpest fact in the filings: **85–93% of every year's revenue is invoiced to one company, Genesis Corporation, whose relationship is disclosed in ten years of statements only as "under common control" — and no filing ever names the common controller** (§1.5, §10). The Fertecon indexation means the price is at least benchmark-referenced, but the entity that intermediates ESSA's entire ammonia output between the plant and its end buyers in Korea/China/Taiwan is a black box sitting inside the related-party note. A circumstantial lead — Commissioner Bhavna Laroya has been founder-director of "Genesis International Investment Holding Pte Ltd" since 2011 — is recorded in §10 but is not confirmation.

Why this matters now rather than abstractly: the upstream side is already secured (the Senoro-Toili PSC feeding the plant was extended 20 years to ~2047, approved Nov 2021), and the balance sheet is clean — all US$495m of project debt was repaid by 3 Jul 2025, two and a half years early, leaving US$126m net cash. So the business itself will still be standing in 2028. What is genuinely open is **on what terms the 2027 contract wall gets rebuilt**: the offtake price basis, the gas price formula (which, as §8.1 shows, quietly takes ~40% of every ammonia price move), and whether the 4%+2% EBITDA fee agreements to owner-affiliated service companies get rolled for another 15 years. The renewal happens between related parties on both sides of the table, and minorities will read the outcome only after the fact. Evidence: §1.5, §2.1, §8.1, §9, §10.

## 1.3 History — recap

The pattern: build one very large asset with development-bank money and a captive related-party contract stack; refinance into local banks once operating; sweep every operating dollar into debt paydown for four years; pay out opportunistically when the cycle gifts a windfall; and, once the debt is gone, consolidate family control of both the register (TAS unwound into personal stakes) and the boardroom (father → VP Commissioner, son → CEO, Bhavna → Commissioner). Growth projects (CCS, SAF) are announced with dates, then slip or pause; the operating asset itself is run with genuine excellence. This is a family cash-machine being tended, not an empire being expanded.

## 1.4 Owner's character

Read from the record only:

**Operating discipline is real.** The LPG plant's zero-trip streak ran 31→43→55→64→76 months across five annual reports (6.5+ years by the FY2025 release); utilisation 98–99.7% against a 90.4% industry standard. PAU ran at 113% of the ~650kt effective rate in 9M25, and the 2026 turnaround finished in 34 days against a 35–38-day plan, zero LTI. Volume guidance is met or beaten; the two internal profit targets disclosed (2021, 2022) were both beaten.

**Financial conservatism after 2021 is also real.** US$495m gross debt to zero in 4.5 years, ahead of schedule, with margin renegotiated down three times in 2024 alone (4.50%→2.00%) before simply repaying the lot. No FX mismatch (USD revenue, USD debt, USD reporting).

**Capital promises are the weak spot.** CCS COD guided Q4 2027 (2024 deck) → 4Q28 (Apr-2025 sell-side) → "CO2 injection by 2030" (Mar-2026 trade press), still pre-FID with no disclosed capex; SAF guided Q4 2027–Q1 2028 ops, FEED signed 1 Dec 2025, then postponed indefinitely 27 Jul 2026 "on strategic considerations and market conditions". Neither project has a company-disclosed capex figure anywhere.

**Distribution behaviour is owner-first but not extractive on its face.** Dividends swing with the cycle (Rp5 → Rp45 → Rp5 → Rp10 → Rp52), 36–48% of each payout goes to the named related-party register, and the standing 4%+2% EBITDA service fees (~US$10m/yr to Akraya + Mega) have run since 2012 regardless of cycle. Against that: minorities got the same per-share dividends, and the deleveraging benefited everyone.

## 2.2 LPG & Processing — the Lematang refinery (13% of FY2025 revenue)

### Product and distribution

Treat it as a stable ~US$16–20m gross profit annuity declining slowly with feed richness.

# 03 — DEMAND

## 3.1 Ammonia and food — the actual mechanism

The question "how does ammonia relate to food production" has a precise answer: **ammonia is the synthetic nitrogen bottleneck for the entire food system.**

Plants need fixed nitrogen to build protein; the atmosphere is 78% N₂ but biologically inert. The Haber-Bosch process (industrial from 1913) fixes it: CH₄ + steam → H₂, then N₂ + 3H₂ → 2NH₃ at ~200 bar. That single reaction is why natural gas is 70–90% of ammonia's cash cost (~30–36 MMBtu per tonne; PAU runs ~30 MMBtu/t LHV) — **ammonia is essentially natural gas converted into a solid-shippable nitrogen carrier.**

Nearly all nitrogen fertilizer starts as ammonia: urea (~0.58 t NH₃ per tonne of urea), ammonium nitrate, DAP/MAP, or direct application. The end-use split of global ammonia (2024, sources diverge): **~70% fertilizer (IEA) to ~81–83% fertilizer (S&P/Eliseev: urea 46% + industrial-grade urea 10% + nitrates ~16% + other fertilizer 9% + direct 2%)**, with the remaining 17–30% industrial — explosives (ANFO for mining), nylon/acrylonitrile, refrigeration, and NOx-scrubbing (DEF/AdBlue).

The scale of the food dependency, with its academic sources: Erisman et al. (Nature Geoscience, 2008) — synthetic nitrogen fed **~48% of the world's population** in 2008; Smil (2004) — ~40% in 2000, and without Haber-Bosch the planet's carrying capacity is roughly 4 billion people; Rosa & Gabrielli (2023) — ~half of the 2019 population. Directionally: every second person alive is fed by crops grown with synthetic ammonia. This is why nitrogen fertilizer demand is one of the least elastic commodity demands that exists — governments treat it as food security (Indonesia's own HGBT subsidised-gas scheme for fertilizer producers, India's urea self-sufficiency drive, China's export restrictions are all food-security policy expressed as ammonia policy).

Two demand layers matter for PAU:

**Conventional demand (now):** NE Asian fertilizer and industrial buyers — Korea, Taiwan, China import ammonia for downstream urea/nitrates/caprolactam. Growth is GDP-ish (1–2%), but the *seaborne* market is thin — 15–20 Mt — so single supply events (Trinidad outages, Gulf disruptions) move prices violently. PAU's ~750kt is roughly **4–5% of the global seaborne market** [calc: 0.75/15–20] — a meaningful marginal supplier to Asia.

**Clean-ammonia demand (2028+):** Japan's METI roadmap targets 3 Mt/yr clean ammonia imports by 2030 for coal co-firing, 30 Mt/yr by 2050; Drewry projects the clean seaborne trade compounding ~90%/yr off a near-zero base over the next decade. This is the demand story behind PAU's CCS project — capture 0.9 Mt/yr CO₂ (~69% of plant emissions) and the same tonnes reprice as "blue" into Japan/Korea premium markets, with JOGMEC and Mitsubishi already MoU'd. The project's repeated slippage (§9.6) is the caveat.

# 05 — KEY VARIABLES

Ranked by earnings weight:

**1. The ammonia price (Fertecon FOB) — dominant.** 87% of revenue. But the gas linkage (§8.1) and the 30% NCI mean far less of a price move reaches shareholders than the revenue line suggests. Arithmetic for +US$10/t on ~750kt sales:

```
Revenue                        +$7.5m
Gas cost claws back ~40%       −$3.0m   [gas-linkage estimate, §8.1]
Pre-tax                        +$4.5m
Tax at ~22%                    −$1.0m
Net profit                     +$3.5m
NCI takes 30% of PAU result    −$1.1m
Owner-attributable             ≈ +$2.4m per +$10/t
```
(KB Valbury's published sensitivity is +$1.8m NP per +$10/t — same order; the difference is their gas-formula assumption.) Range experienced: ASP $288/t (1Q25) to $887/t (FY2022) — a ±$300/t swing moves owner earnings by ~$70m [calc], against FY2025 owner profit of $40.3m.

**2. The feed-gas price formula — invisible but ~40% offsetting.** Never disclosed. Empirically: $4.5/MMBtu floor-ish at low ammonia prices, $12.8/MMBtu in the 2022 spike [calc §2.1]. Two contract questions dominate: what the formula becomes when the GSA is re-cut at 2027, and whether the DCQ stays at 62 MMSCFD against DSLNG's competing DMO claims on the same block.

**3. Turnaround cadence — a known −5–9% volume year every ~3 years.** 2021 (56 days, badly timed pre-windfall), 2023 (3 wks), 2026 (34 days). Between them the plant runs >100% of effective capacity. 2026 guidance: 723,697 t production, 710,000 t sales.

**4. The 2027 contract wall.** Offtake, GSA, Akraya, Mega all expire within a month of each other in late 2027. Renewal terms are set between related parties (§10.1). This is a variable, not a fact — nothing about renewal is disclosed yet.

**5. CP Aramco (LPG) — minor.** 13% of revenue, stable US$16–20m gross; feed richness decline caps it.

**6. Capital redeployment of the net cash.** US$126m cash, zero debt, ~US$100m/yr operating cash flow at mid-cycle, and both growth projects stalled. Whether this becomes dividends (FY2025 payout Rp52/sh ≈ Rp896bn ≈ US$50–55m [calc, FX-dependent], ~125% of owner earnings per one source), CCS capex (~$100–150m per sell-side), or something else, is the biggest open capital-allocation question.

# 10 — RED FLAG

## 10.1 Genesis Corporation — 86% of revenue to a counterparty nobody will name

The single largest fact about this company is intermediated by an entity disclosed for ten straight years only as *"under common control"* — common control with whom is never stated, in any of the five annual reports read. Money flow: every ammonia dollar (85–93% of group revenue, $147m–$681m/yr) is invoiced FOB to Genesis, which resells into NE Asia at a spread nobody outside can see. The pricing is Fertecon-indexed (and was re-indexed to a "relevant regional price index" in Jan 2023 — the index change itself is undisclosed), and annual realized ASPs track benchmarks plausibly (§2.1), so there is no visible mispricing — but with a single related-party customer, the place to look would be the *netback terms* (index minus what discount, who pays freight/insurance/demurrage), none of which is disclosed. Circumstantial: Commissioner Bhavna Laroya founded and directs "Genesis International Investment Holding Pte Ltd" (since 2011); a sell-side note lists Genesis Corp as a related party alongside "Vinod Laroya" and an unexplained "PT Daya Amara Utama". Unconfirmed — and that it *needs* web sleuthing is itself the flag. The offtake expires 3 Dec 2027; its renewal will be negotiated family-to-family.

## 10.2 The 6%-of-EBITDA fee stack — and the half of it that isn't in the related-party note

Akraya (4% of PAU EBITDA, Laroya-directed) + Mega Consultindo Perdana (2% of PAU EBITDA) have identical contract dates (2012/2015/to 3 Dec 2027). Akraya is disclosed as related; **Mega has never appeared in the related-party note** despite the mirrored structure — either it is genuinely third-party (in which case why does a third party hold a 15-year 2%-of-EBITDA liaison contract?), or it is a related party outside the note. In the windfall year the Akraya fee alone was $16.7m — 45.5% of G&A — and the G&A "management fees" line was larger still ($23.9m) with the excess never attributed. Cumulative Akraya fees 2021–25: ~$44.5m [calc], roughly one full year of owner-attributable profit.

## 10.3 The unnamed 30% of the engine

PAU's non-controlling 30% — a stake worth, at the 2023 step-up's own implied price, ~$350m [calc: 117.1m/10.54%×30%, illustrative] — belongs to shareholders never named in any filing. They received $20m (2023) and $15m (2025) of dividends and absorb ~26% of group profit. The 2023 transaction that diluted them 40%→30% was priced at ~2.3x their book value, paid with public shareholders' rights-issue money (§9.2). Who they are determines what the eventual buyout (or their exit) costs.

## 10.4 The auditor file

Recovered by OCR from the scanned report pages (the FS text layer omits them): FY2021 — KAP Gani Sigiro & Handayani (Grant Thornton Indonesia), partner Hanny Prasetyo; FY2022–FY2023 — same firm, partner Tagor Sidik Sigiro (AP.0786); FY2024 — same firm, partner Hanny Prasetyo (AP.1361); **FY2025 — BDO (Tanubrata Sutanto Fahmi Bambang & Rekan), partner Hanny Prasetyo, the same AP.1361**. All five opinions unqualified; FY2025 key audit matter: revenue accuracy. The flag is mild but real: the engagement partner changed *firms* and the audit followed the partner — the FY2025 "new auditor" is the FY2024 partner under a new letterhead. Continuity of the person, not fresh eyes.

## 10.5 Comparatives that move between filings

The same FY2022 figures differ between AR2022 and AR2023's comparative columns, unexplained: board remuneration $445,779 → $306,792; finance income $1,094,066 → $986,875; operating cash flow $318.41m → $318.75m; total current assets $261.1m → $272.5m; LPG segment revenue $50.1m → $45.3m. Individually small; as a pattern, restatement-without-disclosure. Same habit in prose: AR2024's MD&A cites "net profit ~US$129 million" — that is the EBITDA figure, mislabeled net profit in a published annual report. And the PAU ownership percentage appears as five different numbers across (and within) filings (§1.1).

## 10.6 What was checked and came back clean

For balance: no auditor qualification in any year; no litigation disclosed (AR2025 states none material); cash conversion is complete and verifiable (§7.4); covenants met every year; realized ASPs track public benchmarks; volumes reconcile between decks, ARs and press releases; the debt paydown is confirmed by three independent source types; dividend per share is equal for all shareholders. The red flags here are about *disclosure opacity around related parties*, not about evidence of extraction from the listed entity — with the one caveat that the fee stack and the Genesis netback are precisely the places extraction would be invisible.
