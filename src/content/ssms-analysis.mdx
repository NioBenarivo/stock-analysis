## How to read this report: SSMS has five different sets of books

This is the single most important thing to understand before looking at any SSMS number, and it is
the reason so much published commentary on this company is wrong.

Between 2023 and 2025 SSMS absorbed two entities **from its own controlling shareholder**, both
accounted for as **common-control transactions under PSAK 38** (pooling of interests). Under PSAK 38
you do not record an acquisition. You restate history *as if the acquired business had always been
part of the group*, and you push the difference between what you paid and the book value you
received straight into equity, bypassing the income statement entirely.

That has been done twice:

- **December 2023 — PT Citra Borneo Utama (CBUT)**, the refinery. FY2021 and FY2022 were restated.
  FY2022 revenue was restated **from Rp7,261bn to Rp11,240bn (+54.8%)**. Additional paid-in capital
  went from **+Rp540.9bn to −Rp2,852.1bn**.
- **November 2025 — PT Sawit Mandiri Lestari (SML)**, a plantation. FY2024 was restated. FY2024
  revenue went **from Rp10,521.7bn to Rp10,366.8bn**. Additional paid-in capital went from
  **−Rp2,852.1bn to −Rp3,936.7bn**.

The practical consequence:

| Period | Consolidation perimeter | Comparable with |
|---|---|---|
| FY2017–FY2020 | SSMS upstream only; CBUT is a 13–19% cost-method holding | each other |
| FY2021–FY2022 as originally reported | upstream only; CBUT an associate | each other |
| FY2022 restated, FY2023, FY2024 as originally reported | upstream + refinery | each other |
| FY2024 restated, FY2025 | upstream + refinery + SML | each other |

**Do not compute a growth rate across those boundaries.** Revenue "grew" from Rp7.3tn (FY2022) to
Rp10.7tn (FY2023) mostly because a refinery was bolted into the comparative, and then "fell" from
Rp10.5tn (FY2024 as reported) to Rp10.4tn (FY2024 restated) because a plantation was bolted in and
its intercompany sales were eliminated. Neither movement is an operating fact.

Throughout this report I state which basis each figure is on. Where I show a series that crosses a
boundary, I say so on the table.

## Executive summary — the fifteen things that matter

1. **SSMS is not really a plantation company you can value on plantation metrics. It is the listed
   segment of a family supply chain.** In FY2019 **91.06%** of revenue went to related parties. The
   number has ranged 27%–91% across seven years, but the underlying fact never changed: the buyer of
   SSMS's output is chosen by the same people who control SSMS. Only the name on the invoice rotates
   — PT Citra Borneo Utama until 2022, then Borneo Agri-Resources International Pte Ltd (Singapore)
   from 2022 to 2025, at **61.4% of FY2024 revenue**. [AR2019–AR2025]

2. **Then, in Q1 2026, sales to that Singapore counterparty went to exactly zero** — from 46% of Q1
   2025 revenue to nil — with no explanation given. Five weeks later the Ministry of Finance
   announced it had found transfer-pricing evidence at ten CPO exporters and **SSMS was named**; the
   shares fell 8.57% in a day. Those two facts are not formally connected by any source, and I am not
   asserting they are. But an analyst should hold them in the same hand. [press, May 2026]

3. **The two related-party poolings moved roughly Rp5.05tn of value to the controlling shareholder
   without ever touching the income statement.** Rp3.45tn of a receivable owed by the parent was
   settled in CBUT shares (Dec 2023); Rp1.6tn of cash went to the parent for 63.4% of SML (Nov 2025).
   The combined Rp3.94tn accounting difference sits in negative additional paid-in capital.

4. **Equity has been hollowed out by that accounting, and every return ratio is therefore fake.**
   Total equity is **Rp2,941bn against Rp13,584bn of assets** (FY2025). Reported ROE of **40.88%** is
   not a sign of a superb business; it is a small denominator. Debt-to-equity is **362%**. On a
   pre-pooling equity base ROE would be roughly a third of that. [AR2025, `[calculated]`]

5. **The estate is aging and yields are falling, and nobody at the company has said so.** Nucleus
   FFB yield: **27.0 t/ha (FY2022) → 25.0 → 22.7 → 21.2 t/ha (FY2025)**, a **−21.5%** slide in three
   years. Nucleus average age went 14.7 yrs (FY2023) → **16.7 yrs (1Q25)**. The *combined* average
   age then "improved" to 14.8 yrs — but only because the company bought younger trees from its own
   parent. **Replanting hectares are never disclosed in any of the seven annual reports.** [PRES_2024
   through PRES_2026]

6. **The refinery, which is now more than half the group, earns almost nothing.** FY2024 segment
   note: Plantations made **Rp1,675.2bn of operating profit on Rp6,305.5bn of segment revenue
   (26.6%)** — but **only Rp755.5bn of that was external**, so 88% of the upstream's "revenue" is an
   internal transfer price the accounts never disclose. Edible Oil made **Rp122.0bn on Rp9,766.1bn
   (1.2%)**. Consolidating CBUT roughly doubled reported revenue and added
   about 7% of the operating profit. Refinery utilisation halved in FY2024 (91.8% → 47.5%) with no
   root-cause explanation. [AR2024 Note 36]

7. **FY2025 was a genuinely good year, and about half of it was price.** Revenue +42.9% to
   **Rp14,813bn**; net profit to owners **Rp1,160.7bn (+41.6%)**. Decomposed: **volume +23.7%
   contributed Rp2,461bn (55.3%)**, blended price/mix +15.5% contributed **Rp1,986bn (44.7%)**
   `[calculated]`. The volume half is the SML acquisition, i.e. an asset bought from the parent, not
   organic growth.

8. **Palm kernel had the same lauric-oil melt-up seen at AALI, TAPG and NSSS.** SSMS's PK price went
   from **Rp4,974/kg (FY2023) to Rp12,406/kg (FY2025)** `[calculated from AR2025 volume and value
   tables]` — from **37.5% to 71.0% of the CPO price**. Reverting the ratio to FY2023 levels costs
   **~Rp151bn of revenue** at FY2025 volumes, roughly **13% of net profit**.

9. **The MD&A and the audited notes contradict each other on the single most important fact in the
   company.** FY2024's governance chapter: *"the Company did not carry out any transactions with
   affiliated parties related to business activities."* Note 33 of the same document: related-party
   revenue **Rp6,460.8bn, 61.41% of total**. Not a nuance — a flat contradiction inside one
   PDF. [AR2024]

10. **Capex is reported three different ways in a single annual report.** FY2024: **Rp19bn** in the
    MD&A capex table, **Rp230.0bn** in the audited cash-flow statement, **Rp386.2bn** in the segment
    note. FY2023's MD&A says the company *"did not invest in new capital goods"* while the cash-flow
    statement shows **Rp303.5bn**. [AR2023, AR2024]

11. **Free float has fallen from 35.84% (2022) to 24.04% (2025)** while CBI's stake rose from 54.30%
    to **65.83%** — with **no new shares issued** at any point since the 2013 IPO and **no disclosed
    transaction** explaining any of the transfers. Roughly 1.1 billion shares changed hands out of the
    public float over three years, unnarrated. [AR2022–AR2025]

12. **The ultimate beneficial owner changed in 2024 from the founder Abdul Rasyid to Jery Borneo
    Putra**, disclosed only as a changed line in Note 1a, with no narrative anywhere. In the same
    period the founder faced a public document-forgery allegation over his provincial legislature
    seat (June 2025, unresolved). [AR2024, AR2025, press]

13. **The forest-area (kawasan hutan) exposure is real and unaddressed.** A February 2025 Ministry of
    Forestry list of Central Kalimantan plantations inside forest areas names **PT Citra Borneo Indah
    (1,823 ha, 1,230 ha rejected)** and an **SSMS-named entity (3,650 ha and 18,595 ha, 6,753 ha
    rejected)**. **PP 45/2025** sets fines at **Rp25m/ha/year** and paying does not legalise the land.
    Neither Satgas PKH, nor PP 45/2025, nor kawasan hutan appears anywhere in the FY2025 annual
    report. AALI booked Rp572bn for this; NSSS booked Rp108bn and got a qualified opinion. SSMS has
    booked nothing and said nothing.

14. **At Rp920 the stock is 7.6× trailing earnings and roughly 5.2–5.5× EV/EBITDA** — cheap against
    its own history and mid-pack against IDX peers — but it is **3.85× book on the owners' equity**,
    which is the highest multiple in the peer set, precisely because the book was destroyed by the
    poolings. You are not buying a cheap balance sheet. You are buying a cheap earnings stream whose
    counterparty just disappeared. `[calculated]`

15. **The FY2026 budget in the annual report is arithmetically impossible as published.** Management
    projects **revenue of Rp24,114bn, +62.8%**, with total assets *falling* 1.3% — implying asset
    turnover jumps from 1.09× to 1.80×. It requires RBDPO output to rise **95.4% to 1,112,190 MT on
    unchanged 2,500 t/day refining capacity**, which is **139% of nameplate** `[calculated]`. This is
    from a company that delivered **83.9% of its FY2025 CPO target and 49.6% of its CPKO target**.
    Either the second refinery — silently dropped from disclosure in 2025 — is being built and
    nobody has said so, or the budget is not a forecast. §3.11 has the full table.

## 1.1 What the company actually is

PT Sawit Sumbermas Sarana Tbk is an oil palm plantation and, since December 2023, palm oil refining
group operating almost entirely in **Central Kalimantan** — the Pangkalan Bun / Kotawaringin Barat /
Lamandau / Seruyan corridor. As at 31 December 2025 it reports **136,447 ha of managed area**, of
which **93,726 ha are mature** and **76,850 ha are mature nucleus**, across **27 estates**, feeding
**10 mills (670 t FFB/hr)**, one **2,500 t/day refinery-and-fractionation complex** and **two kernel
crushing plants (780 t/day combined)**. It employs **20,040 people** on the group HR tables and
**13,304** on the audited "permanent employees" note. [AR2025]

It is controlled by **PT Citra Borneo Indah (CBI)**, which holds **65.83%**, with a second family
vehicle **PT Putra Borneo Agro Lestari (PBAL)** holding **10.13%** and the public **24.04%**. CBI is
in turn owned by PT Prima Sawit Borneo (48.77%) and PT Mandiri Indah Lestari (46.76%), with four
individuals holding the rest. The founder of the group is **Haji Abdul Rasyid AS**, a former
national parliamentarian and, as of 2025, a member of the Central Kalimantan provincial legislature
for PKB. [AR2025, WEB]

The thing to hold onto: **SSMS is one leg of a larger private group.** The refinery it now
consolidates was the group's; the plantation it bought in November 2025 was the group's; the
Singapore trading company that bought most of its output is the group's; the small rural bank that
lends to it is chaired by one of its own commissioners; the shipping companies that move its cargo
are the group's; and until 2023 nearly a third of its balance sheet was a loan to its own parent.
Everything below follows from that.

### What the 2013 prospectus tells you about how this group operates

The prospectus is 551 pages and worth reading precisely because the pattern it establishes never
changes:

- **Insiders bought in at Rp100 five and a half months before the public paid Rp670.** On 18 June
  2013, CBI, Jery Borneo Putra and Jemmy Adriyanor subscribed 3.9bn new shares at par — 48.6% of the
  pre-IPO share count, an **85% discount** to the offer price, worth roughly **Rp2.2tn of paper gain
  at the offer** `[calculated]`. The same window's proceeds were used to buy minority co-investors
  out of KSA, MMS, ASP and SML up to 93–99.99%. No independent valuation for those buy-outs is in
  the document.
- **Related-party receivables were parked off the balance sheet before the cut-off.** On 29 June
  2013 — one day before the 30 June reporting date — over Rp125bn of intercompany balances from SMU,
  TSA and CBI were transferred into PT Surya Borneo Industri, an unconsolidated affiliate, repayable
  in mid-2014, i.e. after listing.
- **A Rp176bn dividend was pulled out of subsidiary KSA in FY2012**, shortly before the float, while
  KSA's own equity fell 67.7%.
- **The founder is not in the cap table** — yet he personally guarantees subsidiary bank debt, has
  "payment on behalf of" balances with the group, and the prospectus itself concedes he had been the
  subject of NGO illegal-logging allegations. The four named "ultimate shareholders" are asserted to
  be related to one another but no family tree is drawn.
- **SSMS was not participating in the mandatory 20% plasma smallholder programme at IPO**, despite
  already setting aside 14,434 ha for it — disclosed as a risk factor with the note that no sanction
  had yet been issued.
- The underwriting was **86.02% concentrated in one lead** (RHB OSK Securities Indonesia).

Every one of those behaviours — issuing value to insiders at book, moving balances between
affiliates around reporting dates, disclosing a regulatory shortfall as "no sanction yet issued" —
recurs in the annual reports a decade later.

narrative. Here it gets none.

### 1.5.3 The two poolings — Rp5.05tn to the parent, Rp0 through the P&L

**December 2023 — CBUT.** CBI owed SSMS Rp3,451.3bn. It settled by transferring **52.6% of CBUT** — reported in the
press as 1.64bn shares via negotiated market trade, though **neither the share count nor CBI's
residual stake appears in any annual report**; the company text says only "52.6%." SSMS's stake went
26% → **78.22%**. Because both sides were controlled by the same family, PSAK 38
applied: **the Rp3,392.97bn difference between transfer price and book value was booked against
additional paid-in capital**, which went from +Rp540.9bn to **−Rp2,852.1bn**. FY2021 and FY2022
comparatives were rebuilt. Nothing went through profit or loss.

**November 2025 — SML.** A three-step sequence over five months:

1. **26 Jun 2025** — CBI sells its own plantation and mill *into* SML at **Rp216.5bn against a
   Rp119.5bn book value — an 81% premium** `[AR2025]`.
2. **30 Jun 2025** — CBI acquires 99.9% of SML from an unrelated third party. SML is now under
   common control with SSMS.
3. **24 Nov 2025** — SSMS buys **63.4% of SML from CBI for Rp1.6tn**, funded by the new syndicated
   facility. PSAK 38 again: the **Rp1,084.6bn** difference goes to APIC, taking it to
   **−Rp3,936.7bn**. FY2024 is restated.

The governance chapter states the transaction "did not involve any conflict of interest."

Two things to note about the mechanics. First, the intermediate step — CBI selling an asset into SML
at 81% over book **before** SML was sold on to SSMS — means the price SSMS paid embeds a mark-up the
parent booked on itself. Second, PSAK 38 is the correct treatment for a common-control transaction
under Indonesian GAAP; I am not alleging a breach. The issue is what the treatment *conceals*: under
purchase accounting, Rp1.08tn would have been tested as goodwill and could be impaired. Under
pooling, it is a permanent hole in equity that no future write-down will ever visit, and no fairness
opinion or independent valuation is reproduced in the annual report.

**The combined effect:** roughly **Rp5.05tn of consideration** moved from SSMS (in receivable
forgiveness and cash) to the controlling shareholder across two transactions, and **Rp3.94tn** of
accounting difference now sits as negative paid-in capital. That is **134% of FY2025 total equity**
`[calculated]`.

## 2.7 Key variables — what actually moves the model

**1. The CPO price.** Everything else is second-order. As of 14–15 August 2026: Malaysian futures
**RM4,711/t**, the Indonesian KPBN domestic tender **Rp15,700/kg**, and the Ministry of Trade's
reference price **US$996.52/MT** for August 2026. The MPOC's August forecast range is RM4,400–4,650.
Reference-price history through the year: US$915.64 (Jan) → 989.63 (Apr) → 1,049.58 (May) → 1,029.51
(Jun) → 1,000.90 (Jul) → 996.52 (Aug). SSMS's own realised FY2025 CPO price was **Rp17,484/kg**
`[calculated]`, up from Rp13,262/kg in FY2023.

**2. The palm kernel / CPO ratio.** SSMS has **no kernel oil demand of its own beyond its two
crushers**, so it sells kernel and CPKO straight into the lauric market. FY2023 PK at Rp4,974/kg was
37.5% of CPO; FY2025 PK at Rp12,406/kg is **71.0%**. CPKO at Rp26,886/kg is **54% above** the CPO
price. This is a genuine windfall and it is mean-reverting. §3.10 sizes it.

**3. Export levy and export duty.** SSMS exports ~68% of volume. The levy went **7.5% (Jan 2025) →
10% (Jan 2026) → 12.5% (Mar/Apr 2026)**; the duty has run US$74–178/MT through 2026, at **US$148/MT**
in August. At the August reference price, levy + duty together are roughly **US$273/MT ≈ 27% of the
reference price** — a direct deduction from the realised export price. This is the largest single
2026 headwind and it is entirely outside management's control.

**4. The biodiesel mandate.** **B50 took effect nationwide on 1 July 2026**, after being postponed in
January and then reinstated. B50 lifts domestic biodiesel demand from roughly 15.6m to 20.1m
kilolitres a year, absorbing CPO that would otherwise be exported. That is structurally supportive
of the domestic price and of SSMS's realised CPO — and it is also what funds the levy increase.

`EDITED:` an earlier draft of this report claimed SSMS never discusses the biodiesel mandate or the
export levy at all. **That was wrong and is corrected here.** AR2024 carries a dedicated MD&A
subsection on the **Domestic Market Obligation and Domestic Price Obligation**, and discusses the
B40 rollout; AR2025 refers to **B35/B40 as a CPO-demand stabiliser in four separate places** and
explicitly attributes a 39.11% movement in a cash-flow line to higher **pungutan ekspor** and **bea
keluar**. What is genuinely absent is **B50** — but B50 was announced after both reports were
written, so that absence is chronology, not silence. The fair criticism is narrower than I first
put it: SSMS discusses these policies qualitatively and **never quantifies their rupiah impact on
its own margin**, which several peers do.

**5. Fertiliser.** Never disclosed separately. See §2.6 — the only visible datapoint is the combined
fertiliser-and-maintenance line, and it was cut hard into a falling-yield period.

**6. The forest-area regime.** See §3.10 — this is a tail variable, not a base-case one, but it is
the largest single unquantified exposure in the company.

### The USD300m bond, 2018–2023

Issued 23 January 2018 through **SSMS Plantation Holdings Pte Ltd** (Singapore), listed on SGX, 7.75%

group.

### Covenants

- **At 31 December 2023, the parent company itself breached DER, consolidated net debt / LTM adjusted
  EBITDA, and DSCR covenants.** Subsidiary MKM separately breached current-ratio and DSCR covenants
  with BNI and obtained a waiver in December 2023. **The outcome of the parent-level breach is not
  visible in any document I have.** That is a significant gap: a parent-level financial covenant

## 3.10 Scenario arithmetic

Every step shown, per the house convention.

### Scenario A — the palm kernel windfall reverses

```
FY2023 PK price / CPO price      = Rp4,974  ÷ Rp13,262 = 37.5%
FY2025 PK price / CPO price      = Rp12,406 ÷ Rp17,484 = 71.0%

Normalised FY2025 PK price       = 37.5% × Rp17,484     = Rp6,557/kg
FY2025 PK volume sold                                    = 25,875 MT
Revenue at risk = 25,875,000 kg × (12,406 − 6,557)       = Rp151.3bn
After tax at 22%                                          = Rp118.0bn
As % of FY2025 net profit to owners (Rp1,160.7bn)         = 10.2%
```

**And that understates it**, because CPKO at Rp26,886/kg — 54% above CPO — is on the same lauric
curve, and PKO plus PKE is another **Rp1,287bn (8.7%) of revenue**. A full lauric normalisation would
be a multiple of the Rp151bn above. Treat 10% of net profit as the floor of this exposure.

### Scenario B — the forest-area regime

**PP 45/2025 (19 September 2025)** sets fines at **Rp25 million per hectare per year** for
plantations operating inside forest areas, and — critically — **paying does not legalise the land**;
the state reclaims it. The Satgas PKH task force billed **Rp2.34tn across 20 palm companies in
December 2025**, with a broader sweep of 71 companies at up to Rp38.6tn. **The company-level lists
have not been published.**

What *is* published is a February 2025 Ministry of Forestry list, under Ministerial Decree 36/2025,
of 184 Central Kalimantan plantation companies with area inside forest zones. It names:

| Entity | Area applied for | In process | **Rejected** |
|---|---|---|---|
| PT Citra Borneo Indah (the parent) | 1,823 ha | 593 ha | **1,230 ha** |
| SSMS-named entity, entry 138 | 3,650 ha | 1,306 ha | — |
| SSMS-named entity, entry 139 | 18,595 ha | 11,842 ha | **6,753 ha** |

**I have not been able to verify this against the primary decree, and the entity naming is not
exact** — it appears as "PT Sawit Sumber Mas Sarana." Treat it as a lead requiring confirmation, not
a fact. But the arithmetic of the exposure, if it holds:

```
Rejected hectares at the SSMS-named entity                  = 6,753 ha
Annual fine at Rp25m/ha/yr                                  = Rp168.8bn per year
Over a 5-year assessment period                             = Rp844bn

If the full 22,245 ha named across both entries is assessed = Rp556.1bn per year
Over 5 years                                                = Rp2,781bn
```

For scale: **Rp844bn is 73% of FY2025 net profit; Rp2,781bn is 95% of total equity.**

**Neither Satgas PKH, nor PP 45/2025, nor the words "kawasan hutan" appear anywhere in the FY2025
annual report.** A keyword sweep returns nothing. Compare: **AALI booked Rp572bn** for the same
regime and buried it in G&A; **NSSS booked Rp108bn, disclosed the exposure, and received the first
qualified audit opinion in its history** — with its auditor stating that on a full PP 45/2025 basis
the provision would be Rp554bn, cutting net profit in half.

SSMS has booked nothing, provisioned nothing, and said nothing. Its FY2025 audit opinion is clean.
Either the exposure genuinely does not exist — in which case a single sentence saying so would be
worth a great deal — or it has not been recognised.

Note also that SSMS **has** already paid **Rp146.0bn of PSDH/reforestation-fund charges** to the
Ministry of Forestry in Dec-2024/Jan-2025, disclosed only as a 7.3× jump in the "insurance and
permits" G&A line. So the company is demonstrably inside the forestry-charge perimeter.

### Scenario C — the affiliate customer does not come back

```
FY2025 revenue from Borneo Agri-Resources          = Rp3,815.2bn = 25.8% of revenue
Q1 2026 revenue from Borneo Agri-Resources         = Rp0
Q1 2025 BARI share of revenue                      = 46%
Q1 2026 revenue                                    = Rp3,510bn, −2.23% YoY
Q1 2026 net profit                                 = Rp318.1bn, −6.85% YoY
```

Read straightforwardly, SSMS replaced the entire affiliate channel with third-party sales inside one
quarter and lost about 2% of revenue and 7% of profit doing it. **That is a remarkably small
disruption for losing a customer that was nearly half of the prior-year quarter** — which cuts two
ways. It suggests the product is genuinely fungible and the affiliate was not adding value, which is
good. It also suggests the affiliate was not, on this evidence, paying a large premium — which
raises the obvious question of what the arrangement was for.

The Q1 2026 cost lines are the tell: **selling expenses +25.4%** (blamed on export taxes and duties)
and **admin costs +52.6%**. Selling direct rather than through a Singapore affiliate means bearing
export costs on your own books.

```
Hectares that should be immature in steady state    = 3,074 × 4 years = 12,296 ha

                                                      = 0.6% of the mature estate
```

**That is the whole argument in nine lines.** A properly replanting estate carries about 16% of its
area in immature palms at any time. SSMS carries 0.6%. It has spent **Rp13.4bn where it needs
roughly Rp215bn a year**, and the nucleus average age has drifted to 16.7 years while yield fell
21.5% from the 2022 peak.

There are only three ways this ends: a multi-year capex step-up of roughly Rp200bn+ per year with an
associated production hole; continued yield decay; or more acquisitions of mature estate from
related parties. **The company has so far chosen the third**, which is exactly what the SML deal was.

### Scenario E — the export levy

```
Export volume, FY2025                        ≈ 68.4% × 977,795 MT = 668,791 MT
Levy increase, 10% → 12.5% of reference price
At the Aug-2026 reference price of US$996.52/MT, 2.5pp = US$24.91/MT
Annualised cost at FY2025 export volumes     = US$16.66m ≈ Rp272bn at Rp16,300/US$
As % of FY2025 net profit to owners                        = 23.4%
```

`[calculated]` — this is a first-order approximation; the levy applies at product-specific rates and
the mix matters. But the order of magnitude is right, and it is the reason SSMS's Q1 2026 selling
expenses rose 25.4%.

Take that seriously for a moment and it falls apart on arithmetic.

```
Asset turnover implied:
  FY2025 actual      = Rp14,813bn ÷ Rp13,584bn assets = 1.09×
  FY2026 projected   = Rp24,114bn ÷ Rp13,413bn assets = 1.80×   [calculated]
  → a 65% jump in asset turnover with assets shrinking 1.3%
```

To deliver it, the refinery has to nearly double its throughput (RBDPO +95.4%) on **unchanged
2,500 t/day capacity** — which at 569,213 MT in FY2025 was already 71% utilisation. Producing
1,112,190 MT would require running at roughly **139% of the stated nameplate** `[calculated:
1,112,190 ÷ (2,500 × 320 operating days) = 139%`]. That is arithmetically impossible on the
disclosed capacity — unless the **second refinery that vanished from disclosure in 2025 is in fact
being built**, which nothing in the FY2025 annual report or the April 2026 public expose says.

Upstream, CPO production has to rise 39.1% to 733,413 MT against nucleus FFB rising only 23.4%,
which implies either a large step-up in bought-in fruit or an OER improvement of several points.

`EDITED:` **the company's own percentage column in that table is wrong for CPO.** It prints 146.74%
for the 2026 projection against 2025 realisation, but 733,413 ÷ 527,052 = **139.15%**. The "total"
row for plantation-segment production prints "94.885" against a 146.14% ratio, which implies
944,885 — a missing digit. Both errors are in the same table.

The honest summary: **the FY2026 budget requires the best year in the company's history by a wide
margin, on falling nucleus yields, unchanged refining capacity, a 2.5-point higher export levy, and
without the customer that took 30% of FY2025 volume.** Against a seven-year record of missing
operational targets by 15–50%, the base case should be well below it. The one line in the projection
that looks achievable is EBITDA of Rp3,621bn — and even that is +23.4% on a year when price did most
of the work.

# 04 — The argument, and what would change it

## The bear case, stated plainly

SSMS is a controlled company whose economics are not fully observable from outside. Between 27% and
91% of its revenue in any given year has gone to entities its controlling shareholder also controls,
at prices it has never explained; roughly a quarter of its balance sheet was for five years a loan
to that shareholder, on which no impairment was ever taken despite a documented payment deferral;
and it has twice bought businesses from that shareholder using an accounting treatment that puts the
premium permanently into negative equity without ever passing through profit. In the space of one
quarter, its largest affiliate customer went to zero and the state announced a transfer-pricing
finding naming the company.

Underneath that, the operating business is an aging estate that has stopped reinvesting. Mature
nucleus hectares were flat within 0.9% for six years, yield has fallen 21.5% from its 2022 peak,
immature area is 0.6% of mature where it should be 16%, and the fertiliser-and-maintenance line was
cut Rp559bn into the teeth of the yield decline. The growth that has been reported came from buying
a plantation from the parent and consolidating a refinery that earns a 1.2% operating margin.

And the reporting is not reliable enough to check any of this. Capex appears three ways in one
document. OER is described as increasing when it fell, twice, two years apart. Landbank does not
foot. The MD&A says there were no affiliate transactions in a year the notes show 61.4% of revenue
came from one. A promised second refinery simply disappeared from disclosure rather than being
reported as delayed.

## The bull case, stated fairly

At Rp920 you are paying **7.55× trailing earnings, 5.2–5.5× EV/EBITDA and a 5.1% dividend yield**
for a company that just grew revenue 42.9% and net profit 41.6%, that generated Rp1.1tn of operating
cash flow, that has cut leverage to 2.3–2.5× net debt to EBITDA, and that owns roughly 95,000
planted hectares in Central Kalimantan. Strip out the listed value of its CBUT stake and the
plantation business is on about **Rp75m per planted hectare** — very cheap by any transaction
benchmark, and a fifth of what a broker was willing to underwrite six months ago.

The Q1 2026 evidence is genuinely reassuring on one point: when the affiliate channel closed, third
party sales replaced it inside a quarter at a cost of about 2% of revenue. That is what a fungible
commodity should do, and it argues the affiliate relationship was a routing convenience rather than
a value transfer on the revenue line.

The policy backdrop is supportive: **B50 is live from 1 July 2026**, absorbing 4.5m kl/yr more
domestic biodiesel demand and tightening the exportable surplus. SSMS's export book is Asia-facing,
so EUDR is a minor problem for it. Its 100% RSPO and ISPO certification is a real asset in a sector
where it is rare.

And the price already reflects a lot of damage: **−42% from the December close, −52% from the
February high.**

## What would change my mind

- **A published replanting programme with hectares and capex.** The single most useful disclosure
  this company could make. Absent it, the yield decline compounds.
- **A statement about kawasan hutan exposure.** One paragraph confirming or denying that the group
  is inside the Satgas PKH / PP 45/2025 perimeter, with hectares. AALI and NSSS both eventually
  quantified this. SSMS has not acknowledged the topic exists.
- **An explanation of the BARI termination** and the transfer-pricing finding — from the company,
  not the press.
- **A transfer-pricing policy for affiliate sales**, or a benchmarking statement. Seven years of
  27–91% affiliate revenue with no pricing disclosure is not defensible.
- **Segment cost disclosure** so upstream cash cost per tonne is computable again. It has not been
  since FY2022.
- **The resolution of the FY2023 parent-level covenant breach.**
- **Any narrative for the float transfers** that took CBI from 54.3% to 65.8%.

## Open threads I could not close

1. **Was SSMS or CBI in the December 2025 Satgas PKH batches?** The company lists are not public.
   The February 2025 Kemenhut list naming both entities needs verification against the primary
   decree.
2. **What happened to the second refinery?** Promised operational April 2025, never mentioned again,
   capacity unchanged at 2,500 t/day.
3. **Why did BARI stop buying?** And is it connected to the May 2026 transfer-pricing finding?
4. **Why did the refinery's utilisation halve in FY2024?** From 91.8% to 47.5% in its first full year
   inside SSMS, unexplained.
5. **What was the FY2021 audit opinion, exactly?** The letter is not in the PDF's text layer; only
   the MD&A's characterisation is available.
6. **Was the parent-level FY2023 covenant breach waived?** The disclosure cuts off.
7. **Who paid what for the ~1.1bn shares** that moved from the public float to CBI between 2022 and
   2025?
8. **What is PT Hamparan-equivalent here** — i.e. what does the "other" 41,500 ha in the FY2025
   landbank reconciliation actually consist of, and why does the audited note show 104,291 ha
   against the MD&A's 136,447?
9. **What is the CBUT Supreme Court tax case worth?** AR2024 discloses the appeal was rejected on a
   2019 assessment; the amount (Rp3.79bn) looks small but the judicial review is pending and no
   press coverage exists.
10. **What is the SML purchase actually worth?** Rp1.6tn for 63.4% of an entity whose planted area is
    reported as 11,046 ha by one source and 21,000 ha by another, containing an asset the parent had
    marked up 81% over book five months earlier.
