**What it does:** Indonesian branded consumer-goods maker. It sells herbal cooling drinks, personal care, confectionery/food and pet food under its own brands, plus one big licensed brand:

- **Beverages:** Cap Kaki Tiga, licensed from Wen Ken Drug of Singapore; plus Cap Panda, Panther and Malee.
- **Personal care:** Ellips, Sleek, Ovale, Eskulin, Resik-V, Sasha, Samantha, Ristra, Click.
- **Food:** Kino Candy, Segar Sari, Snackit, Kino Nastar.
- **Pet food:** Perro, MAXlife, Kucingku.

It runs six plants in Java (Sukabumi ×2, Cikande, Pandaan, Semarang). It distributes through its own distributor, DLS, plus third-party sub-distributors, and exports about 6% of sales through subsidiaries in the Philippines, Malaysia, Vietnam, Cambodia and China. Founder Harry Sanusi controls it (83% with his holding company). He returned as President Director in August 2025.

**Market:** Rp1,380/share (23-Sep-2026) × 1,378,818,900 shares outstanding (1,428,571,500 issued less 49,752,600 treasury) = **Rp1,903bn market cap** [calculated]. P/E is 15.4x on FY2025 and **8.6x on trailing earnings to Jun-2026**. P/BV is 1.09x. Dividend yield is 3.3% (Rp45 FY2025 DPS). All [calculated], §3.6.

**Period covered:** FY2021–FY2025 audited (FY2021–22 only through comparative columns), 1H2026 from the interim financial statements, plus FY2010–1H2015 from the IPO prospectus and FY2019–20 headline figures from the public-expose decks.

**Sources in hand:** 3 annual reports (FY2023–FY2025, 338–345 pages each), 3 public-expose decks (Jun-2024, Jun-2025, 2026) and the Dec-2015 IPO prospectus (572 pages). Web research filled in the market, peers, 1H2026 and the Dec-2025 share transaction. The full source table is at the end.

**Conventions:** Rp bn unless stated. "Attrib." means attributable to owners of the parent. `[calculated]` marks every figure derived here rather than printed in a filing. **No FY2021 or FY2022 annual report is in hand.** Those years come from comparative columns and the deck charts, so the FY2022 loss can be described but not fully bridged.

## THE ONE THING TO UNDERSTAND FIRST

**KINO's profit is a thin residue between a 44% gross margin and a 31% selling bill. For two years, about half of that residue came from a line the company does not explain.**

Four things follow:

1. **The operating business barely covered its interest bill until 2025.** After selling costs of 29–31% of revenue and G&A of 8%, KINO kept about 5% as operating profit. It paid 3–4% of revenue in interest. The core pre-tax profit before "others" was Rp47bn in FY2023, Rp73bn in FY2024 and Rp101bn in FY2025, on Rp4.1–4.4tn of sales.
2. **"Others – net" is the biggest swing item, and it is not broken down.** It was Rp79.0bn in FY2025, equal to 50% of PBT. The FY2025 note on it discloses only one component: Rp0.3bn of investment-property rent. The FY2024 public-expose deck credits "higher MTI income by Rp16bn YoY" for NPAT growth. MTI is not defined anywhere in the filings in hand (§2.4, Anything odd #1).
3. **1H2026 changes the picture, if it holds.** Revenue grew 21.7% while selling costs grew 18.2% and G&A fell 13.4%. Core operating margin rose to 7.4% and core profit after interest nearly tripled, to Rp140.5bn. This time the gain came from operations, not "others": 1H2026 PBT (Rp152.6bn) is only about Rp12bn above core profit after interest [calculated]. Attributable net profit was Rp151.4bn, already above the whole of FY2025.
4. **Small changes in inputs move profit a lot.** Raw materials and packaging are Rp1,881bn, or 75% of COGS. A 5% move in their cost changes after-tax profit by about ±60%. A 1pp move in A&P/revenue changes it by ±28% (§2.6). KINO's ten-year record is mostly about managing those two lines. Its peak was FY2019 at 46.8% gross margin (Rp516bn NPAT, of which Rp264bn was a one-off bargain-purchase gain). It hit bottom in FY2022 at 36.0% gross margin and a 23.5% A&P ratio, with a Rp950bn loss.

**The balance sheet is the second half of the story.** Current liabilities have exceeded current assets every year from FY2022 to FY2025. The current ratio was 0.87x, 0.83x, 0.73x and 0.67x, and 0.69x in Jun-2026. That is because 72% of bank debt is short-term revolving credit. KINO has needed **covenant waivers from its banks after each of FY2023, FY2024 and FY2025** (five, three and four banks respectively), all granted. The banks keep rolling the facilities, but they do so on waivers.

Three movements worth noting:

- **FY2023–24: the founder sold personally into the market.** His direct stake fell from 180.2m to 57.8m shares. About 38m went to his holding company, taking it to exactly 70.00%. About 84m went to the public [calculated from the registers]. Sale prices are not disclosed.
- **Dec-2025: the holding company bought out the financial investor at a premium.** Nusantara FMCG Limited held its ~11.2% stake from at least FY2022. It sold 128.53m shares to Kino Investindo at **Rp1,556, 16% above that day's close of Rp1,345** (web). The ARs do not say who Nusantara FMCG is. The circumstantial link is Commissioner **Agus Sandianto**, appointed Sep-2024. He was Executive Director of **PT Nusantara Sugihartana Internasional (2014–2025)**, then a commissioner of Indomarco (Indomaret) and Greenfields, and since 2025 a Principal at Ares Management Asia. The shared "Nusantara" name and his appointment while the fund held 11% suggest he was the investor's board nominee. That is **our inference**, not a disclosure.
- **The company's own buyback in 2022 cost 2.8x what the controller paid in 2025.** KINO spent Rp193.5bn at about Rp3,888/share in Feb–Jun 2022. That was the year it lost Rp950bn and its bank debt peaked at Rp2,238bn. Three and a half years later the founder's vehicle bought a block at Rp1,556. The stock is at Rp1,380 today. The buyback shares are still held in treasury.

**The KFI round trip.** KINO booked a Rp264.2bn bargain-purchase gain when it bought Morinaga's stake in Jan-2019. That gain made up about half of FY2019's record Rp516bn NPAT, and it was the base for FY2019's Rp108 DPS. Four and a half years later KINO put KFI into liquidation. The KFI food lines appear to have been absorbed into the parent's Sayung/Cidahu plants: food volume rose 44% in FY2025.

**Governance flags:**

- **The founder removed the professional CEO who ran the turnaround and took the job back himself.** Sidharta Oetama's three years produced the recovery from the FY2022 loss: gross margin up from 36.0% to 43.1%, CCC down from 126 to 63 days, debt down Rp700bn. He and the Finance Director left together at the Aug-2025 EGM. No reason is given in any filing in hand. 1H2026 results under Harry Sanusi are the best since 2019, so the change has not hurt so far.
- **The board is small and thin.** It has two commissioners, one of them independent. The independent chairs the board, the Audit Committee and the Nom/Rem Committee all at once. The commissioner seat changed three times in 2024 (Wibowo → Wong Chee Yann, who resigned after a month → Agus Sandianto).
- **The Linanda name appears three times.** Director Anggara Andrian Linanda became Corporate Secretary in Feb-2025, replacing **Clara Alexandra Linanda**. The Indian subsidiary bought in 2019 is **Linanda Consumer India Pvt Ltd**. No filing states a relationship among them. We record the name pattern only.

**How to read this:** KINO is **an operating-leverage business on a thin margin**. The rupiah matters more than rates. The sugary-drink excise (MBDK) would hit variable #4 directly; as of Sep-2026 it is deferred (web: Finance Minister Purbaya says not before GDP growth exceeds 6%). KINO recovered margin mostly by cutting SKUs and trade spend, not by raising prices.

## 2.7 Demand

- **Indonesian FMCG 2025–26:** AR2025 cites NielsenIQ/Bain: APAC FMCG value +4% (volume +2.8%, price +1.2%) in the 12 months to Jun-2025, with Southeast Asia slowing. Indonesian consumer confidence was 127.2 (Jan-25) and 123.5 (Dec-25), "optimistic but selective". Beauty and personal care is the growth category; food and beverages are the base. KINO's own FY2025 volumes fell in both core segments (−2.5% beverages, −4.0% personal care), so it underperformed the market's volume growth.
- **Herbal cooling drinks (larutan penyegar):** a category created by Cap Kaki Tiga. Its main rival is Cap Badak, from Sinde Budi Sentosa, the former Cap Kaki Tiga licensee. Consumers treat the category as a remedy for "panas dalam" (internal heat). No market-share data was found (gap). It is exposed to the planned sugary-drink excise, and KINO's zero-sugar/"Cooltopia" line extensions look like a hedge against that.
- **Personal care:** Ellips (hair vitamin) is KINO's strongest owned brand ("Best Sales Growth Personal Care" at Guardian 2025; YouGov "most improved"; Watsons Malaysia award). Personal-care revenue per litre is 15x beverages, and it is the segment that rebuilt KINO's margin.
- **Pet food:** a fast-growing category in Indonesia. KINO's revenue rose from Rp9bn to Rp74bn in two years, but at an 11.7% gross margin it is not yet an economic business.
- **1H2026:** revenue +21.7%. That is roughly 4x the pace of nominal consumption growth, so KINO likely took share or restocked the channel. **Which one matters a lot.** A distributor restock after the FY2025 clean-out would not repeat. Receivables rose only 2.6% (Rp976bn → Rp1,002bn) on a 22% sales increase, and CFO doubled (Rp451bn vs Rp213bn). **That argues against channel stuffing.**

## 3.7 Scenario: does the 1H2026 margin hold?

This is the scenario that matters, because the stock at 8.6x TTM prices in some of 1H2026 but not all of it.

Three points follow:

- **At Rp1,380 the market is roughly pricing scenario B/D:** growth fades, margins revert, and KINO earns Rp140–215bn. That is roughly 1.1–1.7x FY2025 but well below the 1H2026 annualised rate.
- **Scenario C is the live risk.** The rupiah was at an all-time low of 18,279 in Jul-2026 (+6.7% YoY at Sep-2026), and KINO's input basket is heavily imported or USD-linked. 1H2026 gross margin already fell 1.3pp. Another 200bp would take most of 2H2026 profit.
- **The asymmetry is favourable from here.** Even scenario C leaves KINO on about 11x FY2026 earnings with falling debt. Scenario E requires only that the FY2019 economics come back: 46–47% gross margin at 18–19% A&P. That is not unprecedented for this company.

## 3.8 Roadmap and what to watch

1. **Gross margin versus the rupiah, quarterly.** The 1H2026 GM was 43.6%. Below 42% in 3Q2026 would mean scenario C is under way. Watch for list-price increases and whether volume holds through them.
2. **"Others – net" / MTI.** Ask the company what it is. If FY2026 "others" returns to Rp60–80bn on top of the 1H2026 operating gains, earnings upgrade sharply. If it doesn't come back, the 1H2026 step-up is the whole story.
3. **Segment mix of the +22%.** Personal care growth is worth about three times beverage growth per rupiah (§3.5). Pet-food growth is worth nearly nothing at 12% GM. The 1H2026 segment note (not in hand) settles this.
4. **Balance-sheet structure:** watch for the revolvers being termed out. A current ratio above 1.0x would end the annual waiver cycle. The CIMB (Jun-2026) and BCA (Jul-2026) renewals should show in the 9M2026 notes.
5. **Receivables and distributor turnover.** The FY2025 write-off was large (Rp187.5bn). Receivables growing faster than sales, or new >90-day balances, would repeat the FY2021–22 pattern.
6. **The controller's intentions.** Kino Investindo paid a premium in Dec-2025 and holds 86% of outstanding shares. The free float of about 14% is above IDX's 7.5% minimum. No tender offer or go-private has been announced; any such move would be a price event. Watch Harry Sanusi's personal selling, which ran in 2023–24.
7. **Governance and disclosure to ask for:**
   - ownership and licence terms of the "KINO", Kino Candy and Segar Sari marks
   - Cap Kaki Tiga's registration status in Indonesia, its royalty and change-of-control terms
   - the MTI / "others" composition
   - per-director pay
   - the new HQ lease terms with Royal Bintang Persada
   - whether any distributor is Sanusi-linked
8. **Idle capacity.** Personal care is at 16% utilisation, pharma capacity has just been cut by 65%, and there is surplus land now being leased out. More rent, or sale-and-leasebacks, would release capital. An impairment of personal-care lines would cut book value.
9. **MBDK sugary-drink excise.** It is deferred for now, but it would reach the 53% beverage segment directly.

# SUMMARY VERDICT

**What it is.** KINO is a founder-controlled challenger in Indonesian consumer goods. It owns a set of real personal-care brands (Ellips, Sleek, Ovale), the licence for the country's best-known herbal cooling drink (Cap Kaki Tiga, to 2041), six plants that are far too big for its volumes, and its own distributor. Its gross margin (44%) is in line with branded peers. What it keeps is not: about 31% of revenue goes back out as trade spend, advertising, delivery and sales force, and 3% goes to the banks. That leaves a 2–3% net margin in a normal year and a 26% loss in a bad one (FY2022).

**What it earns through the cycle.** Over FY2021–25, cumulative net income was a loss of Rp560bn, but cumulative CFO was Rp2.6tn. The loss was mostly provisions and working capital unwinding, not destroyed franchise value. In the recovery years (FY2023–25) KINO earned Rp70–125bn a year, with half of the pre-tax profit from an unexplained "others" line. Core profit after interest was only Rp47–101bn. **1H2026 is the first half-year since FY2019 in which the operating business, not the "others" line, produced a respectable return:** 7.4% core margin, Rp151bn of attributable profit in six months, and CFO covering interest eight times.

**What has to be true for it to work.**

- Gross margin holds at about 43–44% against a record-weak rupiah, through pricing rather than share loss.
- The 1H2026 revenue step-up is sell-out, not distributor restocking. The evidence so far (receivables flat, CFO doubled) supports sell-out.
- A&P stays near 18% of revenue.
- The banks keep waiving a current-ratio covenant the company breaches by construction, or the company terms out its revolvers.

**What would break it.**

1. An input-cost/rupiah shock without price increases, which is the FY2022 mechanism: every 1pp of gross margin is about Rp44bn pre-tax, a third of FY2025 profit.
2. A return to channel-loading. Distributors are where KINO lost about Rp250bn of receivables in FY2021–25.
3. Anything that disturbs Cap Kaki Tiga: the licensor, the Indonesian trademark registration or a change of control. It is the volume engine of the 53% beverage segment and not KINO's to own.
4. Governance drift under a founder with 86% economic control, board pay at 50–77% of profit, and brands and property historically held by or leased from the family.

**Position.** On the filings, **KINO at Rp1,380 is priced for its average, not for 1H2026**. That is 8.6x trailing earnings, about 9x a cautious FY2026 and about 14x a mid-cycle year, with the controller having paid Rp1,556 nine months ago. The operating evidence for a real turnaround is better than the headline suggests: working capital is clean, debt is down Rp900bn from the peak, and the 1H2026 gain came from operations, not the opaque "others" line. **Our reading: modestly undervalued, but the value case depends on gross margin surviving the rupiah, which has just hit a record low.** The downside case (C, about 11x FY2026) is not severe because debt keeps falling on strong CFO. The upside case needs FY2019-type economics that KINO has shown once and never sustained. Three things limit what a minority holder can capture: the 14% free float and thin liquidity, a board with one independent commissioner, and undisclosed brand ownership and "MTI" income. A reader who wants exposure should size it as a **turnaround with operating leverage and a governance discount**, confirm with the 9M2026 gross margin and segment mix, and not treat the Rp3,800 IPO price as an anchor. The business that listed in 2015 has grown sales 2.6% a year since.

*This is an analytical view of the business from its filings, not investment advice. I'm not a financial advisor.*
