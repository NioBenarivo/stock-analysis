**What it does:** sells sports shoes, sportswear, and children's goods in shopping malls. Almost everything on its shelves is somebody else's brand — Nike, Adidas, Skechers, New Balance, Converse, Crocs, Lego and about 150 others — sold either through its own multi-brand chains (Sports Station, Planet Sports, Kidz Station, and Foot Locker under licence) or through single-brand shops it runs on the brand owner's behalf. At the end of 2025 it had 2,239 stores in seven countries; Indonesia is three-quarters of sales, the Philippines is the largest of the six overseas markets. It is a subsidiary of PT Mitra Adiperkasa Tbk (MAPI), the mall-retail group that also runs Zara, Starbucks (via MAP Boga) and department stores in Indonesia.

**Market cap** (the value of all the shares at the market price)**:** about Rp19 triliun at early September 2026 (≈Rp670 a share; 28,504,000,000 shares). MAPI holds 68.83%, the board 0.47%, the public 30.70%.

**Period covered:** audited financial years FY2015–FY2025 (FY2020–21 from summary statements only — the 2021 annual report carries no notes), 1H2026 from the results deck, and the 2018 IPO prospectus for how the company was put together.

**Sources in hand:** the July 2018 IPO prospectus (with audited FY2015–17 statements), annual reports 2019, 2021, 2023, 2024, 2025 (the last three with full audited statements at the back), public-expose decks 2022–2026 and results decks FY2022–1H2026. ## THE ONE THING TO UNDERSTAND FIRST

MAP Aktif owns very little of what makes it money. The brands belong to Nike, Adidas, Skechers and the rest. The shops sit in malls it rents for two to ten years at a time. Its back office — IT, finance, HR, legal, supply chain, even "licensing" — is run by its parent MAPI, for a fee. What MAPA itself brings is the permission to sell each brand in each country, the 2,000-odd store locations it has negotiated one lease at a time, a stock of shoes worth about Rp5.7 triliun, and thirty years of knowing which shoe sells in which mall. The business is best understood as a bundle of permissions rented from three landlords — the brand owners, the mall owners and the parent — and the report keeps returning to what each landlord charges and whether the charge is rising.

The first landlord is the one to watch. In eleven years of filings, MAPA has never published the economic terms — the rate, the length, the expiry — of any brand agreement, and the only contracts it has ever named (in 2018–19) were legacy ones: Reebok, Payless, The Athlete's Foot and a list of smaller brands. The 2018 prospectus admitted that the company was "selling many of the competitors' products" in breach of the non-compete clauses in those contracts, trusting that the principals "have not expressed objection". Since 2023 the agreements note has been one identical paragraph. What the numbers show is that the brand owners' share of the sale is rising: royalties paid to them went from about 2% of revenue before the pandemic to 3.7% in 2024 and 2025, and the money still owed to foreign suppliers doubled in 2024 as the group leaned harder on licensed formats such as Foot Locker. What is left after paying for the goods themselves (the gross margin) gave up 2.6 points of revenue in 2024 and recovered only one of them in 2025. Meanwhile the first Nike-branded and Puma-branded stores appeared in 2026 — in MAPA's own deck, as photo captions, which is how new brand relationships are announced here. Whether brand owners keep choosing MAPA over selling direct is the variable the filings are least willing to talk about. Section 2.1 and §8.5 lay out what can be measured.

The second thing is where the profit is made and where it is spent. Indonesia is a very good business: it has kept 16–18% of every rupiah of sales as profit before tax every year since 2022 and has no bank debt. The six overseas countries, built up since 2018 through acquisitions and more than twenty new subsidiaries, have lost money every year, and in 2024 lost Rp359 miliar before tax on Rp4.4 triliun of sales — the reason group profit fell that year while sales rose 27%. In 2025 the overseas loss shrank to about Rp100 miliar, and that shrinkage, not Indonesian growth, supplied most of the 22% rise in pre-tax profit. Sections 2.3 and 8.3 separate the two.

The third thing is who owns it, because that changed in 2026, three weeks after the latest annual report was signed. MAPA came into being in 2015 when MAPI carved its sports division out and, at the same moment, took Rp1.5 triliun from a private-equity investor (a fund that buys stakes in companies to sell them on later) — not as shares but as a five-year interest-free loan, paired with an option to buy 30% of the new company at its listing. The 2018 "IPO" placed 99% of the offered shares with that same investor's second vehicle, and the cash went straight back to repay the loan to the rupiah; real public shareholders owned 0.66% of the company at the end of 2018. The investor was CVC Capital Partners, by the company's own decks and the press, though no filing names it. CVC sold out of MAPA between 2019 and 2023 for at least Rp5.5 triliun. Then in May–July 2026 a CVC-structured vehicle bought 87% of the parent MAPI for about Rp21 triliun, which puts it, looking through, in control of MAPA again — this time from above, with the private company that had controlled the group since before its 2004 listing — and whose owners were never disclosed — gone from the register. Sections 1.1, 8.1 and 8.2 give the record.

## 1.1 Group structure

Until May 2026 MAPA sat at the bottom of a three-layer chain. A private company, PT Satya Mulia Gema Gemilang (SMGG), held 51% of the listed parent MAPI; MAPI held 68.83% of MAPA. Every annual report since 2021 names one individual, "Mrs. Marisa Kolonas", as the person who ultimately owns and controls the company, but none of them shows how — not her share of SMGG, not what sits between her and it. The audited statements stop one layer higher, at SMGG. A 2019 CNBC Indonesia article, quoting MAPI's own director Handaka Santosa, says the group's founder was Sjamsul Nursalim (who "holds no current stake") and that SMGG's 2004 register showed a company called PT Mitralestari Adiperkasa at 99.99%. ## 1.3 History — recap

The company was built inside MAPI for twenty years, carved out in 2015 with a private-equity investor's money structured as debt rather than shares, and listed in 2018 in a transaction that converted that debt into the investor's equity. The real float arrived a year later when the investor sold down. From 2018 to 2023 management used the balance sheet to buy or build a presence in six other ASEAN countries — through acquisitions in the Philippines, Singapore and Malaysia and through more than twenty new subsidiaries — while Indonesia kept funding it. Since 2024 the pattern has been consolidation: buying out partners, merging entities, clearing bank debt, and slowing store growth. In 2026 the same private-equity name that financed the carve-out bought the parent.

## 1.4 Owner's character

What the record shows, rather than what the letters say:

**Cash is kept in the business, and the parent takes its share by fee, not dividend.** MAPA paid MAPI Rp366 miliar of dividends in 2016–17 while it was wholly owned (82% of profit in one year), then nothing to anyone for five years (FY2017–FY2021), then 8–10% of profit a year. The dividend was cut a fifth in 2025 after a year in which profit slipped 3%, and held flat in 2026 after a year in which profit rose 27%, so the share of profit paid out (the payout) fell to 6.6%. The prospectus had promised "up to 50% from FY2019". Over the same years the management fee to MAPI rose from Rp72 miliar to Rp193 miliar (§1.5).

**Governance disclosure has shrunk as the company has grown.** The 2018–19 filings named some brand contracts and their counterparties; from 2023 the agreements note is a single generic paragraph. The 2021 annual report was published without any notes to the financial statements, and the 2024 and 2025 reports print only three years of highlights. Board changes — the whole post-IPO reshuffle in late 2018, the departure of the CVC-linked commissioners in 2021 and 2023, the President Director's resignation in 2023 — are recorded without a stated reason. Directors' share sales (the President Commissioner sold 10 million shares in 2024; the former President Director sold down to zero before leaving) are recorded without comment. Three annual reports in a row print the IPO proceeds as "Rp897,876 billion", a thousand times the real figure, and the 2025 report swaps the Philippines and Cambodia store counts.

**Independence labels do not always mean independence.** Two board members who were officers of CVC Asia Pacific were labelled "no affiliation" while CVC's vehicle held 7.5% of the company; both served as "Independent Commissioner" and each chaired the audit committee. The 2023 independent commissioner spent eight years as a MAPI director. A commissioner appointed in 2024 who shares the stated ultimate owner's surname (Sintia Kolonas, ex-MAPI director) was flagged "affiliated with the controlling shareholder" in 2024 and "no affiliate relationship" in 2025, with no event in between.

**The group treats MAPA's balance sheet as group collateral.** Nearly every bank facility is contracted in MAPI's name for "MAP and its subsidiaries", with the borrowing limits (covenants) tested on MAPI's combined accounts, not MAPA's; in 2025 guarantees MAPI had given on the Vietnam lines were switched to MAPA; and one facility makes MAPA jointly liable with seven MAPI fashion subsidiaries outside the listed group (§8.2).

**When it counts, management executes.** Sales have grown about 17% a year since 2019 including the pandemic year, and a third a year from the 2020 trough; the bank was repaid within a year of COVID; the 2024 margin stumble was met with a slower store programme (382 gross openings in 2025 against 546 in 2024), a data-driven merchandising push and a return to a 47% gross margin.

# 02 — MODEL

MAPA reports two segments, retail (95% of revenue) and non-retail (5%), and one geography split. Retail is the business; non-retail is a garment factory plus wholesale and department-store concessions. Overseas is treated here as its own subsection because its economics are different from Indonesia's.

## 2.1 Retail

### Product

What sits on the shelf is mostly footwear. Shoes and their accessories have been two-thirds to three-quarters of the stock by value since 2017; clothing is under a tenth, toys and sports equipment the rest. The company groups its stores into three lines — sports (88% of 2025 revenue), leisure (7%: Payless, Staccato, Birkenstock, Dr Martens, Clarks) and kids (5%: Kidz Station, Lego, Hasbro, Smiggle) — and the sports share has risen every year since 2018, when it was 75%. Kids has shrunk from a tenth of sales to a twentieth.

Within sports there are two kinds of store. A **multi-brand store** — Sports Station, Planet Sports, Foot Locker — carries Nike next to Adidas next to New Balance; MAPA chooses the assortment and owns the banner (or, for Foot Locker, licenses it). A **mono-brand store** — Skechers, Converse, New Balance, Crocs, Hoka, and from 2026 Nike and Puma — carries one brand and is run by MAPA as that brand's retail partner or exclusive distributor for the country. The company says it holds "40+" exclusive brands in Indonesia and "most of our top ten" are exclusive, but has never said which, nor what share of sales any brand represents. The 1H2026 footprint gives the only physical split: 1,882 sports stores on 418,000 sqm, 280 leisure on 39,000, 169 kids on 41,000.

Two things in this chain are worth holding onto. MAPA carries the stock: it buys the shoes outright, so unsold shoes are its problem, not the brand's, which is why inventory is 40% of everything the company owns and why §8.4 exists. And MAPA pays in dollars and sells in rupiah: the money it owes suppliers is about half in US dollars, and the group's net short dollar position at end-2025 was USD43 million (about Rp7 miliar of profit per 1% move in the rupiah on the balance sheet alone, before the effect on next season's purchase cost).

The garment factory in Gunung Putri (PT Mitra Garindo Perkasa, 300 machines, 160,000 pieces a month) makes apparel for the licensed brands from mostly local fabric; its output is about Rp280 miliar of cost a year, under 3% of goods sold.

The company owns almost no property. Its fixed assets are shop fit-outs — shelving, lighting, flooring, tills — written off over the life of the lease, plus a head office and the factory. The "right-of-use" line (ROU in the tables) is the accounting value of leases it has signed but not yet paid: since 2020 the rules require a retailer to show its future rent as both an asset (the right to occupy) and a debt (the obligation to pay). MAPA's leases are short — 72% of the rent still owed falls due within two years — which keeps the number moderate and means the store base can be shrunk quickly if it has to be.

What it has bought is listed in §09: a sister company from the parent (2016), a badminton brand (2017), the Philippine business (2020), Foot Locker's own Singapore and Malaysia stores (2023) and its Sports Direct partner's half of the Indonesian venture (2024) — about Rp500 miliar of acquisitions in ten years, small next to Rp4 triliun of fit-out spending. The 79 trademarks covering its own store names — Sports Station, Planet Sports, Kidz Station and the rest — were bought from MAPI before the IPO for Rp395 million in total.

### Process — what it costs to run the stores

Think of each Rp100 of sales. About Rp53 goes to buy the shoe (including Rp3.7 of royalty to a brand owner where the format is licensed). Of the remaining Rp47, rent and the wearing-out of the shop fit-out take about Rp13, store and office staff about Rp10, marketing Rp2.6, warehousing Rp1.6, the parent's management fee Rp1, and card fees, utilities, marketplace commissions, repairs, freight and the rest about Rp6. What is left — about Rp13 — is profit from running the business, before interest and tax (operating profit).

Three mechanisms behind the table. **The gross margin is set by the mix of buying and licensing.** Where MAPA buys from a brand's local wholesale arm (Nike Indonesia, Adidas Indonesia), the brand's profit is inside the purchase price and no royalty appears; where MAPA operates a licensed format, it pays a royalty on sales on top. Royalties rose by two-thirds as a share of revenue between 2019 and 2024, which is consistent with the growth of Foot Locker (licensed from 2021), Sports Direct, Aldo and Payless, and with brand owners charging more for the permission — the filings do not say which. **Occupancy is fixed for the lease term.** Rent is negotiated per store for two to ten years, so a bad year hits margin at once; in 2020, with sales down 36%, selling costs rose to 33% of revenue and operating profit fell to almost nothing. **Inventory is the swing cost.** Shoes bought for a season that do not sell are marked down or written down; the write-down charge has run at about Rp50 miliar a year since 2023, the allowance for stale stock has quadrupled since 2022, and the share of stock older than six months has gone from 17% before the pandemic to 29% at end-2025, back to 24% by mid-2026 (§8.4).

### Distribute — how a shoe is sold

Selling is done in person, in malls. Roughly nine-tenths of sales still happen in a store; online — the company's own sites and apps plus third-party marketplaces — was 10% of sales in 2025 and 11% in the first half of 2026, and the marketplace share of that online sale has risen from 29% in 2022 to 59%, which costs commission (sales commissions and marketplace fees went from 0.4% to 1.0% of revenue in three years). Customers pay cash or card; there is no meaningful credit and receivables are seven days of sales. The MAP Club loyalty programme is run at group level, so points and vouchers clear through the parent.

Prices are recommended by the brand owner and MAPA discounts to clear stock; the filings say nothing about pricing power. The state's take is 11% VAT on the sale (the 2025 rate for non-luxury goods) and import duty paid at the port, which MAPA prepays as income tax and structurally overpays — the import prepayment (Rp405 miliar in 2025) exceeded the parent company's whole tax charge in 2019 and 2024, which is why prepaid taxes sit permanently on the balance sheet.

No customer matters; a handful of suppliers matter enormously. No supplier is disclosed as more than 10% of purchases — the note measures it, oddly, against revenue — but the whole model rests on perhaps five global brand owners.

## 2.2 Non-retail — wholesale, concessions and the factory

The non-retail segment sells to department stores and other retailers (including SOGO, Seibu and Galeries Lafayette, which MAPI affiliates operate — about Rp43 miliar of sales a year through related parties) and supplies the group's own stores with garments from the factory. It reported Rp990 miliar of outside sales in 2025, down 43% on the year, and a segment profit of Rp152 miliar; a further Rp702 miliar of its output went to MAPA's own retail stores and is eliminated. The segment's outside sales had grown to Rp1.7 triliun in 2023–24 and then fell sharply in 2025 with no explanation in the annual report — the most likely reading is a reclassification of overseas wholesale into retail, but it is not stated.

# 03 — DEMAND

**What is being bought.** A pair of branded sports shoes is a discretionary purchase by an urban, mostly Java-based, middle-income consumer, bought in a mall, increasingly for daily wear rather than sport. Demand therefore tracks three things: how many people can afford a branded shoe costing a week or two of the Jakarta minimum wage, how often they visit a mall, and whether sport is in fashion. All three moved in MAPA's favour from 2021 to 2023 and have been mixed since.

**MAPA's own demand series.** Indonesian revenue grew 25% in 2023, 20% in 2024 and 14% in 2025; the first half of 2026 was up 15% at group level. The company has published same-store growth only four times — 8% in 2016, 11% in 2017, 8.5% in 2019, and (via the press) +2.4% in the fourth quarter of 2025 — so most of the growth is new stores, and the underlying like-for-like is probably low single digits. **The structural bull case, in the company's words**, is that ASEAN has "a productive demographic structure, the growth of the middle class, and strong domestic consumption". The structural bear case, from the statistics agency, is that Indonesia's middle class shrank by ten million people between 2019 and 2024, and MAPA's share price fell 40% in 2025 while its profit rose 27% — the market pricing the customer, not the company.

# 06 — VICISSITUDES

**The pandemic (2020–21).** Malls closed in March 2020. Revenue fell 36%, operating profit went from Rp974 miliar to about Rp40–80 miliar (the annual report and a later deck disagree), net profit to Rp4 miliar. The company drew a Rp493 miliar bank loan "to fund operations", suspended the dividend for FY2020 and FY2021, and let stock age: inventory days went to 290 and the share older than six months to 45%. Rent concessions of Rp35 miliar were still being booked in 2022. Recovery was fast — sales +26% in 2021, +62% in 2022 — and by 2022 the operating margin was 16%, the best on record, because the store base had been trimmed and the pent-up demand landed on a lower cost base. What a repeat would do: with occupancy and staff fixed at about 21% of a normal year's revenue, a 30% sales fall takes operating profit to roughly zero again; the difference from 2020 is that the company now has Rp1.5 triliun of cash and no bank debt, but also Rp5.7 triliun of stock instead of Rp2.3 triliun.

**The 2024 margin stumble.** Sales rose 27% and profit fell 3%. Four things stacked: the gross margin lost 2.6 points (royalties +42%, dollar payables doubling, goods-in-transit up 3.5×); overseas lost Rp359 miliar; Rp41 miliar of goodwill and store write-downs; and the parent's fee rose 51%. Without a change in depreciation estimates that flattered pre-tax profit by Rp57 miliar, profit would have fallen about 6%. The response in 2025 — fewer openings, data-led merchandising, overseas losses cut by three-quarters — produced +27%.

**The 2025 share-price fall.** The stock fell from a 2024 range of about Rp800–1,100 to Rp540–910 during 2025, closing the year at Rp670, while profit grew. The visible triggers were the parent group's April 2025 guidance cut (citing the Starbucks boycott at sister company MAP Boga and weak Nike and Converse demand), the middle-class shrinkage narrative, and — probably — the aged-stock ratio reaching 29%. MAPA's market value at end-2025 was Rp19 triliun against Rp8.8 triliun of book equity and Rp1.7 triliun of profit.

**The bond years (2015–19).** MAPA's reported profit in its first five years was distorted by the accounting for the CVC bond: a Rp508 miliar paper gain in 2015 on booking a Rp1.5 triliun interest-free loan at its discounted value, then Rp50–97 miliar a year of non-cash "interest" as the discount unwound, then a Rp244 miliar loss in 2018 when the terms were changed at the IPO. None of it was cash and none of it was tax-deductible, so the effective tax rate ran 31–40%. Underlying profit was about Rp130 miliar in 2015 and Rp630 miliar in 2018 `[calc]`. Anyone reading the 2015–18 series as printed will misjudge the trend (§8.1).

**What has not happened.** No brand loss has ever been disclosed as such; no store-closure programme; no rights issue; no impairment large enough to be named.

# 08 — SECTION

## 8.1 The CVC structure, 2015–2023: a loan, an option, and an IPO that repaid the loan

In March 2015 MAPI needed money and did not want to sell shares in its sports division cheaply. The solution was a private-equity structure in two parts. A Singapore vehicle, Asia Sportswear Holdings, lent Rp1.5 triliun for five years at zero interest, guaranteed by MAPI, and the loan was transferred (novated) into the newly created MAPA along with the business. A Cayman vehicle, Montage Company Ltd, received an option to buy 30% of MAPA from MAPI, exercisable only if MAPA listed. Neither filing names the investor; the company's decks say "2015: MAA partners with CVC Capital Partners" and the press reported both vehicles as CVC's.

An interest-free loan is worth less than its face value, so the accountants booked it at Rp992 miliar and recorded the Rp508 miliar difference as a profit in MAPA's first year, then charged the difference back as "interest" of Rp50–97 miliar a year while the loan aged. The lender's return was never going to come from the loan — it got back exactly Rp1.5 triliun — but from the option.

So the "public offering" of 2018 was, in cash terms, CVC's equity vehicle paying Rp889 miliar to MAPA and MAPA paying Rp889 miliar to CVC's debt vehicle the same year: a conversion of debt into 14.85% of the equity at Rp2,100 a share, with 0.15% of the company sold to anyone else. Real public shareholders arrived nine months later when Montage exercised its option and sold three-quarters of its position at 3.3–3.5 times the IPO price. Known proceeds to Montage: at least Rp5.5 triliun against a known cost of Rp889 miliar plus an undisclosed option price paid to MAPI. The two CVC executives on MAPA's board (both at some point "Independent Commissioner", each chairing the audit committee) were both recorded as having "no affiliation" with any shareholder; the last of them resigned in June 2023, the month after Montage's block sale.

What this cost the minority: nothing directly — the bond went in and out at face — but it means the 2015–18 profit series is unusable as printed, the IPO price was set in a transaction between connected parties rather than by open bidding from investors, and the reference "float" that governed index inclusion and liquidity until 2019 was fictitious.

## 8.2 The parent: fee, guarantees, covenants — and the 2026 change of control

MAPI's take from MAPA comes through four doors. **The management fee** (§1.5): Rp193 miliar in 2025, 1% of revenue, basis undisclosed, not in the related-party table. **The bank lines**: of the roughly Rp6 triliun and USD600 million of facilities listed in the 2025 agreements note, most are contracted by MAPI for "MAP and its subsidiaries" with leverage tests on MAPI's consolidated numbers; MAPA is a co-borrower on lines shared with seven MAPI fashion subsidiaries; and in 2025 the corporate guarantees on the Vietnam facilities moved from MAPI to MAPA. Every facility is reported "not utilised" at each year-end even in years when the statements show Rp350–730 miliar drawn. **Shared infrastructure**: MAP Club loyalty points, the vouchers, the department-store concessions, and — per the fee description — "licensing", which may mean MAPI holds or negotiates some brand relationships on MAPA's behalf; no filing says. **Dividends**: 68.83% of Rp114–143 miliar a year.

The change of control. On 8 May 2026 Pacific Universal Investments, a Singapore company "structured with CVC Capital Partners", bought SMGG's 51% of MAPI at Rp1,395 a share (Rp11.8 triliun) and then, in the compulsory offer to buy out remaining shareholders that Indonesian rules require after a change of control (a mandatory tender), from 18 June to 17 July at Rp1,550, took up another 36.24% (Rp9.3 triliun), ending at 87.24%. Looking through, the vehicle now controls about 60% of MAPA `[calc: 87.24% × 68.83%]`. MAPA's 2025 annual report was signed on 15 April 2026 and so predates the deal; the 24 June 2026 AGMS minutes are not in hand. Open questions that no source answers: whether the change of control at the parent triggers a similar buy-out obligation at MAPA (the rule that a change of control higher up a chain can count as one lower down has exemptions whose application here is not disclosed); whether the fee agreement, the guarantees and the board change; and who the ultimate beneficial owner statement will now name.

## 8.5 What the brand owners take

Royalties are the visible part of the brand owners' take: Rp722 miliar in 2025, 3.7% of revenue, up from 2.2% in 2019, paid on sales in licensed formats. The invisible part is the wholesale margin inside the purchase price of goods bought from Nike, Adidas and the rest. Two things in the record say the visible part is growing because the mix is shifting to licensed and franchised formats — Foot Locker from 2021, Sports Direct and Aldo from 2023, Payless throughout — rather than because rates rose: the royalty line jumped 81% in 2023, the year of the Foot Locker acquisitions and Sports Direct launch; and the licensed formats are exactly the ones expanding overseas. If so, the model is drifting from "buy Nike, sell Nike" toward "run someone's store for a royalty", which carries a lower gross margin, a fixed store obligation and a renewal date. The filings never say which agreements expire when; the prospectus said none renew automatically.

# 09 — IMPORTANT TRANSACTIONS

## 2015 — the carve-out and the bond
MAPI moved its sports, golf, kids and lifestyle business (Rp2.46tn of assets, Rp2.33tn of liabilities including the new bond) into MAPA for Rp218bn of shares, Rp94bn above book value. Effect: MAPA started life with Rp1.5tn of interest-free debt and a Rp508bn paper profit (§8.1).

## 2016 — Mitra Gaya Indah bought from MAPI
Rp180bn cash for the Staccato/Linea (later Birkenstock, Dr Martens) subsidiary, Rp6bn above book; MAPI had injected Rp110bn and taken a Rp25bn dividend out of it just before the sale. The same year MAPA paid MAPI Rp156bn of dividends. Net, cash moved up to the parent.

## 2018–19 — IPO, option exercise, placement
427.56m new shares at Rp2,100 plus 42.84m to management at the same price (three-quarters loan-funded; the recipients sold half their shares once the loans were repaid in 2019); 99% of the offering to Montage; 90% of proceeds to the bond. In April 2019 MAPI sold 417.7m shares to Montage at an undisclosed price and Montage placed ~627m for Rp4.55tn, taking the free float from 0.66% to 23% (§8.1). The 2019 AGMS reshuffled the board without stated reasons and skipped the dividend despite a record Rp693bn profit.

## 2020 — Philippines and Thailand
Planet Sports Inc (Philippines, operating since 1999) bought through a Singapore shell for Rp58bn (Rp52bn goodwill); the Thai company bought from MAPI for Rp49bn (Rp15bn above book, charged to equity — visible only in a later share-premium note). Neither is described in the 2021 annual report beyond "acquisition of overseas subsidiary".

## 2021 — Foot Locker
Licence for Indonesia, Thailand and the Philippines (September 2021); Nicholas Jones, 32 years at Foot Locker, arrives as Vice President Director a month earlier and becomes President Director in June 2023 when the founding President Director, Michael Capper, resigns; Capper held no shares by the end of 2023. Foot Locker Indonesia's assets are Rp730bn by 2025.

## 2023 — the expansion year
Foot Locker's own Singapore and Malaysia companies bought from Foot Locker for Rp233bn (Rp26bn goodwill on Singapore; Malaysia bought for Rp12bn less than its assets were worth, booked as a gain; Rp27bn of goodwill impaired in 2024, entity not named). Sports Direct JV with Frasers (50%, Rp5bn). An unnamed partner paid Rp152bn for 49% of the two Aldo companies. Cambodia entered. Montage exited. 1:10 split. First dividend since FY2016 (Rp114bn, 9.7% of profit). The Philippine minority (ASG Holding) exchanged its stake for Rp30bn of notes settled in shares.

## 2024 — Sports Direct bought out; the board widened
The Sports Direct partner's 50% acquired for Rp5bn eight months after MAPA lent the venture USD10m at 7.75% (Rp162bn per the MD&A; Rp79bn per the audited cash flow — never reconciled) and after MAPA repaid Rp81bn of the venture's bank loan. Three board appointments: Juliani Gozali (ex-MAPI commissioner 2004–14, Gajah Tunggal), Sintia Kolonas (ex-MAPI director; shares the stated ultimate owner's surname), Sameer Prasad (MAP since 2014; holds 31m shares, probably from the 2018 management plan — not stated).

## 2026 — the parent changes hands
Pacific Universal / CVC to 87.24% of MAPI (§8.2). Not yet in any MAPA filing.

# 10 — RED FLAG

## The IPO that was not one
99% of the 2018 offering went to the option-holder's vehicle and the cash went straight back to the bond-holder's vehicle; the prospectus said "15% to the public" and that the company had "no affiliation" with the bondholder. Free float 0.66% for nine months. Evidence: FS2019 note 18 register; note 16 repayment figure; §8.1.

## No brand contract has ever been disclosed
For Nike, Adidas, Skechers, New Balance, Converse, Crocs, Lego, Foot Locker, Sports Direct or Aldo: no counterparty, territory, term, royalty or expiry, in any filing since 2015. The 2018 prospectus admitted a standing breach of non-compete clauses. Brands disappear from subsidiary tables (Skechers, Nike, Adidas, Puma from Vietnam in 2024) without a word. Evidence: STRUCTURE and AGREE2025 notes; §2.1, §8.5.

## The parent's fee: undisclosed basis, +51% in a down year, missing from the related-party table
Rp193bn in 2025, 11% of profit to owners. Disclosed to OJK at Rp115bn on restatement in June 2023, charged at Rp175bn in 2024. Evidence: FS notes 26 and 34d; §1.5.

## "Not utilised" facilities that were used, and guarantees moving downhill
Every bank facility is reported undrawn at each year-end while the statements show Rp350bn (2019), Rp493bn (2020), Rp731bn (2023), Rp492bn (2024) and Rp538bn (mid-2026) of loans; six 2024 facilities were left out of the 2024 report; covenants are tested on MAPI, not MAPA; MAPI's guarantees on the Vietnam lines became MAPA's in 2025. Evidence: AGREE2025 §4; §7.2.

## The ultimate-owner statement and the surname
"Marisa Kolonas" is asserted as ultimate owner in five filings with no chain; a commissioner of the same surname was "affiliated" in 2024 and "not affiliated" in 2025. The founder question is answered only by a 2019 press quote. Evidence: §1.1, §1.4.

## Partners paid and bought out without a price
Rp152bn in from an unnamed Aldo partner (2023); the Philippine minority out for Rp30bn of notes; Sports Direct's half in for Rp5bn plus Rp160bn of loans; a Singapore subsidiary from 90% to 100% and a Philippine one from 95% to merged, unexplained. Evidence: §7.3, §09.

## Disclosure that shrinks and arithmetic that slips
A 2021 annual report with no financial-statement notes; IPO proceeds printed as "Rp897,876 billion" in three reports; Philippines/Cambodia store counts swapped; cash-flow opening and closing rows transposed; a subsidiary marked "dormant" while its brand opens stores. None is material alone; together they say the annual report is not read carefully before it is signed.

## Impairments with no name
Rp27bn of goodwill (2024), Rp14bn (2024) and Rp70bn (2025) of store assets written down; the note says the only unit it tests (the Philippines) is worth more than its book value. Which countries, which stores: not stated.

## Directors' dealings without explanation
The President Commissioner bought 13m shares around the 2023 split and sold 10m in 2024; the founding President Director sold most of his shares in 2022 and held none by the end of 2023, the year he resigned; the 2018 plan's recipients halved their holdings the year their loans fell due. Evidence: §3.8 of the narrative pack; registers in AR2019–25.

# MISSING DATA AND OPEN ITEMS

Ranked by how much each would change the analysis.

1. **The brand agreements.** Counterparty, territory, term, expiry, royalty rate and minimums for the brands that earn the revenue. Most likely to exist in the licence agreements themselves and in OJK material-transaction filings if any exceed 20% of equity; otherwise only management can say. It would decide how much of the gross margin is at the brand owners' discretion and when.
2. **The 2026 change of control's consequences for MAPA.** The 24 June 2026 AGMS minutes, any OJK disclosure on chain-principle tender obligations, and the first filing after July 2026. It would decide whether the fee, the guarantees, the board and the ultimate-owner statement change.
3. **Country-level profit.** Only an aggregate overseas loss (from the tax note) exists. The Philippine, Thai and Singapore statutory accounts (SEC Philippines, ACRA) would show which countries lose money and how much of the 2024 loss was one-off. It would decide whether overseas is a growth option or a drain.
4. **The MAPI management-fee formula** and the OJK affiliate-transaction disclosure of June 2023 (Rp115bn). It would decide whether the fee is a fixed cost, a percentage of sales, or discretionary.
5. **The Montage option exercise price** (April 2019) and who bought the 2018 IPO shares other than Montage. IDX/KSEI records; the 2019 MAPI annual report. It would size what MAPI received for 30% of MAPA.
6. **FY2020–21 audited notes** (the 2021 annual report has none) — on the company website as separate FS filings; it would close the only gap in the eleven-year series.
7. **Same-store sales and sales by brand/format/channel** — never disclosed; it would separate like-for-like from store growth.
8. **The ownership chain** above SMGG before May 2026 and the owners of Pacific Universal after it — MAPI's filings and Singapore company records.
9. **Partner identities and prices**: the Aldo partner (Rp152bn), the Sports Direct buy-out, the Philippine minority, the Singapore 90→100% — FS note 29 of AR2024 and OJK affiliate filings.
10. **Which bank facilities were drawn** each year and at what cost; the lender of the 2019 bridge; the six 2024 facilities omitted from AR2024.
11. **Impaired units** (2024 goodwill, 2024–25 store assets); the 2022 subsidiary disposal (Rp11bn gain); reasons for every board change 2018–2024 and per-person pay.
12. **Market data**: a reliable Indonesian sports-retail market size and MAPA share (the 63% figure is a blog's); per-capita sportswear spend across ASEAN; footwear safeguard-duty terms; a year-end Rp/USD series.
13. **Whether Kanmo Group or MAPA runs Adidas retail in Indonesia** — the web says Kanmo, MAPA's own brand pages say MAPA, and Adidas dropped out of two overseas subsidiaries' brand lists in 2024.
14. **Headcount 2025, shop-in-shop counts, distribution-centre locations and the warehousing provider.**
