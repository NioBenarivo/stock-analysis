## The argument in one page

AALI is not the company its 5-year highlights table implies. Revenue rose 31% in FY2025 to
Rp28.65 trn and net profit rose 28% to Rp1.47 trn, which reads as a recovery. Underneath:

1. **The estate is old and getting older, and the company has stopped fighting it hard enough.**
   65.9% of planted hectares are now over 15 years old, up from 49.0% in 2021. The productive
   4–15 year band has collapsed from 123,794 ha to 76,292 ha — a 38% loss of the estate's prime.
   Average age 16.7 years. Replanting ran at 5,080 ha in FY2025 against a stated target of 8,000
   ha and a nucleus base of 208,063 ha; at 5,080 ha the cycle is 41 years, at 8,000 ha it is 26.
   Own FFB production is 3.82 m t against 5.02 m t in 2019.

2. **So the company buys the difference.** Third-party FFB was 2.41 m t in FY2025 — **38.7% of
   everything the mills processed** — and on top of that AALI buys bought-in CPO to fill its
   sales book. Sales of CPO and derivatives were 1,761 kt against CPO production of 1,198 kt.
   `[calculated]` roughly a third of the palm oil AALI sells, it did not grow. That is why
   "raw materials used and processing costs" is **76.3% of cost of revenue** and why gross margin
   sits at 16% while a pure planter like TAPG runs above 30%.

3. **FY2025's profit was a price event, and the best part of it was kernels, not CPO.** CPO ASP
   +11% to Rp14,316/kg. Palm kernel and derivatives revenue **+92%** to Rp3.12 trn on +16% volume
   — a realised price move of roughly +66% `[calculated]`. Kernel went from 7.5% to 10.9% of
   revenue. This is the same global lauric-oil squeeze that lifted TAPG's kernel prices 52%. It is
   not a franchise improvement and it does not repeat.

4. **A Rp572 billion forestry fine is sitting inside G&A, and the annual report's governance
   chapter says there were no material legal cases.** Note 31 (Operating Expenses) shows a
   **Legal** line of Rp572,073 m in FY2025 (FY2024: Rp81,707 m), described verbatim as
   *"administrative fines arising from regulatory changes in spatial planning within the forestry
   sector."* That is **25.7% of profit before tax** `[calculated]`. The narrative chapter discloses
   a Satgas PKH notification under Perpres 5/2025 and PP 45/2025 and says all subsidiaries have
   paid — with no amount. The two disclosures are never connected.

5. **The balance sheet is now debt-free and the company is shrinking its asset base.** All bank
   debt repaid in FY2025 (Rp3.19 trn paid down; zero borrowings at 31 Dec 2025). But capex was
   Rp782 bn against depreciation of Rp1.42 trn — **capex/depreciation 0.55×** `[calculated]` — and
   total assets fell 6% to Rp27.05 trn. Fixed assets fell 8%; the AR's own words are that
   additions were "lower than the depreciation expense recognised." This is a company harvesting
   its installed base and paying the proceeds out at a 45% payout ratio.

What follows sets out the ownership, then the physical model, then the unit economics that produce
those five conclusions.

## 1.5 Related party transactions

**TL;DR :** AALI's related-party exposure is almost entirely *revenue*, not cost. Between 20% and
32% of everything AALI sells goes to its own two joint ventures. There is no management fee, no
royalty, no licence, no brand fee and no intercompany guarantee anywhere in five years of notes.
The purchase side is trivial — Astra group equipment and insurance, under 1% of COGS. This is a
clean related-party profile by Indonesian standards; the issue is concentration, not extraction.

### The KJA loan — closed out

KJA owed AALI a foreign-currency long-term receivable at **LIBOR + 2.50%**, unsecured, with no
fixed repayment schedule: Rp456,462 m (2020) → Rp418,963 m (2021) → Rp220,234 m (2022) → **repaid
in full on 25 August 2023**. A 50%-owned JV funded by the parent at a floating spread with no
repayment date is exactly the kind of arrangement worth watching; to AALI's credit it was closed
and has not reappeared.

### Pricing

AALI's five annual reports never describe the price-setting mechanism. The 2016 prospectus does,
and nothing suggests it has changed: CPO and PK are sold to Indonesian refiners, oleochemical
producers and large traders through **periodic domestic auctions** — highest bidder, payment before
delivery — and the resulting auction price is the domestic CPO benchmark. Pricing references CIF
Rotterdam, MDEX, domestic supply/demand and export taxes/levies. Sales are invoiced in Rupiah but
priced off USD. Export sales of refined products are routed through **Astra-KLK in Singapore**.

AALI is a **price-taker on 38.7% of its own feedstock** and cannot compress this line.

**4 — Fertiliser.** Inventory drawn down 74% from the 2022 peak. ASTEMIC biofertilizer applied on
51,345 ha (18% of the estate), claimed to cut chemical fertiliser use by up to 25%.

**5 — The export levy and DMO.** AR2025: *"the Government increased export levies on palm oil and
its derivative products"* to fund the biodiesel programme. **No rate, no Rp/tonne, no total paid
is disclosed in any of the five annual reports.** DMO/DPO has been in force since 2022 —
compliance is a precondition for export entitlement — and again no ratio or price is given. For a
company with 36% export volume this is a significant undisclosed cost.

**6 — Rupiah.** Net monetary assets in foreign currency at 31 Dec 2025: **US$128,592 k** plus

**7 — Plantation age.** Covered in §2.7. The slowest-moving and least reversible variable.

| Free cash flow ÷ net profit to owners | 1.86× | 0.26× | 1.23× | 2.13× | **2.33×** |
| Dividends ÷ operating cash flow | 10.8% | 49.8% | 32.0% | 15.0% | **14.6%** |
| Dividends ÷ free cash flow | 14.5% | 200.4% | 62.5% | 20.8% | **18.0%** |

Cash conversion is excellent — 2.7× net profit — because depreciation is large, capex is small and
customers pay in advance. And unlike TAPG, which distributed **99.3% of operating cash flow** in
FY2025, **AALI paid out only 14.6%**. The Astra model is to keep the cash. Over five years AALI
generated Rp16.86 trn of operating cash flow, spent Rp5.57 trn on capex, repaid Rp5.51 trn of
debt, and paid Rp3.38 trn of dividends.

That leaves the obvious question for FY2026: **with no debt left to repay, capex at 0.55× of
depreciation and a Rp16 bn forward commitment book, what is the cash for?**

## 3.6 Scenario arithmetic

Two scenarios, both worked line by line, both using only figures printed in the filings.

### Scenario A — kernel prices revert to FY2024

The FY2025 result was carried by the lauric complex. What happens if it normalises?

```
FY2025 kernel & derivatives revenue                        Rp3,121,148 m
FY2025 kernel & derivatives volume sold                        247.35 kt
FY2025 implied realised price      3,121,148 ÷ 247,350  =   Rp12,619 /kg   [calculated]

FY2024 kernel & derivatives revenue                        Rp1,622,284 m
FY2024 volume sold                                             213.69 kt
FY2024 implied realised price      1,622,284 ÷ 213,690  =    Rp7,592 /kg   [calculated]

Price change                                                      +66.2%

Revenue at FY2024 price, FY2025 volume:
  247,350 t × Rp7,592/kg                                 =  Rp1,878,081 m
Actual FY2025 kernel revenue                                Rp3,121,148 m
                                                           ─────────────
REVENUE FOREGONE                                            Rp1,243,067 m

Kernel is a joint product of FFB already harvested and milled — the incremental
cost of producing it is close to zero, so treat ~100% of the revenue change as
gross profit.

Profit before tax as reported                               Rp2,226,728 m
Less kernel reversion                                      (Rp1,243,067 m)
                                                           ─────────────
ADJUSTED PBT                                                  Rp983,661 m      −55.8%

At the FY2025 effective rate of 30.9%:
  Adjusted net profit                                        ~Rp679,710 m
  Adjusted EPS                                                   ~Rp353      vs Rp765
```

**A full reversion in kernel prices removes 56% of pre-tax profit.** This is a bigger single
exposure than TAPG's, where the same reversion costs ~13% of earnings, because AALI's base
profitability is thinner.

### Scenario B — normalising both one-offs, to find underlying earnings

```
Profit before tax as reported, FY2025                       Rp2,226,728 m

Add back:  incremental legal fine (572,073 − 81,707)          Rp490,366 m
Add back:  asset disposal loss (new line, no 2024 base)        Rp97,589 m
Add back:  plasma net deterioration (139,120 − 39,643)         Rp99,477 m
Deduct:    kernel price windfall (per Scenario A)          (Rp1,243,067 m)
                                                           ─────────────
UNDERLYING PBT                                              Rp1,671,093 m

vs FY2024 reported PBT                                      Rp1,707,128 m
                                                           ─────────────
UNDERLYING CHANGE                                                  −2.1%
```

**On this basis FY2025 was flat, not up 30%.** The reported improvement is a kernel windfall,
partly consumed by a forestry fine. The recurring earnings power of AALI in a
Rp14,316/kg-CPO year is around **Rp1.67 trn pre-tax, ~Rp1.15 trn net, ~Rp600/share**.

`[calculated]` throughout; the add-backs are my judgement of what is non-recurring, and a reader
who thinks the forestry fines will repeat annually (Satgas PKH is an ongoing programme) should
strip that add-back out, which takes underlying PBT to **Rp1,180,727 m** — 31% below FY2024.

risk is that the cliff already arrived and the response has been to buy fruit instead of plant it.

## 3.10 What would change this view

Things worth checking that the filings do not answer:

1. **The Satgas PKH exposure.** Rp572 bn was paid in FY2025 under Perpres 5/2025. Is that final, or
   is it the first tranche? How many hectares were reclassified as forest area, and does AALI
   retain the right to plant them? The report says the fines are settled; it does not say the land
   issue is settled.
2. **How much CPO does AALI actually buy?** The ~582 kt estimate in §2.3 is my arithmetic. A direct
   disclosure of purchased CPO volume would resolve whether the trading leg is a strategic
   position or a margin-dilutive habit.
3. **Why are both joint ventures losing money?** Rp10.15 trn of AALI's share of JV revenue produced
   a Rp77.5 bn loss. ASK is a Singapore trading company and KJA is a refinery. Both being in loss
   in a year of rising prices is odd and unexplained.
4. **The export levy.** Never quantified in five annual reports. On 36% export volume this could be
   several hundred billion Rupiah a year.
5. **What happens to the cash.** No debt, capex at 0.55× depreciation, Rp16 bn of commitments,
   45% payout. Either the payout rises, or capex rises, or the cash pile grows — and the
   FY2026 targets guide to zero expansion.
6. **PT Rimbunan Alam Sentosa** lost 73% of its assets in one year with no disclosure.
7. **RSPO certification.** Member since July 2024, due diligence in 2025, still uncertified. EUDR
   compliance runs through traceability that AALI's 38.7% third-party FFB base makes structurally
   hard. This is the medium-term market-access risk and the four-item risk register omits it.
