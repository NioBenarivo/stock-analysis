**What it does:** DRMA is an Indonesian Tier-1 automotive-parts maker in the Triputra Group. It stamps, welds, machines, moulds and wires parts for motorcycles and cars, and it sells most of them to Astra-group assemblers:

- **Two-wheeler (2W), 62% of FY2025 revenue:** motorcycle frame bodies, swing arms, steering handles, stands, grab rails, mufflers, fasteners, control cables, wiring harnesses, plastic body parts and helmets.
- **Four-wheeler (4W), 23%:** suspension members, hanger beams, cross-members, hood locks, seat frames, pedals, control cables, plastic interior trim (since the Nov-2025 Mah Sing acquisition) and battery casings.
- **Other, 16%:** tools, dies and jigs (DPT), plastic parts for electronics makers (Stanley, Epson), aftermarket parts, PowerAce three-wheelers, and EV/energy products (battery packs, battery-energy-storage systems (BESS), chargers, brushless DC motors).

The group has 9 subsidiaries and 2 equity-accounted JVs: Sankei Dharma (exhausts, with Sankei Giken of Japan) and Dharma Kyungshin (wiring harnesses, with Kyungshin of Korea). It runs about 13 plants on about 50 ha in Cikarang/Bekasi, Karawang and Cirebon. **PT Astra Honda Motor (AHM) alone bought 53.6% of FY2025 revenue.** The controlling shareholders are Dharma Inti Anugerah (47.60%) and Triputra Investindo Arya (14.16%). Both are held about 99.99% by **Like Rani Imanto**, whom the filings style "Ny. T.P. Rachmat", i.e. Mrs. T.P. Rachmat. T.P. Rachmat, the ex-Astra executive who founded the Triputra Group, sits on both holding companies' boards but not their share registers.

**Market:** Rp1,010/share (~23-Sep-2026) × 4,705,882,300 shares = **Rp4,753bn market cap** [calculated]. P/E is **7.3x FY2025 and 6.8x trailing to Jun-2026** (7.4x excluding the Rp50.9bn bargain-purchase gain). P/BV is 1.71x. Dividend yield is **6.9%** (Rp70 FY2025 DPS). The company holds **Rp314bn of net cash** (Dec-2025). EV/EBITDA is about **4.3x**. All [calculated], §3.6.

**Period covered:** FY2023–FY2025 audited (FY2023 only as the comparative/highlights column of AR2025), FY2018–1H2021 from the IPO prospectus, FY2021–FY2022 from AGM/public-expose decks only, and 1H2026 from the investor presentation plus news (the 1H2026 interim financial statements were not retrieved).

**Sources in hand:** 1 annual report (FY2025, 364 pages, with audited FS), the Dec-2021 IPO prospectus (447 pages), and 14 decks from 2022 to Jul-2026 (AGM, public expose, company profile, investor presentations). **No FY2021–FY2024 annual report is in hand.** The full source table is at the end.

**Conventions:** Rp bn unless stated. "Attrib." means attributable to owners of the parent. "Core" means excluding the negative-goodwill (bargain-purchase) gains on acquisitions and the FY2021 land-sale gain. `[calculated]` marks every figure derived here rather than printed in a filing.

## THE ONE THING TO UNDERSTAND FIRST

**DRMA is a Honda-motorcycle business that describes itself as a diversified auto-parts and EV group. The motorcycle side makes the money. So far, everything added around it has diluted margins.**

| FY2025, Rp bn | Revenue | % of group | Gross profit | GM | % of group GP |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Two-wheeler | 3,660.8 | 61.6% | **783.6** | **21.4%** | **73.2%** |
| Four-wheeler | 1,341.8 | 22.6% | 187.6 | 14.0% | 17.5% |
| Other (tools, plastics, aftermarket, 3W, EV/BESS) | 937.1 | 15.8% | 99.3 | 10.6% | 9.3% |
| **Total** | **5,939.7** | 100% | **1,070.5** | **18.0%** | 100% |
| *of which sold to PT Astra Honda Motor* | *3,182.8* | *53.6%* | | | |

*Segment note 36, AR2025; shares [calculated].*

Four things follow:

1. **One customer is more than half the business, and its segment is three-quarters of the profit.** AHM's share of revenue rose from 50.1% (FY2024) to 53.6% (FY2025) as AHM purchases grew 15.4% against group revenue growth of 7.8% [calculated]. AHM's supply agreement with DRMA runs to **31-Mar-2028**. It lets AHM **reallocate orders without compensation** for quality or delivery failures (Note 32). At the IPO, AHM was already half of trade receivables (prospectus, Jun-2021).
2. **2W growth has come from more DRMA content per motorcycle, not from market growth.** DRMA's 2W revenue per national motorcycle wholesale rose from about **Rp410k (FY2022) to Rp571k (FY2025), +39%**. National wholesales rose 23% over the same period (5.22m → 6.41m units, AISI) [calculated, §2.2]. The additions are new parts (wiring, smart keys, sensors, plastics, cables via subsidiaries) and deeper Honda sourcing. This is the one real compounding engine in the group.
3. **The diversification is a lower-margin business.** 4W revenue fell from Rp1,818bn (FY2023) to Rp1,342bn (FY2025), −26%, as national car wholesales fell 20% (1.006m → 0.804m units). 4W gross margin dropped from 16.1% to 13–14%. "Other" earns about 10.6%. The EV, battery, 3W and solar lines ("Dharma Connect") are not separately reported. Their revenue sits inside "Other" at the group's lowest margin. The battery-recycling subsidiary (DER) still has Rp25m of assets two years after it was founded.
4. **Core profit has been flat for three years, while cash and dividends have grown.** Core attributable EPS was Rp118 (FY2023), Rp123 (FY2024) and Rp128 (FY2025) [calculated]. That is about +4% a year, on revenue that went sideways from Rp5.5tn to Rp5.9tn. Meanwhile CFO exceeded net income (128% cumulative FY2023–25). Bank debt fell from Rp510bn to Rp279bn, and DPS rose from Rp36.4 to Rp70. **In a flat business the return to shareholders has come from payout, not growth.** 1H2026 (revenue +13.8%, net profit +17.5%) is the first sign of that changing. Much of the 4W jump (+36%) coincides with consolidating Mah Sing, which was acquired in Nov-2025 (§3.2).

**What the price says:** at 6.8x trailing earnings, 4.3x EV/EBITDA, with net cash and a 25% after-tax return on invested capital (§3.5), the market is pricing DRMA as a **cyclical supplier to a single customer**, not a compounder. That is a fair description of the risk. It looks harsh on the evidence of ten years of rising content per Honda motorcycle.

**Governance observations:**

- **This is an Astra-alumni board serving an Astra customer base.** Five of the ten board members built careers at Astra companies: Irianto (Gaya Motor), Soekamto (AHM, Federal), Darmawan (Astra Otoparts, TAM, TMMIN), Immanuel (AHM) and Hadi Kasim, via Triputra, whose founder was Astra's former President Director. DRMA's four largest customers by receivable are all Astra-linked or Honda-linked (AHM, Astra Daihatsu, TMMIN, HPM). Each director individually declares "no affiliation". **That declaration is formally correct and commercially beside the point.** The relationship with Astra is the moat, and it is also the concentration.
- **The independent commissioners are thin.** There are 2 independents out of 5 commissioners after Rachbini left. The Audit Committee chair, Soekamto, ran the Pako group, a Triputra affiliate that Hadi Kasim (President Commissioner and a DIA director) had previously run. The label "independent" is doing limited work. There is **no separate Nomination and Remuneration Committee**: the board says the company's scale doesn't require one.
- **Two current executives double as controller-group executives.** Hadi Kasim is a DIA director. Immanuel Pakaryanto is the Triputra Group's head of human capital and sits on its nomination/remuneration operations. The controller's HR head sets DRMA's pay framework.
- **Pay is reasonable relative to profit, but the two disclosures don't agree.** Note 30 (FS) gives key-management compensation of **Rp63.1bn (FY2024) and Rp51.5bn (FY2025)**: commissioners Rp6.2bn and directors Rp45.4bn. The AR governance chapter (p.208) gives **commissioners Rp1.84bn and directors Rp13.89bn (Rp15.7bn)** for FY2025. The 3.3x gap is unexplained. It probably reflects subsidiary-board pay or bonuses included in the FS figure. **On the FS figure, pay is 7.9% of attributable profit** [calculated], against 49–77% at KINO.
- **Auditor:** KAP Tanubrata Sutanto Fahmi Bambang & Rekan (BDO), partner Arrie Oktoberry. Fee Rp720m. Opinion unqualified; **the only Key Audit Matter is the MSI purchase-price allocation**. There is no KAM on revenue, inventory or receivables. Receivables carry **zero expected-credit-loss allowance**. Prior-year audit tenure is not disclosed.
- **Litigation and sanctions:** none disclosed at the IPO or in FY2025.
- **Drafting quality is sloppy in places.** The AR2025 RPT section refers readers to "Note 30 of the financial statements of PT Federal International Finance", an unrelated Astra finance company, so the text was evidently templated from an Astra document. The highlights table swaps the gross and net margin rows. The dividend table repeats the FY2024 profit figure for FY2023. None of this is material. All of it is a sign of how carefully the non-audited sections are checked.

## 2.1 Product

DRMA is a **build-to-print contract manufacturer**. It wins a part on a new motorcycle or car model through the OEM's tender. The OEM designs the part and usually owns the tooling (AHM's contract includes penalty clauses for tooling custody). DRMA then makes it to schedule for the model's life, typically 4–6 years. Price is set per part at award and revised with the OEM. **DRMA owns no vehicle brand and no core IP in its main business.** It has 3 registered trademarks at the IPO (class 12). The exceptions are its own small lines: PowerAce three-wheelers, the "DC Battery" auxiliary battery, BESS units and helmets.

The balance sheet is so under-geared that rates barely matter.

## 2.7 Demand

- **Motorcycles (2W):** Indonesia is the world's third-largest motorcycle market. Wholesales were 6.49m units (2019), **3.66m (2020)**, 5.06m (2021), 5.22m (2022), 6.24m (2023), 6.33m (2024) and **~6.41m (2025)**, back to 2019 levels. AISI expects 2026 to be stable. Early 2026 ran slightly above 2025 (1.1m in Jan–Feb). Demand is financed: roughly 70–80% of bikes are bought on credit through multifinance companies (industry estimate), so **multifinance credit standards and rates are the lead indicator**. Honda holds about 76% of the market (2025). DRMA's exposure is to Honda's share times the total market times its content per bike.
- **Cars (4W):** Gaikindo wholesales were 1.03m (2019), 0.53m (2020), 0.89m (2021), **1.05m (2022)**, 1.01m (2023), 0.87m (2024) and **0.80m (2025, −7.2%)**. The 2026 target is 850,000. A new regional "opsen" vehicle surtax from 2026 raises ownership costs. BEV sales reached 103,931 in 2025 (about 12% of the market, per AR2025), mostly Chinese brands imported CBU under duty exemptions that **expired 31-Dec-2025**.
- **The EV shift cuts both ways for DRMA.** Chinese EV makers (BYD, VinFast, Wuling, Chery) must now build locally under a **1:1 local-production commitment (2026–27)** and rising TKDN (local content) targets: BYD is said to commit 60% local content from 2027, VinFast 80%. As of late 2025, **no local sourcing deals had been signed** with Indonesian component makers (GIAMM, via Bisnis). The share of the market held by Japanese brands, i.e. DRMA's Astra customers, is being eroded by Chinese EVs. **DRMA's 4W franchise is tied to the incumbents.** Management names the new entrants as a 2026 growth driver. Nothing in the filings shows a contract with any of them.
- **Electric motorcycles:** about 17,000 units in 2025 (0.3% of the 2W market). Negligible for now. Frame, swing arm and handle content survives electrification, while mufflers do not. The muffler business sits partly in DRMA and partly in SDI.
- **Hyundai/Kia US exports via DKI:** the one real export growth line. DKI's revenue rose 19% to Rp844.8bn. It is exposed to US tariff policy on Korean-brand vehicles assembled in the US from imported harnesses (not analysed in the filings).

## 3.8 Roadmap and what to watch

1. **2W gross margin in 3Q2026.** 3Q25 was 19.3% for the group and 22.5% for 2W. If 3Q26 2W margin stays at about 19%, the pass-through lag has become a squeeze (possibly AHM cost-down plus steel and rupiah). If it recovers to 21–22%, 1H was just timing.
2. **MSI's contribution and 4W organic growth.** Ask for MSI's revenue and margin. Strip it out of 4W growth to see whether DRMA is gaining share with the Astra 4W assemblers or just buying revenue. Also watch whether the MSI pension liability (Rp41.5bn) turns into restructuring cost.
3. **New Chinese and Vietnamese EV OEMs.** Management names BYD, VinFast and others as growth drivers. Any signed supply contract would be the first diversification away from the Astra and Honda ecosystem that matters at group scale. Nothing is disclosed yet.
4. **The AHM contract renewal (31-Mar-2028)** and the DPP-AHM contract (Mar-2028). Previous renewals were routine (2013, 2018, 2023). A shorter term, new cost-down clauses or dual-sourcing language would be the warning sign.
5. **Capital allocation.** The FY2025 payout was 50.5%. Cash was Rp658bn at Mar-2026. The Jababeka and MM2100 land and factory purchases will absorb some of it. Watch whether management keeps buying cheap foreign-owned sub-scale plants (the TCH/MSI pattern) or raises the payout further. A 60–70% payout is affordable.
6. **DKI (Hyundai/Kia Alabama).** Watch DKI revenue (+19% in FY2025), DRMA's related-party sales to DKI (Rp183bn), and US tariff treatment of Korean-brand vehicles assembled in the US from imported parts.
7. **Dharma Connect / EV lines.** Ask for revenue disclosure. PowerAce dealer targets were cut from 100 dealers by 2025 (PUBEX 2023) to 53. DER (battery recycling) is dormant. Until these lines report numbers, treat them as marketing.
8. **Governance housekeeping.** Reconcile the pay disclosures (Rp51.5bn vs Rp15.7bn). Watch the undocumented 2026 board changes (Yosaphat off the deck board slide, Dian Eka Hartiningsih on), the Negara-line share trades, and continued sales by the founder-era executives Iwan and Hadi, who have trimmed since the IPO.
9. **Receivables ECL.** Balances more than 90 days overdue rose from Rp1.4bn to Rp12.0bn with no allowance. This is immaterial now; it should not keep rising.

# SUMMARY VERDICT

**What it is.** DRMA is the Rachmat family's automotive-parts maker. It is, in substance, **Astra Honda Motor's principal outside supplier of motorcycle chassis and metal parts**, with a smaller and lower-margin 4W business, two partner-controlled JVs and a set of early-stage EV/energy products bolted on. About 54% of revenue and about 80% of operating profit trace to Honda motorcycles [calculated, §3.5]. It owns no brand of consequence and no proprietary technology. It owns a 30-year incumbency on AHM programmes, a disciplined cost base (selling + G&A at 6.4% of revenue) and a clean balance sheet (net cash, no covenant stress).

**What it earns through the cycle.** Across FY2018–25 the gross margin ranged from 11.8% (COVID) to 18.2%, and core net margin from 0.4% to 10.8%. At today's scale (Rp5.9tn revenue) a normal year is about **Rp600bn of core attributable profit (Rp128/share), 25% ROIC, and Rp550–650bn of free cash flow**. FY2023–25 was a plateau. A falling car market cancelled out rising content per motorcycle. **1H2026 (+17.8% attributable profit) is the first sign of the plateau breaking.** About half of the 4W part of that growth is the MSI acquisition.

**What has to be true for it to work.**

- Honda keeps about 75% of Indonesia's motorcycle market, and AHM keeps outsourcing frames and chassis to DRMA. The contract runs to Mar-2028.
- Content per motorcycle keeps rising: +11.6% a year over FY2022–25.
- OEM price revisions keep passing through steel, copper and the rupiah within a quarter or two.
- Management keeps paying out about 50% rather than reinvesting in sub-scale acquisitions and EV projects at a fraction of the 2W return.

**What would break it.**

1. **An AHM sourcing change**: in-sourcing, dual-sourcing or a major model loss. That is 54% of revenue under one contract that allows reallocation without compensation.
2. **A motorcycle credit crunch** (2020 showed −44% volume and a near-zero profit year).
3. **A sustained input-cost shock** that OEMs refuse to recover: 5% of materials unrecovered is 25% of profit.
4. **Diworsification.** Capex running at 1.4–1.7x depreciation into 4W, battery and 3W lines that earn 4–8% operating margins, plus cheap-but-weak acquisitions, would slowly dilute a 25% ROIC business.

**Position.** On the filings, **DRMA at Rp1,010 is cheap for its quality and fairly priced for its concentration.** It trades at 6.8x trailing earnings, 4.3x EV/EBITDA and a 6.9% dividend yield, with net cash and a 25% after-tax ROIC. The narrow FY2026 range (EPS Rp127–145, §3.7) means the downside over the next year is mostly already in the price. The upside needs either 2H2026 to repeat 2H2025's 11.6% net margin on higher revenue (scenario A, about +13% EPS) or a re-rating toward SMSM-type multiples, which the Honda dependence argues against. **Our reading: undervalued on returns and cash, with most of the discount justified by one customer.** It is a **high-yield hold with operating leverage to the motorcycle and car cycle**, not a structural compounder. The two things that would change that view are a named contract with a new (Chinese/Vietnamese) OEM, or EV/energy revenue disclosed at a margin above 10%. Governance is better than the IDX median. RPTs are minimal and pay is proportionate, and the controller has not sold. The independent board is thin and Astra/Triputra-networked. The cleaner bull case would be a controller that keeps raising the payout rather than buying more sub-scale plants.

*This is an analytical view of the business from its filings, not investment advice. I'm not a financial advisor.*
