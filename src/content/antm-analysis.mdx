*(legal name Perusahaan Perseroan (Persero) PT Aneka Tambang Tbk; traded as PT ANTAM Tbk from 2017 until the 2026 return to "Persero")*

**What it does.** Indonesia's state-owned diversified miner and, much more importantly by sales, the
country's dominant seller of gold bars. Four businesses sit under one name:

1. **Gold and silver.** A small underground mine at Pongkor (West Java), a refinery in Jakarta that
   is the only one in Indonesia on the London bullion market's "Good Delivery" list, and a national
   sales network for "Logam Mulia" gold bars. Almost all of the gold it sells is bought from others.
2. **Nickel.** Open-pit laterite mines in South-East Sulawesi, North Maluku and on Gag Island (West
   Papua). Most of the ore is sold to other companies' smelters inside Indonesia; the rest feeds
   ANTAM's own ferronickel plant at Kolaka, whose output is exported.
3. **Bauxite and alumina.** A bauxite mine and a chemical-grade alumina refinery at Tayan (West
   Kalimantan).
4. **Minority stakes** in bigger nickel, alumina and electric-vehicle-battery projects run with
   partners — Tsingshan and Eramet at Weda Bay, the CATL group in East Halmahera, and the state
   aluminium company Inalum at Mempawah.

**Where.** Head office Jakarta. Operations in West Java, Banten, South-East Sulawesi, North Maluku,
West Papua and West Kalimantan.

**Size.** FY2025 revenue Rp84.6 trillion; profit attributable to ANTAM's shareholders Rp7.2 trillion.
First half 2026 (press, company figures): revenue Rp62.7 trillion, profit Rp6.9 trillion. Market
capitalisation Rp75.7 trillion at 31 December 2025 (annual report); about Rp78.6 trillion at Rp3,270
on 11 September 2026 `[calculated on 24.03bn shares]`.

**Period covered.** Audited accounts FY2020–FY2025 continuously; operating data FY2019–1H2026;
press and regulation to 11 September 2026.

**Sources in hand.** Five annual reports (FY2021–FY2025), six ASX half-year and full-year activity
reports (FY2021 to 1H2026), six investor presentations (Public Expose 2021, Mandiri Investment Forum
2022, FY2023, FY2024, FY2025, 1Q2026). The upload list also named a 2019 Corporate Presentation and a
2020 Public Expose deck; neither reached the workspace. Full list in *Sources*.

## THE ONE THING TO UNDERSTAND FIRST

ANTAM is listed and priced as a mining company. By what it sells, it is mostly a gold shop.

About four fifths of ANTAM's revenue is gold. But in 2025 its own mine produced only about 2% of
the gold it sold — under one tonne, against more than 37 tonnes sold. The rest was bought in:
until 2024 mostly from foreign bullion banks, dealers and refiners in London, Singapore and Australia, and since
November 2024 increasingly from PT Freeport Indonesia's new refinery in Gresik. Freeport is a sister
company — both sit under the same state mining holding, MIND ID. ANTAM takes that gold, turns it
into branded bars in Jakarta, and sells them to Indonesians through wholesalers, its own
boutiques, an app, and two state institutions, Bank Syariah Indonesia and the pawnshop company
Pegadaian.

A shop earns the gap between what it pays and what it charges. For ANTAM that gap has been about
a tenth of the selling price, and out of it come the costs of refining, shipping, insuring and
selling. That makes the gold business very large and quite thin. Its profit depends on how many
tonnes it can move, on the price it pays its suppliers (never disclosed, including for the
Freeport contract), and on whether the gold price rises or falls while bars sit in stock. In 2025 supply ran short: ANTAM sold about 29 tonnes in the first half and only about 8 in the
second, a fall that showed up across the third quarter and deepened after a mud rush at Freeport's Grasberg
mine in September.

The actual mining profit comes from somewhere else: nickel ore. Nickel is less than a fifth of
sales, yet in 2025 it earned about 29 sen of operating profit on every rupiah sold, against about 9
sen for gold. The nickel future, however, is being shared out. Half of ANTAM's nickel reserves sit in
one subsidiary, PT Sumberdaya Arindo, 49% of which was sold in 2023 to a company in the CATL
battery group. The downstream plants being built on that ore — a smelter, a high-pressure acid
leach plant, battery materials, battery cells — are majority-owned by the CATL group; ANTAM holds 40% of the smelter company and 30% of the HPAL
company directly, and the Indonesian side's share of the battery companies may sit partly with a
state battery holding rather than with ANTAM. ANTAM's own ferronickel plant in East Halmahera, paid for by a 2015 share issue,
has never left "commissioning" and has been written down three times.

Above both businesses sits an owner that uses the company as a policy tool. The state has made ANTAM
the supplier to its new bullion banks, the buyer of Freeport's gold, the local partner in the
battery project, and a dependable dividend payer — 100% of profit for FY2023 and FY2024. Board seats
change hands frequently and increasingly go to former police, military and intelligence officers
and ministry officials.

So three different engines move ANTAM's profit: the volume and spread of a gold shop, the price and
government quota of nickel ore, and one-off items — write-downs on a state-mandated smelter, a Rp1.4 trillion legal provision released
in 2025 when an old gold-bar dispute turned in ANTAM's favour, and a Rp1.1 trillion "regulatory cost"
that the accounts do not explain.

Where the evidence sits: the gold shop in §2.1 and §8.1; nickel's weight in §8.2; the CATL
arrangement in §8.3 and §9.3; the owner in §1.4; the one-off items in §7.5 and §10.

The company states it has **no individual ultimate beneficial owner**: control runs to the
Republic. There is no family here to trace. The questions that matter instead are which state
bodies give instructions, and which of ANTAM's counterparties are also state-owned (§1.5).

Two things about this tree are worth holding on to.

**The chain grew a layer in 2025.** Until 2017 the state held ANTAM directly. From 2017 its 65% was
parked in the aluminium company Inalum as the head of a mining holding; from March 2023 it moved to
MIND ID; in 2025 the sovereign fund Danantara was inserted above MIND ID. The 2017 and 2025 steps each added a layer between the state and the listed company.

**The subsidiaries that matter most are not all fully ANTAM's.** Sumberdaya Arindo holds about half
of the group's nickel reserves (§8.4), and 49% of its profit now belongs to the CATL group. The
stakes in the right-hand column are "associates" — companies ANTAM part-owns but does not control.
Their sales and costs never appear in ANTAM's revenue; only ANTAM's share of their profit does, as
one line below operating profit.

## 1.3 History — recap

For most of its life ANTAM was a mining company that also refined gold. Over the last decade
three things changed its shape.

First, the gold refinery became the main business. Indonesians bought far more gold bars than ANTAM
could mine, so it bought gold abroad and sold it at home, and gold grew from about 70% to about 80%
of revenue.

Second, nickel stopped being something ANTAM exported and became something it sells to other
people's smelters inside Indonesia — first because ore exports were banned, then because the
Chinese-built smelter industry in Sulawesi and Halmahera needed ore.

Third, the state began using ANTAM as an instrument of policy: a rights issue to build a smelter,
a holding company above it, stakes sold to CATL to anchor a battery industry, Freeport's gold
routed through it, and a return to "Persero" status in 2026. The operating company underneath has
changed less than the structure above it.

## 1.4 Owner's character

There is no controlling family, so "character" here means what the state's recorded decisions
show. Six patterns stand out.

**Management is appointed from outside and does not stay long.** In December 2021 every director and two of the five commissioners were replaced in one meeting. In 2025 the President Director
changed twice: Nicolas Kanter (a former head of Vale Indonesia) left in June; his successor,
Achmad Ardianto, was removed in December; the current President Director, Untung Budiharto, is a
retired army general whose last posts were commander of the Jakarta military command and a senior
role at the counter-terrorism agency. The board of commissioners had three different compositions
during 2025. In June 2026 the finance director changed again.

**Board seats increasingly go to the security apparatus and ministries.** Recent commissioners
include a retired three-star police general (who also serves as special staff to the Energy
Minister), a former secretary-general of the state intelligence agency, a special staffer to the
head of the intelligence agency, a former member of the state audit board who was earlier a member
of parliament, and senior deputies from the economic coordinating ministry. Some left because
they were appointed to other state companies — one to the board of Bank Mandiri. Two of the five
commissioners have mining careers; the President Director does not.

**The owner sets pay, and had not done so.** Commissioners' and directors' pay is decided by MIND
ID. At the time the FY2025 annual report was issued, MIND ID had not yet decided 2025 pay; the board
was still being paid under the previous year's letter.

**It takes cash out when it can.** The dividend payout rose from 35% of profit (FY2020) to 50%
(FY2021–FY2022) to 100% (FY2023 and FY2024), then 70% for FY2025. In 2024 dividends exceeded the
cash left after investment (§7.4); in 2025 ANTAM borrowed US dollars again.

**It gives ANTAM national jobs.** Build a smelter in East Halmahera (2015). Keep the gold price
stable for households. Anchor the CATL battery project. Buy Freeport's gold so it is not exported.
Supply the new bullion banks. Several of these have cost ANTAM money: the East Halmahera plant has
been written down three times (§10.4), and a power contract signed for it was cut to three years so
the site could serve the battery project.

**Filing discipline has been poor.** The ASX suspended trading in ANTAM's securities for late
reports roughly ten times between 2020 and 2025 — five times in 2023 alone and again in March–April
2025 for the late FY2024 accounts. The FY2025 legal section still says the directors "faced no legal
cases in 2023", a sentence carried over from an old report.

Four channels matter.

**Freeport's gold.** In 2025 ANTAM bought Rp19.7 trillion of gold from PT Freeport Indonesia — about a
third of all the gold and silver it bought, and the single largest reason related-party purchases
jumped to 40% of cost of revenue. The contract (7 November 2024) sets a minimum of 30 tonnes a year.
It was treated as ordinary business, so it went through none of the independent-review steps that
Indonesian rules require for affiliated transactions. What ANTAM pays per gram is not disclosed
anywhere (§10.2).

**State institutions as gold customers.** Bank Syariah Indonesia bought Rp9.3 trillion of gold in
2025, up from Rp0.6 trillion in 2020; Pegadaian bought Rp4.2 trillion. Both launched state "bullion
bank" services in February 2025. Together with one private reseller, PT Sinar Inti Maju, these three
buyers took about 27% of ANTAM's revenue in 2025.

**MIND ID Trading, the holding's Singapore trading arm.** It was set up to market the holding's
exports. In 2022 it bought 79% of ANTAM's ferronickel. By 2024 that had fallen to almost nothing,
and ANTAM now sells ferronickel directly to Korea, China and India. In the other direction, ANTAM
bought Rp5.9 trillion of goods from MIND ID Trading in 2025; the accounts describe this only as
"purchases of goods for production activities".

**Associates.** Sales of bauxite to Borneo Alumina Indonesia (40% ANTAM, 60% Inalum) reached
Rp0.95 trillion in 2025 — and at year-end Borneo Alumina owed ANTAM more than the whole year's sales
(§10.6). Earlier, the gold miner Nusa Halmahera Minerals (25%) was both a supplier and a customer.

Key management pay is modest by the size of the business: about Rp47–56 billion a year for the two
boards combined in 2021–2024.

## 2.1 Precious metals and refining

### Product

What ANTAM sells here is mostly **branded gold bars** — 99.99% pure, stamped "Logam Mulia", in sizes
from fractions of a gram upward, plus themed and gift editions. Around the bars sit smaller
products: silver, jewellery, refining services for other miners, and "BRANKAS", a service that
stores customers' gold in ANTAM's vault. By value, gold bars are essentially the whole segment:
silver was Rp0.2 trillion and refining services Rp0.2 trillion of the Rp66.8 trillion in 2025.

What the customer is paying for is not the metal alone — they could in principle buy gold
anywhere — but the brand and the certainty that it is real. The Jakarta refinery's place on the
London Bullion Market Association's Good Delivery list is what makes an ANTAM bar the default
savings bar in Indonesia and gives it a resale market at pawnshops and banks.

### Purchase — the mine ANTAM owns

Cibaliung mine closed in 2022 and its licences have been relinquished; the Papandayan prospect has

contained about 5.2 tonnes of gold — around seven years at 2025's rate, and the reserve was
lengthened that year mainly because output fell; a higher assumed gold price added ore tonnes but
barely any contained gold.

Three things fall out of this table.

The spread is roughly a tenth, and has been for years. In most years ANTAM sold its gold for 5–8%
more than the world price, which is the premium a household pays for a small, branded bar.

2024 was the exception. ANTAM sold a record 43.8 tonnes, but kept only about half its usual spread
per gram. The accounts show the stock of gold and silver more than doubling that year, so some of
that year's purchases were metal not yet sold — but even allowing for that, it was a year of high
volume and poor margin, when ANTAM was paying up for imported gold.

In 2025 the spread per gram widened sharply while the realised price fell slightly *below* the
world average. The likely explanation is timing: ANTAM sold almost four fifths of its 2025 volume
in the first half, when the gold price was lower, then ran short of supply in the second half as
the price rose. Stock bought earlier and sold later at a higher price inflates the spread. That is a
windfall of a rising market, not a permanent improvement.

### Distribute — channels, customers and share of market

ANTAM sells gold entirely at home. Wholesalers and resellers take the majority; its own boutiques
about a quarter; the app and online channels a growing share — 20% of tonnes in the first quarter
of 2026, up from 5% a year earlier.

Until 2023 the MD&A claimed ANTAM held 53–60% of Indonesia's domestic gold "absorption" (World Gold
Council data): 28 tonnes of 47 in 2021, 26 of 45 in 2023. That claim does not appear in the FY2024
or FY2025 reports or in any 2025–2026 presentation.

Customers are becoming concentrated and state-linked. In 2025 the three largest named buyers took
27.5% of all revenue:

year later only Rp0.9 trillion. Money received before delivery is cheap working capital; losing it
is one reason cash lagged profit in 2025 (§7.4).

### Distribute — government take

Because the gold is mostly bought, the royalty — up to 16% of value since April 2025 — applies only
to the small amount mined at Pongkor. Gold bars are exempt from VAT. A new export duty on gold of
7.5–15%, effective 23 December 2025, makes it less attractive for Indonesian refiners to export,
which should push more domestic output towards ANTAM.

## 2.2 Nickel

### Product

Indonesia's nickel sits in laterite — a red, soft, wet ore near the surface. It comes in two
layers. The deeper **saprolite** is richer (ANTAM's reserves grade roughly 1.3–1.9% nickel) and goes to rotary-kiln electric furnaces (RKEF) that make ferronickel or nickel pig iron for stainless steel. The
upper **limonite** is poorer and goes to high-pressure acid leach (HPAL) plants that make
battery-grade nickel.

ANTAM sells two things: **nickel ore**, all of it to smelters in Indonesia (exports have been banned
since 2020), and **ferronickel** from its own plants, all of it exported. The mix has flipped: in
2020 ferronickel was 71% of nickel revenue; in 2025 it was 14%.

### Purchase — the smelters

ANTAM owns two ferronickel plants. **Kolaka** (27,000 tonnes of nickel a year) is the workhorse; even if it made all of 2025's
ferronickel, it ran at only about 60% of capacity. **East Halmahera** (13,500 tonnes) was funded by the 2015 rights
issue, first tapped metal in September 2023, and has been labelled "commissioning" in every
presentation since. Its power contract with the state utility PLN was cut from thirty years to three
in May 2023 because the surrounding land was earmarked for the CATL battery project. It was written
down by Rp0.5 trillion in 2023, Rp0.1 trillion in 2024 and Rp1.1 trillion in 2025 (§10.4).

On both plants' combined capacity, 2025 output of 16,064 tonnes was about 40% utilisation. Sales
were lower still — 10,528 tonnes — because, in the company's words, "adjustments to regulations related to minimum
product selling prices" held shipments back and stock piled up. Ferronickel inventory tripled in 2025.

### Process — cost

Mining laterite is simple and done largely by contractors: strip the soil, dig, stockpile, truck
and barge. The cost lines that matter are contractor fees, fuel and — increasingly — royalty. The
government's cut on ore rose from 10% of value (2022 rules) to a sliding 14–19% (April 2025),
depending on the nickel price. Group-wide royalties in cost of revenue (all commodities) went from
Rp0.4 trillion in 2020 to Rp2.0 trillion in 2025.

Ferronickel is the opposite: a furnace business whose largest cost is energy. ANTAM disclosed a
cash cost exactly once, for 2023: about US$14,400 per tonne of nickel. That year it sold ferronickel
at an implied US$14,860 per tonne — a margin of about 3% before depreciation `[calc]`. No cash cost
for ore, gold or bauxite has been disclosed in any document in this set.

### Distribute — nickel ore

The price of every tonne of ore sold in Indonesia is anchored by a government benchmark, the HPM,
which sets a floor based on nickel content, the LME price and a correction factor. ANTAM's MD&A
says it sells high-grade ore at "significant premiums". From 15 April 2026 the benchmark formula
also counts iron, cobalt and chromium in the ore; industry estimates quoted in the press put the
effect at a 100–140% rise in the benchmark. ANTAM's first-quarter 2026 ore price of US$66.8 per wet
tonne was already a quarter above its 2025 average.

How much ANTAM can sell is set by its annual mining work plan (RKAB), approved by the Energy
Ministry. The national approved quota was cut from about 379 million tonnes in 2025 to 250–260
million for 2026. ANTAM's 2025 output was about 4% of the 2025 quota `[calc]`; its 2026 target (16.2
million tonnes) is flat. In the first half of 2026 it produced 7.8 million tonnes.

Buyers are not named. The one visible arrangement is at Gag: PT Universal Metal Investment prepaid
US$45m for future ore, and Gag pledged part of its stake in Jiu Long Metal Industry as security.

### Distribute — ferronickel

All exported, priced off the LME with adjustments, historically through MIND ID Trading and now
direct to stainless-steel mills. The destination mix swings sharply: China 54% in FY2024; South
Korea 67% in FY2025; China 43% in 1Q2026 (presentations).

## 2.3 Bauxite and alumina

### Product

Two products from one site at Tayan, West Kalimantan: **washed bauxite** (aluminium ore) and
**chemical-grade alumina** — a refined white powder used in water treatment, ceramics and refractories
rather than for making aluminium metal. The alumina is exported to more than a dozen countries.

### Supply chain and process

```
  Tayan bauxite mine ──► washing ──┬──► Indonesia Chemical Alumina, Tayan (300,000 t/yr, Bayer process)
                                   │        └──► chemical-grade alumina, export (Asia, Middle East, Europe)
                                   └──► domestic buyers since the June 2023 export ban,
                                        increasingly Borneo Alumina Indonesia's SGAR refinery at Mempawah
                                        (40% ANTAM / 60% Inalum; commercial 1Q2026)
```

The Tayan refinery was a long-running problem. Built with Japanese loans, it lost money, was written
down by Rp1.3 trillion in 2021, and was in default on principal to its Japanese lenders from 2020;
those loans disappear from the 2023 accounts without a disclosed settlement. The bauxite and alumina segment
has made an operating profit every year since 2022, and the refinery ran at about 61% of capacity in 2025.

### Distribute

Since raw bauxite exports were banned in June 2023, bauxite is sold only inside Indonesia. The
natural buyer is the new SGAR smelter-grade alumina refinery at Mempawah, which is 40% ANTAM's and
reached commercial operation in the first quarter of 2026 after slipping from a first-half 2025
target. Volumes hit records in 2025 (2.8 million tonnes of bauxite mined, 182,000 tonnes of alumina
made), but the segment is only 3% of revenue.

# 03 — DEMAND

## 3.1 Gold

**Globally,** gold has been in a historic rally. The average price was about US$1,800 an ounce in
2020–2022, about US$2,390 in 2024 (LBMA averages, approximate), then US$3,431 in 2025 and US$4,135 in the
fourth quarter of 2025 alone (World Gold Council). ANTAM's implied selling price in the first half of 2026 — about Rp2.8 million
a gram `[calc: Rp50.39tn gold revenue ÷ 18,080 kg]` — was more than half as high again as its 2025
average. Global demand for bars and coins rose 16% in 2025 while jewellery demand fell 18%: people
bought gold to hold, not to wear.

**In Indonesia,** gold bars are a mass-market savings product. ANTAM's own estimate of national
"absorption" was about 45–47 tonnes a year in 2021–2023. Demand was strong again in early 2026
(press reports, citing the World Gold Council, of bar and coin demand roughly doubling quarter on
quarter in a first quarter, likely 2026), although exact
country tonnages were not obtained for this report.

**Three things are growing the domestic market ANTAM sells into:**

1. *Rising prices have pulled in buyers* rather than deterring them — clearest in 2024's record
   volume; 2025's lower volume was a supply problem, not a demand one.
2. *State bullion banks.* Bank Syariah Indonesia and Pegadaian launched "bullion bank" services in
   February 2025 — gold savings accounts, gold-backed loans and custody. By early 2026 they reported
   about 153 tonnes of gold under management combined. They need physical bars, and ANTAM is the
   primary-market supplier.
3. *Policy that keeps gold at home.* The export duty from December 2025 and the Freeport offtake
   both route domestic gold towards the domestic market.

What would reduce demand is a sharp fall in the gold price — households that bought at the top sell
bars back — or a weaker rupiah that makes gold unaffordable.

## 3.2 Nickel

**By use,** stainless steel still takes most of the world's nickel (historically about two thirds);
batteries are the growth use, slowed by lower-than-hoped electric vehicle growth and by battery
chemistries that use no nickel (LFP). A clean 2020–2026 split was not obtained for this report.

**For ANTAM the relevant demand is Indonesian smelter demand for ore.** Chinese-backed smelters in
Sulawesi and Halmahera have been built faster than local mines can supply them. The clearest proof
is imports: Indonesia imported 15.8 million tonnes of nickel ore in 2025, 97% from the Philippines,
and imports from the Philippines rose another 65% in the first half of 2026 after the quota cut.
Ore is scarce inside Indonesia even though Indonesia has more ore than anyone. That scarcity is what
lets ANTAM sell high-grade ore at premiums and why its ore price rose in early 2026.

# 04 — SUPPLY

## 4.1 Nickel

**Indonesia is the world's nickel supply.** It mined about 2.2 million tonnes of nickel in 2024 — 59%
of the world total (USGS). The next producer, the Philippines, mined about one seventh as much. The
market has been in surplus for about five years, which is why the LME price fell from US$25,800
(2022) to about US$15,000 (2025) despite Indonesia's export ban.

**The government now manages supply through four levers**, and all four moved in 2025–2026:

| Lever | What changed | Effect on ANTAM |
|---|---|---|
| Quota (RKAB) | National approved ore output cut from ~379 Mt (2025) to 250–260 Mt (2026); RKAB returned to one-year approvals (Sept 2025) | Caps volume; raises ore prices for everyone with a quota |
| Benchmark price (HPM) | From April 2026 the floor price also counts iron, cobalt and chromium; press estimates +100–140% | Raises the minimum price ANTAM can charge for ore |
| Royalty | PP 19/2025 (April 2025): ore 10% → 14–19%; ferronickel 2% → 4–6%; gold to up to 16% | Takes a larger share of any price rise |
| Forest-area fines | Decree 391/2025 (December 2025): Rp6.5 billion per hectare of nickel mining in forest without a permit | A one-off charge; likely the source of ANTAM's Rp1.09 trillion "regulatory cost" (§10.3) |

**Where ANTAM sits among producers.** ANTAM's 16 million tonnes of ore in 2025 was about 4% of the
national quota. It is a mid-sized ore seller with unusually long reserves and state ownership, not a
dominant producer. The large listed nickel producers — Vale Indonesia (INCO), Merdeka Battery (MBMA),
Harita (NCKL) — run their own processing at scale; a quantified peer comparison was not obtained
for this report.

## 4.2 Gold supply to ANTAM

The gold ANTAM sells comes from three places, and their relative size is shifting:

| Source | What it is | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Pongkor mine | ANTAM's own | 0.7 t in 2025, declining |
| PT Freeport Indonesia, Gresik | Precious metals refinery attached to Freeport's copper smelter; nameplate 50–60 t/yr | Minimum 30 t/yr to ANTAM from Nov 2024. Guided ~28 t for 2025; about 12 t bought `[calc]`. After the Grasberg mud rush (8 Sept 2025) the refinery ran at roughly 15 t/yr; 26 t guided for 2026 — "100% offtaken by ANTAM" (Freeport CEO, press) |
| Merdeka group | Domestic gold miner | 6 t/yr supply agreement signed 2025 (MD&A) |
| Imports | London, Singapore and Australian banks, dealers and refiners | Were the main source 2020–2024; now the balancing item |

A government that wants gold to stay at home, a sister company whose output is committed to ANTAM,
and an export duty on everyone else's gold all make ANTAM the natural collection point for
Indonesian gold. The cost is dependence: when Freeport's supply fell, ANTAM's only quick substitute was imports, and
sales fell by about three quarters between the first and second halves of 2025.

## 4.3 Bauxite

Indonesia banned raw bauxite exports in June 2023. Domestic refining capacity is still being built,
so bauxite miners without a refinery lost their market. The new smelter-grade refinery at Mempawah
(SGAR, targeting about 1 million tonnes of alumina in 2026) exists to feed Inalum's aluminium smelter. ANTAM is better placed than most: it owns the
Tayan refinery and 40% of SGAR, so its bauxite has two domestic homes.

# 06 — VICISSITUDES

ANTAM has not made a loss since 2015, but its profit has moved around a lot, and the causes have
been as much policy and one-offs as prices.

| Period | Shock | What it did to ANTAM |
|---|---|---|
| 2014–2015 | Ore export ban (2014) and weak nickel price | Losses; no dividends for two years; the state-backed rights issue followed in 2015 |
| 2020 | COVID; nickel ore export ban from 1 January | Revenue fell from Rp32.7 trillion (2019, per two presentations; a third prints the years reversed) to Rp27.4 trillion; ore sales fell to 3.3 Mt; profit Rp1.1 trillion |
| 2021 | Recovery; Tayan refinery write-down | Revenue +40%, but a Rp1.3 trillion write-down on the alumina refinery cut gross margin to 16.5% from 20% before it |
| 2022 | Nickel price spike (LME squeeze); first gold-bar lawsuit provision | Highest nickel and ferronickel prices, though nickel profit was below 2021; Rp1.0 trillion legal provision booked |
| 2023 | Nickel price fell; fewer gold tonnes (26 t) | Revenue −11%; East Halmahera plant written down; profit held up by ANTAM's share of Weda Bay's profit and the Feni Haltim sale gain |
| 2024 | Late quota approvals (ore sales 45% of target); expensive imported gold | Record 43.8 t of gold at half the usual spread; gross margin fell to 9.4% |
| 2025 | Gold rally; ore output highest in a decade; gold supply squeeze; Gag suspension | Profit doubled to Rp7.9 trillion; one-offs netted to slightly negative; second-half gold sales ~8 t |
| 2026 | National quota cut; new benchmark formula; a further royalty rise reported but not confirmed | Ore price up; gold volume down (18 t in 1H vs 29 t in 1H2025) but profit up a third on price |

**The pattern.** ANTAM's two businesses tend to offset each other. Nickel and gold have rarely had a very bad year together (2023, when both fell, is the exception), and the gold shop's profit comes from spread rather than the level of the price. The
worst combination would be a fall in the gold price while ANTAM holds a large stock bought at high
prices, at the same time as a slump in nickel ore prices and another year of quota delays. On 2025
volumes, a 10% fall in gold (stock loss about Rp0.5 trillion) with ore US$10/wmt lower (about Rp2.0
trillion) would remove roughly a quarter of 2025's pre-tax profit before any one-offs `[calc]`.

cash-pooling arrangements with state banks; the limits of that exposure are not disclosed.

# 09 — IMPORTANT TRANSACTIONS

## 9.1 The 2015 rights issue and the East Halmahera plant

ANTAM raised Rp5.38 trillion in November 2015 by issuing new shares at about Rp371 each, Rp3.49 trillion of it from the state, to build a 13,500-tonne ferronickel plant at Buli, East Halmahera. Construction
dragged: the plant was reported 98% complete at end-2021 but did not tap metal until September 2023.
Rp1.1 trillion of the proceeds was still unspent at end-2021 and Rp0.2 trillion at end-2025, now
reassigned to working capital. The plant has been written down in 2023, 2024 and 2025 and is still
"commissioning" (§10.4).

## 9.2 From Persero to holding company and back

In November 2017 the state moved its 65% into Inalum and ANTAM dropped "Persero" from its name. In
March 2023 the stake moved to MIND ID. In 2025 the new state-enterprise laws (No. 1 and No. 16 of 2025)
put Danantara above MIND ID and redefined state enterprises to include companies where the state
holds the golden share; the FY2025 accounts say ANTAM "once again" qualifies. Shareholders approved
the name PT ANTAM (Persero) Tbk in December 2025; the ministry approved it on 13 February 2026. The
practical consequence is supervision: state-enterprise status brings audit authority for the state
audit board (BPK) and ministry rules on pay, appointments and dividends.

## 9.3 East Halmahera to the CATL group, 2022–2023

- **Aug–Sept 2022:** East Halmahera nickel licences moved at book value into two new subsidiaries,
  Sumberdaya Arindo (the larger reserve) and Nusa Karya Arindo.
- **16 Jan 2023:** agreement to sell 49% of Sumberdaya Arindo to Hong Kong CBL. **4 May 2023:**
  agreement to sell 60% of Feni Haltim (the industrial-estate company).
- **28 Dec 2023:** both closed. Sumberdaya Arindo: Rp6.45 trillion cash (about US$417m) plus the
  reserve earn-out — about twelve times the subsidiary's book value `[calc]`. Because ANTAM kept
  control, the Rp5.76 trillion gain went to equity. Feni Haltim: Rp0.78 trillion, booked as a
  Rp0.57 trillion profit because control was lost.
- **22 Dec 2023:** HPAL joint-venture agreement (ANTAM 30%); effective June 2025.

## 9.4 Jiu Long Metal Industry, October 2024

Gag Nikel — whose old Contract of Work obliges it to support downstream processing — paid US$102.5m
for 30% of Jiu Long, a Tsingshan-group nickel pig iron plant. About three fifths of the price was
goodwill. On the same day Gag took a US$45m prepayment for future ore from PT Universal Metal
Investment, secured by a pledge of 13% of Jiu Long's shares, and lent Jiu Long US$18m. Jiu Long's
2025 profit attributable to the stake was Rp15 billion on a Rp1.8 trillion carrying value.

## 9.5 The Freeport gold agreement, November 2024

A sale and purchase agreement between two MIND ID companies: Freeport sells ANTAM at least 30 tonnes
of refined gold a year from its Gresik refinery. ANTAM classified it as routine business. No price,
discount or term is disclosed. In its first full year it supplied Rp19.7 trillion — a third of
ANTAM's gold purchases — — about 12 tonnes at ANTAM's average purchase price `[calc]`, well under the
30-tonne minimum, with supply falling from the third quarter and cut further by the Grasberg accident.

## 9.7 Management changes

The December 2021 extraordinary meeting replaced every director; Nicolas Kanter came from Vale
Indonesia to run the company. The June 2025 annual meeting removed him and several commissioners,
created a Commercial Director post (filled by a 35-year-old internal finance executive) and made
the HR director President Director. The December 2025 extraordinary meeting removed that President
Director after six months and appointed a retired army general, alongside a new board of
commissioners chaired by Irwandy Arif, a mining professor and former President Commissioner of Bukit Asam. The ASX

## 9.8 The gold-bar lawsuits and the Budi Said reversal

Starting in 2019–2020, several buyers who had paid for gold at ANTAM's Surabaya boutique sued for bars
never delivered. The criminal cases that followed centred on a broker and boutique staff offering gold below the
official price. The largest claimant, Surabaya businessman
Budi Said, won in the civil courts up to a first Supreme Court review in 2023, which ordered ANTAM to
deliver 1,136 kg of gold or its value. ANTAM's provision for the gold-bar cases as a group — of which Budi Said's was the largest — was
Rp1.0 trillion in 2022 and Rp1.5 trillion by 2024.

The criminal track ran the other way: Budi Said was convicted of corruption and money laundering
(a 16-year sentence was upheld when his appeal to the Supreme Court was rejected in July 2025, per
press). On 14 March 2025 a second Supreme Court review annulled his civil victory. ANTAM released
Rp1.38 trillion of the provision into 2025 profit.

ANTAM separately sued to cancel the related purchases and recover 5.9 tonnes of gold against a
Rp3.6 trillion refund; a verdict came in March 2025 and ANTAM said it would take no further action,
without saying who won. The 5.9 tonnes plus 1.136 tonnes matches the roughly 7 tonnes Budi Said was
reported to have bought in 2018, so this is very likely the same set of transactions (inference from
press figures). Other buyers' suits have mostly been decided in ANTAM's favour; one Supreme Court appeal was lost and
that case remains open.

# 10 — RED FLAG

## 10.1 Profit from revaluing a promise the company measures itself

When ANTAM sold 49% of Sumberdaya Arindo, part of the price was an earn-out: the CATL group pays
US$377.9 for every extra tonne of nickel proven in the company's reserves within 36 months. The
asset was booked at Rp0.49 trillion in 2023, raised to Rp0.69 trillion in 2024, and to Rp1.45
trillion in 2025 on an assumed 633,913 extra tonnes. Rp0.62 trillion went into 2025 profit — 6% of
pre-tax profit.

The reserve estimate that drives this figure is produced by ANTAM for a company ANTAM controls, and
the group's consolidated nickel reserve rose 8% in 2025, with the MD&A citing "resource reclassification"
and "cut-off adjustments". Whether Hong Kong CBL accepts those tonnes, and on what timetable it must
pay, is not disclosed. Two further oddities in the same deal: the Feni Haltim sale implies a value for
100% of about Rp1.3 trillion, yet the retained 40% was valued on about Rp0.8 trillion and then booked
three days later at a lower figure again, with no explanation; and the earn-out's 2024 revaluation
has no visible line in the 2024 income statement.

## 10.2 Rp19.7 trillion of gold from a sister company, with no price

Freeport and ANTAM share a parent. In 2025 Freeport became ANTAM's largest supplier at Rp19.7 trillion,
and related-party purchases jumped from 13% to 40% of cost of revenue. The contract was treated as
routine, so no independent fairness opinion was required, and the accounts give no pricing basis for
this or any related-party deal. A discount to the world price would transfer value from Freeport's
shareholders (MIND ID 51%, Freeport-McMoRan 49%) to ANTAM's; a premium would do the reverse, at the
expense of ANTAM's 35% public holders. There is no way to tell which.

## 10.3 A Rp1.09 trillion "regulatory cost" nobody explains

A new line appeared in 2025 general and administrative expenses: "regulatory costs", Rp1.09 trillion
— more than a tenth of operating profit. The MD&A says only that it is "in line with mining operational
activities conducted in accordance with applicable regulations". The whole amount sits unpaid in
accrued expenses.

The most likely explanation is in a separate note. In December 2025 the Energy Ministry set fines
for mining in forest areas without a permit — Rp6.5 billion per hectare for nickel, Rp1.8 billion for
bauxite — and ANTAM states that "the estimation of potential impact … has been reflected" in its 2025
accounts, without an amount. If the whole charge were nickel fines, it would correspond to roughly
170 hectares of unpermitted clearing `[calc]`. Neither the hectares, the sites, nor whether the
fine is final are disclosed. ANTAM received two forestry warning letters for its Kolaka unit in 2023,
and Gag Nikel was suspended by the ministry from June to September 2025.

## 10.4 A plant funded by shareholders that has never started

The East Halmahera ferronickel plant was the stated purpose of the 2015 rights issue. Ten years later:
first metal in September 2023; "commissioning" in every presentation from FY2023 to 1Q2026; its
thirty-year power contract cut to three years in May 2023 so the land could serve the CATL
industrial estate; ANTAM owing PLN Rp0.72 trillion for mobilising the power plant (partly carried as a deferred-cost
asset, while the payable to PLN has sat at exactly Rp719.9 billion at two year-ends) plus a Rp0.65
trillion contingent bill for dismantling it that is not provided for; and
write-downs of about Rp1.7 trillion across 2023–2025. The 2025 write-down was taken even though the
test used a higher nickel price assumption. Shareholders who subscribed in 2015 paid for a smelter;
the site's future is now someone else's smelter.

## 10.5 The gold-governance cases

Two separate criminal matters touch ANTAM's gold business. The Attorney General's Office prosecuted
a "gold commodity governance" case covering 2010–2022 (2010–2021 in press reports) — reported in the press as about 109 tonnes of gold stamped with the ANTAM brand outside proper
procedures, with six former general managers of ANTAM's refinery unit and seven private parties named as
suspects by the prosecutors (Kejaksaan; Tempo); ANTAM says a verdict came on 27 May 2025 and that it records no
financial impact. Separately, the anti-corruption commission (KPK) named PT Loco Montrado a corporate suspect in August 2025 — the
same company that won a Supreme Court appeal against ANTAM (December 2023) on a claim of about
Rp859 billion over a 2017 refining contract, which ANTAM is challenging. For both the LoMon and the gold-bar suits ANTAM withholds case-by-case provision detail on the
grounds that disclosure would prejudice the litigation.

The brand is the gold business's main asset (§2.1). Criminal findings that ANTAM's brand was put on gold outside proper procedures are the most direct
threat to it; the filings treat them as a
legal footnote.

## 10.6 2025's cash and a receivable larger than the sales

In 2025 operating cash flow was 61% of profit. Among the reasons: Borneo Alumina Indonesia, the
associate building SGAR with Inalum, owed ANTAM Rp983 billion at year-end against Rp949 billion of
sales to it during the whole year — more than twelve months' sales unpaid. Separately, receivables
more than 90 days overdue rose 3.4-fold to Rp688 billion, and gold customers' prepayments
fell by three quarters. None of this is large relative to ANTAM's cash, but it is a sharp change in
a single year, and the associate balance is a state-to-state receivable with no stated terms.

## 10.7 Commitments without totals, and pledged shares

ANTAM paid Rp3 billion for 30% of an HPAL company whose plant is estimated at US$1.4 billion, and is on
the Indonesian side of a battery chain with a further US$1.9 billion of materials capex and
cell plants beyond that. The FY2025 MD&A says there were "no material capital commitments" at year-end,
yet the finance director guided Rp6 trillion of 2026 capital spending, much of it for this chain. At
Gag, 13% of Jiu Long's shares — about 44% of Gag's 30% stake — are pledged to an ore buyer that prepaid US$45m. At Bahodopi Utara and Matarape, licence data fees of about Rp0.37 trillion were funded by a loan from
an "investor" who is not named, on terms not given; the Bahodopi Utara licence was revoked in 2022 and
reinstated in 2024.

## 10.8 Smaller things that add up

- **Filing lapses:** about ten ASX suspensions for late reports since 2020; an AML regulator (PPATK)
  warning and small fines in 2024 and 2025.
- **Pomalaa jetty and conveyor:** under investigation by the South-East Sulawesi prosecutor's office
  since 2024 after an NGO complaint that the conveyor was inoperative.
- **Reserve numbers that don't reconcile:** the 2022 nickel reserve is 381.9 Mt in the FY2022 report
  and 461.8 Mt in the FY2023 report; the bauxite reserve jumped 63% in 2024 largely by reclassifying
  prospects; the 1Q2026 deck shows Tayan's reserve above the whole-company bauxite reserve.
- **Dividend tables** in the FY2022 and FY2024 MD&A put the previous year's dividend under the
  current year's heading.
- **Undescribed purchases:** Rp5.9 trillion bought from MIND ID Trading in 2025, described only as
  "goods for production activities".
- **Vanishing loans:** the Tayan refinery's Japanese loans, in default from 2020, disappear from the
  2023 notes with no disclosed settlement.
- **Old receivables:** Rp0.43 trillion still owed at end-2023 by Bumi Resources Minerals for a stake
  sold in 2017; a 2021 arbitration award against Dexin (US$2.4m) still unpaid.
- **Stale disclosures:** the FY2025 report describes a nickel pig iron JV (Antam Niterra Haltim) as
  active though liquidation began in 2022, and repeats a 2021 "planned for 2024" date for Gorontalo
  Minerals' plant.

- **Auditor change and late FY2024 accounts:** FY2021 and FY2023 were audited by Tanudiredja,
  Wibisana, Rintis & Rekan (PwC network); FY2024 by an auditor the FY2025 report calls only "other
  independent auditor", with its report dated 8 April 2025 — during the ASX suspension for late accounts;
  FY2025 by Purwantono, Sungkoro & Surja (EY network), whose one key audit matter is impairment testing
  on Rp7.6 trillion of plant (14% of assets) showing signs of impairment.

What was checked and found clean: opening balances tie across the three audited reports; no
comparative figure was restated; the FY2025 auditor's report is unmodified, and the FY2021 and FY2023 reports describe
unmodified ("present fairly") opinions; no
board member holds more than a token shareholding; no regulator sanctioned an individual board member
in 2025.

Ranked by how much each would change the analysis.

1. **Gold purchase prices, above all Freeport's.** Decides whether the gold shop's spread is a

   transaction section and any OJK filing on the deal are the places to press.

   relations; the ASX activity reports are the likeliest place for it to reappear.
3. **What "regulatory costs" (Rp1.09 trillion) are.** Decides whether 2025 carries a one-off forest
   fine or a recurring cost. Should be in the FY2025 accrued-expense and G&A notes' detail, or the
   1H2026 interim accounts.
4. **The 1H2026 interim financial statements.** Only press headlines were available (revenue, profit,
   assets, equity, cash). They would show the gold spread at 2026 prices, the finance-cost jump, and
   any change in the legal provision and earn-out. On IDX since late August 2026.
5. **ANTAM's total capital commitments to the CATL and Huayou chains** — equity calls, guarantees,
   shareholder loans, and whether ANTAM or IBC holds each downstream stake. Decides future borrowing
   and dividend capacity. Likely in the circulars for the June 2025 and later shareholder meetings,
   and in the FY2026 accounts.
6. **The earn-out terms** — who certifies the extra tonnes, payment dates, and whether HKCBL has
   accepted the 2025 reserve. AR2023 note 4a has the formula but not the mechanics; the SPA summary
   in the 2023 disclosure to OJK may have more.
7. **FY2025 dividend confirmation and 2026 payout policy.** Press gives Rp5.04–5.05 trillion (70%);
   the AGMS minutes of 10 June 2026 are the primary source.
8. **Peer comparison** — margins, cash costs and valuations of INCO, MBMA, NCKL and the gold retailer
   HRTA. Not obtained; needs their FY2025 reports.
9. **Indonesian gold demand in tonnes (WGC country data) and ANTAM's current market share.** The share
   claim stopped after FY2023; WGC's downloadable country tables would restore the series.
10. **Customer names for nickel ore and ferronickel**, and the pricing of ore sales to Tsingshan-linked
    smelters (including via Universal Metal Investment). Not in any filing.
11. **Forest-area exposure** — hectares cleared without permits by site, the status of the Kolaka
    warnings and the Gag suspension. Would size the fine risk beyond 2025.
12. **Royalty basis.** In 2024 the state-contribution table shows nickel ore royalty of Rp0.26 trillion,

13. **The 2019 Corporate Presentation and 2020 Public Expose decks** named in the upload never arrived.
    They would extend the operating series back and resolve the 2019/2020 revenue figures, which two
    later decks print in opposite order.
14. **Board pay for 2025** — not yet determined by MIND ID when the annual report was issued.
