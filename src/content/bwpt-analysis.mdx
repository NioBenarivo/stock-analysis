**What it is:** an upstream Indonesian oil palm planter. 74,339 planted hectares on the audited books (87,000 ha on the company's own narrative basis), 7 mills with 400 tonnes FFB/hour of installed capacity, estates in Central, South, East and West Kalimantan, one estate in West Sumatra and one in Keerom, Papua. It produces crude palm oil and palm kernel, sells 100% of it domestically, and 70% of that to two Sinar Mas group refiners.

**Listing:** IDX: BWPT. 31,525,291,000 shares outstanding, of which 402,922,800 (1.28%) are held in treasury. Share price Rp 86–93 in mid-August 2026 depending on source; market capitalisation **Rp 2.68–2.93 trillion** (~USD 165–180m). All-time high Rp 1,790 in April 2012; all-time low Rp 50 in January 2024.

**Control:** PT Rajawali Capital International 37.70% · FIC Properties Sdn Bhd (Felda, Malaysia) 37.00% · public 24.02%. Post-period, PT Rajawali Kapital Agro bought a further 2.55% on 11 March 2026, taking the Rajawali bloc to 40.25%.

**Period covered:** FY2018 (comparative) through FY2025 audited, plus Q1 2026 and H1 2026 unaudited, plus the 2014 rights-issue prospectus for the pre-history. Full source table at the end.

**Currency:** all figures in Rp million unless stated. Derived figures are marked `[calculated]`.

## THE ONE THING TO UNDERSTAND FIRST

BWPT's entire equity market capitalisation is about **Rp 2.9 trillion**. In December 2016, FIC Properties — a wholly-owned vehicle of Felda, the Malaysian federal land development agency — paid **USD 505.4 million** for 37% of it, at Rp 775 a share, a **95.86% premium** to the market price of the day. That stake is worth roughly **USD 56–61 million** today at Rp 86–93 a share `[calculated at the ~Rp 17,850/USD rate implied by The Edge's own June 2026 valuation of the block]`: an ~88% loss on a state agency's balance sheet, funded by a Malaysian government facility that runs to 2043.

Felda has been trying to get out ever since. It exercised a put option in 2019 and again in May 2022. On **14 June 2024 a SIAC tribunal awarded in its favour**, ordering Rajawali to buy the stake back for **USD 505,415,919 plus 6% per annum from 11 May 2017** — reported at the time as **USD 678.09 million**, and still accruing at roughly USD 30 million a year. The Singapore International Commercial Court **dismissed Rajawali's set-aside applications on 16 December 2024**. Rajawali responded by filing a *non-eksekuatur* petition in the Central Jakarta District Court on **6 February 2025** to block enforcement in Indonesia. As of the last citable reporting — The Edge Malaysia's cover story of late June 2026 — **the award remains unenforced and the petition is still outstanding**.

So the arithmetic that governs this security is: **an award worth roughly 12x the market value of the stake it concerns, and about 4x the entire company's market capitalisation, hangs over the controlling shareholder** `[calculated]`.

Read the 2026 corporate calendar in that light and it stops looking like ordinary housekeeping:

| Dec 2025 – Jan 2026 | Rp 451,470m of affiliated internal reshuffling; PT Papua Sawita Raya emptied of three subsidiary stakes | Chain simplification ahead of something |

None of that changes a single hectare or a single tonne. What it changes is BWPT's ability to send money to its shareholders — at precisely the moment its controlling shareholder is facing a USD 678m claim. The quasi-reorganisation is a *permission* step: it costs nothing, produces nothing, and consumes 92% of the paid-in surplus the company built over its whole history (APIC falls from Rp 4,015,083m to Rp 307,130m), leaving almost no buffer to absorb the next loss. Total equity is unchanged at Rp 2,827,113m. See §1.5.

Underneath the control fight sits a real operating business that is finally working — FY2025 revenue Rp 5,761,296m, EBITDA Rp 1,666,000m, net profit Rp 379,107m, net debt/EBITDA down to 2.6x from 18.9x in 2020 — but which is also smaller every year (planted area down 42% since 2018), increasingly a trading book rather than a farm (48.8% of cost of sales is now bought-in raw material, §2.4), dependent on two customers for 70% of revenue (§2.5), and carrying forest-area regularisation applications that the Ministry of Forestry **rejected across 11,191 hectares in February 2025**, of which **4,803 hectares are still inside the group** — 6.5% of its audited planted base, with criminal exposure attached (§2.3, §3.7).

Everything else in this report is detail on those two facts: who is fighting over the company, and what is actually left inside it.

### The 2014 transaction, which is still the company's defining event

The prospectus is far more candid than any subsequent annual report, and three things in it matter.

**One: the seller controlled both sides.** BWPT paid **Rp 10,530,000m** — 449.5% of its own pre-deal equity — to buy Green Eagle Holdings and stakes in two further vehicles from Green Eagle Palm Ltd (BVI), PT Rajawali Corpora and PT Mitra Hamparan Lestari, all ultimately controlled by **Peter Sondakh**. The purchase was funded by a Rp 10,808,671m rights issue that Rajawali itself backstopped as buyer of the rump, and whose rights the three largest existing holders had pre-agreed — three days before the SPA was signed — to transfer to Rajawali. **Stephen Kurniawan Sulistyo sat simultaneously as a director of the selling entity and as President Commissioner of the buyer.** The transaction was nevertheless classified as an *affiliated* transaction rather than one carrying a *conflict of interest*, which meant no independent-shareholder vote was required.

**Two: the price exceeded the appraisal.** KJPP FAST appraised the equity acquired at **Rp 8,264,683m** as at 30 June 2014 (GEH Rp 6,744,309m + MKJ Rp 500,256m + MSP Rp 1,020,117m). Adding GEH's own audited shareholder loan of Rp 1,637,837m gives a combined **~Rp 9,902,520m**, against the **Rp 10,530,000m** paid — a **5.96% deviation** measured against the price, waved through against the same appraiser's own 7.5% "fair" tolerance under Bapepam-LK Rule VIII.C.3, and justified by *post-cutoff-date* events: GEH raised its stakes in eleven subsidiaries by 5% each on 24 July 2014, and acquired MKJ and MSP on 11 August 2014 — both after the 30 June valuation date. **Rp 4,487,760m of goodwill** was recognised on a pro-forma basis.

And the classification turns on that appraisal in a way worth naming: the transaction was held **not** to be a *benturan kepentingan* under Rule IX.E.1 **precisely because the independent appraiser had called the price fair.** It was still a material transaction under IX.E.2 — Rp 10.53tn against Rp 2,342,586m of equity, **449.50%**, more than twenty-two times the 20% threshold — so an EGM was required. But a materiality EGM is a majority of *all* shareholders, including the blocks that had already pre-committed their rights to the buyer. A conflict EGM would have required a majority of *independent* shareholders. The whole difference between those two votes rests on one appraiser's 5.96%-against-a-7.5%-threshold conclusion.

**Three: Rajawali-linked capital was in the register a year before the deal.** The November–December 2013 private placement issued **405,100,000 shares at Rp 850** — and that is the exact size of the 9.00% block later held by "Pegasus CP One Limited", one of the three holders that pre-agreed on 19 September 2014 to hand its rights to Rajawali. The shares placed in 2013 are the shares pre-committed in 2014.

**Four: nobody has ever been named.** The beneficial owners of Matacuna Group Limited, Pegasus CP One Limited and Hi-Point Resources Limited — nominee blocks totalling ~27% of the pre-deal company and central to the control transfer — are not disclosed anywhere in the 488-page prospectus. Neither is any discussion of whether a mandatory tender offer under Rule IX.H.1 was triggered, sought, exempted or waived on what was a textbook change of control.

**That deal is still being expensed today.** At FY2025, **Rp 907,189m** of accumulated bearer-plant amortization relates to purchase-price allocation from the 2014 acquisition (Rp 720,297m at FY2024, Rp 458,233m at FY2021), and **Rp 960,285m** of the original goodwill still sits on the balance sheet — 9.4% of total assets. A material share of the annual amortization charge inside cost of sales is the price Rajawali charged in 2014, not the cost of growing palm.

`One caution on that series.` AR2021 presents Rp 411,631m of bearer-plant amortization charged to COGS *"of which Rp 458,233m relates to PPA amortization"* — a component larger than the total it sits inside, which is arithmetically impossible as written. AR2022 reframes the same Rp 458,233m as a **cumulative balance** within accumulated amortization rather than a one-year charge, which resolves the impossibility but means the two consecutive reports describe the same number in two different ways. The cumulative reading is the one used above.

### Four things about this note

**1. The interest-free loan with no maturity.** PT Rajawali Corpora is owed **Rp 603,022m — 8.2% of total liabilities — at 0%, with no fixed repayment schedule and no fixed maturity.** In a company paying 9.75–11.00% on its public bonds and floating bank reference rates on Rp 3.9 trillion of bank debt, an interest-free, undated Rp 603bn from the parent is worth roughly Rp 60bn a year of avoided interest. It is also, structurally, quasi-equity that ranks as debt — and it can be called at any time.

**2. The 4.75% loans, and their novation.** RCI extended two facilities — Rp 200,000m from 30 June 2020 and Rp 500,000m from 28 June 2021 — both at a flat **4.75% fixed**, both with maturities repeatedly extended (now to 2030). Whether 4.75% is a market rate is explicitly not addressed in any filing. On **12 November 2025 both facilities were novated from RCI to PT Rajawali Corpora** at identical principal, rate and terms. The stated reason: none. The effect: Rajawali's direct credit exposure to BWPT was moved out of the entity that is the respondent in the Felda arbitration, in the year the enforcement fight was live.

**3. The Rp 220,000m deposit that was never converted.** RCI paid in Rp 170,000m in 2021 and topped it up by Rp 50,000m in 2022 as a "deposit for future stock subscriptions". It sat in non-current liabilities for four years. Share capital never moved. On **26 May 2025 the agreement was amended to make it withdrawable, and it was refunded in full during 2025.** A shareholder placed Rp 220bn with the company on terms that looked like equity, left it there through the worst of the leverage, and took it back the year before the quasi-reorganisation.

**4. No pricing basis is disclosed for anything except the loans.** The Nettocyber IT services, the Mitra Satu Solusi IT services, the Permadani head-office lease and the Rajawali Corpora corporate guarantee all carry **no arm's-length statement, no benchmark and no transfer-pricing basis in any year.** And two disclosures in the FY2025 report contradict the notes on their face:

## 1.5 Dividends and reinvestment history

**BWPT has never paid a dividend in the period covered — FY2018 through FY2025, eight consecutive years.** The only dividend outflow anywhere in the series is Rp 4,550m paid by a subsidiary to its non-controlling interests in FY2019.

The reason is legal, not discretionary. Article 71(2) of Indonesia's Company Law bars distribution while cumulative retained earnings are negative, and BWPT's accumulated deficit ran as follows:

### What is not in the cost note

## 2.6 Key variables — what actually moves the model

Six variables, each tied to a number in the financials.

### 1. CPO realised price — Rp 5,041,442m = 87.5% of revenue

The dominant variable by a wide margin. FY2025 ASP Rp 14,113/kg on 358,120 t sold.

**Sensitivity:** a Rp 500/kg move in realised CPO price is worth **Rp 179,060m of revenue and, on own production, essentially all of it drops to operating profit** — 10.7% of FY2025 EBITDA, 15.0% of operating profit and 47% of FY2025 net profit `[calculated]`. A 10% move in CPO price moves net profit by roughly 130%.

Policy sets the floor. Indonesian domestic palm demand is now **51.4% biodiesel** (12.7m t of 24.7m t in 2025), against 39.7% food and 9.3% oleochemical — and food demand is *shrinking* (−3.64% in 2025). **B50 became mandatory on 1 July 2026** under Kepmen ESDM 257.K/EK.01/MEM.E/2026, a ten-point step up from B40. MPOC's view is that B50 is what holds CPO above RM 4,400/t despite weak global food demand. For an Indonesian planter, the demand base is now an energy-policy variable, not a food variable.

### 2. Bought-in raw material — Rp 2,035,245m = 48.8% of cost of sales

The fastest-moving line in the business and the one with the worst economics. FFB purchases Rp 1,010,312m at ~Rp 3,465/kg; CPO purchases Rp 1,024,933m at ~Rp 13,620/kg `[calculated]`.

**Sensitivity:** the CPO trading spread is Rp 493/kg on ~75,250 t, i.e. roughly **Rp 37,000m of gross profit** on Rp 1,024,933m of purchases. A 4% adverse move in the purchase price — Rp 545/kg — wipes that out entirely and turns the trade negative. This leg carries roughly 25% of cost of sales for 2% of gross profit. It is a volume business masquerading as a margin business.

### 3. Extraction rate — each 1pp of OER ≈ Rp 173,000m of revenue

OER 23.3% in FY2025, 23.93% in Q1 2026, up from 22.2% in FY2021–22. KER 4.5%, up from 3.8%.

**Sensitivity:** on 1,230,718 t of FFB processed, 1pp of OER is **12,307 t of CPO = Rp 173,700m of revenue at Rp 14,113/kg, at near-zero incremental cost** `[calculated]`. That is 10.4% of FY2025 EBITDA per percentage point. The 1.7pp improvement since FY2021 is worth roughly Rp 295bn a year — more than the entire FY2025 capex programme, delivered by mill housekeeping.

### 4. Tree age and yield — 12.5 t FFB/ha against a 20–24 t potential

Average tree age was 12.9 years at FY2021; on that trajectory it is roughly **17–18 years now**. Immature area has been stuck at **271 hectares — 0.36% of the base — since FY2019**. A healthy estate replants 4–5% of area annually.

**Sensitivity:** the gap between BWPT's 12.5 t/ha and a well-run 20 t/ha estate, on 87,000 ha, is **652,500 tonnes of FFB — 60% more than the company currently produces**. Closing even a third of it would be transformational. Not closing it means yield begins declining from here, because the trees are past peak.

This is the variable with the longest lead time and the least disclosure. **No age-band profile and no replanting programme exist in any filing.**

### 5. Interest cost — Rp 425,283m = 7.4% of revenue and 36% of operating profit

Net debt Rp 4,389,459m at FY2025. Interest expense has fallen every year since FY2019's Rp 911,984m, but still consumes more than a third of operating profit.

**Sensitivity:** a 100bp move on Rp 4.5 trillion of mostly floating-rate debt is **Rp 45,000m — 11.9% of FY2025 net profit** `[calculated]`. The new bond and sukuk paper prices at 9.75–11.00%; the bank book is on floating reference rates. Deleveraging from 18.9x net debt/EBITDA in FY2020 to 2.6x in FY2025 has been the single largest driver of the earnings recovery — Rp 487bn of annual interest saved since FY2019, against FY2025 net profit of Rp 379bn.

### 6. Export levy and the domestic policy wedge — a structural discount BWPT cannot escape

Indonesia's CPO export levy rose from 10% to **12.5% on 1 March 2026** under PMK 9/2026, on top of an export duty of USD 148/MT against an August 2026 reference price of USD 996.52/MT — together roughly **27% off the top of any exported tonne**.

But the levy is *tiered to force downstreaming*: 12.5% for raw CPO, 12% for olein and stearin, 10% for refined product, and **7.25% for branded packaged product and biodiesel**. **BWPT, selling raw CPO domestically at the mill gate, captures none of that 5.25pp gradient.** Its buyers — SMART, Apical, Wilmar — do. That is the economic content of being a pure upstream planter in 2026: the policy is explicitly designed to move margin downstream, and BWPT has no downstream.

The DMO/DPO regime, still in force in 2026 under Permendag 43/2025 as amended by Permendag 20/2026, is the tail risk with no Malaysian equivalent. Indonesia banned CPO and cooking oil exports outright from 28 April to 23 May 2022. It can do so again.

## 2.7 Demand

Global palm oil consumption was **75.05 million tonnes in 2024/25**, projected at 77.73m t for 2025/26. Indonesia is both the largest producer (~58–60% of world supply) and the largest single consumer (~20m t), accounting for roughly a third of global demand as well.

**The Indonesian domestic split in 2025 is the number that matters for BWPT:**

| End use | 2025 (m t) | Change | Share |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| **Biodiesel** | **12.7** | **+10.97%** | **51.4%** |
| Food | 9.8 | −3.64% | 39.7% |
| Oleochemical | 2.3 | slight rise | 9.3% |
| **Total domestic** | **24.7** | +3.82% | 100% |

*Components sum to 24.8m t against a stated 24.7m t total — GAPKI rounding.*

More than half of Indonesia's domestic palm oil now goes into the fuel tank, and domestic food demand is contracting. Under B40 in 2025, biodiesel absorbed 12.7m tonnes. B50, mandatory from 1 July 2026, raises the blend another ten points — with a three-month transition to 30 September 2026 to clear B40 stock, roughly 30 participating fuel suppliers, and Pertamina and AKR holding the largest quotas. The government claims an FX saving of Rp 157.28 trillion in 2026 against Rp 140 trillion under B40.

Export demand shifted materially in 2025: Africa +991k t, China +644k t, Malaysia +516k t, and **India −859k t** — the largest single decline. Indonesian exports were 32.3m t (+9.51%) at USD 35.87bn (+29.23%), a realised export price of USD 1,221/t against USD 1,084/t in 2024. *(GAPKI's 32.3m t is all palm products; the 30–31m t figure in §2.8 is a CPO-only trade estimate. Different baskets, not a conflict.)*

The EU is in structural decline — roughly 5m t against 6.6m t sustained through 2015–2020, ahead of EUDR — but the fall is being more than absorbed by Indonesian biodiesel.

**For BWPT specifically, all of this is second-order.** It sells domestically to refiners. Its demand is those two refiners' demand, which in turn is driven by the mandate. It has no direct export exposure, no biodiesel capacity, and no way to participate in the levy gradient. It is a price-taker at one remove.

| **Malaysia** | ~25% | 16.5–17.5m t |

Indonesian CPO production: 46.88m t (2021), ~46.7m t (2022), 50.07m t (2023), 48.16m t (2024, GAPKI basis), **51.6m t (2025, +7.26%)**. Total palm oil including PKO 56.5m t in 2025.

**BWPT's share of national production is 286,972 t of 51.6m t = 0.56%** `[calculated]`. It is a small player in a fragmented industry.

Two supply-side facts frame the medium-term price outlook.

**National yield is stuck at 3.6 t CPO/ha** across 16.83 million hectares, and the reason is the smallholder estate. Smallholders farm **6.8 million hectares — 40–41% of national area** — at 2–3 t CPO/ha against a 4–6 t potential, on ageing trees.

**The replanting programme is failing.** PSR realisation: 49,658 ha against a 120,000 ha target in 2024 (41%); 37,499 ha against 120,000 ha in 2025 (31%); **9,063 ha against a target cut to 50,000 ha by May 2026 (18%)**. The grant was raised from Rp 25m to Rp 60m per hectare and the target itself was cut by 58% — an admission of failure. Barriers are land legality, administrative delay and weak company–smallholder partnerships.

The net effect is bullish for price and bearish for volume growth: Indonesian supply cannot easily expand, because the moratorium constrains new planting and the replanting programme cannot renew the existing base, while B50 adds a step-change to demand. That is the sector tailwind BWPT is riding.

The complication for BWPT is that **it has the same ageing-tree problem as the smallholders it competes with, on its own nucleus estate, with 0.36% immature area and no replanting programme.**

Malaysia, for contrast, produced 20.28m t (+4.9%) off 5.70m ha in 2025 — a third of Indonesia's area producing 39% of its volume, i.e. materially higher yield per hectare.

## 3.8 Roadmap and what to watch

The company's stated strategy, from the 2026 public expose, is the same five-pillar template it published in 2024 and 2025, with no Rupiah figure attached to any pillar in any year: best agronomy practices; digitalisation and mechanisation; capex for mills, biogas, a kernel crushing plant and new planting; plasma smallholder collaboration and trading; ESG strengthening. It names B40 (not B50) as the long-term domestic demand driver and flags aggressive biofuel policy, EUDR, export controls and tariffs as 2025 headwinds.

`Assessment:` this is not a plan. There is no capex budget, no replanting hectarage, no yield target, no production target and no timeline in any of the three decks. The only quantified forward guidance the company has ever published is the FY2025 revenue and profit target — Rp 4,766,837m and Rp 332,220m — which it beat by 20.9% and 14.1%, and which was set so conservatively that beating it was close to arithmetically certain.

### What to watch, in priority order

**1. The Central Jakarta District Court ruling on Rajawali's non-eksekuatur petition.** Filed 6 February 2025; no decision reported as of August 2026. This is the largest binary event over the controlling shareholder, and it governs whether the 37% Felda block ever moves. If enforcement is allowed, Rajawali owes ~USD 678m and rising against a stake worth ~USD 56–61m — and the only obvious asset it could monetise to pay is BWPT itself.

**2. The first dividend.** The quasi-reorganisation removed the legal bar in June 2026. Nothing has been declared. If a dividend appears in the 2027 AGM cycle, the sector's yield-investor bid becomes available to a stock that has never had it, and the re-rating case is real. If none appears, the quasi-reorganisation was housekeeping for a purpose not yet disclosed.

**3. Whether BWPT appears on any published Satgas PKH list**, and what happens to the 4,803 live rejected hectares. See §3.7.

**4. Gross profit, not revenue.** H1 2026: revenue +17.3%, gross profit +2.5%. The bought-in leg is now 48.8% of cost of sales and nets to approximately zero cash contribution once mill cost is allocated to it. Watch whether the CPO purchase line keeps growing — if it does, revenue growth will keep decoupling from earnings.

**5. The immature hectare line.** It has read 271 ha since FY2019. Any material replanting programme will show up there first, and nowhere else. Until it moves, yield decline from an ageing estate is the base case.

**6. The FY2026 covenant statement.** FY2025 was the first fully clean year since at least FY2017. Bank Mestika's covenants become effective in FY2026, and Rp 500bn of new bond and sukuk paper was issued in January 2026.

**7. Whether the audited financial statements are actually printed in the FY2026 annual report.** They were missing in whole or part from four of the seven reports in this set. See the Sources note.

**8. Rajawali's stake.** 37.70% held unchanged for nine years, then 40.25% in March 2026 via a different vehicle. Further creep would suggest preparation for something — a tender offer, a squeeze-out, or a defensive block ahead of an enforcement ruling.

# SUMMARY VERDICT

**What the business is.** BWPT is a sub-scale, ageing, upstream-only Indonesian palm oil planter — 74,339 audited planted hectares, seven mills, 0.56% of national CPO production — that has spent the last four years successfully digging itself out of a debt hole created by a 2014 related-party acquisition it paid Rp 10.53 trillion for. Net debt/EBITDA has gone from 18.9x to 2.6x, covenant breaches from four subsidiaries across three banks to none, and net profit from a Rp 1.4 trillion loss to a Rp 379 billion profit. That recovery is real and management deserves credit for it.

**What it earns through the cycle.** On its own fruit, roughly **Rp 7,830 per kilogram of CPO in full margin — a 55% margin — and about Rp 22.4 million of EBITDA per audited planted hectare** at an ASP of Rp 14,113/kg. That is a genuinely good asset-level return, and it exists because the estate is largely written down and the trees are already in the ground. At the FY2023 ASP of Rp 11,015/kg the same estate produces roughly Rp 13.4 million of EBITDA per hectare and leverage goes back above 4x. So: comfortably profitable at Rp 14,000/kg, marginal at Rp 11,000/kg, loss-making below that. Given that Indonesian biodiesel policy is what holds the price where it is, this is an energy-policy equity wearing an agriculture costume.

**What would have to be true for it to work.** Four things, in descending order of tractability. CPO has to stay above roughly Rp 12,000/kg, which B50 makes plausible. The company has to start replanting — an estate averaging ~17 years with 0.36% immature area is running down its own productive capital, and 12.5 t/ha against a 20–24 t/ha standard is the visible cost of not doing it. The quasi-reorganisation has to be followed by an actual dividend, because that is the only route to the yield-investor bid that every listed peer already enjoys. And the Felda block has to resolve one way or the other, because a 37% holder that has spent seven years and a Singapore arbitration trying to leave is an overhang no operational improvement can offset.

**What would break it.** A CPO price back at 2023 levels, with leverage at 2.6x and a current ratio of 0.67x, would put the covenant record back where it was in 2023. The 4,803 live rejected forest-area hectares would cost about 23% of net profit and a Rp 152 billion write-off — survivable. What is not quantifiable, and therefore what should worry a holder most, is the roughly 80,000 hectares of unplanted concession sitting in the exact geography a task force that has already returned 2.47 million hectares to the state is still working through, with its company list unpublished. And a Central Jakarta ruling that permits enforcement of a USD 678 million award against a controlling shareholder whose most saleable asset is this company would put the free float in play on terms the free float does not control.

**The position.** This is not a plantation investment; it is a claim on a control dispute with a plantation attached. The operating business is improving, fairly valued at 7–8x earnings and ~1x book, and structurally handicapped by ageing trees, no downstream, one buying group taking half its output, and a trading book that adds a third of its revenue and, once mill cost is allocated to it, essentially none of its profit. Every material corporate action of the last twelve months — the internal restructuring, the loan novations, the deposit refund, the stake creep to 40.25%, the estate sale, the quasi-reorganisation — is better explained by the shareholder register than by the estate. **Anyone underwriting the equity is underwriting Rajawali's intentions, and Rajawali has not disclosed them.**

The most useful summary of this company's history is a single arithmetic fact. In 2014 it issued 27 billion shares at Rp 400 to buy assets from its incoming controlling shareholder at a price 5.96% above the independent appraisal. Those shares are worth about Rp 90 today. In the eleven years since, the company has paid no dividend, disposed of 42% of its planted area, booked Rp 2.03 trillion of losses on those disposals, accumulated a Rp 4.52 trillion deficit at the peak, and in June 2026 erased that deficit by consuming 92% of the paid-in capital those 2014 shares represented. **The 2014 transaction has now been fully written off against the equity that funded it.** What remains is 74,339 hectares of decent, ageing palm, Rp 4.4 trillion of net debt, and two 37% shareholders in court.

### Coverage limitations, stated before being asked

**Seven consecutive annual reports were read in full, FY2019 through FY2025.** FY2018 figures throughout this report come from AR2019's comparative column, not from a FY2018 filing, and are labelled as such in the tables.

**FY2023 note-level detail is partly OCR-derived.** The audited FY2023 financial statements were image-only in the source PDF and were recovered by OCR for this report. Totals were cross-checked against AR2024's audited comparative column and tie. Component-level figures within the FY2023 notes — particularly the segment table — carry OCR uncertainty and are flagged where used.

**The auditor's opinions for FY2021, FY2022, FY2023 and FY2025 were not in the text layer of the filings** and were recovered by OCR. All four are unmodified with no emphasis-of-matter and no going-concern paragraph. That four of seven annual reports from a listed company should have image-only financial statement or auditor's report pages is itself worth recording.

**Two claims in this report rest on sources outside the filings and should be read with that in mind.** The forest-area table in §2.3 is transcribed from web publications of the Ministry of Forestry's SK 36/2025 annex rather than the ministry PDF, and matches entities to BWPT by name only — high confidence, formally unverified at registry level.
