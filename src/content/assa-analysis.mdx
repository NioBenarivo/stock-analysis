**What it does:** Indonesia's largest independent car-rental and fleet-leasing company (ASSA Rent, about 30,000 vehicles). Around that fleet it has built a set of connected businesses: selling its own ex-rental cars (Caroline.id, through listed subsidiary ASLC), a used-vehicle auction house (JBA Indonesia), a driver-outsourcing arm (DMS), truck logistics (ASSA Logistics / Cargoshare) and an e-commerce parcel courier (**Anteraja**, via PT Tri Adi Bersama, 49.5% owned). All operations are in Indonesia. It sits in the orbit of the Triputra group (T.P. Rachmat).

**Market:** Rp605/share (21-Sep-2026, 10:44 WIB) × 3,691,137,517 shares = **Rp2,233bn market cap** [calculated] (IDNFinancials shows Rp2,215bn). P/E 5.4x TTM, P/BV 1.02x, trailing dividend yield 8.3% [all calculated, §3.6].

**Period covered:** FY2020–FY2025 audited, plus 1Q24/1Q25/1Q26 from the public expose decks and 1H26 from press coverage.

**Sources in hand:** 5 annual reports (FY2021–FY2025, 486–577 pages each) and 3 public expose decks (Jun-2024, Jun-2025, Jun-2026). Web research filled in the market and peer context. The full source table is at the end.

**Conventions:** Rp bn unless stated. "Attrib." means attributable to owners of the parent. `[calculated]` marks every figure derived here rather than printed in a filing. FY2020 figures come from the comparative column in AR2021 (no FY2020 report in hand).

## THE ONE THING TO UNDERSTAND FIRST

**ASSA's earnings recovery since 2023 is mostly Anteraja, and ASSA owns less than half of Anteraja.**

ASSA consolidates PT Tri Adi Bersama (TAB, the Anteraja courier) with only a **49.5%** stake. That has been the stake since a 12-Apr-2022 capital injection from Garibaldi (Boy) Thohir, done at par. So all of TAB's revenue, costs and debt sit in ASSA's consolidated statements, but 50.5% of TAB's profit belongs to other shareholders. TAB's own summarised P&L (from the non-controlling-interest note in each annual report) shows why this matters:

| Rp bn | FY2021 | FY2022 | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| TAB (Anteraja) profit for the year | 5.8 | **(199.2)** | **(176.3)** | 142.5 | **326.6** |
| ASSA group profit for the year (total) | 159.6 | 3.7 | 19.4 | 330.1 | 596.6 |
| Group profit **excluding TAB** [calculated] | 153.8 | 202.9 | 195.7 | 187.6 | 270.0 |
| ASSA's 49.5% share of TAB [calculated] | 2.9 | (98.6) | (87.2) | 70.5 | 161.7 |
| Profit attrib. to ASSA shareholders | 142.6 | 103.0 | 103.8 | 243.7 | 417.7 |
| TAB share of attrib. profit [calculated] | 2% | n.m. | n.m. | 29% | **39%** |

*TAB figures are before intercompany elimination (AR2025 Note 22 and the equivalent notes in AR2022–AR2024), so "group excluding TAB" is an approximation.*

Four things follow:

1. **Outside Anteraja, the group earned about Rp190–200bn a year from FY2022 to FY2024, with no growth.** The headline story (profit up from Rp3.7bn to Rp596.6bn) is a courier going from losing about Rp200bn a year to earning about Rp330bn a year. The rental/used-car/auction core only stepped up in FY2025, to about Rp270bn.
2. **The rental core barely covers its own interest.** The rental segment holds 96% of fixed assets (Rp5,325bn) and 96% of bank debt (Rp4,073bn). In FY2025 it made Rp303.5bn of operating profit. Allocating finance charges pro-rata to bank debt (~Rp283bn) leaves about **Rp20bn** of pre-tax profit [calculated, §3.5]. The equity return on the fleet comes from selling the cars at the end of their rental life (used-vehicle segment, Rp170.7bn operating profit), not from renting them.
3. **Consolidated ratios overstate what ASSA shareholders own.** 30% of FY2025 group net profit (Rp178.8bn) went to non-controlling interests: TAB's other 50.5% holders and ASLC's 22.4% public float. Headline net margin is 10.0%; the attributable margin is 7.0%.
4. **The market seems to be pricing ASSA as a leveraged rental company and giving little value to Anteraja.** At Rp2,233bn, the market cap roughly equals ASSA's stake in listed ASLC at market (~Rp653bn) plus 49.5% of Anteraja at 5x FY2025 earnings (~Rp808bn) plus about Rp770bn for a 30,000-car fleet carrying Rp4.1tn of debt (§3.6).

So the variable that matters most is **whether Anteraja's 15.6% FY2025 net margin can last** in a parcel market known for price wars. The second is **used-car residual values**. Rental utilisation matters less than either (§2.6).

**Reading it:**

- **On the sales side, RPT is negligible.** ASSA does not depend on group companies for revenue (\<0.5%). Group firms are small rental customers.
- **On the buying side, RPT doubled in FY2025.** Related-party purchases rose from Rp104bn to Rp250bn, from ~10% to ~19% of rental-vehicle capex. The Daya Adicipta companies are the controlling group's own vehicle dealers and distributors. ASSA says the pricing is "equivalent to independent-party" terms, but no discount-versus-list data is disclosed. This is the line to watch: when a fleet buyer purchases from its controlling shareholder's dealerships, value can leak through small price differences.
- **Tokopedia was the largest related-party line of all.** From FY2020 to FY2022, TAB paid Tokopedia a "service operation fee" (Rp262bn, then Rp579bn, then Rp393bn). That was 14.4% of the whole group's cost of revenue in FY2021. Tokopedia is classified as an "other related party", which fits a GoTo/Tokopedia-group shareholding in TAB (a 2022 press report put GoTo at 22.5% of Anteraja; the ARs do not give TAB's cap table). The arrangement was terminated in Aug-2022, and Anteraja's express revenue fell from Rp3,152bn to Rp1,433bn in FY2023. A **new agreement was signed in Nov-2024**. The fee is so far only Rp18.7bn, but it marks the return of that channel.
- **Related-party balances are small** (receivables Rp3.7bn, payables Rp2.7bn at FY25). No loans to or guarantees for related parties were found.

**AR2022 contradicts itself on material affiliated transactions (p.234).** The Indonesian text says there were none in 2022. The English text on the same page says there were. Unresolved.

## 2.1 Product

## 2.5 Distribution — how the product is sold

- **Rental:** B2B corporate long-term leases, sold directly through 18 branches and 22 representative offices. Customers are multinational and domestic corporates, state-owned enterprises and government. Contracts run 3–5 years at fixed monthly rates, which gives visible revenue but little room to reprice mid-contract.
- **Used cars:** Caroline.id retail (18 showrooms plus online) and JBA wholesale auction. JBA consignors include leasing and multifinance companies (repossessions), fleets and dealers. ASLC's guidance mentions "strengthening non-leasing supply sources", which suggests leasing-company repossession flow is the swing factor.
- **Courier:** Anteraja serves marketplace sellers (Tokopedia again since Nov-2024; others not disclosed) and B2B. Cargoshare serves B2B freight.

## 2.8 Supply

- **Rental:** TRAC (Astra/SERA) is the largest player; its fleet is not publicly quantified (gap; widely reputed to be larger than ASSA). ASSA has ~30,000 units and MPM Rent (MPMX) >16,000. - **Used-car supply:** fed by fleet retirements and leasing-company repossessions. Repossession flow falls when credit quality is good, which is the likely cause of JBA's "supply challenges" in 1H26.

1. **Anteraja profit, quarterly.** The proxy is the NCI line in each quarter's P&L: NCI profit minus ~22% of ASLC's profit ≈ 50.5% of TAB. 1H26 NCI profit was Rp70.3bn against Rp88.6bn in 1H25. Watch whether that fall continues in 9M26.

# SUMMARY VERDICT

**What it is.** ASSA is a debt-funded car-rental fleet with a vertically integrated resale chain (Caroline, JBA). It also controls, with 49.5%, an e-commerce courier that swings between losing and making about Rp300bn a year. The rental fleet is stable, contracted and cost-plus, and on its own it earns roughly its cost of debt. The resale chain gives it a thin positive spread. Anteraja is where the earnings volatility lives, and ASSA shareholders get only half of its profits while carrying all of its consolidation risk.

**What it earns through the cycle.** Excluding Anteraja, the group made about Rp175–200bn a year of attributable profit in FY2022–24 (201.6 / 191.0 / 173.2) and about Rp256bn in FY2025 [calculated]. That is the recurring earnings base. On today's Rp2,233bn market cap it is **~8.7x**, which is fair rather than cheap for a business earning ~6–9% on its assets against 7% debt. Anteraja's half then adds anywhere from −Rp130bn to +Rp160bn a year, depending on the year.

**What has to be true for it to work.**

- Anteraja keeps a positive margin; it does not need 15.6%, but it must stay out of the FY2022–23 losses.
- Used-car resale margins stay near 18%.
- Rates don't rise sharply from 5.75%.

If those hold, a 5.4x P/E, 8.3% yield, 1.6x ND/EBITDA and debt falling relative to EBITDA make ASSA cheap, and the Rp825 → Rp605 fall since April has overshot.

**What would break it.**

1. A marketplace moves its parcels to an in-house courier again (SPX / TikTok-Tokopedia) and TAB falls back into loss. ASSA would absorb 100% of the cash burn in consolidation.
2. The Chinese-brand price war pushes used-car prices down 10%+ while fleet book values stay where they are. The disposal gain disappears, and the rental core earns below its cost of debt.
3. Rates rise another 100–200bp with 3–5-year contracts unable to reprice.

**Position.** On the filings, **ASSA is modestly undervalued, but for identifiable reasons**. Our reading is that the market is right to heavily discount Anteraja's FY2025 margin: the only forward data point (1H26 NCI) already points down, and the filings stopped disclosing volumes exactly when the courier's problems began. The margin of safety is the ex-Anteraja business at ~8.7x plus a covered 5–8% dividend. It is not the headline 5x P/E. The asymmetry is unfavourable in one respect: most of Anteraja's upside goes to outside partners, while its downside hits the consolidated cash, tax charge and bank covenants in full. A reader who wants exposure to the rental/resale franchise should value ASSA as the ex-Anteraja business and treat the courier as an option. Pay for the courier only with evidence that its margin is structural, which means volumes and the customer mix.

*This is an analytical view of the business from its filings, not investment advice. I'm not a financial advisor.*
