**What it is:** an integrated palm-oil planter (111,900 ha planted across East, Central and West Kalimantan; 12 mills, 675 t FFB/hr; CPO 631 kt in FY2025) wearing two smaller costumes — a Central-Java wood-products business (engineered flooring and panels, ~10% of revenue) and a "renewable energy" segment (palm-kernel shells and wood pellets to Japan, Bio-CNG, ~2% of revenue). Controlled jointly by the **T.P. Rachmat family (30.65%)** and the founding **Oetomo family (29.21%)** — the same Astra-alumni/Triputra ecosystem that controls TAPG.

**Market:** ~Rp1,390/share, market cap **Rp14.6 triliun** (early Aug 2026); 10,599,842,400 shares. FY2025: revenue Rp12.31 T, EBITDA Rp4.08 T, net profit Rp1.83 T. Net debt Rp2.76 T at Jun-2026 (0.7× EBITDA) and falling.

**Period covered:** FY2015–1H2026. **Only one audited filing is in hand — the FY2025 annual report (FY2024 comparatives).** Everything before FY2024 comes from the company's own investor decks and newsletters, which present audited totals but are not the audited statements. The source table at the end says exactly which figure came from where.

## THE ONE THING TO UNDERSTAND FIRST

**DSNG's palm segment earns more than the whole company. Everything else the company shows you subtracts.**

The FY2025 segment note (FS Note 41, confirmed in the FY-2025 public expose): palm EBITDA **Rp4,133 miliar** against consolidated EBITDA of **Rp4,078 miliar**. Wood products — the founding business, two plants in Central Java, a 66%-held flagship (TKPI) — lost Rp14 miliar before tax in 2025 after losing Rp16 miliar in 2024, and its goodwill has now been impaired two years running (Rp6.3 miliar, then Rp5.0 miliar, both parked inside G&A). Renewable energy — the segment the decks lead with, Bio-CNG photos on every cover — swung to a Rp8.8 miliar pre-tax loss in 2025 as Japanese shell prices fell 14% and the pellet plant ramped. By 1H2026 it got worse: wood lost Rp48 miliar in six months (flooring volumes −64% YoY into the US tariff wall) and renewables lost Rp4 miliar.

So the real company is this: **one crop, largely one place**. 88% of revenue and >100% of pre-tax profit is palm, and 56% of the planted estate is a single contiguous ~61,700 ha block at Muara Wahau, East Kalimantan — the company itself calls it "roughly the size of Singapore". That block mills fruit at a **23.3% oil-extraction rate, the best in the listed Indonesian peer group** (SGRO 20.8%, AALI ~21–22%), sells 100% of its CPO domestically to three refiner groups (Sinar Mas, Wilmar, Apical/RGE = 54% of consolidated revenue), and in 2025–26 is enjoying a price windfall that is substantially **policy-made** — the B40→B50 biodiesel mandate that took effect 1 July 2026 holds domestic CPO near Rp15,800/kg.

Why it matters: every judgment about DSNG is a judgment about that estate. It is aging — average 15 years, prime-age share **fell from 75% to 63% in one year** as a cohort aged out of the prime band — and replanting (5,500 ha felled cumulatively since 2022 against ~111,900 ha planted) is running at roughly half the ~4,500 ha/yr a 25-year cycle requires. FFB production fell 4.8% in 1H2026 partly because of replanting fellings. Meanwhile the diversification stories (wood, pellets, Bio-CNG, and unexplained breadcrumbs — a "Fruits Business" and "Corn Business" in the org chart, dormant poultry subsidiaries, a Rp434 miliar non-operating Sukabumi land company, a director whose 2025 training log is all Sustainable Aviation Fuel conferences) consume attention and, so far, capital without profit.

The counterweight — and it is a real one — is the balance sheet. Net debt has gone from Rp6.1 triliun (4.7× EBITDA, 1.65× equity) in 2019 to **Rp2.8 triliun (0.7×, 0.2×)** at June 2026; the 2020 bonds were fully repaid in July 2025 and DSNG is now bond-free. The evidence for all of this is in §2.2 (estate and age), §3.2 (the deleveraging), and §3.7 (what the windfall is worth if policy or kernels turn).

Two things stand out from the shape of this history. First, DSNG builds rather than buys: in 45 years there are only four bolt-on acquisitions (PWP, TKPI/KPAS, APR, BAS/BPN), all small relative to the organic estate. Second, the 2018 BAS/BPN deal — the largest — was disclosed with deliberate vagueness in the narrative sections (targets unnamed, "±17,000 ha") and priced only deep in the goodwill note. At ~Rp123 juta/planted ha including assumed debt it was, with hindsight, decent buying: POSCO paid ~Rp113 juta/planted ha for SGRO's worse estate in late 2025 in a control transaction.

Verbatim from the AR: *"Pemilik manfaat akhir dari kepemilikan saham Perseroan adalah Ny. T.P. Rachmat L.R. Imanto dan Andrianto Oetomo"* — the stated UBOs are **Mrs. T.P. Rachmat (née L.R. Imanto)**, not Theodore Permadi Rachmat himself, and **Andrianto Oetomo**, the President Director. This mirrors TAPG exactly, where the same Mrs. Rachmat is the disclosed controller of Triputra Investindo Arya. DSNG is the third leg of the Astra-alumni palm ecosystem: T.P. Rachmat (ex-Astra CEO), the late Benny Subianto's family (Persada Capital; daughter Arini Subianto sits on DSNG's board and is President Director of the 7.44% vehicle), and the operating Oetomo family, whose brothers run the company day-to-day.

Points worth holding onto:

1. **Neither family alone controls; together they do.** 59.86% between two blocs whose relationship is a 45-year partnership, not a shareholders' agreement disclosed anywhere in the AR. The FS, strikingly, states **no ultimate controlling party at all** (no "entitas induk terakhir" note) — the largest single holder is 28.28%.
2. **The balance shifted in 2025.** Triputra added ~69.3m shares (Rachmat bloc now clearly the larger at 30.65% vs 29.21%) while **PT Mitra Aneka Guna sold 150m shares** and slipped under the 5% disclosure line. The decks' shareholder slides (PE2021–PE2025) identify the ~6.32% MAG stake as the **"Liana Salim Lim family"** — a fourth, silent family that has now half-exited. None of this movement is explained anywhere.
3. **A conflict between sources:** the decks put the Subianto family at 9.76–9.92% (2021–2024) while the AR2025 chart shows 7.44%. Either Persada-side sub-5% holdings were counted in the decks or there was a sell-down; unresolvable from the documents in hand.
4. Free float ≈ 33.9% of the register, but Simply Wall St's look-through (Jul 2026) puts the true public at ~26.8% once sub-5% family and insider holdings are stripped. Shareholder count jumped 4,805 → 7,336 during 2025 as the stock doubled in Q3.

For calibration across the peer group I have torn down: SSMS ran 27–91% related-party revenue; CSRA priced 58.5% of COGS through affiliates; SMAR is structurally a conduit for its parent. DSNG sits at the clean extreme with TAPG (4.2% of COGS) — the Triputra-ecosystem names are consistently the cleanest RPT disclosures on the exchange. The family's economics flow through the two channels that are visible and pro-rata: **dividends** (the three family blocs collect ~67% of every payout) and **board remuneration** (Rp83.8 miliar). One caveat: clean RPT notes show trading relationships, not perimeter deals — the things to watch are transactions at the edge of the group (the Panyindangan asset build-up, the SWA coal-land release below), which never pass through an RPT note.

Two RPT-adjacent items that deserve the label even though the FS doesn't give it:

- **The SWA coal carve-out (FS Note 42):** since 2012, SWA has agreed to release **1,770 ha of its Muara Wahau HGU** to three coal miners (PT Persada Multi Bara, PT Khazana Bumi Kaliman, PT Bumi Kaliman Sejahtera) for compensation revised to Rp254,189 juta; Rp36,467 juta of advances received sit in current liabilities; the agreement expires 28 May 2026. Whether these miners connect to any shareholder ecosystem is not disclosed — the Rachmat/Subianto orbit is historically coal-heavy (Adaro/Alamtri).
- **Plasma guarantees:** subsidiaries guarantee cooperative (KUD/KSU) bank loans — ≈ **Rp112 miliar outstanding** at end-2025, off balance sheet.

The business model in one paragraph: grow FFB on ~102k mature ha in Kalimantan (plus buy ~19% of mill throughput from third parties), mill it at the peer group's best extraction rate into CPO that is sold 100% domestically to three refiner groups at KPBN-linked prices, crush most of the kernels into PKO (a lauric oil priced off the coconut/PKO complex, not CPO), and monetize the residuals — shells and methane — into Japan's biomass-power market. The wood business buys smallholder sengon/jabon logs and imported oak in Java, makes panels (90% to Japan) and flooring (Europe/North America), and currently loses money doing it.

Note what the product slate is **not**: no refinery, no biodiesel plant, no oleochemicals. DSNG is a pure upstream seller into other people's downstream — which is why Pefindo caps the rating at idA+ ("limited downstream integration") and why the B50 policy reaches DSNG only through price, not through FAME contracts.

## 2.4 Process — how the product is made and what it costs

### The physical process

FFB is harvested (mechanization push: harvest, transport, roads), hauled to 12 mills inside the estate ring, sterilized/pressed within 24h of cutting (the FFA 3.01% and the OER 23.32% are the quality evidence — fruit gets to the mill fast because the estate is contiguous), producing CPO + kernels + shells + POME. Kernels go to the 400 t/d crusher for PKO; shells go to Japan; POME goes to the two Bio-CNG plants (850 m³/hr bio-methane + 2.9 MW — powering mills and converted FFB trucks, saving ~2M litres of diesel/yr per plant rather than earning revenue). Wood: smallholder logs → Temanggung/Pingit plants → panels (12,000 m³/mo) and flooring (150,000 m²/mo, with imported oak face layers).

## 2.6 Key variables — what actually moves the model

Ranked by profit leverage. Anchor: FY2025 revenue Rp12,315 miliar, PBT Rp2,500 miliar.

**1. CPO price (≈74% of revenue).** Every Rp1,000/kg (Rp1 juta/t) on 629 kt of sales = ±Rp629 miliar of revenue and, with costs fixed short-term, roughly ±Rp600 miliar of PBT — **±24% of FY2025 PBT** [calculated]. The FY2025 windfall was mostly this: ASP +13.2% = +Rp1,056 miliar. The price is set by the domestic balance, which is now policy-managed: B50 (§2.7) pulls demand up; the export levy (10% → 12.5% from 1 Mar 2026) pushes the domestic price down relative to export parity to feed BPDPKS. DSNG is a pure price-taker with zero downstream buffer.

**2. The kernel complex (PK+PKO ≈ 12% of revenue, outsized in the 2025 beat).** PKO ASP +62.5% and PK +61.9% in 2025 — the lauric-oils shock (coconut shortage) that also drove TAPG's and SGRO's beats. PKO revenue alone rose ≈ Rp429 miliar [calculated]. This is a different commodity cycle from CPO and it mean-reverts: full reversion to 2024 kernel ASPs costs ≈ Rp570 miliar of revenue ≈ 23% of FY2025 PBT [calculated, §3.7]. 1H26 PKO ASP was still climbing (Rp32.28 juta/t) — the risk is loaded, not passed.

**3. Own-fruit volume — the estate clock (100% of the milling margin).** FFB own 2,189 kt (+3.8% in 2025, −4.8% in 1H26). Drivers: age curve (§2.2), replanting fellings, weather lag (the 2016 and 2021 production holes were 12–24-month El Niño echoes; NOAA's late-2026 El Niño projection lands in 2027–28 production). Each 100 kt of own FFB ≈ 23 kt CPO ≈ Rp340 miliar revenue at 2025 prices, at estate cash cost rather than market fruit cost — roughly Rp150–200 miliar of PBT [estimated].

**4. Purchased-FFB dependence (19.1% of throughput and rising when own fruit dips).** Margin-dilutive in rising markets, volume-protective in falling ones. The 2025 COGS jump (+19%) was explicitly attributed to it. Sensitivity: ±5pp of external share on 2.7 Mt throughput ≈ ±135 kt of fruit at a ~Rp1 juta/t margin discount vs own fruit ≈ ∓Rp135 miliar PBT [estimated].

**5. OER (23.32%, the competitive moat).** ±0.5pp = ±13.5 kt CPO on 2.7 Mt throughput ≈ ±Rp196 miliar revenue at 2025 ASP, nearly all margin [calculated]. The 2025 slip (23.92 → 23.32) cost ≈ Rp235 miliar — bigger than the entire wood segment's gross profit. Fruit mix (more external fruit = lower OER) and estate age both push this down over time.

**6. Wood segment (10% of revenue, negative PBT, optionality negative near-term).** 1H26: flooring volumes −64% YoY, segment EBITDA negative, US Section 232 tariffs (Oct 2025) plus final US CVD of 40.87% (all-others, Jul 2026) on Indonesian hardwood plywood — engineered flooring is excluded from the CVD scope but panels are not, and 90% of panels go to Japan anyway. Worst plausible case is a ~Rp100–150 miliar/yr PBT drag plus eventual TKPI impairment (goodwill remaining Rp54,910 juta); best case is the 2021–22 cyclical Rp100–270 miliar EBITDA returning.

**7. IDR/USD.** Now minor: net USD liability USD 22.0m unhedged (a 3% move = ±Rp8.6 miliar). Was the story in 2015–2020 (the "core profit" adjustment existed because of it); deleveraging killed it.

**8. Policy wildcards.** B50 execution (quota vs full mandate), levy trajectory, EUDR (palm 100% domestic — DSNG is *less* EUDR-exposed than exporters; its wood/pellet lines to Japan/Korea are outside EUDR's bite), and the kawasan-hutan enforcement wave (§2.7) where DSNG's silence is either cleanliness or a gap.

## 2.7 Demand

**Domestic CPO demand is being legislated upward.** GAPKI's 2025 full-year data: Indonesian consumption 24.7 Mt (+3.8%), of which biodiesel 12.7 Mt (+11.0%) under B40. The B50 mandate — postponed in January 2026, then reversed and signed 17 June 2026 (Kepmen ESDM 257.K/2026), **effective 1 July 2026** with a 3-month B40-stock transition — requires roughly 19–20 M kL of FAME annualized ≈ 17–19 Mt of CPO [estimated], an incremental ~4–6 Mt/yr of domestic demand ≈ 10% of national production. That, against 2025 ending stocks of just 2.06 Mt (−20% YoY), is why KPBN holds near Rp15,800/kg in August 2026 and why BMD trades above RM4,800/t. Funding: the export levy was raised to 12.5% precisely to pay for it — producers fund their own demand subsidy; the net effect on an upstream domestic seller like DSNG is still strongly positive (levy is paid by exporters; the domestic price pull is enjoyed by all).

Export demand (32.3 Mt, +9.5% in 2025 at USD 1,221/t average) matters to DSNG only via the price transmission. EUDR entered application for large operators 30 Dec 2025 with Indonesia at "standard risk" — a documentation cost for exporters, largely bypassing DSNG's domestic CPO sales.

Wood demand: US housing/renovation weak and now tariffed (Section 232 from 14 Oct 2025; furniture step-ups Jan 2026); Japan panel demand recovering from the 2023 inventory glut; Europe soft. Biomass demand from Japanese power (shells, pellets) is contract-backed (erex 15-yr, ≤140 kt/yr) but price-soft — shell ASP −14% in 2025.

## 2.8 Supply

Indonesian CPO production 51.6 Mt in 2025 (+7.3%) — a record, and the reason prices needed B50 to hold. DSNG's 631 kt is **\<2% of national output** [calculated]; it is a price-taker at national scale but a meaningful E. Kalimantan node (and its Muara Wahau block is one of the largest contiguous private estates in the country). Supply growth nationally is yield-driven now: the moratorium era plus NDPE ended greenfield expansion for listed players; the national tree stock is aging exactly like DSNG's, and smallholder replanting (PSR) chronically underruns. Structurally supportive for the decade price.

The enforcement overlay: **Satgas PKH (Perpres 5/2025)** had by May 2026 taken over 5.9 M ha of plantations-in-forest-estate, transferring 4.1 M ha to state-owned Agrinas Palma Nusantara and collecting ~Rp10.3 T in administrative fines. Peers took direct hits (SGRO: Rp700 miliar fine, 2,986 ha surrendered; SIMP: Rp2.34 T charge; AALI: Rp572 miliar). **DSNG discloses nothing** — no PKH mention, no kawasan-hutan contingency, no fine, and my web pass found no seizure or fine reported against any DSNG subsidiary. The only oblique acknowledgment is a one-clause reference to "enforcement of plantation-land legality compliance" as a 2025 external challenge (AR p.133). Either DSNG's titles genuinely sit outside forest estate (consistent with its early HGU vintage and its RSPO/SPOTT standing), or exposure exists and is undisclosed. The 25,559 ha still without HGU is the place to keep asking (§3.8).

**Kernel-reversion case:** PKO and PK ASPs revert to FY2024 levels (PKO 17.81, PK 7.41) from 1 Jan 2027 — but markets discount it during 2026. The balance sheet yawns at this (net debt would still fall); the equity story would not: at 10× the earnings hit, that is a ~Rp9–10 T valuation, ≈ −35%.

**What would break it (not model-able):** a kawasan-hutan finding against the 25,559 untitled ha; an El Niño hit landing in 2027–28 fruit; a forced choice on TKPI. None are in any of the three cases above.

# SUMMARY VERDICT

**What this business is:** the best small palm estate on the IDX, run conservatively by the two families who built it, temporarily dressed as a three-segment "sustainable agribusiness". One contiguous East Kalimantan block produces a 23.3% OER and ~5 t of CPO per hectare — top of the listed class — sold entirely into the domestic refining complex at prices currently underwritten by the B50 mandate. Everything else — wood, shells, pellets, Bio-CNG — is somewhere between a rounding error and a small leak.

**What it earns through the cycle:** palm EBITDA of Rp40 juta/ha at 2025 prices, perhaps Rp25–30 juta/ha at mid-cycle (Rp12–13 juta/t CPO) [estimated] — comfortably above its cost of capital on any replacement-cost basis. Group EBITDA through the last full cycle: Rp1.3 T (trough 2019) to Rp4.1 T (peak 2025), with net profit spanning Rp178 miliar to Rp1.83 T. The company converts earnings to cash (cumulative CFO 1.7× cumulative net profit since 2018) and has just finished converting cash to a fortress: bond-free, 0.7× levered, one bank, Rp2.6 T of liquidity.

**What has to be true for it to work from here:** (1) domestic CPO prices hold in the Rp14–16 juta/t band — which is a bet on Indonesian biodiesel policy surviving its own funding math; (2) replanting accelerates to ~2× its current pace before the 2005–10 cohort's decline steepens, and the Verdant seed delivers the claimed yield uplift; (3) the families keep doing what they have done for a decade — no empire-building with the free cash flow, no related-party drift, dividends stepping up (Rp47 is a start; the payout band's ceiling of 30% has never been tested upward).

**What would break it:** a policy reversal (B50 throttled + levy raised is worth ~−35% of fair value on my stress math); the kernel-complex reversion that is coming regardless (~Rp570 miliar of PBT, timing unknown); a kawasan-hutan finding on the untitled 25,559 ha; or the quiet diversification breadcrumbs maturing into a capital-allocation mistake just as the core business needed its replanting decade funded.

**Position:** at 7.9× earnings, 4.3× EV/EBITDA and ~Rp125 juta of palm-only EV per planted hectare, the market prices DSNG as an average plantation. It is not an average plantation — it is a top-decile operator with a bottom-half disclosure habit (cash costs, capex plans, purchased-fruit volumes and segment cash flows have all been quietly withdrawn from disclosure) and a mid-life estate. The operational quality, balance sheet, and governance cleanliness justify a premium to the pack; the estate age and policy dependence cap it. On the numbers in hand this is the highest-quality name of the fourteen I have torn down in this series, priced with no premium for being so. The honest risk statement: you are buying the 2026–2028 window at a fair price and trusting the families to buy the 2030s with it.
