**What it does.** Runs other people's shops. MAPI operates about 4,000 retail stores, cafés and
department store floors across Indonesia and six other South-East Asian countries, selling roughly
150 international brands it does not own — Zara, Starbucks, Skechers, Sephora, Marks & Spencer,
Adidas, Nike, Apple, SOGO — under contracts with the companies that do own them. It also runs the
country's largest branded-goods wholesale and distribution business, and a garment factory.

**Where.** Indonesia is 83% of sales, concentrated in Jakarta and the big Java malls; the rest is
the Philippines, Vietnam, Thailand, Malaysia, Singapore, Cambodia and a new company in India.

**Size (4 September 2026).** Share price Rp1,425; 16,600,000,000 shares; market capitalisation about
**Rp23.7 triliun** (roughly US$1.4bn). FY2025 revenue Rp43.1 triliun, net profit Rp2.7 triliun, of
which Rp2.2 triliun belonged to MAPI's own shareholders.

**Period covered.** FY1999–4M2004 from the IPO prospectus; FY2021–FY2025 audited; 1H2026 unaudited
from the company's own deck; the change of control in 2026 from press and exchange reporting.

**Sources in hand.** Four annual reports with their full audited accounts (FY2022–FY2025), the
November 2004 IPO offering memorandum in two parts, and six investor presentations from August 2021
to the first half of 2026. Full list, with page counts and exactly what each covers, at the end.

## THE ONE THING TO UNDERSTAND FIRST

MAPI owns almost nothing that makes it money.

It does not own the brands. Zara belongs to Inditex, Starbucks to Starbucks, Skechers to Skechers.
MAPI holds contracts — permissions — that let it open shops under those names in a defined
territory, in exchange for a fee. It does not own the shops either: every store is rented space
inside somebody else's shopping mall. What MAPI actually owns is the fit-out, the till systems, the
stock on the shelves, the staff contracts, and the permissions themselves.

That is a legitimate way to build a retailer, and MAPI has built a big one. But it means the whole
business rests on a stack of agreements with outside parties, and **not one of those agreements has
ever been disclosed**. In twenty-two years of audited accounts not a single brand counterparty has
been named, and not one royalty rate, territory or renewal mechanism given. The 2004 listing
prospectus did better — it named twenty-seven brand owners and said which rights were exclusive —
but in neither era, in any document, is there **one expiry date for any brand, ever**. The audited note that is supposed to describe the group's significant agreements
runs to some thirty sub-notes; twenty-one of them describe bank facilities, and exactly two short
paragraphs describe the brand contracts, in words like "a certain percentage as stated in
the agreement".

Those two paragraphs do, however, say three things that matter enormously.

The first is that the group must buy specified goods from the brand owner. So the brand owner sets
both the fee and the cost of the merchandise.

The second is that MAPI is banned from selling anything that competes with the licensed product,
and that breaking that ban terminates the agreement. In the FY2022 accounts management added a
sentence that has quietly disappeared from every year since: *although the Company has sold some
competing products, management believes the licensors are aware and have not objected.* A retailer
of 150 brands is structurally in breach of some of its own contracts and is relying on goodwill.

The third is the one to hold on to: **several of those agreements give the brand owner an
irrevocable option to buy the equity of the MAPI subsidiary that operates the brand.** Which brands,
which subsidiaries, at what price, over what period — none of it is stated, in any year.

Now put that beside what happened in 2026. On 8 May the founding family's holding company sold its
51% of MAPI to a Singapore vehicle called Pacific Universal Investments for Rp11.8 triliun, and a
follow-on offer to the public took the new owners to 87.2%. The 2004 prospectus had carried an
explicit warning under its own heading — *several of the Group's key licenses contain a prohibition
on a change of control in the Group's shareholding* — and said that in such an event the group "may
have its licensing or distribution arrangements with several key principals terminated if the latter
do not provide consent". A change of control has now happened. Nobody outside the company can tell
which permissions it touched, because nobody outside the company knows what the permissions say.

And the audited accounts for FY2025, signed weeks before the deal completed, do not mention it. The
entire events-after-the-year-end note is a Rp13.9 miliar tax assessment.

Everything else in this report — the margins, the rent, the overseas losses, the two listed
subsidiaries, the 25% share price fall in August 2026 — sits on top of that one fact. MAPI is a very
well-run collection of permissions whose terms are invisible, now owned by people whose identity is
also invisible.

Evidence for the contract point is in §2.1 and §8.3; for the ownership change, §1.1 and §9.10; for
the option clause, §10.1.

# 01 — OWNER

## 1.1 Group structure

### The control chain, before and after May 2026

For twenty-two years the answer to "who owns MAPI" was one line in the annual report: a private
company called PT Satya Mulia Gema Gemilang held 51%, the public held the rest, and the reports
named **Mrs. Marisa Kolonas** as the person behind it. Nothing was ever published about Satya Mulia
itself — no shareholders, no directors, no accounts. The 2004 prospectus is the last document that
opened it up at all, and it stopped one hop short: Satya Mulia was 99.99% owned by **PT Mitralestari
Adiperkasa**, which the prospectus describes nowhere.

The mechanics were ordinary. Under Indonesian rules, anyone who takes control of a listed company
must offer to buy out everybody else at a regulated price. So the buyer paid Rp1,395 a share for the
51% block and then offered Rp1,550 to the public. About Rp9.3 triliun of shares were tendered.
Total cash out was roughly **Rp21 triliun**, against a company that had a market value of about
Rp19 triliun at the end of 2025.

could not be retrieved. The signing date of the sale agreement is not in any source reached.*

Three things about this are worth stating plainly.

**The private equity firm is not where you would expect it.** CVC Capital Partners is widely
reported as the buyer, and CVC has form here — it backed MAPI's sports arm in 2015 and owned
Matahari Department Store before that. But CVC's 49% sits only in the two vehicles that bought the
*public* shares. The 51% control block was bought by Pacific Universal Investments on its own. So
CVC's economic interest attaches to the 36% tendered, not to control. Who stands behind Pacific
Universal Holdings is not disclosed anywhere.

**Two of the buyer's directors were already sitting on MAPI's board.** Sean Gustav Standish Hughes
has been a MAPI director since 2024 and was a commissioner in 2020–21; Zoee Ho Ziwei became a
commissioner in June 2024. Both are named in reporting as directors of the buying vehicle. That is
close to two years of overlap between the board of the target and the board of the buyer, and MAPI's
own annual report describes both of them as "unaffiliated".

**The float is now too small for the exchange's new rule.** In March 2026 the Indonesia Stock
Exchange raised the minimum public shareholding from 7.5% to 15%, applying to companies already
listed, with 12.5% required by March 2027 and 15% by March 2028. MAPI's 12.76% clears the first step
and misses the second. No source connects MAPI to the rule, and no plan to delist, go private or
sell shares back into the market has been announced — but arithmetic says something has to change
before March 2028. *This link is my inference, not a company statement.*

## 1.3 History — recap

Four patterns come out of that timeline, and each of them is still running.

**The company was assembled, not founded.** Four of the five people who incorporated MAPI in 1995 were
gone within two years, and the fifth was dead by 1998. The cap table was then rebuilt twice more — once when a founder died in 1998
and his estate sold out, and again in 2001 when Satya Mulia arrived holding 2.5% and left the same
day holding 84%, having subscribed for new shares while the incumbent holder subscribed for none.
**No price is disclosed for any of those secondary transfers**, in the prospectus or anywhere else.
Every share that changed hands between the founding and the listing changed hands at an
undisclosed value.

**Growth has come from taking on other people's brands, not from building anything.** Read the
timeline as a list of names — Reebok, Sports Station, Kidz Station, Marks & Spencer, Lotus,
Starbucks, SOGO, Zara, Foot Locker, Apple, Ace Hardware — and the business model is visible without
a financial figure. MAPI's skill is winning the Indonesian rights to a brand, opening it fast and
running it competently. In 2026 that skill took the Ace Hardware licence off a listed competitor,
and the brand's US head office approached MAPI rather than the other way round.

**Pieces get carved off and floated, and the pieces have kept their own investors.** The two most
distinctive things about MAPI's structure — a listed sports subsidiary and a listed food subsidiary
— both came from selling a minority stake to a private equity firm and then taking the business
public. CVC bought into the sports arm in 2015 and it listed in 2018; General Atlantic financed the
food arm in 2016 and it listed in 2017. Both financings came with options over the shares. This is
a family that raises money by selling slices of the business rather than by borrowing.

**And in 2026 the family sold the whole thing.** After thirty-one years, at Rp1,395 a share — a
price that valued the company at about ten times the earnings attributable to its own shareholders,
and below the level the shares had traded at through most of 2024.

## 1.4 Owner's character

There is no way to interview the owners, so the record has to speak. Five things in it.

**They did not pay themselves dividends for most of the company's life, and barely pay them now.**
MAPI paid nothing at all for the pandemic years, resumed in 2023, and has since paid out 6.3%, 7.0%
and 9.4% of profit. At the 2004 listing the stated policy was 10–15% of profit up to Rp100 miliar
and 16–20% above it. Even the current payout is below what the company told the public it would do
twenty-two years ago.

| Paid in | For year | Per share | Total | % of that year's profit |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 2023 | FY2022 | Rp8.00 | Rp132,317m | 6.3% |
| 2024 | FY2023 | Rp8.00 | Rp132,317m | 7.0% |
| 2025 | FY2024 | Rp10.00 | Rp166,000m | 9.4% |

**They pay the executives a great deal more than they pay themselves as shareholders.** In FY2025
the directors and key employees took Rp542 miliar between them in short-term pay. The dividend that
year was Rp166 miliar. Executive pay has grown 3.6 times since 2021, while the commissioners' pay
has barely moved and the payout ratio has crept from 6% to 9%.

| Rp m | FY2021 | FY2022 | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Commissioners | 8,737 | 11,284 | 10,177 | 10,717 | 10,895 |
| Directors (from FY2024, plus "key employees") | 150,709 | 352,569 | 403,070 | 453,074 | **541,765** |
| Dividend paid to all shareholders in the same year | – | – | 167,841 | 176,702 | 201,540 |

No figure is given for any individual, in any year. From FY2024 the directors' line is merged with
"key employees", so it can no longer be read as a board number at all. There is no share plan at MAPI
level: the reports state flatly, every year, that MAPI runs no management incentive programme and no
employee share scheme. There is one a layer down — an arrangement to sell MBA shares to key managers
in three tranches, carried in equity at Rp13 miliar, whose valuation was rewritten when the General
Atlantic bond's maturity moved (§9.6). And **no commissioner or director owns a single MAPI share.**

**Nobody has ever bought shares alongside the public.** The one exception runs the other way. MAPI
bought back 6.3 million of its own shares in late 2015 for about Rp21 miliar, and then took **nine
years** to sell them again, finally placing the last 60.4 million (post-split) on 3 February 2025
for Rp79 miliar. It is a profitable trade and a fair use of a buyback. It is also the only time in
twenty-two years that anyone connected to the company traded the stock in size.

**They are unusually good at not owing money.** MAPI runs thirty-odd bank facilities across more than twenty
lenders in six currencies. At the end of 2025 the **twenty-one facilities described in the
significant-agreements note were entirely undrawn**, and only eight lenders had money out at all. Set the shop leases aside and the group holds more cash
than debt in every year of the last five. That is a deliberate posture and it has survived a
pandemic that closed the stores.

**And they built the disclosure the way they built the company — the minimum.** The 2004 prospectus
at least named twenty-seven brand principals and said which rights were exclusive. The modern
accounts name none. Store floor area was published every year until FY2024 and dropped from the
FY2025 annual report. Same-store sales — the single number every retailer in the world reports —
has never appeared in any annual report or any of the six investor decks. It is not that MAPI
discloses badly. It is that MAPI discloses exactly what the rules require and nothing else, for
thirty-one years, under two different owners.

## 1.5 RPT

"Related-party transactions" means business the company does with people connected to it — its own
shareholders, its part-owned associates, companies run by the same managers. It matters because
those are the deals where money can leave a listed company on terms nobody negotiated at arm's
length.

On the face of it, MAPI's are trivial.

| | FY2021 | FY2022 | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Revenue from related parties, % of total | 0.30% | 0.55% | 0.42% | 0.10% | **0.07%** |
| Purchases from related parties | none | none | none | none | not stated |
| Trade payable to related parties, Rp m | 79,722 | 140,341 | 12,484 | 15,474 | 11,815 |
| — as % of total liabilities | 0.83% | 1.25% | 0.08% | 0.10% | 0.08% |
| Consignment commission earned from Samsonite, Rp m | 55,273 | 147,369 | 139,207 | 36,247 | 31,673 |

Under half a percent of revenue at the peak, no purchases at all, and shrinking. Compared with most
Indonesian family-controlled listed companies, that is remarkably clean, and it should be said
plainly.

Four things qualify it.

**The shrinkage is not because the trading stopped.** Samsonite Indonesia and Dom Pizza Indonesia
were both footnoted in FY2024 as no longer being related parties. MAPI still owns 40% of Samsonite
Indonesia and still sells its bags. What changed is the label, not the commerce. Dom Pizza then
reappears in the FY2025 list as an associate, with a footnote saying that in 2024 it was not one.

**The controlling shareholder barely transacts with the company at all.** Satya Mulia appears in
the related-party note as the parent in every year the accounts are in hand — FY2022 to FY2025 —
with no transaction recorded against it. The one exception is historic: the 2004 prospectus records
Satya Mulia giving a **Rp25 miliar corporate guarantee** to support a Bank Niaga facility, in an era
when the company's bank security also included a related party's land and machinery and a third
party's land. Nothing of that kind appears in any modern year.

**No pricing basis is ever stated.** Not "arm's length", not "same terms as third parties", nothing
— in any year. And the management fees MAPI charges its associates for running their tax, HR,
shipping, IT and licensing are described in words but almost never in numbers. The only figures
anywhere are an aggregate Rp6.5 miliar of fee income in each of FY2021 and FY2022, and a Rp1,340
million non-cash movement in FY2025.

**And the disclosure has moved around.** The percentage of revenue earned from related parties, set
out in the related-party note itself in earlier years, appears in FY2025 only in the revenue note —
and the related-party note that year gives no revenue or purchase figure of its own.

The one related-party arrangement that does matter in money terms is not in this note at all. It is
the financing from General Atlantic over the food business, and it gets its own section (§9.4 and
§9.9), because at its peak it put Rp430 miliar of interest-free debt on the balance sheet, generated
a **Rp54 miliar accounting profit out of nothing more than extending its own maturity**, and left a
brand-new option over about a tenth of MAPI's food subsidiary sitting in the accounts seven months
before the change of control was completed.

# 02 — MODEL

## 2.1 The chassis — how one MAPI store makes money, and what it costs

Everything MAPI does is a variation on one machine. Understand the machine once and the four
segments become four settings on it.

### Product — the four ways money arrives

MAPI's revenue line is not one thing. It is four, and they behave differently.

| FY2025 | Rp m | % of revenue | What it is |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Retail sales | 39,339,608 | 91.3 | MAPI buys the goods, owns them, and sells them in its own shop. All the risk, all the margin |
| Wholesale sales | 2,053,626 | 4.8 | MAPI buys the goods and sells them on to another retailer. Thin margin, high volume |
| Consignment commission — net | 1,348,585 | 3.1 | Somebody else's goods sit in MAPI's department store. MAPI never owns them and books only its cut |
| Rent and service revenue | 137,662 | 0.3 | MAPI is the landlord — mostly space inside its own department stores let to other brands |
| Other | 205,180 | 0.5 | |
| Sales returns and discounts | (1,453) | — | |
| **Net revenue** | **43,083,208** | **100.0** | |

The consignment line needs unpacking, because it makes the department stores look far smaller than
they are. Rp4.5 triliun of goods passed across MAPI's counters in 2025 on consignment,
mostly in the department stores. MAPI reports only the Rp1.35 triliun it kept — a cut of about **30 sen in every rupiah**

### Supply chain — the physical flow

```
  BRAND OWNER (Inditex, Starbucks Corp., Adidas, Apple, Skechers …)
       │  grants a permission: licence, franchise or distribution right
       │  charges a royalty — about 3.4% of MAPI's revenue in total
       │  in several cases REQUIRES MAPI to buy the goods from it
       ▼
  MAPI buys the merchandise  ── Rp25.2 triliun of purchases in FY2025
       │                        (Apple alone = 15.1% of group revenue)
       ▼
  DISTRIBUTION CENTRE (Cileungsi, Bogor) + a garment factory at Gunung Putri
       │  warehouse operating cost Rp555 miliar
       ▼
  4,023 STORES, 1,242,613 m² ── every one of them RENTED from a mall owner
       │  rent + depreciation of the rented space = about 9.5% of revenue
       │  shop staff = about 7% of revenue
       ▼
  CUSTOMER  ── 91% walks into a shop; 10% buys online, and two-thirds of
                that online business now runs through other people's marketplaces
```

Nothing in that chain is owned outright except the fit-out, the stock and the factory. The
permission is rented from the brand. The floor is rented from the mall. Even the online storefront
is increasingly rented from Shopee and TikTok.

### Purchase — what the balance sheet actually holds

MAPI's assets are not shops. They are stock (28.5% of the balance sheet), the fit-out and equipment
inside rented floors (18.8%), the accounting value of the rented floors themselves (17.0%) and cash
(15.4%). The full statement is at §7.1.

Stock is the single largest asset and has been for years. It also turns slowly for a fashion

Three readings of that table.

**Rent is bigger than it looks, and it comes in two halves.** Since 2020 an accounting rule called
PSAK 73 requires a company to treat the fixed part of a shop lease as if it had bought the floor and
borrowed the money: the floor appears as an asset and is depreciated, and the payments split into
interest and principal. But the *variable* part of the rent is not treated that way.
MAPI's own accounting policy says so in terms: variable rents that do not depend on an index or rate
are left out of both the lease liability and the leased-floor asset, and the payments are expensed
in the period the event that triggers them occurs. So the "rent and service charge" line in the
table above is the part of occupancy MAPI does **not** capitalise — mostly turnover-linked rent and
service charges, though the company never splits it. Add the three pieces together and the real cost
of occupying 1.24 million square metres of Indonesian mall space is:

| Rp m, FY2025 | | % of revenue `[calculated]` |
|---|---:|---:|
| Depreciation of right-of-use assets | 2,006,515 | 4.7 |
| Cash rent and service charge — the part not capitalised | 1,727,324 | 4.0 |
| Interest on lease liabilities | 350,768 | 0.8 |
| **True cost of occupancy** | **4,084,607** | **9.5** |

That split is the most important unpublished number in the company. A retailer whose rent is mostly
turnover-linked has far less operating gearing — when sales fall, the rent falls with them — than one
paying flat rent. MAPI does not disclose the split. What can be said is that the uncapitalised half
has grown from 3.4% of revenue in 2022 to 4.0% in 2025, faster than sales, which is what you would
expect if landlords have been shifting more of the rent onto a percentage as their bargaining
position improved. *(Provenance: the policy wording is read directly from note 3 of the FY2025
accounts; the split itself is inference, not disclosure.)*

**The royalty is the price of the permissions, and it has risen.** Royalty appears in two places —
inside the cost of goods for merchandise licences, and as a separate selling expense for franchise

## 2.2 Specialty Stores — Active

This is the sports, sports-leisure and kids business, and it is where MAPI makes its money. It sits
in the separately listed **PT Map Aktif Adiperkasa (MAPA)**, 68.83% owned.

**Product.** Two things at once. A chain of multi-brand sports shops MAPI has owned for thirty
years — Sports Station (400 stores, acquired in 1995), Planet Sports (63), Kidz Station (106) — and
a set of single-brand shops for other people's names: Skechers (246), Converse (170), Foot Locker
(82), plus Adidas, Nike, Puma, New Balance, Crocs, Birkenstock, Asics, Onitsuka Tiger, Dr. Martens,
Aldo, Reebok. MAPI also describes itself as Indonesia's leading distributor for Active and Tech brands, which is
where much of the Rp2.05 triliun wholesale line is likely to arise — though no split is published.

**Supply chain and purchase.** Almost all of MAPA's regional expansion has been bought rather than
built: Planet Sports Philippines through a Singapore holding company in 2020, Foot Locker Singapore
and Malaysia in 2023, Aldo's Thai and Malaysian operators, a Converse joint venture in Singapore and
Malaysia. Nineteen of MAPA's thirty-four subsidiaries are outside Indonesia.

**Process.** MAPA's own reported margin runs a little ahead of the group: net profit of Rp1,702
miliar on Rp19,280 miliar of revenue in FY2025, 8.8%, against 6.3% for the group. Its store area has
grown 84% in four years, from 270,129 m² to 497,546 m².

**Distribute.** 2,239 stores at the end of 2025, 2,331 by June 2026 — over half the group's entire
store count and 38% of its floor area.

**What matters here.** Sixteen brands are marked *non-exclusive* on MAPI's own first-half 2026 brand
map, and almost all the big ones are in that list — Nike, Adidas, Puma, Asics, New Balance, Onitsuka
Tiger, Birkenstock, Mizuno, Salomon, Saucony, On, Wilson. That is the first time in six investor
decks MAPI has published which brands it holds exclusively and which it does not, and it sits oddly
beside the annual report's standing claim of "150+ exclusive brands" in Indonesia. In the segment
carrying most of the group's profit, MAPI looks like a preferred retailer rather than a gatekeeper —
and a brand that is non-exclusive can appoint a second Indonesian partner without breaching
anything.

## 2.3 Specialty Stores — Fashion, Beauty and Technology

This is what MAPI owns outright, and it divides into two very different businesses.

**The fashion and beauty half** is the six Inditex fascias — Zara (20 stores), Massimo Dutti, Pull &
Bear, Stradivarius, Bershka, Oysho — plus Marks & Spencer (34), Lacoste (72), Sephora (24), Boots
(33), Tommy Hilfiger (30), Cotton On, Mango, Kiko Milano, Boss, and in 2025 a push into genuine
luxury with Chloé, Christian Louboutin, Gianvito Rossi and Aquazzura. Each brand sits in its own
company. Zara's company alone holds Rp1.07 triliun of assets. Store count here has been almost
static — 594 in 2024, 614 in 2025 — while floor area barely moved, which says this business is now
about productivity per store rather than more stores.

**The technology half** is a different animal entirely. Digimap and Digiplus, built from the 2019
purchase of an Apple retailer called Infinite, sell Apple products. In four years the format has
gone from 60 stores to 244 and from 6,872 m² to 24,506 m². And it now dominates the purchasing side
of the group:

**Apple South Asia Pte. Ltd. is the only supplier ever disclosed as exceeding 10% of MAPI's
revenue — 14.0% in FY2023, 10.2% in FY2024 and 15.1% in FY2025.** One supplier is a sixth of group
revenue, and close to a quarter of what the group buys. Phone retail is a low-margin, allocation-driven
business — the retailer's profit depends on how much stock the manufacturer chooses to give it, and
the manufacturer sets the retail price. This is almost certainly the largest single reason group
gross margin has fallen from 45% to 42%, and it is why the fourth quarter of 2025 jumped: the
iPhone 17 launched in October. It also means MAPI competes head-on with Erajaya, a separately listed
company that runs the rival iBox chain. *A common error worth correcting: iBox is Erajaya's, not
MAPI's.*

## 2.4 Department Stores

The oldest part of the business and now the smallest but the most profitable per rupiah of sales.

**Product.** SOGO (18 stores), Seibu (1), Galeries Lafayette (1), Alun Alun Indonesia, and — the
growth story — **The Foodhall**, a premium supermarket that has gone from 36 stores in 2022 to 49 in
2025. On store count, MAPI's department store division is now mostly a supermarket division.

**Process.** This is the segment that runs on other people's stock. In 2004, the last time MAPI
published the mix, 73% of what crossed a SOGO counter belonged to the brand that supplied it and
89% at Lotus; MAPI took a commission and booked nothing else. The modern split is not disclosed in
any year, but the group commission rate of about 30% has held steady since 2021. That is why the segment's reported revenue is small (Rp2.85 triliun) while
its floor area is enormous (338,618 m², 27% of the group's total space on 1.7% of its stores).

**What it earns.** The best margin in the group — a segment result of 13.2% of segment revenue in
FY2025, against 10.2% for specialty retail. But the revenue has not moved in three years: Rp2.78
triliun in 2023, Rp2.84 triliun in 2024, Rp2.85 triliun in 2025, on floor space that has barely
changed. This is a stable, cash-generative, structurally shrinking business — department stores are
losing share to specialty retail and e-commerce everywhere, and MAPI's own numbers say the same.

# 03 — DEMAND

## 3.1 Who buys, and whether they still can

MAPI sells discretionary goods — trainers, handbags, a coffee, a phone — to Indonesians with money
left over after necessities. So the demand question is not "is Indonesia growing" but "is the layer
of Indonesians who shop in malls growing".

The headline numbers say yes. Indonesian output grew 5.11% in 2025 and 5.61% and 5.29% in the first
two quarters of 2026, and household spending was the single largest contributor — 2.67 percentage
points of the 5.29% in the second quarter of 2026. Bank Indonesia's monthly retail survey showed its
sales index peaking in March 2026 for the Ramadan and Lebaran season, with clothing among the
categories growing year on year.

The commentary says something else. Through 2025 and 2026 Indonesian public debate has been
dominated by a shrinking middle class, deflation in some months, and layoffs — over 23,000 recorded
by early July 2026. In August 2026 the Trade Minister publicly denied that store closures were being
caused by weak purchasing power, which is the kind of statement only made when a lot of people think
the opposite.

Both can be true at once if spending is concentrating at the top, and MAPI's own behaviour fits that
reading: in 2025 it added Chloé, Christian Louboutin, Gianvito Rossi and Aquazzura — genuine luxury
— while Kidz Station, its mid-market children's chain, shrank from 121 stores to 106, and its
mid-market food business went into loss.

## 3.2 What the customer buys, and where

Demand is concentrated in three places. **Malls**, because that is where every MAPI store is — no
high-street or standalone exposure at all. **Jakarta and Java**, still about two-thirds of sales,
though Jakarta's share has fallen ten points in three years. And increasingly **online**, at about a
tenth of sales, but through marketplaces MAPI does not control.

The single largest demand swing factor right now is not fashion. It is Apple product launches: group
revenue in the fourth quarter of 2025 jumped on the iPhone 17, and Digimap's share of MAPI's online
sales tripled from 11% in 2022 to 35% in the first half of 2026. Phone launches are lumpy, and the
timing sits with Apple.

## 3.3 The regional bet

MAPI's stated growth plan is ASEAN — the Philippines, Vietnam, Thailand, Malaysia, Singapore,
Cambodia and now India — on the argument that the forces that built Indonesian mall retail are
running a few years behind next door. Overseas revenue has gone from 7% of the group in 2021 to 17%
in 2025. The demand thesis may be right; the financial result so far is not, and §8.4 sets out why.

# 04 — SUPPLY

Retail supply, for a company like MAPI, means three separate scarcities: floor space to trade from,
permissions to trade with, and competitors for both.

## 4.1 Mall space

Jakarta's shopping-centre stock is about 4.4 million square metres and grew about 1% in the year to
mid-2026, with only around 62,000 m² of new completions expected in Jakarta by the end of 2026.
Very little new supply is the landlord's friend. Average occupancy is reported between 73% and 78%
depending on whose basket you use — but the useful detail is that the two ends have separated:
**premium and upper-middle malls run at about 90%, while the average is dragged to the low 70s by
weaker stock.**

That split favours MAPI, which trades almost entirely in the top tier and is often the anchor tenant
a mall needs to fill its space. The 2004 prospectus made the point openly: being able to offer a
mall owner a one-stop solution for a large block of floor "helps the Group secure sought-after space
at competitive rates". That bargaining power is probably why occupancy cost has stayed in a 9–10%
band of revenue while MAPI added 1,363 stores in three years. Jakarta rents rose about 1.9% year on
year in the second half of 2026, with roughly half of new leasing demand coming from food and
beverage. A slowly tightening market, not a distressed one.

## 4.2 Permissions

The other scarce input is the right to sell a brand, and here MAPI has a structural advantage that
is hard to attack: a brand entering Indonesia needs a partner with mall relationships, logistics,
staff, and the ability to open twenty stores at once. There are very few such partners. That is why
Ace Hardware's US head office approached MAPI in 2026 after ending a licence that a listed
competitor had held since 1996, and why Marks & Spencer handed MAPI its Philippine franchise in the
same year.

The threat runs the other way. Brands that get big enough eventually want the margin themselves.
Inditex raised its stake in its Indian Zara joint venture to 80% in late 2025 — a brand owner taking
control of a licensed market from a local partner. Whether anything similar is contemplated in
Indonesia could not be established, and MAPI has never disclosed the structure of its Zara
arrangement at all.

# 05 — KEY VARIABLES

Five inputs move this model. Each is expressed against **FY2025 net revenue of Rp43.1 triliun and
operating profit of Rp3.99 triliun**.

**1. Gross margin — 41.7% of revenue, and falling.** The largest single lever by a wide margin. One
percentage point of gross margin is Rp431 miliar, or **11% of operating profit**. It has fallen 3.6
points in two years. The two mechanical causes are the growth of low-margin Apple retail and
markdowns on stock that is 29% over six months old. Neither is discussed by management.

**2. Occupancy cost — about 9.5% of revenue.** Rent, the depreciation of leased floors and lease
interest together. One percentage point here is Rp431 miliar. What makes this variable different
from most retailers' is that a large but undisclosed part of it is turnover-linked, so it flexes
with sales instead of amplifying a downturn. The unpublished split between fixed and turnover rent
is the single most valuable missing number in the accounts.

**3. Store payroll — 7.0% of revenue, on 33,111 people.** Note that **63% of the workforce is
non-permanent**, which is a deliberate flexibility choice and the second reason this cost base bends
rather than breaks in a downturn.

**4. Royalty — 3.4% of revenue and rising.** Rp1.48 triliun in FY2025, against Rp487 miliar in 2021.
The rate is set by counterparties MAPI does not name and cannot be modelled. Its direction is up.

**5. Apple.** One supplier at 15% of group revenue, whose product cycle determines the shape of the
group's fourth quarter and whose margin structure is dragging group gross margin down. Not a price
variable so much as a mix variable — every rupiah of Apple revenue dilutes the average.

Two further sensitivities worth naming without a weight, because they are binary rather than
continuous. **The permissions themselves** — a lost licence is a lost subsidiary, and several
agreements let the counterparty buy that subsidiary outright. And **the currency**, since MAPI buys
merchandise in dollars, euros and pounds (Rp1.31 triliun of dollar trade payables at the end of
2025) and sells in rupiah, with the 2004 prospectus noting that mall rents themselves carried
exchange-rate clauses.

# 06 — VICISSITUDES

**1998 and the founding.** MAPI was three years old at the Asian financial crisis. The record shows
its founding shareholder dying in May 1998 and his estate selling out; the company survived and its
sports and department store formats came out of the other side.

**2020–2021, the pandemic.** The clearest stress test available, and the numbers are stark. Revenue
fell to Rp14.8 triliun in 2020 and the group made an operating loss of Rp57 miliar and a net loss of
Rp585 miliar. Indonesian malls were under restriction from April 2020 to December 2022 — MAPI's own
FY2022 deck carries the full phase-by-phase timeline, which is the best record in the filing set of
what actually closed the shops.

Three things happened that are worth remembering for the next downturn. Landlords forgave rent —
**Rp942 miliar in 2021 alone**, which is why the cash rent line reads 0.4% of revenue that year.
Online sales went from 1.6% of sales in 2019 to 13.0% in 2021. And no dividend was paid for two
years. The business bent; it did not break; and the recovery was violent — revenue up 46% in 2022
and profit up more than five-fold.

**Late 2023 onwards, the boycotts.** Consumer boycotts of Western brands over the war in Gaza have
cost MAPI its food business's profitability. Revenue there fell 19% in 2024 and the segment has lost
money for two years running. The company names the cause in a single clause and has offered no
recovery plan. This is a live, unresolved shock.

**2024–2026, the middle-class squeeze.** Not yet visible in MAPI's revenue, which grew 23% in the
first half of 2026, but visible in the mix: luxury brands added, mid-market children's stores
closing, gross margin falling.

What a repeat of 2020 would do now, mechanically: revenue is 2.9 times its 2020 level; the cost base
is more variable than it was (turnover rent, 63% non-permanent staff); the group holds Rp5.0 triliun
of cash against Rp2.9 triliun of debt; and twenty-one described banking facilities were entirely
undrawn at the last year end. MAPI is better set for a downturn than it was, and its owners have just paid Rp21
triliun for it.

## 8.2 Why the share price fell 25% in a month

On 4 August 2026 the shares closed at Rp1,895, up 22% on the day the tender result was announced.
A month later they were at Rp1,425. Nothing in the company changed in between.

The mechanical explanation is that the bid was withdrawn from the market. For six weeks anyone
holding MAPI had a guaranteed exit at Rp1,550 from a buyer taking everything offered; when the offer
closed on 17 July and settled on 29 July, that floor disappeared, and with it 74% of the tradeable
float. What is left is 12.76% of a company with an 87% owner — thin, hard to trade in size, and
likely to be dropped or reduced in the indices that were previously forced to hold it. Confirmed
index consequences could not be established, and MSCI had Indonesia under a review freeze from
1 September 2026.

The price now sits barely above the **Rp1,395 the family accepted for control** and well below the
Rp1,550 the public was offered.

## 8.3 The permissions, in the only two forms they have ever been disclosed

This deserves a section of its own because it is the business.

**In 2004**, the prospectus listed seven material contracts and twenty further distribution and
licensing arrangements — Reebok, Starbucks, Marks & Spencer, SOGO, Kinokuniya, The Athlete's Foot,
Nine West, Wilson, Speedo, Spalding, Skechers, Ellesse, Morgan, Lush, Women's Secret, Springfield,
Bandai, LeapFrog, OshKosh B'Gosh and more. It said which were exclusive and which were not, gave
jurisdictions, and gave commencement dates for six.

It gave **no expiry date, no royalty rate, no minimum purchase figure, no renewal mechanism and no
termination provision for a single one of them** — in a document whose purpose was to sell shares in
the company those contracts constitute. The commercial facts in it are three: Marks & Spencer
charged no royalty but required a minimum annual advertising spend of unstated size, Starbucks
charged licence fees and royalties at unstated rates, and most agreements carried an unquantified
annual minimum advertising requirement. Accrued royalties of Rp11.7 miliar were the largest single
accrual on the balance sheet, against no disclosed rate for anything.

**In 2025**, the audited accounts give two paragraphs and name nobody. What they add is the
aggregate cost — about Rp1.48 triliun, 3.4% of revenue — and four contractual features: a required
minimum purchase of goods from the rights holder, the non-compete whose breach terminates the
agreement, the option over the operating subsidiary, and the requirement that the group disclose its
whole existing brand portfolio to any prospective new principal.

In 2004 MAPI ran fifty brands and named twenty-seven counterparties. In 2025 it runs over 150 and
names none, and its own brand-portfolio pages are published as images, so even the brand names
cannot be read as text. The only place the brands appear in the audited accounts is the subsidiary
list, where each is attached to the company that operates it — which is, incidentally, exactly the
list a brand owner exercising a purchase option would work from.

## 8.4 The overseas expansion has not made money

MAPI reports revenue by country. It does not report profit by country. But the tax note contains a
line that adds back the result of overseas subsidiaries to reconcile Indonesian taxable profit, and
that line is the only window there is:

| Rp m | FY2021 | FY2022 | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Overseas revenue | 1,270,889 | 2,693,110 | 4,329,819 | 6,465,016 | 7,348,262 |
| — % of group | 6.9% | 10.0% | 13.0% | 17.1% | 17.1% |
| **Overseas result before tax** | **loss 168,707** | profit 67,429 | loss 24,743 | **loss 357,320** | **loss 123,098** |
| Overseas tax paid despite the loss | (16,816) | 35,252 | 43,617 | 32,916 | 41,633 |

Six countries and a seventh opening, revenue nearly six times its 2021 level, and cumulative losses
of about Rp606 miliar over five years. The FY2024 loss of Rp357 miliar swung Rp333 miliar against the group in a
year when pre-tax profit fell Rp369 miliar — so it accounts for most of the fall, alongside a
gross-margin decline that §7.5 ranks as the larger cause.

The geography note breaks out Vietnam, the Philippines and Thailand and lumps Singapore, Malaysia,
Cambodia and India into "Others" — Rp1.54 triliun of revenue, 3.6% of the group, never split. There
is no profit, asset or capital spending figure for any individual country in any year.

One country is visibly shrinking: **Vietnam has gone from 58 stores in 2023 to 40 at the end of
2025**, while its revenue rose. Malaysia went the other way, from 85 stores to 129 in six months.

## 8.5 What the parent company itself earns

MAPI the listed entity, as opposed to MAPI the group, is mostly a holding company. Its own pre-tax
profit in FY2025 was **Rp633 miliar** against the group's Rp3,540 miliar — and Rp388 miliar of that
was dividends received from its own subsidiaries, which are not taxable again.

That matters for two reasons. It is the reason MAPI's own corporate tax bill is small (Rp42 miliar
in FY2025 on Rp633 miliar of parent profit). And it means the dividend the public receives is
funded by dividends flowing up from MAPA, MAPB and the operating companies — so a decision by MAPA's
board about its own payout is, in practice, a decision about MAPI's.

# 09 — IMPORTANT TRANSACTIONS

## 9.1 March 2004 — buying SOGO from itself

Three months before the listing, MAPI and its investment subsidiary bought the entire share capital
of PT Panen Lestari Internusa, holder of the Indonesian SOGO rights and at that point about 47% of
group sales. It was a transaction between companies under the same ultimate control, so under
Indonesian accounting the previous five years of results were restated as though PLI had always been
inside the group. The restatement adjustments for 1999 and 2000 are marked **unaudited**, and the
prospectus gives two different start dates for the pooling in two different chapters.

**No price is disclosed for PLI, anywhere.** What is disclosed is that fifteen months earlier, MAPI
had agreed to transfer its trade and related-party receivables — including amounts owed by its own
vice-president director and by a close relative of a key employee — to two other companies as an
advance payment for PLI shares totalling Rp131 miliar. And that PLI's equity fell 32% in 2003
"primarily due to a payment of dividend" — the year before MAPI bought it.

## 9.2 August 2004 — the pre-listing tidy-up

Par value split 2,000 to 1, authorised capital doubled, and **Rp120 miliar of retained profits
converted into share capital** with no new cash. Cosmetic, legal, and standard practice — but it
converted distributable reserves into permanent capital immediately before selling shares to the
public.

## 9.3 2015 — CVC buys into the sports business

MAPI's own milestone timeline records a "strategic partnership with CVC for the Active Division".
Three years later that division listed as MAPA. Eleven years later CVC is back, alongside the vehicle
that now controls the whole company.

## 9.4 June 2016 — the interest-free bond over the food business

MAPI gathered its four food companies under a new holding company, MBA, and on the same day issued
an **unsecured, non-interest-bearing bond of Rp355 miliar** to a General Atlantic vehicle, signed a
governance agreement with it, and granted it an **option over 12.3% of MBA**. MAPI received Rp2
miliar for granting the option.

Because the bond paid no interest, it was recorded at a fair value of Rp239 miliar, and the Rp116
miliar difference was booked as the value of the option — a derivative liability, revalued every
year. In September 2018 a further Rp75 miliar was added, taking the bond to **Rp430 miliar** and
raising the option's exercise price by the same amount.

## 9.5 2017 and 2018 — floating the two arms

MBA listed in June 2017 (MAPI to 79.1%); MAA listed in July 2018 (MAPI to 83.5%, then 68.8% in 2019
after an option exercise). Together the two flotations put **Rp1.2 triliun of gain straight into
MAPI's equity** without passing through profit.

## 9.6 December 2020 — Rp54 miliar of profit from extending a maturity

The 2016 bond's maturity was extended by letter. Under the accounting rules the change was large
enough to count as a substantial modification: the old bond was treated as extinguished, a new one
recognised at a fair value of Rp376 miliar, and **the Rp54 miliar difference booked as a gain on day
one**. No cash moved. The December 2020 letter extended the bond to November 2022; a further letter in February 2023 took
it to November 2023, and it was rolled once more to November 2024.

## 9.7 March 2022 — selling down Burger King

16.76% of the Burger King operator sold, generating the **Rp382 miliar gain** that flattered FY2022.
MAPI's residual stake has since drifted from 12.24% to 11.20% to 10.63% in three consecutive annual
reports, with no transaction disclosed for either step. The FY2022 report calls the Rp283 miliar a
"selling price"; the FY2023 report calls Rp382 miliar a "one-time gain" for the same event. They are
different things described in the same words.

## 9.8 2023 and 2024 — Foot Locker in, Domino's out

Foot Locker's Singapore and Malaysian businesses bought in July 2023 for Rp354 miliar in total —
producing goodwill of Rp26 miliar on one and, unusually, a **bargain purchase gain of Rp12 miliar**
on the other. In September 2024 the entire 33.5% stake in Domino's Pizza Indonesia was sold for
Rp48.5 miliar, and on 2 December a joint venture in sports retail was taken to 99.99% and
consolidated.

## 9.9 October 2025 — the bond repaid and immediately replaced

On **15 October 2025** MAPI repaid the Rp430 miliar bond in full, ending nine years of rolled
maturities and terminating the attached option. Six days earlier, on **9 October**, it had signed a
new governance agreement and a **new two-year option** with a General Atlantic entity over exactly
the same **211,112,568 MBA shares**, for a premium of **Rp31,858 million a year**.

Three things changed. The debt disappeared. MAPI's consideration for granting the option went from
Rp2 miliar once, in 2016, to Rp32 miliar a year. And the option moved accounting homes — the old one
was a liability marked to market each year; the new one is booked in **equity**.

The timing is what makes it worth its own entry. This was signed and settled seven months before
control of MAPI changed hands, and General Atlantic separately holds about 20% of MAPB directly.

## 9.10 May–August 2026 — the change of control

Covered in §1.1. Two features belong here. The audited FY2025 accounts, signed in the first quarter
of 2026, **make no mention of the transaction at all** — the events-after-the-year-end note runs to
one paragraph about a Rp13.9 miliar tax assessment. And no chain-principle tender offer for the two
listed subsidiaries, MAPA and MAPB, has been announced, required, waived or explained anywhere.

# 10 — RED FLAG

## 10.1 Brand owners hold standing options to buy MAPI's operating subsidiaries

The clause, from the FY2025 accounts, and appearing in substantially the same words in every set
from FY2022 onward:

> *"Several agreements grant the counterparty an irrevocable option right to purchase the equity
> ownership of the related subsidiaries at a certain period, on terms set in the agreements."*

Which agreements, which subsidiaries, over what period, at what price — none of it, in any year.

Why this is the first flag rather than a footnote: MAPI's structure puts one brand in one company.
So an option "to purchase the equity ownership of the related subsidiary" is an option to take the
entire Indonesian business of that brand — the stores, the staff, the leases, the customer base —
out of MAPI at a price MAPI's shareholders have never seen. Zara sits in a company with Rp1.07
triliun of assets. Sephora, Marks & Spencer, Lacoste, Apple all have their own.

Set beside it the 2004 risk factor that has never been withdrawn or updated: *several of the Group's
key licenses contain a prohibition on a change of control in the Group's shareholding*, and in such
an event those arrangements "may be terminated if the latter do not provide consent". A change of
control completed on 8 May 2026. No filing says whether any consent was sought or given.

## 10.2 The audited accounts do not mention the takeover

The FY2025 financial statements were signed after the deal had been publicly announced (September
2025) and before it completed (May 2026). Their entire subsequent-events note is a Rp13.9 miliar tax
assessment. A pending change of control of the issuer is exactly the kind of thing that note exists
for. It is not there. The stronger version of the same point: **the FY2023 accounts carry no
subsequent-events note at all.**

## 10.3 The top of the ownership chain has never been visible, under either owner

In 2004, the chain ran MAPI → Satya Mulia → **PT Mitralestari Adiperkasa**, and stopped. Mitralestari
is described nowhere in the prospectus. From 2005 to 2025 the reports named Mrs. Marisa Kolonas as
ultimate owner but published nothing about Satya Mulia. Since May 2026 the chain runs MAPI → Pacific
Universal Investments → **Pacific Universal Holdings Limited**, and stops. No jurisdiction, no
shareholders, no directors, no beneficial owner.

Twenty-two years, two owners, and the top of the chain has never once been shown.

Two adjacent facts, offered as facts rather than conclusions. **Sintia Kolonas** — a name matching
one of the five 1995 founders and sharing a surname with the stated ultimate owner — was a director
of MAPI from 2018 to 2021, was appointed a commissioner in June 2024, and remains on the board after
the sale. And **Sean Gustav Standish
Hughes** and **Zoee Ho Ziwei** are named in reporting as directors of the buying vehicle while
simultaneously serving on MAPI's own board; MAPI's annual report describes both as "unaffiliated",
and both attended only three of the board's six meetings in each of 2024 and 2025. For balance, MAPI
does disclose one affiliation, in unusually direct language: Handaka Santosa, the director who runs
the department stores, "is affiliated with the Main or Controlling Shareholders of the Company".
An Indonesian financial blog has argued from these overlaps that beneficial ownership may not have
changed hands at all. That is a blog's inference, not a filing, and it is recorded here as a
hypothesis to be tested against the tender-offer statement and the FY2026 annual report, not as a
finding.

## 10.4 Two lapsed bank facilities and one cancelled outright

Not a large item, but the kind that gets missed. At the date the FY2025 accounts were issued, two
facilities had already expired and were being extended after the event — Bank of the Philippine
Islands, expired 31 December 2024, and Bank Rakyat Indonesia, expired 19 January 2026 — and **DBS
Bank Vietnam had cancelled all of its facilities outright**, effective 16 June 2025, notified in
August. HSBC's Thai facilities to the Active subsidiary were also cancelled.

None of this is dangerous for a group holding Rp5 triliun of cash against Rp2.9 triliun of drawn
debt. It matters because MAPI's own accounts describe twenty-one facilities as available and
undrawn, and at least three of those descriptions were no longer true when the accounts were
signed.

## 10.5 A joint venture whose value is permanently halved by an eliminated land gain

MAPI's half share of PT Kebon Melati Mas's net assets is Rp376 miliar. Deducted from it, every year
and always at exactly Rp248,944 million, is an **"elimination of gain on sale of land"**. The
partner is never named, the counterparty to the land sale is never named, and the venture's only
disclosed activity is losing money — Rp5 miliar in 2025, Rp12.6 miliar in 2024.

One connection the accounts do not draw: **Sintia Kolonas, appointed a commissioner of MAPI in June
2024, was President Director of PT Kebon Melati Mas from 2020 to 2022** — and was a Director of MAPI
itself from 2018 to 2021. She shares a surname with the person the annual reports name as ultimate
beneficial owner, and a name with one of the five 1995 founders. Her own board profile describes her
as unaffiliated. Alongside the venture sits a dormant MAPI subsidiary called PT Kebon Melati
Properti holding Rp376 miliar of assets and doing nothing.

This is a small item in a Rp33 triliun balance sheet, and it is the least explained thing in it.

## 10.6 The company has admitted breaching its own non-compete clauses

From the FY2022 accounts: *"Although the Company has sold some competing products, management
believes the licensors are aware and have not objected."* The same note states that violating the
non-compete provision **triggers termination of the agreement**. From FY2023 the sentence
disappears, replaced by a description of how the company notifies principals of new brands.

A retailer with 150 brands will inevitably carry products that compete with one another. The flag is
not that it happens; it is that management put in writing that it relies on licensors choosing not
to object, and then stopped saying so.

## 10.7 Disclosure that quietly gets thinner

Not a scandal; a pattern, and worth naming because it is consistent.

- The related-party note gave the percentage of revenue earned from related parties every year to
  FY2024; in **FY2025 that note gives no revenue or purchase figure at all**, and the percentage
  survives only as a cross-reference inside the revenue note.
- **Net sales floor area**, published in every annual report through FY2024, is dropped from FY2025
  and survives only in the investor deck.
- Board pay was disclosed as two aggregates for FY2024 and, from that year, the directors' figure is
  **merged with "key employees"**, so it can no longer be read as a board number.
- The **independent auditor's report is listed in the financial statements' own contents page in the
  FY2023, FY2024 and FY2025 annual reports and does not appear in the copies supplied** — only the
  FY2022 report carries the opinion text and its key audit matters. The opinion type, date and
  report number have to be recovered from the following year's shareholder-meeting agenda, which is
  also the only place the change of audit firm is visible: from Imelda & Rekan for FY2022 and FY2023
  to Liana Ramon Xenia & Rekan for FY2024 and FY2025, both Deloitte member firms. Non-audit fees
  paid to the auditor have meanwhile risen to **29% of the audit fee** (Rp3.5 miliar against Rp11.9
  miliar in FY2025), from 19% in FY2023.
- **Same-store sales growth has never been published**, in any annual report or any of six investor
  decks — the one metric every retailer in the world reports. The listed sports subsidiary publishes
  one for itself; its parent does not.
- And there is one disclosed regulatory sanction to record: on **3 November 2025 the financial
  regulator sanctioned MAPI** for late submission of the audit committee's evaluation of the
  **FY2019** accounts — six years after the event.

## 10.8 What is not a red flag, and should be said

For balance, four things that would be flags at most Indonesian family-controlled listed companies
and are not here. Related-party dealing is genuinely small — under 0.6% of revenue at its peak, no
purchases from related parties in any year, and no transaction with the controlling shareholder
ever. There are no pledged assets and no loans to directors. Reported earnings convert into cash:
Rp20.9 triliun of operating cash flow against Rp10.2 triliun of profit over five years. And the
balance sheet is in net cash before leases in every one of the last five years, with covenants met
by a wide margin. The problems in this company are problems of disclosure and structure, not of
money going missing.

# MISSING DATA AND OPEN ITEMS

Ranked by how much each would change the analysis.

**1. The brand agreements — every commercial term of all of them.** No expiry date for any brand has
ever been disclosed, in 2004 or since; no royalty rate per brand, no minimum purchase or store
commitment in figures, no exclusivity per brand except the partial map in the 1H2026 deck, and no
change-of-control clause. *Where it likely exists:* nowhere public. It would be in the agreements
themselves, and possibly summarised in a tender-offer statement or an information memorandum
prepared for the 2026 buyer. *What it would decide:* whether MAPI's earnings stream is contractually
durable or renewable at somebody else's discretion — which is the whole investment question.

**2. Which subsidiaries the brand owners' purchase options cover, and at what price.** *Likely
location:* the agreements; possibly the FY2026 accounts if the new owner improves disclosure. *What
it decides:* how much of the group can be removed from it without MAPI's consent.

**3. Whether the 2026 change of control triggered any licence consent or termination right.**
*Likely location:* the tender-offer statement, the FY2026 annual report, or any subsequent-events
note in the FY2026 accounts. *What it decides:* whether the takeover is an ownership event or an
operating one.

**4. Primary documents on the takeover itself** — the sale agreement signing date, the identity of
Pacific Universal Holdings' owner, any statement of intent on delisting or re-floating, and whether
a chain-principle tender offer was required for MAPA or MAPB. Every relevant exchange announcement
PDF returned an access error during this research. *What it decides:* whether the 12.76% float is a
resting state or a waypoint to something else.

**5. The split between fixed and turnover-linked rent.** The accounting policy confirms variable
rents are expensed rather than capitalised, so the split exists in the company's own records.
*Likely location:* a fuller lease note, or the MAPA and MAPB standalone accounts. *What it decides:*
how much operating gearing MAPI actually has — the difference between a fixed and a variable cost
base of roughly 4% of revenue.

**6. A reconciliation between the two lists of bank facilities.** The loan note names the eight
lenders with money out and their rates; the significant-agreements note describes twenty-one
different facilities, all undrawn. Nothing joins them, so the group's total committed borrowing
capacity cannot be established.

**7. Same-store sales growth, and store openings and closings by year.** Without them, the 320 net
store additions in FY2025 cannot be separated into 616 openings and about 296 closures with any
confidence, and there is no way to tell how much of the 14% revenue growth is new space rather than
better trading. MAPA publishes a same-store figure for itself (+2.4% in the fourth quarter of 2025);
MAPI does not.

**8. Profit by country.** Only the tax note's add-back reveals the overseas result at all, and
Singapore, Malaysia, Cambodia and India are never separated from one another.

**9. Board remuneration per person**, and a split between directors and "key employees" from FY2024.

**10. What the FY2021 and FY2022 restatements changed, line by line.** The explanatory note is
referenced in every filing and its content was not reachable in this extraction.

**11. The FY2023–FY2025 audit opinions and any key audit matters.** The reports list the auditor's
report in their contents and do not print it. The audit firm changed between FY2023 and FY2024 —
from Imelda & Rekan to Liana Ramon Xenia & Rekan, both Deloitte member firms — and the FY2024 report
reference (00081/2.1460/AU.1/05/0556-4/1/III/2025) carries the same accountant registration number
as the FY2022 and FY2023 reports, with a suffix that in Indonesian practice counts consecutive years
of engagement. Whether that means continuity of the signing partner across the firm change could not
be confirmed.

**12. Sources never supplied.** No quarterly or half-year financial statements were available, so
1H2026 rests entirely on an unaudited investor deck with a six-line balance sheet. No FY2020 or
earlier modern-basis accounts, leaving a ten-year gap between the prospectus series ending in April
2004 and the earliest figures here. And no MAPA or MAPB standalone accounts, which between them
would answer several of the questions above.
