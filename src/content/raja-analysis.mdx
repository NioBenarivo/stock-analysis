**What it does:** A Jakarta-based, privately-controlled energy holding company that has spent fifteen years assembling a mix of small, mostly toll-like positions along Indonesia's oil-and-gas chain. It runs three business lines, which the Company itself labels **downstream** (gas trading — buying pipeline gas from upstream producers such as Pertamina EP and Medco and reselling it to industrial and power customers in Sumatra and Java, through PT Panji Raya Alamindo, 67%-owned with Tokyo Gas holding 33%), **midstream** (fee-earning infrastructure — a share of a 360 km crude-oil pipeline in the Rokan area, 245 km of gas pipelines, 110 mmscfd of gas compression, an LPG terminal, water treatment, and since 2025 49% stakes in the Hafar offshore-EPCI and shipping companies alongside Petrosea), and **upstream** (non-operated participating interests in producing oil and gas blocks — Cepu, operated by ExxonMobil; Jabung, operated by PetroChina; and since mid-2026 Madura Strait, operated by Husky-CNOOC — all held through the separately-listed subsidiary **PT Raharja Energi Cepu Tbk (RATU)**, 69.6%-owned). All operations and revenue are in Indonesia. Reports in US dollars.

**Market:** Rp725/share (investing.com, late September 2026, post the 1:5 stock split of 16-Jul-2026) × ~21.06bn shares = **Rp15.27tn ≈ US$904m market cap** [calculated at ~Rp16,900/US$, the rate implied by the Company's own FY2025 profit translation]. Trailing P/E ≈22.9x (investing.com); 52-week range Rp516–1,695 (post-split basis).

**Period covered:** FY2021–FY2025 plus quarterly/half-year points to 1H2026. **No annual report or audited financial statement was in hand.** Everything below is built from ten investor-presentation and public-expose decks (March 2024 → March 2026), supplemented by press coverage that quotes the audited FY2025 statements and the 1H2026 interim statements. That is thin coverage by the standard of the other teardowns in this project — the gaps it creates are named where they bite (§ "NOT DISCLOSED" at the end lists them).

**Conventions:** US$ million unless stated. "Net profit (deck)" = profit for the year **including** non-controlling interests, which is what every RAJA deck charts; "net profit attributable" = profit to owners of RAJA, which is what the audited statements and EPS use — the gap between the two is itself a finding (§1.3, §3.2). Per-share figures before 16-Jul-2026 are pre-split unless marked. `[calculated]` marks every derived figure; "press" marks a figure taken from news coverage of a filing rather than the filing itself.

## THE ONE THING TO UNDERSTAND FIRST

**RAJA's reported profit growth is no longer coming from the businesses it consolidates. It is coming from oil price, equity-accounted associates and one-off acquisition gains — and half of RAJA's own market value is simply its stake in a listed subsidiary priced at 10–11x book.**

Four facts, each evidenced later:

1. **Core operating profit fell in 2025.** Revenue rose 4.8% to US$266.7m and gross profit rose 4.2% to US$72.2m, but operating expenses jumped 27% (US$19.9m → US$25.3m) and **operating income fell 17.6%, from US$48.4m to US$39.9m** (press, citing the FY2025 audited statements; §3.2). What rescued the bottom line was **share of profit of associates, which tripled from US$6.2m to US$19.7m** — the 49%-held PT Petrogas Jatim Utama Cendana (the vehicle holding the Cepu interest) plus five months of the newly acquired Hafar group. Strip the associate line out and pre-tax profit from the consolidated businesses fell roughly 20% [calculated].

2. **The 1H2026 "profit +88%" headline is mostly oil and accounting.** Net profit (deck basis) rose from US$15.4m to US$28.9m on revenue up only 2.8%. Management itself names three drivers: the Sengkang gas-compressor contract running a full half (genuine, recurring), **Cepu associate income on an average crude price of ~US$117/bbl versus ~US$74/bbl a year earlier** (cyclical), and **a gain on bargain purchase from the Madura Strait acquisition** (one-off, amount not disclosed in anything read). RATU's own FY2026 net-profit target of US$26m against the US$15.5m it has already booked in 1H implies a much weaker 2H — i.e. RATU's management does not expect 1H to repeat.

3. **Minority leakage has become large.** The decks chart profit of US$35.0m for 2025; the audited attributable figure is US$27.2m. **22% of 2025 profit belonged to minorities** (2023: 5%) [calculated], because RAJA floated 30% of RATU in January 2025 and has always shared the gas-trading arm one-third with Tokyo Gas. Every deck headline overstates what reaches a RAJA shareholder by roughly a fifth.

4. **RATU is half the market cap.** At Rp3,980 (29-Sep-2026) RATU is worth Rp10.81tn; RAJA's 69.6% is **Rp7.52tn — 49% of RAJA's entire Rp15.27tn market value** [calculated]. RATU trades at 27.6x trailing earnings and 10.7x book (ajaib.co.id) and has already fallen 69% from its 52-week high of Rp12,900. RAJA shareholders are therefore carrying a large, mark-to-market bet on RATU's rich valuation, and the look-through value of everything else RAJA owns — the gas trader, the pipelines, the compressors, Hafar — is only about Rp7.75tn (~US$459m) for ~US$51m of 2025 segment EBITDA [calculated, §3.6].

Layer on top of that a controlling shareholder — **Happy Hapsoro**, who controls roughly three-quarters of the votes through direct holdings and two private vehicles — and a board populated with former ministers, a former Kadin chairman and a sitting-era political figure, in a business (gas allocation, SOE pipeline contracts, PSC interests) where government relationships are a real input. That is not a red flag by itself, but it is the context against which a string of related-party-adjacent deals (§1.4) and a share register that the Company's own decks did not update after the controller sold a 11.8% block (§1.2) should be read.

The investment question is therefore not "is RAJA growing?" — on the deck numbers it plainly is — but **how much of the growth is repeatable operating cash earned by assets RAJA controls, versus oil-price-linked associate income, one-off gains and a listed-subsidiary valuation it does not control.** Section 3.7 tries to separate the two.

## 1.4 Related party transactions

The audited FY2025 statements (as quoted in press) split revenue into **US$256.2m third-party and US$10.46m related-party — 3.9% of revenue** [calculated]. The counterparties are not named in anything read. Items worth flagging, none concluded by the Company:

- **Hafar co-investment with Petrosea (Aug-2025).** Petrosea is controlled by the Prajogo Pangestu/Barito group; RAJA's President Director sits on Petrosea's board of commissioners. The two companies bought the Hafar group together (PTRO 51%, RAJA 49%) for ~Rp790bn. The press framed it as a "duo konglomerat" deal. Whether RAJA treated it as an affiliated transaction under POJK 42/2020 is not stated in the decks.
- **Cepu via an East Java BUMD.** RAJA's Cepu exposure is not a direct PI: it is 49% of PJUC, whose 51% is held by Petrogas Jatim Utama, a regional-government-owned enterprise. The Gresik pipeline (2014) is also a BOT with the same BUMD group. Long-standing, and a normal Indonesian structure for regional participating interests, but the terms of the RAJA–PJUC arrangement (how RATU's 49% of PJUC becomes a stated 2.2423% PI, and on what economic split) are not in any deck.
- **CEO ownership of the parent.** Djauhar Maulidi's 10% of Sentosa Bersama Mitra gives him an indirect ~3.6% economic interest in RAJA [calculated: 10% × 36.08%] — alignment, but also a reason to watch whether dividend and buyback decisions favour the control block.
- **Subsidiary IPO then resale.** RATU floated 20% in new shares at Rp1,150 in January 2025, but RAJA's stake is shown at 70% by June 2025 and 69.6% by December 2025 — i.e. RAJA's holding fell by ~10pp more than the IPO dilution alone explains [calculated: 99.9% × 0.8 = ~80%]. A secondary sell-down by RAJA (or an allocation to other parties) is implied; no deck says to whom or at what price. With RATU later trading as high as Rp12,900, the price of any secondary shares matters.

## 2.7 Demand

- **Gas:** The Company's own industry slide (ESDM "Neraca Gas Bumi 2024–2033") shows domestic gas demand broadly stable to 2033 while domestic supply declines naturally, with the government targeting 12 bscfd of production by 2030. RAJA's argument is that the gap must be filled by new supply projects and imports (LNG), raising the value of transport, regasification and trading capacity — the logic behind the LNG terminal, LNG plant, LNGC vessels and Kasuri stake.
- **Oil:** Indonesia is a net importer; upstream output is price-taking. The 1H26 average of ~US$117/bbl cited by management is exceptional and should not be annualised.
- **Fuel logistics:** BBM pipelines (e.g. East Kalimantan) are driven by Pertamina's push to replace road/sea fuel distribution — a policy-backed, SOE-contracted demand line.

## 2.8 Supply / competition

- **Gas trading** competes with PGN (state gas utility, dominant in transmission and distribution) and other private traders; allocation (50 mmscfd) is the scarce input, not customers. Upstream gas supply in Sumatra is declining.
- **Midstream** competes for SOE tenders with Pertagas/PGN subsidiaries and other private contractors; RAJA's edge is its track record with Pertamina and PLN and, plausibly, its board's relationships.
- **Upstream** is non-operated — RAJA has no control over development pace or cost at Cepu, Jabung or Madura.
- **Peers (IDX):** PGAS (gas utility), ELSA/Elnusa (services), MEDC (E&P), ENRG (E&P), and RATU itself. A full peer table was not built for this report (research gap).

## 3.8 Roadmap and what to watch

1. **Cepu and Jabung volumes, quarterly.** 1Q26 showed the first material decline (Cepu 136k, Jabung 47k). If 2Q–3Q26 confirm it, upstream earnings fall once oil normalises.
2. **The Madura bargain-purchase gain** — its size in the 1H26/9M26 statements tells you what share of 2026's growth is accounting.
3. **Sumatra gas-pipeline disruption** — duration and whether it hits Panji Raya's allocation or only transport; downstream revenue was −26% YoY in 1Q26.
4. **BBM pipeline construction start (Q3-2026)** — already a year behind the Oct-2024 plan. A further slip would say a lot about the greenfield pipeline.
5. **LNGC/VLGC closing (Q4-2026)** — price, funding (debt?), and charter terms.
6. **Kalimantan LNG FID** — at ~US$300m it would be ~3.7x 2025 EBITDA; funding structure (partner equity? RATU? new debt?) decides whether RAJA's balance sheet can carry it.
7. **Panji Raya IPO (rumoured 2026)** — would crystallise a second listed sub, and add a second layer of minority leakage.
8. **The 11.8% block held at CGS International** — whether it is a pledge; any forced-sale risk if RAJA's price keeps falling.
9. **Associates vs cash** — whether PJUC and Hafar actually upstream dividends proportionate to the income booked.

# SUMMARY VERDICT

**What it is.** RAJA is a well-connected, acquisitive energy holding company with three distinct businesses: a low-margin gas trader (56% of revenue, ~24% of EBITDA) that is stagnant and currently disrupted; a genuinely good, contract-backed midstream infrastructure book (~38% of EBITDA) that has compounded EBITDA roughly ninefold since 2021 and just nearly doubled again on the Sengkang compressor; and a non-operated upstream portfolio (~37% of EBITDA) held through a listed 69.6% subsidiary, whose earnings come mainly from equity-accounted Cepu income on a field that has started to decline.

**What it earns through the cycle.** On FY2025, US$27m attributable; on a normalised 2026–27 view with oil back in the US$70s and one-offs stripped out, roughly **US$35–40m attributable** [calculated, §3.7]. At ~US$904m market cap that is **~23–26x normalised earnings and a ~1.8% yield** — a growth multiple for a business whose consolidated operating income fell in 2025.

**What has to be true for the current price to work.** (1) Midstream keeps adding contracts at the Sengkang pace — the BBM pipeline, vessels and LNG projects must convert from slides into EBITDA on time; (2) acquisitions keep being struck at prices that produce bargain-purchase gains rather than impairments, and are funded without pushing net debt/EBITDA (on an ex-associates basis) much above ~2x; (3) RATU's own rich valuation holds, since it is half of RAJA's market cap.

**What would break it.** An oil-price normalisation coinciding with Cepu decline would hit the listed subsidiary first and RAJA's look-through value immediately; a prolonged Sumatra gas disruption would keep downstream flat-to-down; and the LNG plant, if taken to FID on RAJA's balance sheet, would roughly quadruple the Company's capital commitments relative to its EBITDA. Governance context — a controller holding ~three-quarters of the votes, a possibly-pledged 11.8% block, four subsidiaries dropped from the org chart without explanation, and an unexplained ~10pp RATU sell-down — raises the bar for trusting the acquisition strategy.

**Position.** The midstream business is the part worth owning and it is growing well; but at today's price, the investor also pays a full multiple for oil-price-inflated, minority-diluted upstream earnings, a thin-margin trading book and a string of pre-FID projects. On the evidence in hand — ten decks and press coverage of the audited numbers, no annual report — **RAJA looks fully valued to expensive on normalised earnings, with the bull case resting on execution of projects that have a history of slipping.** The single most useful next step is to read the FY2025 annual report (the notes on associates, related parties, acquisitions/bargain purchase, and segment assets), which would either confirm or overturn several of the inferences above.

*This is an analytical view of the business from company presentations and press coverage of its filings, not investment advice. I'm not a financial advisor.*
