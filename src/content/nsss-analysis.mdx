## The short version

NSSS is the cleanest pure-upstream palm business of the three Indonesian planters torn down so far — and that is both the whole investment case and the whole problem.

**What it is.** A five-subsidiary, single-province, single-segment oil palm grower in Central Kalimantan. 28,121 ha of nucleus estate, 1,882 ha of plasma, two mills totalling 120 t/hr, and nothing else. No refinery, no kernel crusher, no biodiesel plant, no biogas, no trading arm, no exports. It grows fruit, presses it, and sells CPO and palm kernel to three domestic buyers. That is the entire business.

**The eleven things that matter:**

1. **It is 96.4% self-supplied — the exact inverse of AALI.** Neither the annual reports nor the decks ever disclose an own-versus-purchased FFB split. But it can be derived two ways that agree: FY2025 CPO output of 124,647 t at a 22.2% OER implies **561,473 t of FFB processed [calculated]**, against 541,109 t harvested from its own estate. The gap is **20,364 t, or 3.6% of throughput [calculated]**. AALI buys 38.7% of its fruit. That single ratio explains most of the gross-margin gap between them — **54.3% at NSSS versus 16% at AALI**.

2. **The second mill turned NSSS from an FFB seller into an FFB buyer, and that is the real FY2024–25 story.** In FY2022 and FY2023 NSSS *sold* raw fruit to other people's mills — 22,930 t and then 47,415 t, 8.0% of FY2023 revenue. From FY2024 that line vanishes from every document with no explanation. It vanished because BSP's mill started in December 2023 and NSSS suddenly had somewhere to put the fruit. Selling fruit at Rp1,996/kg became selling CPO at Rp12,802/kg.

3. **FY2025's profit doubling was operating leverage and palm kernel, not CPO price.** Revenue +35.2%, net profit +112.7%. Decomposing the +Rp523.9 bn of revenue: **volume contributed Rp286.0 bn (54.6%) and price Rp237.8 bn (45.4%) [calculated]**. Within price, **palm kernel alone contributed Rp99.8 bn — 19% of the entire revenue increase from a product that is 15% of revenue**, because PK ASP rose 48% in the lauric-oil squeeze. CPO ASP rose only 9%. Meanwhile COGS rose **1.6%** against volume up 18%. Unit cost per kg of CPO fell 14%.

4. **The audit opinion went qualified in FY2025 and almost nothing else in the report acknowledges it.** On 6 February 2025 the Ministry of Forestry's **SK No. 36/2025** named NSP and BSP as operating inside forest areas without forestry permits. NSSS booked **Rp108 bn** (Rp51,687 m of fixed-asset impairment + Rp56,718 m of fine provision) covering **2,023 ha — 1,762 ha at NSP and 261 ha at BSP**. EY qualified the opinion because, on a full PP 45/2025 calculation, it believes G&A would be **Rp368 bn higher**, other operating expenses **Rp78 bn higher**, and tax **Rp114 bn lower**. That restates FY2025 net profit from Rp647.8 bn to **~Rp315.8 bn — a 51% cut [calculated]**. In the same report, the governance chapter states there were no legal cases and no administrative sanctions in 2025, and the risk register never names SK 36 or PP 45.

5. **The estate is young — and that is a dated asset, not a permanent one.** 89.6% of the nucleus sits in the 7–20 year prime band and **nothing is over 20 years**. Average age 9.8 years. But the corollary is a wall: the bulk was planted 2009–2015, which means roughly **17,000 ha crosses 20 years between about 2029 and 2035**, against a new-planting run rate of 617 ha (FY24), 738 ha (FY25) and **82 ha in 1H26** — 5.5% of the FY2026 target of ~1,500 ha. At the FY2025 pace, replacing 17,000 ha takes **23 years [calculated]**.

6. **Yield is doing the heavy lifting and is genuinely good.** FFB per mature hectare went 15.9 → 18.1 → 18.3 → **20.7 t/ha** across FY2022–FY2025 **[calculated]**; CPO per mature hectare reached **4.76 t/ha [calculated]**. OER hit 22.2%, the best in the four-year record and above the 21.5% the company guided.

7. **One customer is half the company.** PT Sinar Mas Agro Resources and Technology Tbk has been **75% → 70% → 65% → 55% → 52%** of consolidated sales across FY2021–FY2025. The top three are 85%. None are classified as related parties — though a Director, the Head of Internal Audit and an Audit Committee member all spent large parts of their careers at that same customer.

8. **Related-party disclosure is a single line item, four years running, and it contradicts the MD&A every year.** Note 29/30 discloses exactly one thing: an office lease with ultimate parent PT Samuel International. For FY2022, FY2023 and FY2024 **no amount was ever given**. FY2025 finally disclosed one — **Rp9,785 m** over three years. Every one of the four MD&As simultaneously states there were no material affiliate transactions that year.

9. **The controlling shareholder's name has changed twice with no disclosed corporate action.** PT Mitra Agro Dharma Unggul (59.11% at end-2022, 49.83% at end-2023) simply disappeared from the register during FY2024 and was replaced by **PT Samuel Tumbuh Bersama at 43.58%**. The August 2026 deck then lists the largest holder as **PT Samuel Sekuritas Indonesia at 41.6%**. The named ultimate beneficial owner — **Eunice M. Satyono** — appears in FY2022, FY2023 and FY2025 but is **absent from FY2024**, which names only a corporate entity.

10. **The deleveraging is real and nearly finished.** Net gearing went **351.3% (Dec-21) → 327.6% → 175.5% → 118.1% → 51.7% (Dec-25) → 35.1% (Jun-26)**. Gross debt fell from Rp2,156 bn to Rp1,766 bn while equity tripled. All of it is with one lender, Bank Mandiri, at 8.25%. That single-lender concentration is not listed as a risk factor in any of the four annual reports.

11. **The stock has already round-tripped.** Rp194 (Dec-24) → Rp925 (Dec-25) → **Rp1,255 intraday in Feb-26** → **Rp372 on 17 Aug 2026**, a ~70% drawdown from the peak, coinciding with — but not publicly attributed to — the July 2026 forestry-permit disclosure. At Rp372 the market cap is **Rp8.85 tn [calculated]**, putting it on roughly **13.3× trailing P/E and ~9.1× EV/EBITDA [calculated]** — still above AALI (7.8× / 2.2×) and TAPG (9.2× / 6.2×) on the snapshot multiples available.

**What the bull case rests on:** a young, high-yielding, fully self-supplied estate with 54% gross margins, a balance sheet that has gone from crisis leverage to near-neutral in four years, 17,098 ha of unplanted landbank, a third mill coming, and B50 demand underneath the CPO price.

**What the bear case rests on:** a Rp446 bn pre-tax provision gap the auditor has already put in writing; 52% customer concentration; a planting programme running at 5% of its own target; a replanting wall arriving around 2030; disclosure that contradicts itself in the same document, year after year; and a valuation that still carries a premium to larger, better-disclosed peers.

# 01 — MANAGEMENT

## 1.1 What NSSS is, and where it came from

PT Nusantara Sawit Sejahtera Tbk is a **holding company**. That is not a description, it is the legal position: Article 3 of its Articles of Association gives its business activity as *"holding company activities and other management consulting"*, and FS Note 1a states the Company itself only began commercial operations in **2020** — eleven years after it was established. Everything physical happens at five subsidiaries.

- **Established:** Deed No. 01 of Engawati Gazali, S.H., dated **1 April 2009**; amended by Deed No. 14 of 12 June 2009; approved by the Ministry of Law and Human Rights on 26 June 2009 (AHU-28662.AH.01.01).
- **Converted to a public company:** Deed No. 04 of Bertha Suriati Ihalauw Halim, S.H., 21 September 2021.
- **Latest AoA amendment:** Deed No. 18 of Esther P.E. Jovina, S.H., M.Kn., 4 November 2022.
- **Head office:** Menara Imperium, 30th floor Suite D, Jl. H.R. Rasuna Said Kav. 1, Guntur, Setiabudi, South Jakarta 12980.
- **Parent chain per FS Note 1a:** PT Samuel Tumbuh Bersama is the parent entity; **PT Samuel International is the ultimate parent entity**.

Note the small oddity already: the Company's *stated* line of business is management consultancy, its risk register says subsidiaries *"make periodic payments to the Company for various management consulting activities"*, and yet the related-party note never names a management-fee arrangement. More on that in §1.7.

This is a company halfway through a J-curve, not a steady-state operator.

### The disappearing controlling shareholder

This is the single most under-explained item in four years of filings.

At 1 January 2024 the >5% table shows **PT Mitra Agro Dharma Unggul at 49.83%**. At 31 December 2024 that name is gone and **PT Samuel Tumbuh Bersama holds 43.58%**. The FY2024 report's "Corporate Action" section states there was no stock split, share dividend, bonus issue or par-value change in 2024. The "Significant Company Changes" section describes no share transfer or restructuring. A 49.83% controlling block changed hands — or changed name — and the annual report does not say which, or when, or on what terms.

Then it happens again. The investor decks track the largest holder as "PT Samuel Tumbuh Bersama" through the May 2026 deck (39.8%), and then the **August 2026 deck lists "PT Samuel Sekuritas Indonesia" at 41.6%**. Samuel Sekuritas Indonesia is also one of the four IPO joint-lead underwriters, and Commissioner Robiyanto sits as an Independent Commissioner of it. Whether this is a genuine transfer, a group reorganisation, or an editorial error in the deck, no document says.

## 1.7 The qualified opinion — SK 36/2025, PP 45/2025, and a Rp446 bn gap

This is the most important disclosure in the corpus, and it is also the one the annual report works hardest not to talk about.

### What happened

On **6 February 2025** the Ministry of Environment and Forestry issued **Decision Letter (SK) No. 36 of 2025**, identifying palm oil plantation companies operating inside forest areas without the required forestry permits. **PT Nusantara Sawit Persada and PT Borneo Sawit Perdana — the two subsidiaries that are 74% of the estate and both mills — are on that list.**

**Government Regulation (PP) No. 45 of 2025** sets out how administrative fines for the companies named in SK 36 are calculated.

NSSS's own disclosure of 2 July 2026 puts numbers on the exposure: **PT Nusantara Sawit Persada 1,762 ha and PT Borneo Sawit Perdana 261 ha — 2,023 ha in total**, being **7.2% of the 28,121 ha nucleus estate [calculated]**. Management said operations continue normally, that the matter does not affect going concern or ISPO certification, that it is "still clarifying land area data based on all licensing documents" with the ministry, and that it has taken no legal action.

That is the deliberate opposite of AALI (which runs a refinery and a trading leg in Sulawesi at a 4.88% gross margin) and of TAPG. It is why NSSS's gross margin is 54% and AALI's is 16%, and it is also why NSSS's earnings are a pure levered bet on the CPO and PK price with no internal hedge.

**In FY2023, 8% of NSSS's revenue was raw fruit sold to somebody else's mill at Rp1,996/kg.** In FY2024 that line went to zero and was quietly deleted from every subsequent document — no explanation, in any annual report or deck, ever.

The explanation is mechanical and it is the most important operational fact in the four-year record: **BSP's mill started in December 2023.** Before that, NSSS had 26,767 ha of estate feeding a single 60 t/hr mill. It was growing more fruit than it could press, so it sold the surplus at raw-fruit prices. From FY2024, with 120 t/hr, it presses everything and buys a little more on top.

The value of that switch is straightforward: 47,415 t of FFB sold in FY2023 at Rp1,996/kg produced Rp94.6 bn. Put through the mill at a 22.2% OER and a 4.8% KER, the same fruit yields about 10,526 t of CPO and 2,276 t of PK, worth roughly **Rp173.0 bn at FY2025 prices [calculated]** — before milling costs. That is the second mill paying for itself.

### Land rights

The Group's land is held as **HGU** (Hak Guna Usaha — a right-to-cultivate leasehold, not freehold). FS Note 11 says only this: the HGU titles are *"valid until 2048 and 2049"* and management believes they can be renewed. That is the entire land-rights disclosure in four annual reports. There is **no hectare-by-certificate table, no expiry schedule, no breakdown by subsidiary, and no discussion of renewal risk** — which compares poorly with AALI, whose 2016 prospectus published a full HGU expiry-bucket table. It also means that when SK 36/2025 named 2,023 ha as sitting inside forest areas, there was no published land-title map against which an outside reader could check the claim.

The gap between the 29,616 ha of original HGU and the 28,121 ha planted implies roughly 1,495 ha of permitted-but-unplanted land, before counting mill footprints, roads and conservation set-asides. Separately, the 1Q25 deck shows nucleus planted area of 27,607 ha plus **landbank of 17,098 ha** for a printed total of 45,037 ha *(the two figures actually sum to 44,705 — a 332 ha discrepancy inside the company's own chart)*. **Three things fall out of this table.**

**First, the yield ramp is the engine.** FFB per mature hectare rose **30.2% in three years**, from 15.86 to 20.65 t/ha, and CPO per mature hectare rose **44.7%**, from 3.29 to 4.76 t/ha. **Third, the utilisation story does not fully reconcile.** FY2025's 78.0% checks out exactly against 720,000 t. That is legitimate, but it means **the FY2024→FY2025 utilisation series as published (92% → 78%) reads as a deterioration when the underlying throughput actually rose 12.8%**. The decks never explain the basis change. And on either basis, the 100% utilisation the company guided for FY2025 in May 2025 was missed by a wide margin.

### How the price is actually set

The disclosure is thin but specific:

- **CPO is sold FOB** — delivered onto the buyer's vessel. **PK is sold Franco** — delivered by truck to the buyer's warehouse.
- Product ships to **Gresik and Marunda**; customer representatives are in Jakarta.
- Two sales mechanisms: **(1) long-term** — 2,000-tonne cargo calculations submitted monthly to the buyer; **(2) on-the-spot** — additional cargo offered to other customers.
- All sales are direct; no intermediaries.
- The risk-management section states CPO price fluctuation is mitigated *"via 8-day average pricing with long-term partners"* — i.e. the contract price references an 8-day moving average of the reference price rather than a spot print.

That 8-day averaging is the only pricing formula disclosed anywhere, and it explains why NSSS's realised ASP tracks but lags the KPBN/Inacom domestic benchmark.

## 2.7 The key variables — what actually moves this business

Stripped down, NSSS's earnings are a function of six things. Ranked by how much of the FY2025 profit move each explains:

### 1. CPO price — the base, but not the driver of FY2025

Realised CPO ASP Rp13,930/kg in FY2025, +8.8% YoY. The current domestic reference (KPBN Inacom) was **Rp15,700/kg on 14 August 2026**, and Bursa Malaysia FCPO was **RM4,711/t** — both above the FY2025 realised level and above MPOB's own RM4,000–4,300 full-year guidance band. Realised 2Q26 ASP of Rp14,693/kg confirms the pass-through is happening with the usual lag.

The company's own published sensitivity has changed: **"every 10% CPO price hike translates to 28% bottom line increase"** (1Q25 deck) became **"every 10% increase in CPO prices results in 19% increase in net profit"** (1H26 deck). The 19% figure is the more conservative and reflects the higher profit base. On FY2025 numbers the *arithmetic* sensitivity is higher than either:

```
SCENARIO C — CPO price sensitivity, FY2025 base

    CPO revenue                              Rp1,704,150 m
    +10% on price only (volume unchanged)    Rp  170,415 m
    Incremental COGS                                 nil     (COGS is not price-linked;
                                                              NSSS grows 96.4% of its fruit)
    Incremental pre-tax profit               Rp  170,415 m
    Tax at 22% statutory                     Rp   37,491 m
    Incremental net profit                   Rp  132,924 m

    As % of FY2025 reported net profit             20.5%
    Company's stated 1H26 sensitivity              19.0%    ← consistent
    Company's stated 1Q25 sensitivity              28.0%    ← was consistent with the
                                                              smaller FY2024 profit base
```

The 19–20% band is right, and it means **a 10% move in CPO is worth roughly a fifth of net profit**. That is high gearing to a single commodity, and it cuts both ways.

### 2. Palm kernel price — the swing factor nobody modelled

PK ASP +47.6% in FY2025 and PK at 96.0% of the CPO price by 2Q26. PK contributed **Rp99.8 bn of the Rp523.9 bn revenue increase — 19.0% of it [calculated]** — from a product line that is 15.4% of revenue. This is the least durable input in the model and the one with the least visibility: the corpus contains no PKO or kernel price forecast, and the web pass could not source a current, dated PKO quote.

**Reversion cost:** if PK/CPO returned to the FY2024 ratio of 61.4%, PK ASP at FY2025 CPO prices would be Rp8,553/kg, worth Rp228,357 m on FY2025 volume — **Rp81,294 m less revenue, and essentially all of it gross profit, equal to 9.8% of FY2025 gross profit or 12.5% of reported net profit at the statutory tax rate [calculated]**.

### 3. Mill utilisation and OER — the operational levers

At 78.0% utilisation there is **158,527 t of unused annual FFB capacity [calculated]**. Filling it with own fruit requires either yield (which is already compounding) or hectares (which are not being planted). Filling it with purchased fruit changes the margin structure, since bought FFB carries a cash cost where own fruit carries only field cost.

OER is worth Rp78.2 bn of revenue per percentage point at FY2025 throughput. The company guided 21.5% and delivered 22.2%.

FY2026 mill-utilisation guidance was **cut from ~90% to ~80% between the May and August 2026 decks** — three months apart, for the same fiscal year. That is the clearest signal in the corpus that volume growth is slowing.

### 4. Fertiliser — the biggest cost, disclosed once

Rp275 bn in FY2024 (30.4% of COGS), down 13.2% from ~Rp317 bn in FY2023. Then silence until the 1H26 newsletter flags **"raised fertiliser costs"** as a margin headwind. Gross margin in 1H26 was **47.2% against 53.6% in 1H25 [calculated]** — 6.4 points of erosion on flat volumes, which is the fertiliser cycle turning. Fertiliser inventory on the balance sheet tells the same story from the other side: "supporting materials" inventory jumped from **Rp35,758 m to Rp116,282 m (+225%)** during FY2025, which is a company buying ahead of a rising price.

### 5. Policy — B50, the levy, and the DMO

NSSS sells nothing abroad, so the **export levy (12.5% of the HPE reference price under PMK 69/2025 as amended by PMK 9/2026, = USD124.56/t in August 2026) and export duty (USD148/t) do not hit its P&L directly**. They hit it indirectly and powerfully, by setting the domestic price: every dollar of levy and duty is a wedge that depresses the domestic CPO price relative to the world price. A producer selling 100% domestically bears that wedge in full without any of the offsetting export volume.

**B50** — the 50% biodiesel blend — took effect **1 July 2026** after being delayed from early 2026. MPOB estimates it requires **~3 million additional tonnes of palm oil a year**, against Indonesian production of ~52–56 million t. That is the single largest demand-side support under the CPO price, and NSSS's own outlook section cites it.

**The DMO is the risk on the same axis.** Because B50 competes with cooking oil for the same feedstock, the government issued a new trade rule in **mid-July 2026 requiring palm cooking-oil producers to prioritise domestic supply**. A tightening DMO caps exportable volume nationally, which pushes more CPO into the domestic market — precisely the market NSSS sells into. The exact ratio was not verifiable in the research pass; it is the policy variable most worth watching for a domestic-only producer.

### 6. Weather

BMKG has an **active El Niño on record, forecasting drier conditions across Indonesian palm regions through October 2026**. MPOC, MPOB and field reporting all corroborate. Numeric yield impacts were not verifiable. For NSSS the exposure is asymmetric: dry weather cuts its own FFB yield (bad for volume) while supporting the CPO price (good for margin), and its 96.4%-self-supplied structure means it cannot buy its way around a production shortfall the way AALI can.

**Indonesian planted area is 16.83 m ha, of which smallholders hold ~41%.** The PSR replanting programme has, cumulatively over 2017–2025, actually replanted only **290,701 ha** against 417,008 ha of technical recommendations — about 70% delivery on a programme that is itself a fraction of what the ageing smallholder estate needs. The 2026 first-phase target is 50,000 ha. Indonesia's aggregate supply is therefore structurally constrained by an ageing smallholder base that is not being renewed, which is a medium-term price support and a direct read-across to NSSS's own §2.2 replanting arithmetic — the national problem is the company's problem, ten years earlier.

**The wild card is Satgas PKH.** Between February 2025 and May 2026 the task force recovered **5.8 million hectares** from palm and mining activities and collected **Rp10.2 trillion** (Rp3.4 tn in administrative forestry fines plus Rp6.8 tn from land recovery), per the Attorney General. Permits were revoked from 29 entities covering 733,180 ha and forest-utilisation permits cancelled for 22 entities covering 1,045,219 ha. **PT Agrinas Palma Nusantara**, a state entity, has received 4.1 million ha of reclaimed land. At the 1-million-hectare mark in April 2025, **369 companies** were already affected. Fastmarkets estimates the campaign could jeopardise **2–5 million tonnes of Indonesian CPO output**.

This is simultaneously a supply-side price support for the sector and, for NSSS specifically, the source of its qualified audit opinion. It is the rare case where the same policy is bullish for the commodity and bearish for the individual name.

### Demand

- **B50 from 1 July 2026** requires ~3 m t/yr of additional palm oil. Indonesia's 2025 biodiesel consumption was 12,704,000 t under B35→B40, +10.97%. Domestic consumption overall was 24,772 thousand t in 2025, +3.82%.
- **India** is the largest global buyer and is actively reviewing a reversal of its 2024 edible-oil import duty cut (20% → 10%). A duty increase would be a clear negative catalyst. India's H1 FY2025-26 vegetable oil imports rose 13% on a palm surge.
- **China** demand is expected to be limited in 2026 on ample soybean-oil stocks.
- **The EU is in structural retreat.** Imports have declined for years under the RED II/III palm biodiesel crediting ban; one secondary source puts 2025/26 EU imports at 1.9 m t (unverified). **EUDR was postponed in December 2025** — general operators now from **30 December 2026**, micro and small operators from **30 June 2027**, with simplifications and a further Commission review due 30 April 2026.

**For NSSS, none of the export-market variables matter directly** — it sells 100% domestically. They matter entirely through the price. And on the traceability question, NSSS's position is structurally better than AALI's for a reason it has never claimed credit for: at **96.4% own fruit**, plot-level traceability is nearly trivial. If it ever wanted an EUDR-compliant export channel, it would need RSPO certification — which it does not have and has never mentioned — but the underlying supply chain would already qualify. AALI, with 38.7% third-party fruit, cannot say that.

**Cash cost per kilo of CPO fell 35% in one year** — from Rp5,610 to Rp3,641 — and the great majority of that is the palm kernel credit, which grew from Rp162 bn to Rp310 bn. Strip the PK windfall by holding the PK price at its FY2024 ratio to CPO (61.4%), and FY2025 cash cost would have been **Rp4,293/kg [calculated]** rather than Rp3,641 — still a Rp1,317/kg improvement on FY2024, but only two-thirds of the reported Rp1,969/kg.

Put differently: **about a third of FY2025's unit-cost improvement is a by-product price that has no reason to persist.**

For reference, the FY2023 cash cost of Rp6,565/kg against an ASP of Rp10,796/kg is the stress case this business has actually lived through — and it produced a 0.18% net margin, because Rp169 bn of interest sat underneath. The difference today is that the interest bill is Rp152 bn against a much larger gross profit.

Both are what a business does when it suddenly has cash — and the fertiliser stockpile is a rational response to the price signal that shows up as a cost headwind in 1H26.

The FY2022–FY2023 excess is structural: Indonesia has no group tax consolidation, so losses at loss-making subsidiaries cannot shelter profits at profitable ones. In FY2022, Rp15.4 bn of unrecognised fiscal losses plus Rp10.4 bn of permanent differences nearly doubled the effective rate.

**FY2025's 16.2% is the opposite problem and it is not repeatable.** The reconciliation shows the rate was pulled below statutory by Rp17,129 m from utilising previously unrecognised fiscal losses, Rp14,607 m of deferred tax on previously unrecognised losses, and Rp10,857 m of deferred tax adjustments — and, at the parent-company level, by the fact that the **Rp71,405 m intercompany dividend it received is non-taxable income**. Unrecognised fiscal losses have now been drawn down to Rp1,058 m (from Rp4,131 m), so that shelter is essentially exhausted.

Read this table carefully, because it is the first crack in a three-year run.

**Prices were up and volumes were flat.** CPO ASP +8.4%, PK ASP +16.3%, FFB production +1.9%. Revenue grew 11.0% — almost entirely price. In FY2025, 54.6% of the revenue growth was volume; in 1H2026 it is barely any.

**And gross profit went backwards.** Down 2.2% on 11% more revenue means costs rose about 24% — the company attributes it to "raised fertiliser costs," the reverse of the tailwind that carried FY2024 and FY2025. The 6.4-point gross-margin compression is the largest single-period deterioration since FY2023.

**Net profit still grew 6.5%, but only because the interest bill keeps falling.** Operating profit was up 1.6%; net profit was up 6.5%. The gap is deleveraging, which is a finite resource — gearing is already at 35.1% against a FY2026 target of 68.8%, so most of that benefit has now been taken.

**New planting was 82 hectares.** Against a full-year target of ~1,500 ha and a prior year of 738 ha, that is 5.5% of target at the halfway mark, described as a "timing issue." Meanwhile mill-utilisation guidance for the year was cut from ~90% to ~80% between the May and August decks.

Positively: the second interim dividend of Rp5/share is a 67% increase on the first and takes the payout ratio to ~35.6%, and net gearing of 35.1% is far ahead of plan.

The honest summary of 1H2026 is that **the operating leverage phase is over and the price-taking phase has begun.** From here, earnings growth requires the CPO price to keep rising, because volume is flat, costs are turning, and the interest tailwind is nearly spent.

## 3.11 What would change the view

**Things that would make this materially better:**

1. **Resolution of SK 36/2025 at or near the Rp108 bn already booked**, with a clean FY2026 audit opinion. That removes a Rp446 bn overhang and roughly doubles the reported earnings base relative to the auditor's restatement.
2. **New planting actually running at 1,500 ha a year.** That would take the replanting horizon from 23.6 years to 11.6 and give the 17,098 ha landbank an economic value. It would also give the third mill something to press.
3. **A disclosed cash cost per tonne**, an own-versus-purchased FFB split, and a quantified fertiliser cost line. All three are standard for the peer group; none exists here. Their absence is why the analysis above had to derive them.
4. **A second lender.** Any diversification away from 100% Bank Mandiri would remove a concentration that is currently unacknowledged.
5. **Payout normalising toward the stated 30% policy.** The 1H26 dividend at ~35.6% suggests this is happening.

**Things that would make it materially worse:**

1. **The PP 45 exposure resolving above Rp554 bn.** Press coverage has floated "trillions." The auditor's Rp446 bn is the documented floor of the gap, not the ceiling of the risk.
2. **Losing the affected 2,023 ha outright** rather than paying a fine. The company has already impaired Rp42,103 m of mature bearer plants on that land, which is an accounting admission that the trees may go. 2,023 ha at FY2025 productivity is roughly **41,775 t of FFB, 9,274 t of CPO and 2,005 t of PK — about Rp152 bn of revenue a year, 7.6% of FY2025 sales [calculated]**.
3. **Palm kernel reverting.** Worth ~Rp81 bn of gross profit at FY2025 volumes, and PK is at 96% of the CPO price — a level with no historical precedent in this company's record.
4. **Losing or repricing the Sinar Mas relationship.** 52% of revenue with one counterparty, no disclosed contract term, and an 8-day averaging formula that is the only pricing protection described anywhere.
5. **A tightening DMO.** More CPO forced into the domestic market is a direct hit to a producer that sells only domestically.
6. **The 2030 replanting wall arriving with the current pipeline.** 1,912 ha of 0–3 year palms against 25,196 ha of prime cohort.

**The open threads I could not close with this corpus:**

- What was the September 2025 EGM's change to the IPO use-of-proceeds plan? Rp175.2 bn was unspent at end-2024 and the amendment is nowhere described.
- What corporate action moved 49.83% of the company from PT Mitra Agro Dharma Unggul to PT Samuel Tumbuh Bersama during FY2024, and what turned that into "PT Samuel Sekuritas Indonesia" at 41.6% by August 2026?
- Who pays the Rp890 m of annual "management fee income," given it appears in *consolidated* other operating income and therefore cannot be intercompany?
- Which related parties provide the unnamed "cash deficit guarantee" on the entire Rp1.75 tn debt stack?
- Why does PT Hamparan Mitra Abadi carry Rp48.4 bn of assets and zero planted hectares four years after acquisition?
- What is the actual field and mill workforce? The disclosed 191 "permanent employees" cannot be it.
- Why is the FY2025 annual report's average palm age 9.8 years when the 1Q26 and 1H26 decks say 11.5 years for the same estate one quarter later?
- Why do the bearer plants — the largest asset on the balance sheet — carry no insurance?

## Appendix C — Method notes

- **All figures are IDR million** unless stated otherwise. Figures taken from investor decks in IDR billion have been converted and are marked where the conversion matters.
- **[calculated]** marks any figure I computed from disclosed inputs. Every material calculation is shown in full in the scenario blocks so the assumptions can be checked or replaced.
- **FY2021 figures** come from the comparative column of the FY2022 annual report, not a primary FY2021 filing.
- **1H2025 and 1H2026 figures** are sums of the company's own quarterly deck disclosures. The decks do not publish quarterly COGS, finance items, tax or EPS, so half-year gross margin is computable but half-year effective tax rate is not.
- **Balance-sheet figures for Jun-25 through Jun-26** are estimates derived from disclosed ratios, because the decks stopped publishing absolute balances after 1H25.
- **Market and policy data** in §2.7, §2.8 and §3.8 comes from a dated web-research pass on 17 August 2026 (Palm Oil Magazine, GAPKI, MPOB, MPOC, S&P Global Commodity Insights, Council of the EU, Bisnis.com, stockanalysis.com, and others cited inline). That pass hit its own limit partway through; items it could not verify are flagged as such rather than estimated.
- **Where two company sources disagree**, both figures are shown in Appendix A and the one used in the body is stated.
- **Corrections are marked in place** rather than rewritten — the source typos in Appendix A items 17, 18, 19, 20 and 21 are reproduced as published with the corrected reading noted.
