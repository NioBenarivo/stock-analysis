## THE ONE THING TO UNDERSTAND FIRST

**GEMS is one coal mine wearing a group's clothes — and GEMS does not mine it.**

Two facts, both from the FY2025 filings, control everything else in this report:

**1. Concentration.** The company presents itself as five concessions across four provinces,
66,204 hectares, 21 subsidiaries. In reality:

| | Production FY2025 | % of group |
|---|---:|---:|
| **BIB** (PT Borneo Indobara, South Kalimantan) | **51.41 Mt** | **93.56%** |
| KIM Block (Jambi) | 1.82 Mt | 3.31% |
| BSL (South Sumatra) | 1.72 Mt | 3.13% |
| TKS, EMS Group (WRL/BSA) | **0** | 0% |
| **Total** | **54.95 Mt** | **100%** |

Of the 21 subsidiaries, **11 are "not yet operating"** — including every entity in the EMS
Group and TKS, which between them hold 87.8 Mt of booked reserves that have never produced a
tonne. Two of those non-producing units (BSA and WRL) were **impaired again in FY2025**
(US$3.60m, after US$15.02m across BSA/WRL/TKS in FY2024). BIB alone holds **527.0 Mt of the
group's 844.4 Mt reserves (62.4%)** and generates roughly 93% of the cash. Any GEMS model that
is not a BIB model is decoration.

**2. GEMS does not operate the mine.** The consolidated group employed **674 people** at
31 December 2025 (596 by the FS count; 25 at the listed parent itself) to produce 54.95 million
tonnes. Group headcount has gone 415 (2022) → 557 (2023) → 572 (2024) → 596 (2025) while
production rose 43%. That is **81,500 tonnes of coal per employee.** It works because virtually the entire
physical operation is outsourced:

- **Mining services US$986,977,240 = 60.08% of cost of revenues** [calc], paid to third-party
  contractors.
- Two contractors alone — **PT Putra Perkasa Abadi (US$538.25m) and PT Cipta Kridatama
  (US$302.78m)** — took **US$841.03m, equal to 51.2% of total COGS and 85.2% of the entire
  mining-services line** [calc].
- **PT Cipta Kridatama is a related party.** It sits in the same group as **PT Radhika Jananta
  Raya, which owns 30% of GEMS.** So does the road-maintenance counterparty (PT Tunas Inti
  Abadi) and the port operator (PT Pelabuhan Buana Reja). At BIB, the mine contractor, the haul
  road and the jetty are all supplied by the same 30% shareholder's group — under contracts
  running to **16 February 2036**, i.e. the exact expiry date of BIB's Coal Contract of Work.

GEMS's own capital base is trivially small relative to what it moves: **net property and
equipment of US$112.72m (9.42% of assets)** against **US$2.41bn of revenue** — a fixed-asset
turnover of 21.7×. This is not a mining company in the capital-intensive sense. It is a
**concession-holder and coal marketer** that rents its mining capacity from contractors, one of
whom is its own shareholder.

**3. `NEW IN v2` — the reserve base has not been replaced since 2021, and the depletion is exact.**
With seven years of reserve tables side by side, the pattern is unambiguous:

| Year-end | Group reserves (Mt) | Change | Production (Mt) | BORNEO reserves (Mt) | Change | BIB production (Mt) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2019 | 1,025.0 | — | 30.83 | 663.0 | — | 28.73 |
| 2020 | 1,032.9 | **+7.9** | 33.46 | 693.9 | **+30.9** | n/d |
| 2021 | **1,037.7** | **+4.8** | 29.11 | **702.8** | **+8.9** | 25.45 |
| 2022 | 993.2 | −44.5 | 38.40 | 664.5 | −38.3 | 34.86 |
| 2023 | 949.9 | −43.3 | 46.12 | 625.1 | −39.4 | 42.14 |
| 2024 | 899.2 | −50.7 | 50.69 | 578.3 | −46.8 | **46.80** |
| 2025 | **844.4** | **−54.8** | **54.95** | **527.0** | **−51.3** | **51.41** |

Through 2019–2021 Salva Mining was *upgrading* the resource faster than GEMS mined it — BORNEO
grew 663.0 → 702.8 Mt while producing roughly 90 Mt. From 2022 the additions stop dead. Over
2022–2025 the group produced **190.16 Mt** and reserves fell **193.3 Mt**; BORNEO produced
**175.21 Mt** and its reserves fell **175.8 Mt** [calc]. From FY2023 onward the reserve table is
**literally the prior year minus that year's production** — in 2024 and 2025 the BORNEO change
(−46.8, −51.3) matches BIB output (46.80, 51.41) to the first decimal.

The reason is in the footnotes. Salva Mining Pty Ltd issued a fresh JORC statement in
**January 2020, January 2021, January 2022 and January 2023** — and then stopped. The FY2023,
FY2024 **and** FY2025 reports all cite the **same January 2023 statement**, "after considering
coal production up to December 20XX." **There has been no independent reserve work for three and
a half years.** Exploration spend over the same period: US$0.49m (2024), US$1.82m (2025).

Read the rest of this report through that lens: GEMS's margin is the spread between a
government-set coal price and a contractor's invoice, on a single deposit in South Kalimantan
that is being depleted without replacement, with roughly ten years of reserve life left against
an eleven-year licence.

# 01 — MANAGEMENT

## 1.1 History and milestones

GEMS is a 1997 shell that became a Sinar Mas asset in 2009 and has been a passed parcel between
strategic partners ever since. The mines predate the listed entity: BIB and KIM were both in
commercial operation from **2005**, before GEMS owned either.

Read as a strategy: **buy one very good CCoW (2006) → get a rich parent (2009) → list to
monetise (2011) → rotate strategic partners (GMR in 2011, out in 2022) → simplify the holding
chain (2023) → harvest.** GEMS has not acquired a new producing concession since 2018, and the
last two acquisitions (EMS 2016, BSL 2018) are, respectively, still not producing and producing
1.72 Mt at a loss-making adjacent unit. **The 2006 purchase is the entire company.**

> **`REVISED v2` — I was wrong about the GEAR transfer.**
> Version 1 called the August 2023 change *"the single largest unexplained item in the file"* and
> framed it as a ~60-point transfer between two brothers' separate vehicles. The earlier annual
> reports show that reading was wrong on the key fact.
>
> The **FY2022 report's ownership chart** shows **DSSA owned 77.5% of GEAR**, and GEAR owned
> 62.4998% of GEMS. DSSA's *effective* economic interest was therefore
> **77.5% × 62.4998% = 48.44%** [calc]. After August 2023 DSSA held **51.00% directly**. So the
> restructuring **moved DSSA from ~48.4% indirect to 51.0% direct** — it collapsed a Singapore
> intermediate holding company out of the chain and slightly increased the parent's stake. That
> is an ordinary group simplification, not an unexplained transfer of value, and it is consistent
> with GEAR converting from *Limited* to *Pte. Ltd.* (i.e. delisting) in the following year.
>
> What *is* genuinely notable, and survives: at the top of the FY2022 and FY2023 charts sits
> **PT Sinar Mas, controlled jointly by Franky Oesman Widjaja, Indra Widjaja and Muktar
> Widjaja**. By the FY2024 chart PT Sinar Mas has disappeared, PT Sinar Mas Tunggal sits directly
> above DSSA, and the controllers are named separately — **Franky controlling DSSA, Indra
> controlling GEAR and SMC**. The family's joint holding structure was formally separated between
> 2023 and 2024. The GEMS filings document the outcome and never explain it, but it is a
> reorganisation of the *owners*, not an extraction from the *company*. I have removed this from
> the risk list.

**Why the low CV matters.** BIB's 4,000–4,200 kcal/kg product is at the bottom of the seaborne
thermal spectrum. It is priced off **ICI-4 (Indonesian Coal Index, 4,200 kcal GAR)**, not off
Newcastle 6,000 kcal. In FY2025 ICI-4 averaged **US$46.08/t** while NEWC averaged
**US$105.63/t** [calc]. GEMS therefore captures roughly **44 cents of every Newcastle dollar**.
The compensations are: very low sulphur (0.2–0.6%), low ash (5–7%), shallow strip ratio, and
proximity to a coastline.

**The one high-CV asset is idle.** TKS holds **6,600 kcal/kg ar** coal in Central Kalimantan —
by far the most valuable specification in the group — and has produced **nothing** since its IUP
was issued in 2009. Its IUP **expires in 2028**. It holds 0.6 Mt of booked reserves. It was
impaired in FY2024. GEMS has never explained why the best coal in the portfolio has never been
mined; the most likely answer is that 11,455 ha in North/East Barito is too far inland to be
economic without river infrastructure it does not have.

**GEMS realises 96.7% of ICI-4, to the decimal, in both years.** ICI-4 fell 14.5%; GEMS ASP fell
14.6%. There is no meaningful pricing skill, no hedging, no term-contract cushion. **To model
GEMS revenue: take your ICI-4 forecast, multiply by 0.967, multiply by tonnes.** Everything else
is second-order.

For reference, NEWC 6,000 kcal averaged US$105.63 in 2025 (2024: US$135.16, −21.8%). GEMS
captured **42.2% of NEWC** in 2025 vs 38.6% in 2024 — the low-CV discount narrowed slightly.

### 2. The HBA regime — a policy variable that became a price variable

From **1 March 2025** the Ministry of Energy and Mineral Resources made the **Harga Batubara
Acuan (HBA)** mandatory for all coal *export* sales, replacing ICI as the transaction reference,
and made it the royalty base. HBA is set **twice monthly** (1st and 15th). At 1 August 2026:

| HBA grade | CV (kcal/kg) | US$/t |
|---|---:|---:|
| HBA 1 | 6,322 | 124.44 |
| HBA 2 | 5,300 | 93.27 |
| **HBA 3** | **4,100** | **65.48** |
| HBA 4 | 3,400 | 45.27 |

**Note the gap: HBA-3 at 4,100 kcal is US$65.48 while ICI-4 at 4,200 kcal was running in the
mid-40s.** The government benchmark sits materially *above* the market index for GEMS's grade.
If HBA is genuinely enforced as the export transaction price, either GEMS's realised prices rise
sharply, or its royalty base is inflated relative to what it actually collects, or buyers walk.
**This is the biggest single unresolved uncertainty in the FY2026 model** and the AR does not
discuss it.

### 3. Stripping ratio — the cost variable GEMS cannot control

| | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Group SR (bcm/t) | 5.98 | 5.23 | **5.41** |
| Mining services (US$/t) | — | 16.57 | **17.96** |

The arithmetic [calc]: at FY2024's contractor rate of **US$3.17 per bcm**, each 0.1× of strip
ratio adds 0.1 bcm per tonne, i.e. **US$0.317/t** — **US$17.2m of pre-tax profit on 54.16 Mt.**
Separately, the *rate itself* rose 4.8% to US$3.32/bcm, costing another ~US$0.50/t. BIB's own SR
has run 4.11 (9M2021) → 4.98 (9M2022) → 5.41 (1H2023). **The direction is one-way**: strip ratios
on a maturing open pit do not go back down.

### 4. Contractor rates — 60% of COGS, negotiated behind closed doors

PPA, CK, AEK, Petrosea, KMMB, EST. Rate structures, escalation clauses, fuel pass-through
mechanisms and take-or-pay volumes are **not disclosed anywhere**. Given CK's shareholder
relationship, this is the largest governance-adjacent disclosure gap in the file.

### 5. Government production quota (RKAB) — new in 2026, and binding

- Indonesia produced **~790 Mt in 2025** (vs a 739.67 Mt target, and 836 Mt in 2024 by the AR's
  own inconsistent figures — the AR quotes both "declining to 790" and "790, a 6.8% increase
  from target" in the same document).
- For **2026 the government cut the national quota to ~600 Mt**, explicitly to support prices.
  Approved RKAB volumes reached only ~580 Mt by mid-2026.
- Industry (APBI, IMEF, Aspebindo) is lobbying for **700–720 Mt**; a revision process opened in
  July 2026.
- **GEMS's own FY2026 guidance is 54–58 Mt production and 54–58 Mt sales** — i.e. flat to +5.6%
  — which is only achievable if the quota is relaxed or GEMS is favoured in allocation.

A 600 Mt national cap against 790 Mt of 2025 output is a **24% supply cut**. If enforced, ICI-4
rises; if GEMS's own RKAB is cut proportionally, volume falls. The two effects partially offset
in revenue but **not in cost**: fixed and semi-fixed costs per tonne rise when volume falls.

### 6. DMO — 25% minimum, and the US$70 cap

GEMS supplied **18.95 Mt = 35% of sales** domestically, comfortably above the 25% floor, through
BIB, KIM Block and BSL. Coal sold to PLN for public electricity is **capped at US$70/t** — but
since BIB's product realises ~US$45/t, **the cap does not currently bind on GEMS**. It becomes a
constraint only if ICI-4 recovers above ~US$72. Export eligibility is now conditioned on DMO
compliance, so the 35% overshoot is deliberate insurance.

### 7. Export levy and windfall tax

Export levies of up to **5% (≈US$5–7/t)** apply, and a **windfall tax has been proposed** though
implementation is delayed pending technical review. **PP 8/2025** additionally requires **100% of
natural-resource export proceeds to be held in a special Indonesian bank account for a minimum
12 months** unless used for specified obligations — a direct constraint on GEMS's ability to
repatriate and distribute export dollars, and a plausible partial explanation for the
US$59.59m Danantara purchase and the US$7.92m increase in restricted funds.

### 8. Freight

US$196.02m, 8.12% of revenue, US$3.62/t — down 17.0% per tonne. GEMS charters; it owns no
vessels. Exposure is to dry-bulk Supramax/Panamax rates and to route mix (China short-haul vs
India long-haul). The India collapse mechanically reduced freight per tonne.

### 9. Currency

Reporting currency USD; **35.0% of revenue is Rupiah-denominated** (US$843.93m) and 63.6% of
trade receivables are in Rupiah (US$152.71m). Royalty, salaries, most overheads and part of the
debt are Rupiah. FX gain was only US$0.19m in 2025 (2024: US$6.20m). A weaker Rupiah is **net
positive** for GEMS (costs fall faster than domestic revenue) but the exposure is modest and
partially self-hedged.

### 10. Interest rates

Modest. Bank debt US$133.84m at 5.80%–6.80% (USD) and 7.90%–8.25% (Rupiah); interest expense
US$6.17m plus other financial charges US$6.40m. Both rate bands *fell* ~1pp during 2025.

## 2.7 Demand

**Global.** The IEA's assessment, as reported in the AR, is that **2025 is the peak year for
world coal demand at ~8.85 billion tonnes**, entering a plateau and then decline as the energy
transition accelerates. That framing — from the company's own annual report — is the single most
important statement about GEMS's terminal value.

**China (50.3% of GEMS revenue, ~52% of volume).**
- Chinese coal **imports fell from 542.70 Mt (2024) to 490.27 Mt (2025), −9.7%**.
- Chinese domestic production **rose 1.24% to ~4,830 Mt**.
- The mechanism is straightforward: China is substituting domestic output and renewables for
  imports, and low-CV Indonesian coal is the marginal, most easily displaced supply.
- GEMS's China revenue nonetheless fell only 2.9% — it **gained share in a shrinking market**,
  which is the strongest operational fact in the FY2025 accounts.

**India (6.4% of revenue, down from 12.1%).**
- Imports flat at ~247.64 Mt; domestic production **+4.98% to 1,042 Mt**.
- GEMS's India revenue **halved**. India is executing the same import-substitution playbook as
  China, faster.

**Southeast Asia (the stated strategic focus).**
- **Vietnam: 65.44 Mt imported in 2025, +2.6% yoy (+1.6 Mt)** — expanding coal fleet.
- **Philippines: first import decline since the pandemic** in 1H2025.
- Thailand: mature, importing to maintain grid stability, transitioning faster than ASEAN peers.
- GEMS's ASEAN revenue: Philippines US$70.57m (+5.8%), Vietnam US$23.74m (+54.8%),
  **Cambodia US$28.36m (+603%)**, Thailand US$5.26m (new), Malaysia US$5.37m (−68.0%).
  In aggregate ASEAN is ~5.5% of revenue — real growth, from a base too small to replace India.

**Indonesia domestic (35.4% of revenue) — the actual growth market.**
- PLN and IPP demand, plus the **cement, paper and smelter** complex.
- The nickel/aluminium downstreaming build-out (Weda Bay, Ruipu, Hua Chin, Yashi, Lipe) is
  captive-power demand that did not exist five years ago and pays market prices.
- Domestic ASP now exceeds export ASP. **Domestic is no longer the dumping ground; it is the
  premium channel.**

## 2.8 Supply

**Indonesia**
- 2024 production: ~836 Mt (AR); 2025: **~790 Mt**; **2026 quota: ~600 Mt**, with only ~580 Mt of
  RKAB approved by mid-2026 and a revision process under way.
- 2025 allocation: exports 514 Mt (65.1%), domestic consumption 254 Mt (32.0%), stocks 22 Mt
  (2.8%).
- Exports Jan–Oct 2025 **fell ~20.25% by value**, with volume down and unit prices corrected.
- Indonesia is the world's largest thermal coal exporter and the **swing supplier of low-CV
  seaborne coal**. The 2026 quota policy is an explicit attempt to use that position as pricing
  leverage — an OPEC-style experiment in coal.

**GEMS's position within it [calc]:** 54.95 Mt of ~790 Mt = **7.0% of Indonesian production**;
35.21 Mt of 514 Mt = **6.9% of Indonesian exports**. GEMS describes itself as a "Top 5 Indonesian
coal company by production volume" (2021 deck). It is a **price taker with a policy-maker's ear**,
not a price setter.

**The competitive picture that matters:** GEMS's cost position. Total cash cost excluding royalty
was **US$29.27/t in 2025** (2024: US$30.00/t) against an ASP of US$44.57. That is a **34.3% cash
margin before royalty**, on 4,000 kcal coal, which places BIB in the low-cost quartile of
Indonesian thermal producers. **The asset is genuinely good.** The strip ratio of 5.41 and
20 km haul distances at Satui are the reason.

**Downstreaming (DME) — the policy that isn't happening.** APBI's own assessment, quoted in the
AR, is that coal downstreaming through **Dimethyl Ether** is *"still gradual and does not fully
offset market pressures."* GEMS has **no DME project, no gasification project, no mine-mouth
power project in operation** (BSL is licensed for mine-mouth power but does not operate one).
Unlike Adaro or Bukit Asam, GEMS has made **no move whatsoever toward energy transition or
downstreaming.** Its entire forward plan is: mine the same coal, sell it to the same people.

### Break-even arithmetic, line by line

At 54.16 Mt of sales:

```
  Semi-fixed costs                                     US$ 306.49m
  Variable cost per tonne                              US$  26.52
  Royalty                                              12.94% of revenue
  Other net expense                                    US$  12.51m

  Let P = break-even ASP.
    Revenue          = 54.16 P
    Royalty          = 0.1294 × 54.16 P   = 7.008 P
    Variable         = 26.52 × 54.16      = 1,436.3
    Semi-fixed                            =   306.5
    Other                                 =    12.5

    54.16 P − 7.008 P − 1,436.3 − 306.5 − 12.5 = 0
    47.152 P = 1,755.3
    P = US$37.23 per tonne
    → implied ICI-4 = 37.23 / 0.967 = US$38.50 per tonne
```

At the quota-constrained 41.20 Mt, the same arithmetic gives **P = US$39.36/t, implied ICI-4 =
US$40.70/t.**

> **`REVISED v2` — this break-even is real but it is not the doomsday number I implied.**
>
> ICI-4 printed US$40.68 in July 2025 and US$41.88 in June 2025, so on a *frozen FY2025 cost
> base* GEMS had only ~US$2.20/t of headroom at the year's low. Version 1 stopped there.
>
> The nine-year history says the cost base does not freeze. GEMS actually operated through
> **2019 at an ASP of US$35.72/t and 2020 at US$31.25/t** — both *below* this US$37.23 break-even
> — and earned US$66.77m and US$95.86m of net profit respectively, because all-in cost per tonne
> at the time was **US$32.13 and US$27.43**, not US$37.80. Royalty is a straight percentage of
> revenue; contractor rates, freight and stockpile fees all reset downward in a slump; and
> management demonstrably cut capex to 35–41% of budget in 2021–2022.
>
> So read the arithmetic as a **short-run** break-even — valid for one or two years before rates
> reprice, and therefore the right number for a 2026–2027 view. Over a full cycle the floor is
> lower and the company has already proven it can sit below this line for two consecutive years
> while still paying dividends (US$53m in FY2019, US$125m in FY2020 — the latter at a 130.4%
> payout funded partly from retained earnings).
>
> The genuine asymmetry is the **quota** scenario, because a volume cut does *not* deflate the
> semi-fixed US$306m block. That is why the FY2026 RKAB revision matters more to GEMS
> shareholders than anything management will do — not because US$40 coal is fatal, but because
> US$44 coal on 41 Mt is worse than US$40 coal on 54 Mt.

    US$17.96 (2025), +59%, against a 25% rise in ASP over the same period.
15. **KMS (Karya Mining Solutions)** — first year of commercial operation, US$20.1m of assets.
    If GEMS is in-sourcing mining services, this is the beginning of the most important strategic
    change in a decade. If it is a vehicle to reduce dependence on CK, watch CK's share of the
    mining-services line in FY2026.
16. **Every KIM/BBU hauling contract expired 31 December 2025 and was not renewed.**
17. **TKS (6,600 kcal) IUP expires 2028; EMS Group IUP expires 2027** — both idle, both impaired.
    WRL has been booked at exactly 87.2 Mt every year since 2019 without producing a tonne.
    Either they get developed in the next 12–24 months or 16,194 ha and 87.8 Mt simply lapse.
18. **Working capital**: VAT + tax claims of US$212.61m (17.8% of assets) and rising.

# SUMMARY VERDICT

**What GEMS actually is:** a single, genuinely low-cost, low-CV coal deposit in South Kalimantan
(BIB — 93.6% of production, 62.4% of reserves, ~65% of assets), operated entirely by third-party
contractors, marketed by a 674-person organisation, wrapped in 20 other subsidiaries of which
half have never produced anything, and owned 89.6% by two family blocs.

**What it does well.**
- BIB is a good asset: strip ratio 5.41, 0.2–0.6% sulphur, 5–7% ash, short haul to a private
  jetty at Bunati, cash cost ex-royalty of **US$29.27/t** — low-quartile Indonesian.
- Volume execution has been excellent: **22.6 → 54.95 Mt since 2018**, with FY2025 hitting
  guidance exactly.
- It gained share in China in a year Chinese imports fell 9.7%.
- Balance sheet is conservative — no bonds ever, D/E 0.69× (the lowest in nine years),
  current ratio 1.50×, receivables 94% current with zero credit-loss allowance.
- Sinar Mas related-party dealing is small and clean: **no management fee, no royalty, no licence
  fee, no intercompany loan, no guarantee** anywhere in Note 35.
- It is a serious dividend payer: **US$2.11bn distributed over seven years, 84.9% of cumulative
  net profit and 83.2% of cumulative operating cash flow**, at a **6.25% yield** — and it kept
  paying through the 2019–2020 trough.
- **`ADDED v2` — it has survived worse than this.** In 2019–2020 GEMS ran at US$31–36/t ASP and
  US$2.15–2.82/t of net profit, and still distributed US$178m. FY2025's US$4.80/t is roughly
  double that base.

**What should give you pause.**
- **The 2021–22 boom was the anomaly, not the base.** Gross profit per tonne: US$11–15 through
  2017–2020, US$33.79 in 2022, US$14.24 in 2025. Anyone anchoring on 2022 is anchoring on a
  once-in-a-generation price event.
- **Price sensitivity is 6.1×.** A 10% ICI-4 move is a 61% earnings move. Short-run break-even
  ICI-4 is **US$38.50** at full volume, **US$40.70** at quota-constrained volume — and ICI-4
  printed **US$40.68 in July 2025**. Over a full cycle the floor is lower, because cost deflates
  with price.
- **`STRENGTHENED v2` — reserves have not been replaced since 2021 and the auditor's reserve
  source is three and a half years stale.** Salva Mining refreshed the JORC statement every
  January from 2020 to 2023, then stopped; FY2023, FY2024 and FY2025 all depreciate the same
  January-2023 numbers by production. BORNEO: 702.8 Mt (2021) → 527.0 Mt (2025), a fall of
  175.8 Mt against 175.2 Mt produced — **zero net additions in four years.** Reserve life on the
  only asset that matters is **10.3 years** against a licence expiring 2036.
- **Reinvestment has stopped, and the seven-year record shows it is a habit not a blip.** Capex
  realisation of 35% (2021), 41% (2022), 71% (2025) against budget; FY2025 capex/D&A **0.52×**;
  mine-property additions of **US$257,699**; exploration US$1.82m; no new concession since 2018;
  no downstreaming; no roadmap.
- **`STRENGTHENED v2` — the shareholder-as-contractor problem has a documented ramp and a
  documented governance gap.** Related-party cost of sales went **0.17% (2021) → 5.21% → 10.32%
  → 19.13% (2024)** immediately after RJR bought 30%; Cipta Kridatama's billings rose 3.7× in
  three years; and for two of those years CK's own President Director was **Vice President
  Director of GEMS**. Contracts for mining, haul road and port all run to **16 February 2036**,
  the exact CCoW expiry. Every report states there are no conflict-of-interest transactions.
- **`NEW v2` — two consecutive audited reports contradict each other about FY2024.** The FY2024
  AR says no supplier exceeded 10% of sales; the FY2025 AR says PPA was 18% of FY2024 revenue.
  No restatement note.
- **Unexplained items in the FY2025 accounts**: key management pay +115% in a −46% year
  (disclosed at two different values in the same document), insurance +129%, and the FY2023
  stripping-asset amortisation of US$41.99m that is 24–31× adjacent years.
- **US$59.59m lent to the state at 2% for 5–7 years** while borrowing at 5.8–8.25%.
- **Reclamation provisioned at US$0.019 per tonne ever mined** — US$6.9m against 66,204 ha.
- **10.39% free float with 0.2% quarterly turnover.** There is no market discipline here and no
  practical exit at size. The float has been enlarged exactly once in fifteen years (2021).

**The way to hold this in your head.** GEMS is not a mining company and not a growth company. It
is an **eleven-year cash-extraction structure on one licence**, with the extraction rate set by
ICI-4 and the Indonesian energy ministry, the cost set by two contractors (one of whom is a
shareholder), and the proceeds paid out at ~85% of operating cash flow — a payout ratio it has
now sustained across nine years, two price troughs and one boom.

`REVISED v2:` version 1 closed by saying the market was wrongly pricing GEMS as "a normal
cyclical coal miner." The nine-year record makes that too strong. GEMS **is** a cyclical coal
miner, and a durable one — it earned money at US$31/t coal, it has never issued equity, it has
no bonds, and its balance sheet is the cleanest it has been in nine years. What it is *also* is a
**wasting asset**: the reserve clock (10.3 years), the licence clock (Feb 2036), and the
contractor clocks (Feb 2036) all strike together, and nothing in seven years of filings suggests
a plan for what happens after.

So the valuation question is not "is 11.15× earnings too high for a coal miner." It is **"how
much of a 4.10× book multiple survives the moment the market starts discounting a terminal date
rather than a cycle."** Today the market is still pricing the cycle. The reserve tables say the
terminal date is the real variable, and it is eleven years out — inside the horizon of anyone
holding this for the 6.25% yield.
