**What it does:** Indonesia's largest automotive component group — two reported segments (Manufacturing, supplying parts to 2W and 4W assemblers; Trading, distributing replacement parts through a national dealer and retail network) sitting on top of a third, unconsolidated business of twenty joint ventures and associates run with Japanese partners.

**Where:** Indonesia (55 plants), plus Vietnam, the Philippines and China. Exports to 50+ countries.
**Ownership:** PT Astra International Tbk 80.00% · public float 20.00% (no holder ≥5%).
**Scale, FY2025:** net revenue Rp19,906,774m · profit to owners Rp2,205,022m · total assets Rp22,615,479m · total equity Rp16,964,382m · net cash Rp3,932,768m · 4,819,733,000 shares.
**Market:** Rp2,490/share at 4 June 2026 → market cap ≈ Rp12.00tn; P/E 5.27x, P/B 0.74x, yield 9.12% (Investing.com). Year-end 2025 close Rp2,690 → Rp12.97tn (company disclosure).

**Period covered:** FY2016–FY2025 from ten annual reports read in full, plus the April 2013 rights-issue prospectus, three public-expose decks (9M23, 1H24, 9M25), and web research to 26 August 2026. **All figures Rp million unless stated.** Derived figures marked `[calculated]`.

## THE ONE THING TO UNDERSTAND FIRST

**AUTO's consolidated income statement describes rather less than half of the business it belongs to.**

At 31 December 2025 the balance sheet carried **Rp7,431,925m of investments in associates and joint ventures — 32.9% of total assets** `[calculated]` — across roughly twenty unconsolidated entities. They contributed **Rp1,176,891m of equity income, 44.6% of profit before tax** `[calculated]`. For the four joint ventures whose standalone accounts are still disclosed, combined revenue on a 100% basis was **Rp14,349,337m against AUTO's own Rp19,906,774m**. From FY2016 to FY2021, when six were disclosed rather than four, that ratio ran **100%–124%**: the disclosed slice of the JV network alone turned over more than the listed company did.

Headcount says the same thing. AUTO describes a group of **55 companies and 30,051 employees**; the consolidated statements cover about **7,200 people**. Roughly three-quarters of the people working under the Astra Otoparts name are employed by companies whose revenue never touches AUTO's top line. (Definitions differ and the company never reconciles them — see §07.5.)

Three consequences run through everything below.

**The accounting.** Equity income arrives net of the investee's tax and is not taxed again. As it has grown from 35% to 45% of pre-tax profit, the effective tax rate has fallen mechanically from 25.5% to 11.1% `[calculated]`. Margins, ROE and the tax rate all flatter a consolidated business that is more ordinary than it looks.

**The cash.** Ten years produced **Rp5,888,238m of equity income and Rp3,770,230m of cash dividends — 64.0% conversion** `[calculated]`. The Rp2,118,008m gap compounds inside the carrying value, and has widened since FY2022. AUTO holds 25.7%–50% stakes and never control, so it decides none of it.

**The disclosure.** From FY2022 two of six material JVs — PT AT Indonesia and PT Aisin Indonesia, both loss-makers in most disclosed years, neither ever impaired — stopped being individually disclosed. The unnamed "individually immaterial" bucket has grown from **25.3% of the JV book in FY2016 to 54.8% in FY2025** `[calculated]`.

The visible business is itself two businesses with opposite economics. **Trading — 46.9% of external revenue — produced 67.4% of segment gross profit** `[calculated]`, at a 24.0% margin against Manufacturing's 9.6%. Trading rides a 168-million-unit vehicle parc growing ~7m a year; Manufacturing rides a new-car market that fell from 1,048,040 units in 2022 to 803,687 in 2025. Hence AUTO's contribution to Astra International rose 15% in 9M2025 while the division containing it grew 1% and the car market shrank 11%.

Evidenced in §08.1 (the JV network entity by entity), §08.2 (segment unit economics), §07.5 (tax and equity income), §10 (what the withdrawn disclosure conceals).

## 1.3 History — recap

**AUTO was assembled, not built.** The parent is the surviving shell of a 1996 merger of Astra's component companies. Everything since has been equity stakes taken alongside Japanese partners — Denso 1975, GS 1972, Kayaba 1976, Akebono 1981, Inti Ganda Perdana 1982. The oldest operating businesses in the group predate the listed company by twenty years and most are not consolidated.

**The float is a manufactured product, not a legacy.** From the 1998 IPO to 2013 Astra's stake *rose*, to 95.65%, with the float shrinking to 4.35%. The 2013 rights issue was underwritten by Astra and the prospectus modelled only 95.65% or 96.52%. What happened sat outside both: within weeks Astra placed ~15% into the market for Rp2.8tn to create today's 20% float, which has not moved in thirteen years.

**Every downturn has produced the same two moves:** push the aftermarket network harder, and diversify sideways rather than deeper — retail formats, then EV charging, then medical devices, then heavy-equipment and railway components. There is no year in the decade in which AUTO acquired a significant new manufacturing business.

## 1.4 Owner's character

Read from the record, not asserted.

**They create liquidity when it suits them and do not chase it back.** Astra manufactured the float in 2013 and has held 80.00% unchanged since. No creeping acquisition, no buyback, no tender offer, no statement on AUTO's listing status was found.

**When they do buy minorities out, the controller sets the terms.** In March 2021 Jardine Matheson acquired the ~15% of Jardine Strategic it did not own at **US$33.00 cash — a 20–40% premium to market and roughly 43% below Jardine Strategic's own market-value NAV of US$58.22**. It ran as a Bermuda amalgamation: Jardine Matheson voted its own ~85% to clear the 75% threshold, there was no majority-of-the-minority condition, two of nine directors were independent, and dissenters' only remedy was a court appraisal that could not block the deal. Anyone reasoning from AUTO's 0.74x book to a buy-in *at* book should note that this group's one recent precedent priced minorities at a premium to a depressed market and a deep discount to assets. (AUTO is an Indonesian issuer under OJK/IDX rules — a different rulebook, not researched here.)

**Revealed preference right now: the parent is buying its own shares, not AUTO's.** An ASII extraordinary meeting in July 2026 approved a **Rp8 trillion buyback**, 563.49m shares repurchased to date. ASII has announced appetite for cheap equity and is spending it on look-through exposure to AUTO — plus United Tractors, Astra Agro and the rest — at a holding-company discount rather than a control premium.

**Assets move between Astra pockets on undisclosed terms.** On 28 June 2024 AUTO and subsidiary Indokarlo Perkasa sold 89,773 m² at Cibinong to **PT Saka Industrial Perkasa**, 99% indirectly ASII-controlled, for Rp201.5bn — two packages, same day, combined gains ~Rp163bn, **54.9% of that year's other income and ~6.5% of pre-tax profit** `[calculated]`, no valuation basis disclosed, and **Chiew Sin Cheok on both boards** (§9.7).

**The board is the parent's board.** At 31 December 2025, President Commissioner **Gidion Hasan**, Commissioner **Thomas Junaidi Alim Wijaya** (also VP Director of Astra Honda Motor, AUTO's largest customer at 22.4% of revenue) and President Director **Hamdani Dzulkarnaen Salim** were all concurrently Directors of PT Astra International Tbk. Three further commissioners are former ASII Directors. Every one of those biographies carries the boilerplate line that the individual has "no affiliation with major or controlling shareholders."

**Independence has a long tenure.** Bambang Trisulo served as Independent Commissioner from May 2007 until his death in July 2025 — **eighteen years**; Agus Tjahajana Wirakusumah since 2015. Two of three FY2025 Audit Committee members hold active positions elsewhere in the Astra group.

**People leave without a stated reason, and come back.** Simon Collier Dixon (2016), Djangkep Budhi Santoso and Hugeng Gozali (2018), Aurelius Kartika Hadi Tan (2022), Yusak Kristian Solaeman and Agus Baskoro (2023), Chiew Sin Cheok, Lay Agus and Kusharijono (2025) all leave with no explanation. Two returned: **Djangkep Budhi Santoso**, an AUTO Director 2007–2018, came back in October 2025 as *Independent* Commissioner and Audit Committee Chairman; **Yusak Kristian Solaeman**, a Director 2014–2023, spent two years running PT Isuzu Astra Motor Indonesia and returned in April 2025 to a newly created Vice President Director role.

**Disclosure narrows over time.** Six material JVs became four in FY2022. Litigation content, substantive in FY2016–FY2019, disappears from the contingencies note by FY2022. The unrecognised-JV-losses line stops after FY2018. The 9M25 public-expose deck dropped gross profit, operating profit, EBITDA, every margin, every balance-sheet line, the industry-volume slide and the strategy slide that both earlier decks carried.

Whether that is correct allocation away from a low-return activity or under-investment in the one thing AUTO controls is the open question; manufacturing gross margin never regaining its FY2016 level is at least consistent with the second reading.

How the contracts work (2013 prospectus, ~50 agreements — the only place this is set out):

- **No fixed annual volume commitment in any of them.** Quantities run on periodic purchase orders; the agreement sets the relationship, the specification right and the term.
- **One to five years, auto-renewing annually** unless terminated.
- **A service-parts obligation of up to ten years after a model is discontinued** (several Hino and Daihatsu agreements). This is the mechanism that turns an OEM programme win into a decade-long aftermarket annuity — and why Manufacturing and Trading are less separable than the segment note implies.
- **Model-dedicated tooling can be OEM-owned**: one Mitsubishi agreement supplies the jigs, restricts them to Mitsubishi parts, and lapses when the line stops.
- Several technology licences are tied to a *vehicle programme* rather than a term — Inti Ganda Perdana's Akashi Kikai licence ends if Daihatsu production ends; Akebono's Lucas licence ends with "vehicle 692N".
- No price-down clause appears in any summary. Absence is not proof it does not exist.

**Exports: no revenue figure, percentage or destination split is disclosed anywhere in the decade.** The last numbers are from 2013 — **total-company** export revenue of Rp716.9bn, 8.7% of the Rp8,277.5bn group total, of which the **manufacturing segment's own export line was only Rp22.3bn**; almost all of the export book sat in Trading. ## 4.1 The Indonesian component industry

The structure was set in the 1970s by the ATPM system, under which a global OEM appoints one sole national agent to assemble, import, distribute and service its brand. **Astra is ATPM for Toyota, Daihatsu, Isuzu, UD Trucks, Peugeot, BMW and Lexus; Indomobil for Nissan, Suzuki, VW, Renault, Audi and Hino.** The component base grew as Japanese keiretsu suppliers formed local joint ventures alongside those two groups — which is exactly what AUTO's associate list is.

**The Ministry of Industry's own three-phase strategy is the load-bearing fact for AUTO's long-run demand:** phase 1 (through 2026) creates the market through CKD assembly; phase 2 (2027–2029) mandates **domestic battery packs and modules**; phase 3 (2030+) requires **domestic battery cells**. Phases two and three name batteries and nothing else. With the 2030 re-weighting — assembly 10% → 20%, R&D 20% → 10% — and painting and joining now counting toward content, the ladder rewards local *assembly* and local *battery* value-add. **A Tier-1 making brake parts, gaskets, shock absorbers, propeller shafts or lead-acid batteries is not the named beneficiary of the 60% and 80% steps.**

Two things cut the other way and should be held open. Indonesian industry press frames the 2027 step explicitly as an opportunity for local component makers (Kompas, 5 Aug 2026 — headline only, body robots.txt-blocked), and AUTO's own FY2025 outlook credits TKDN implementation as a support for 2026. Meanwhile the incentive package includes a **0% import duty on EV components**: the state is simultaneously making it cheaper to import the component and compulsory to localise it. The ICE local-content regime, the regulation numbers and component import tariffs are all **NOT FOUND**.

# 05 — KEY VARIABLES

**1. Equity income from associates and joint ventures — 44.6% of pre-tax profit (Rp1,176,891m).** The largest earnings variable and the one AUTO controls least: it swung from +Rp522,555m (2019) to −Rp76,932m (2020), a Rp600bn move that turned an Rp817bn profit year into a loss year. Return on the book has risen from 5.3% to 17.1% `[calculated]`. **A 10% move is Rp118bn of pre-tax profit, ~4.5% of the total.** §08.1.

**2. Trading volume and margin — 46.9% of revenue, 67.4% of segment gross profit.** Gross margin up in eight of nine years, 19.7% → 24.0%, driven by the 168m-unit parc, the retail build-out and channel mix (B2C 14% → 17% of trading revenue in two years). **One point of trading gross margin ≈ Rp98bn of gross profit** `[calculated]`. The variable that has done the most work in five years and the least exposed to the new-vehicle cycle.

**3. Astra-group OEM build volume — 60% of manufacturing OEM revenue, which is ~32% of group revenue** `[calculated]` **(a different construct from the 29.5% of revenue billed to named Astra OEMs as related parties in §8.3).** Toyota, Daihatsu and Isuzu are 50.5% of 4W wholesales; Astra Honda Motor is 76.2% of 2W and 22.4% of AUTO's revenue alone. The segment moves with build rate, not retail sales — which is why CBU exports partially offset the domestic collapse in 2025. **The 2020 volume shock took manufacturing gross margin to 4.5% and Rp560bn out of gross profit in one year.**

**4. Raw materials — 45.2% of cost of revenue (Rp7,473,988m).** Never disaggregated by the company. **A 5% move is Rp374bn — 11% of gross profit and 14% of pre-tax profit** `[calculated]`: the largest single-line sensitivity in the business, and the least visible.

**5. The GS Battery transfer price — Rp2,600,411m, 15.7% of cost of revenue.** No pricing basis in ten years. AUTO bears 100% of the purchase cost but takes only 50% of the profit it creates, so **a 5% change in the transfer price is Rp130bn of AUTO cost against Rp65bn of AUTO equity income — a Rp65bn net swing, 2.5% of pre-tax profit** `[calculated]`.

**6. IDR/JPY, and IDR/USD second.** Yen-denominated royalties, technical fees, tooling and components against a yen down ~21% versus the rupiah from its 2020 peak. Dollar net asset, yen net liability — they compound. Disclosed 10%-move sensitivity Rp20.3bn after tax (FY2022).

**7. Dividends actually received from investees — Rp661,746m in FY2025, 64.0% ten-year conversion.** Distinct from variable 1 because it is the cash, and it dominates the investing line: FY2025 investing cash flow fell 65% "due to lower dividend received," not from any change in capex. Rp661.7bn received against Rp925.4bn paid out to owners.

**8. Labour — 10.3% of cost of revenue and falling.** Wages up ~6% a year against 4.4% revenue growth, offset by a consolidated headcount down 34% from 2017 to 2024. The offset has largely been taken; the wage inflation has not stopped.

The lesson is in the middle rows: **a market that halved took manufacturing revenue down 36% and its gross profit down 66%; trading revenue fell 8% and its gross profit 5%.** That is the whole argument for thinking about the two segments separately.

The swing that actually produced the loss was the equity book — **Rp599bn, larger than the entire manufacturing segment's gross profit that year** — driven by PT AT Indonesia (−Rp77.4bn share) and PT Aisin Indonesia (−Rp104.6bn), OEM-linked casting and clutch businesses with no aftermarket buffer. **The two entities that broke 2020 are the two that stopped being individually disclosed from FY2022.**

A near-135% effective tax rate finished it: Rp116,071m of pre-tax profit met a Rp157,200m charge including a Rp59,116m deferred-tax remeasurement from the COVID-era rate cut. Management cut key management pay 45.1%, skipped the interim dividend entirely (the only skip in the decade), and took cash capex to Rp292,820m — 0.61x that year's depreciation charge of Rp480,819m. **No formal severance or restructuring programme is named in any year**, though curtailment credits appear in the 2020, 2021 and 2023 employee-benefit roll-forwards.

**FY2024 is the year to look at: gross profit fell — the only decline in the decade outside 2020 — while net profit rose 10.4%.** The bridge, in management's own words, was "gain from the sale of fixed assets that were no longer used for manufacturing activities, as well as increase in interest income" — the two same-day disposals to PT Saka Industrial Perkasa, ~Rp163bn of gain, 54.9% of other income. **Excluding those gains and adjusting for tax items, FY2024 pre-tax profit was ~Rp2,367,340m against the reported Rp2,489,163m, roughly 5% lower** `[calculated]`. ## 6.3 Where a repeat would land

The 2020 template applied to FY2025 numbers: a halving of vehicle demand takes roughly **−Rp750bn of manufacturing gross profit, −Rp110bn of trading gross profit, and an equity-income swing from +Rp1.18tn to near zero** `[calculated]` — a pre-tax result close to breakeven.

Three things are better than 2019: **net cash of Rp3,932,768m against Rp30,293m**, with Rp3.1tn undrawn; Trading is a larger share of gross profit (67.4% vs 63.3%); the equity book is bigger and earns more. Three are worse: the fixed-asset base is nine years older (NBV 37.5% of cost vs 50.1%); more than half the JV book is now undisclosed, so the AT Indonesia / Aisin failure mode would be invisible until it hit the aggregate; and the structural threat is new — **2020 was a demand shock that reversed, while the loss of internal-combustion content per vehicle does not.**

## 9.1 The 2013 rights issue and the sell-down that followed

April 2013: 963,946,600 shares at **Rp3,100**, ratio 100:25, gross proceeds **Rp2,988,234,460,000**, with Astra International as standby buyer committed to exercise its own rights *and* take up anything the public left. Total shares after: 4,819,733,000 — unchanged to this day.

**Three-quarters of a Rp3tn equity raise went to repaying debt**, including Rp700bn to refinance the bridging loan for the Pakoakuina stake — an acquisition the offering chapter declines to name, and which the business chapter of the same document identifies. **The sell-down.** The prospectus modelled exactly two outcomes for Astra's stake — 95.65% (full pro-rata subscription) or 96.52% (public abstains, Astra takes the residual). **The actual outcome was 80.00%, outside both.** Astra's share count barely moved through the issue (3,688,203,070 → 3,855,786,337, a gain of ~167.6m against a pro-rata entitlement of ~922m) while the public's holding grew by almost exactly the number of new shares created. External reporting fills the gap: in **May 2013 Astra sold ~15–16% for Rp2.8 trillion (US$286m)** in a marketed secondary placement bookbuilt 23–28 May with UBS, HSBC and Mandiri, explicitly "to increase liquidity of Astra Otoparts shares"; **Toyota Tsusho took 4.90%**, confirmed by AUTO's own FY2016 twenty-largest-shareholder table (236,167,000 shares = exactly 4.90%). The prospectus flagged the plan in the abstract but **no filing in the set gives the placement's date, price or full buyer list**, and whether Toyota Tsusho still holds the stake is not disclosed after FY2016.

Ranked by how much each would change a decision to own the company.

## 10.1 Rp2.6 trillion a year of purchases from a company AUTO half-owns, with no pricing basis in ten years

GS Battery has been AUTO's largest supplier every year at **15.7%–22.0% of consolidated cost of revenue**, and AUTO owns 50% of it. Every year's note discloses credit terms and nothing else — **no arm's-length assertion, no market comparable, no transfer-pricing policy, in any of ten filings.**

The money flow: AUTO pays GS Battery Rp2.6tn a year, sells the batteries through its own network at a 24% gross margin, receives Rp71bn of dividends and books Rp109bn of equity income. **AUTO bears 100% of the purchase cost but takes only 50% of the profit that cost creates.** The 2013 prospectus disclosed that **AUTO was GS Battery's largest customer at 57.7% of its revenue** — so the price is set between two parties with no external reference on either side. This is not evidence of mispricing; it is the absence of any evidence either way on the largest cost line in the business, sustained for a decade.

**Unrecognised share of JV losses** — Rp29.3bn and Rp13.1bn (FY2017), Rp98.5bn (FY2018), never with the entity named, and the line disappears from FY2021. **Litigation** — substantive through FY2016–FY2019 (a Supreme Court land dispute already lost twice on appeal and unprovisioned; the Pakoakuina Bitung case; the Rp1tn Liliana Setiawan claim resolved October 2017); from roughly FY2022 the contingencies note contains **zero contingencies**. **The 9M25 deck** dropped gross profit, operating profit, EBITDA, every margin, every balance-sheet line, the industry-volume slide and the strategy slide that both earlier decks carried, and introduced a "core earnings" measure with no stated formula.

## 10.12 Internal contradictions inside single documents

Small individually, a pattern together. **The FY2025 organisation chart disagrees with its own Note 1c on three entities' ownership** (Ardendi Jaya Sentosa 85% vs 100%, Senantiasa Makmur 34% vs 100%, Aisin Indonesia shown as a 100% direct line against 34% held indirectly). The FY2024 governance checklist states seven Directors while the composition table two pages earlier lists eight. FY2016 states ROE as both 3.2% and 3.5% for the same year. **Related-party trade receivables on the balance sheet disagree with the related-party note's own total in every year from FY2021 to FY2025** (Rp2,161m, Rp1,692m, Rp1,692m, Rp355m, Rp6m — the identical FY2022/FY2023 gap argues against rounding), where FY2016–FY2020 tie exactly. The dividend-table header prints years one column ahead of the row labels beneath, uncorrected across at least two reports. FY2023 explains both selling and G&A *increases* with the verbatim sentence FY2022 used for a G&A *decrease*.

## 10.13 What is not a red flag

Worth stating, because the list above is long. **The primary financial statements are clean.** Ten consecutive pairs of filings tie exactly at the headline level — revenue, gross profit, pre-tax profit, profit for the year, profit to owners, total assets, total liabilities, total equity — with no unexplained restatements. The associates and joint-venture notes reconcile to the rupiah against the income statement and balance sheet in all ten years. There is no negative additional paid-in capital, no PSAK 38 common-control pooling, no qualified opinion in any year the opinion could be verified, no off-balance-sheet vehicle, no going-concern language, and no equity raised since 2013. Operating cash flow exceeded accounting profit cumulatively over the decade — the opposite of the usual warning sign. The company is net cash by Rp3.9 trillion.

**The flags above are overwhelmingly about what is not disclosed, not about what is disclosed wrongly.**

# MISSING DATA AND OPEN ITEMS

**1. The pricing basis for related-party purchases, above all GS Battery.** Rp2.6tn a year, 15.7% of cost of revenue, from a 50%-owned counterparty, no arm's-length statement in ten years. *Might exist in:* an OJK affiliated-transaction disclosure or independent appraisal filed with IDX at a contract renewal; possibly the prospectus's "Perjanjian-Perjanjian Penting" chapter (pp. 114–135), not in the slices read. *Would decide:* whether the 24% trading gross margin is earned or transferred — and therefore whether §8.2's segment economics mean what they appear to.

**2. AT Indonesia and Aisin Indonesia after FY2021.** Aisin lost money in all six disclosed years and AT Indonesia in four of six; both were dropped from disclosure, both are still 34–40% owned, and one has been recapitalised since. *Might exist in:* the entities' Indonesian statutory filings, or Aisin Corporation's segment disclosures. *Would decide:* whether Rp412m+ of carrying value inside "Others" is impaired, and whether the FY2020 failure mode is still live.

**3. The 100%-basis revenue and profit of the "Others" bucket.** Rp2.77tn of carrying value, Rp498bn of earnings, no entity-level figures anywhere. *Might exist in:* only the investees' statutory accounts. *Would decide:* the true scale of the network and whether 42% of equity income from unnamed entities is broad-based or concentrated.

**4. Export revenue and destination split.** Never disclosed in the decade; the last figures are 2013's total-company export of Rp716.9bn (8.7% of revenue) against a manufacturing-segment export line of just Rp22.3bn. *Would decide:* how much of manufacturing is insulated from domestic demand — the most important unknown for 2026, given CBU exports (+12.2%) offset domestic weakness (−10.6%) in 2025.

**5. Production volumes, plant capacity and utilisation.** Never disclosed for any plant or product; the FY2025 blended "≈90%" is the only figure in ten years. *Would decide:* whether manufacturing's margin problem is price, mix or utilisation — three different diagnoses with three different fixes.

**6. Whether Akebono's parent-level distress reaches the Indonesian JV.** AAIJ's liabilities doubled to Rp1.12tn in three years and net assets fell Rp368,769m in FY2024, against a parent reportedly down 95.1% in profit, and a Karawang relocation that would explain the borrowing. *Might exist in:* Akebono Brake Industry's Japanese annual securities report segment note. *Would decide:* whether Rp454bn of carrying value and Rp72bn of annual equity income are at risk.

**7. Quantified ICE content loss, and the fate of the 12V auxiliary battery.** No sourced analysis found (leads: PwC's automotive supply-chain study, L.E.K.'s aftermarket study). **The premise that lithium will not displace lead-acid in the 12V role is an assumption in this report, not a finding** — no source either way, and GS Battery's own profile lists no lithium product. HEV volumes, where ICE content survives longest, are NOT FOUND for any year. *Would decide:* the terminal value of roughly half the product portfolio.

**8. Indonesian replacement-parts market size and AUTO's share.** Only a 5.2% CAGR is public; every vendor paywalls the absolute figure. No genuine/aftermarket/counterfeit split, no Chinese-import penetration, no e-commerce share, no named competitor to Aspira or Shop & Drive. *Would decide:* whether Trading's margin expansion is share gain or category growth.

**9. The 2013 private placement's terms.** Astra's move from 95.65% to 80.00% is evidenced only by a statement of intent in the prospectus and a register entry three years later; price, date and buyer list are absent from every filing, and Toyota Tsusho's 4.90% is not confirmed after FY2016. *Might exist in:* IDX disclosures from May–June 2013 and the FY2013–FY2014 annual reports (outside this scope).

**10. OJK/IDX voluntary-delisting and mandatory-tender-offer rules.** The Jardine Strategic 2021 precedent is Bermuda law and does not transfer to an Indonesian issuer; the rulebook that would govern any AUTO buy-in was not researched. *Would decide:* the mechanics and pricing floor of the scenario a 0.74x book valuation invites.

**11. Per-person board remuneration and audit fees.** Aggregate only, all ten years, across the combined boards of the company and its subsidiaries, with no headcount of who is covered. Audit fees never disclosed. Individual insider shareholdings were not addressed at all until FY2024/FY2025, which now affirmatively state that none hold shares.

**12. Named third-party customers and suppliers.** None in a decade. The company states no individual third party exceeds 10% of revenue or purchases — which, given AHM at 22.4% and GS Battery at 15.7%, is a statement about *third* parties only.

**13. Smaller specific gaps.** Selling-expense and G&A line detail for FY2016, FY2020, FY2024, FY2025 (aggregates complete). Factory-overhead detail for FY2024–FY2025. Named lenders for six of ten years. Construction in progress for nine of ten years. Consolidated headcount for FY2018–FY2020 and FY2025, and the reconciliation between the ~7,200 consolidated and 30,051 group figures. The Rp2,118m FY2019 dividend-received reconciliation gap. The Evoluzione loan maturity (30 Jun 2028 in five filings, 1 Dec 2027 in AR2021) and AUTO's exact stake (37% in the notes, 36.96% on the FY2025 chart). The FY2025 interim dividend total — Rp284,364m on the per-share figure against one press report of Rp641.02bn. AUTO's share price after 4 June 2026, and 3Q2026 results (due late October 2026).

**14. Sources never supplied.** FY2006–FY2015 annual reports (FY2006–FY2008 and FY2010–FY2014 exist on disk but were outside the agreed ten-year scope; **FY2009 and FY2015 are absent from the set entirely**), standalone audited financial statements from IDX for any year, quarterly filings, and the FY2026 interim statements behind the 1H2026 press release.
