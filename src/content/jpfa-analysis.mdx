## THE ONE THING TO UNDERSTAND FIRST

JPFA is a feed mill that sells 57–60% of its output to itself, and the price at which it does so decides where the group's profit appears — but not how much there is.

**The feed segment is the constant.** Animal Feed booked total sales of Rp31.7tn (2022), 33.3, 33.5, 35.8tn (2025) and a segment result of Rp2.61tn, 2.74, 2.38, ~3.13tn — a 7–9% margin in every year, including the two bad ones. Of those sales only Rp13.4–14.9tn a year left the group; Rp18–21tn (57–60%) went to the breeding and commercial-farm segments at transfer prices (§8.1). External feed is just 24–26% of net sales.

**The bird segments are the cycle.** Poultry Breeding (DOC) and Commercial Farm (live bird) together earned Rp368bn (2022), −374bn (2023), +2,967bn (2024), ~+3,065bn (2025). The whole of the FY2023→FY2024 profit jump (operating profit Rp2.21tn → Rp5.06tn) is those two segments swinging Rp3.3tn; feed fell Rp364bn in the same year. What moved them was not JPFA — it was the government-managed live-bird/DOC price: five culling and hatching-egg-cutting circulars in 2023 alone, a 2024 supply regulation, PS culling again in mid-2025 (§04, §06). Read the feed line for the franchise, the bird lines for the price cycle, and the consolidated margin (1.8% → 7.1% net) as the difference.

**Three structural facts sit underneath.** (1) Rp7.0–9.5tn a year of raw material — 19–28% of everything JPFA buys — comes through Annona Pte Ltd, a Singapore company owned by the parent, on a "maximum 5% margin" whose base is never defined; Annona is also the single largest creditor, with Rp2.1–2.8tn of trade payables outstanding at every year-end (§1.5, §10.1). (2) Half the debt is a US$350m bond that matured on 23 March 2026; at 31 December 2025 the whole Rp5.8tn was current, pre-funded 55% with USD cash, and the filings in hand stop there — the 1H2026 deck then shows interest-bearing debt up Rp4.4tn to Rp16.3tn and gearing back from 41.5% to 64.1% with no explanation (§7.2, MISSING DATA #1). (3) Not one physical volume — feed tonnes, chicks, kilograms of bird, tonnes of shrimp — is disclosed anywhere in ~1,200 pages of filings; every unit-economics statement in this report is therefore an inference (§8.5).

Evidence: segment table §8.1, RPT §1.5, the cycle §04–§06, financials §07.

## 1.3 History — recap

Fifty-five years in three moves: a feed business assembled by acquisition (1971–1992) and then re-consolidated by merging the listed breeding subsidiary back in (2009–2012); a downstream and adjacent build-out funded by USD bonds and a private-equity placement (2013–2021: processing, food, vaccines, beef, aquaculture, corn drying); and, since 2022, a generational handover under which the parent was taken private and the listco has been de-levered, re-rated and is now re-leveraging. The physical footprint — 15–16 mills, 30 hatcheries, 78–79 breeding farms — has barely moved since 2020; revenue growth (Rp37tn → 61tn, 2020–2025) has come from price and from pushing more of the bird through the group's own farms and slaughterhouses.

## 1.4 Owner's character

Read from the record:

- **Third-generation family, run through Singapore.** The listco's President Director (35) and a Director (33) are siblings resident in Singapore, both career Japfa Ltd. Two other board members (VP Director Tan Yong Nang, Commissioner Hendrick Kolonas) are disclosed as affiliated with the controlling shareholder through positions or shares in Japfa Pte Ltd. The professional layer beneath — COO Upstream Arif Widjaja, Finance Director Leo Handoko Laksono (ex-Multibreeder President Director), Rachmat Indrajaya — is long-tenured. The President Commissioner is an 84-year-old former intelligence chief in the seat since 2010; the "independent" VP Commissioner is the company's own VP Director of 1997–2021.
- **They took the parent private, not the listco.** The 2025 scheme bought out Japfa Ltd's 18% minority at S$0.62 while leaving JPFA listed at 55%: the family now controls the group's procurement arm (Annona), its management-services arm (ATS) and its sister operations privately, on the other side of every related-party line in the listco (§1.5).
- **They pay when they can, not by rule.** Dividends: Rp60 (FY2021), Rp50 (FY2022), nil (FY2023 — the year profit fell to Rp946bn), Rp140 in two tranches (FY2024), Rp140 (FY2025). Payout 34–55% of attributable profit, no stated policy; 55.43% of every rupiah goes to Japfa Pte Ltd (Rp902bn in each of 2025 and 2026 [calculated]).
- **They hedge, and they pay for it in G&A.** Every USD bond has carried a bought FX option collar; the premium amortisation (Rp113–133bn a year) is booked in general and administrative expenses, not finance cost, so reported interest cover flatters by ~15% (§7.5).
- **They keep the numbers stale where it suits.** The market-share slide has cited "Frost & Sullivan 2021 data" in five consecutive decks and then "2026 data" in the 1H2026 deck; per-capita consumption for Malaysia halved between two decks (50.5 → 31.1 kg) as the source changed; the group headcount ">37,000" has not moved in three decks.
- **They do not explain corporate housekeeping.** A 13.9% stake in a duck-farm subsidiary was bought from an individual for Rp35bn in March 2024 and sold back to him for Rp35bn in December 2025; a feed mill (Margomulyo, Surabaya) disappeared from the facility list in 2024; a Rp275bn purchase of 2%-coupon bonds from Danantara Investment Management (the state investment agency) at a 20% day-one fair-value loss carries no stated purpose (§9, §10).

What to read in it. Annona is the import channel: soybean meal and other imported feed inputs (the parent buys in bulk for six countries) are sold on to JPFA at cost plus a margin "capped at a maximum of 5% p.a." — the notes never say 5% of what, nor what was actually charged. On Rp7.0tn a year, each point of margin is Rp70bn of JPFA profit moving to the private parent. The trade payable to Annona has been the group's largest single liability after the bond every year: 57–69% of all trade payables, carried at 90–127 days against ~30 days for the trade book as a whole — so the parent also lends the working capital, in USD (USD trade payables track the Annona balance almost exactly: Rp2.9tn in 2023). The share has fallen from 28% to 19% of raw materials as JPFA dries more local corn itself; it has not gone away. No royalty, brand or technical-assistance fee is paid to Japfa Pte Ltd — the ATS fee (Rp71bn, +92% since 2023) is the only management charge. Board pay rose 109% over three years (Rp91bn → 189bn) against attributable profit up 182%; the FY2023 figure (12.5% of a collapsed profit) is the one that shows the pay is not tied to results. Arm's-length assurance appears only in the MD&A ("reviewed by the Audit Committee"); the FS carries none.

### Product
Complete and concentrate feeds for broilers, layers, breeders and ducks (Comfeed, Benefeed brands), in mash, pellet and crumble; plus aquafeed reported under Aquaculture. Sold by the tonne to independent farmers and distributors; no customer above 10% of sales in any year (checked, absent). Exports (Brunei, Timor-Leste, and the group's own Myanmar/Vietnam units) rose fourfold in 2023 from a tiny base.

External feed sales grew 1.7% a year 2022–2024 and 7.6% in 2025 while internal feed grew faster: the group's own farms are the growth customer. FY2024 feed profit fell 13% in the year group profit tripled — corn spiked on the 2023 El Niño shortfall and import ban, and feed prices were held while the farms recovered; FY2025 recovered as corn eased and the national crop hit a five-year high (16.55m t).

### Supply chain
Corn is the binding input and the one JPFA has built the most around: 14 standalone corn-dryer sites (six in Lampung, two in Sumbawa, Bima, Brebes, Makassar, Gowa, Sidrap, Gorontalo, Kotamobagu) plus dryers at the mills, and corn-trading subsidiaries (SUL, Sentra Satwatama, Celebes Agro Semesta) bought in 2018–19 — a response to the 2017 ban on feed-corn imports that made the global corn reference "no longer applicable" (the company's own footnote). Soybean meal is imported, largely from South America, in USD, and arrives through Annona (§1.5). The local-vs-import split, tonnes and Rp/kg paid are not disclosed in any year.

### Purchase — what it owns
Fixed assets Rp14,995bn = 37% of total assets (FY2025); the parent's 13 mills plus IAG's two. Capex Rp2,391bn (FY2025, +49%) is reported by asset type only — buildings, machinery, CIP — never by segment after FY2024 (feed capex Rp456bn, 321, 219 in FY2022–24: the mills have been run for cash while breeding and downstream took the money). No tonnes-per-year capacity is printed for any mill; the 1H2026 deck's "22% of national feed capacity" is the only sizing.

The eight-point fall in raw material as a share of sales (70% → 62.5%) between 2023 and 2025 is the whole margin story: sales prices (live bird, DOC) rose while corn and SBM did not. Raw material is 80% of manufacturing cost and Annona supplies 19% of it.

### Distribute
List-price selling through distributors and direct to larger farms; receivable days 17–19 for the group, so feed is close to cash. Pricing is cost-plus with a lag — the FY2024 feed margin dip shows the lag runs against JPFA when corn spikes and farmers cannot pay. The government's 2025–26 corn floor (HPP Rp5,500/kg) and ceiling (HET Rp7,000/kg), and subsidised SPHP corn to independent farmers only, cap what integrators can pass through. Selling expense for the group Rp2,699,886m = 4.4% of net sales: salaries 27%, freight 21%, advertising and promotion 25% (Rp674bn, ×2.5 since 2022 — the consumer-food push).

## 2.2 Poultry Breeding (DOC) — 6% of net sales, 16–17% of segment profit in good years

### Product and supply chain
Grandparent stock is imported under annual government quota (national quota 578,000 head in 2025 → 800,000 in 2026 [web]; JPFA's allocation not disclosed), multiplied through 79 parent-stock farms into hatching eggs and day-old chicks at 30 hatcheries. A new GPS farm at Tanjung Bintang (Lampung) and a hatchery at Berau (East Kalimantan) were added in 2025. ## 4.2 The managed cycle

Indonesian broiler supply is quota-managed at the top and rationed by instruction at the bottom:

| Lever | Mechanism | Record |
|---|---|---|
| GPS import quota | All broiler genetics enter as imported grandparent stock under annual quota, allocated across ~27 breeders | 578,000 head (2025) → 800,000 (2026, +38%, linked to MBG); 15–18 months later that is chick supply |
| Culling / hatching-egg cutting | Ditjen PKH circulars order early culling of parent stock and destruction of hatching eggs when live-bird prices fall below cost | 2019; 2021; 2023 ×5 circulars (PS culled 3.9m + 1.9m + 2.5m + 1.6m; HE cut ~105m); Apr 2025 (19-day HE cutting + PS culling at 54 weeks, Java/Sumatra/Sulawesi — JPFA "fully implemented, no material impact") |
| Reference prices | Kementan/Bapanas farm-gate reference (HAP) for live bird and eggs | Rp18,000/kg floor (Jun 2025) vs market Rp15,000–17,000; Rp19,500/kg from 15 Jul 2026 |
| Permentan 10/2024 | From 1 Jan 2027 DOC producers must sell ≥50% of final-stock DOC to independent farmers (88% for layer DOC) | JPFA sells 39% externally (FY2024 segment note) — the gap to 50% is ~Rp0.9tn of DOC sales [calculated: (0.50 − 0.39) × 8.0tn], a little narrower than CPIN's (36% external) |
| Corn | Feed-corn imports banned since 2017; HPP floor Rp5,500/kg (Bulog absorbs); HET ceiling Rp7,000/kg (Nov 2025); SPHP subsidised corn to independent farmers only; Bapanas seeking added import quota (Aug 2026) | Domestic corn Rp6,600–6,935/kg in H1 2026 against the Rp5,800 HAP — the input the integrator cannot hedge |

Read together with the structural DOC surplus (~3.5bn produced vs ~3.2bn demanded, 2025): this is an industry that overproduces chicks by ~10% and is periodically told to destroy them. The integrator with the most captive downstream (JPFA: 57% of feed and 61% of DOC internal, 15 slaughterhouses) rides the cycle; the independent farmer buying JPFA's external DOC and feed is squeezed first. The 2026 GPS quota expansion is 2027's oversupply risk, arriving in the same year as the 50% external-DOC rule.

## 4.3 JPFA's own supply position

Volumes are not disclosed, so position is inferred: ~1.3bn DOC (25% of ~3.5bn national output) and feed sales consistent with 21–22% of national capacity on ~36m t of national output, i.e. ~7–8m t of feed [calculated, order of magnitude; the segment's Rp35.8tn total sales ÷ ~Rp8,500/t ≈ 4.2m t, so either the market-share claim or the price assumption is off — MISSING DATA #2]. US$350m at 5.375%, refinancing the 2022 notes at 5.5%; US$2m bought back at ~80 cents in 2022 when the market priced the trough; collar hedges with Credit Suisse and DBS, novated to UBS in Feb 2024 after Credit Suisse failed; a second US$100m layer in Aug 2025. The Santosa family (81.67%) bought out Japfa Ltd's minorities at S$0.62 and delisted it on 3 Jun 2025. JPFA's disclosure says "no effect on management or operations". # 10 — RED FLAG

## 10.1 The parent's trading arm on the other side of the purchase ledger
Rp7.0tn of FY2025 raw-material purchases (Rp9.5tn in 2022) — 19–28% of everything bought — from Annona Pte Ltd, wholly owned by the family's private holding, on a margin "capped at 5% p.a." of an undefined base, with no arm's-length statement in the audited notes and Rp2.1–2.8tn owed to Annona at every year-end at 90–127 days. The transfer-pricing local file covers 19 group entities and is prepared by the statutory auditor (Rp1.2bn fee). Money flow: 1% of margin = Rp70bn a year to the private parent, in USD, before the 55.43% dividend share. What would settle it: Annona's own accounts (Singapore, UEN 200920521R) and the actual margin charged.

## 10.2 Six related-party leases and insurances with no amounts
Office and parking from PT Omega Propertindo, insurance from PT Pan Pacific Indonesia (founded by Commissioner Hendrick Kolonas) through a related broker, a Four Seasons apartment, a villa and a boat — all "same key management personnel", all disclosed by name in every year, none quantified. Small in all likelihood; the pattern is the point.

## 10.3 A US$350m maturity the filings walk up to and then go silent on
The whole Rp5.83tn was current at 31 Dec 2025, 55% pre-funded, and the notes contain no subsequent-events disclosure on a bond that fell due five weeks after the audit report was signed (28 Feb 2026). Six months later gross debt is Rp4.4tn higher and the identifiable uses do not explain it (§7.2). Until the 1H2026 interim statements are in hand, the capital structure of this company is not known.

## 10.4 Profit that arrives through a 49% JV financed by the listco
Griya Mitra Poultry (§9.8): a seven-month-old JV with an individual generating Rp61bn of profit on Rp15bn of capital, buying Rp338bn from JPFA on credit. Either it is an exceptionally good poultry farm, or JPFA's sales to it carry the margin and the 51% partner carries the paper. Terms, the partner's identity beyond a name, and why 49%, are not disclosed.

## 10.5 Rp275bn of 2% Danantara bonds bought at a 20% loss
Five- and seven-year private-placement paper from the state investment agency's manager, bought in Oct–Dec 2025 at par when fair value was Rp220bn; the Rp55bn day-one loss is being amortised, not expensed. No purpose stated. It is the first appearance of the state as a financial counterparty in JPFA's accounts, in the year the state's free-meals programme became the demand driver and its GPS quota decision the supply driver.

## 10.6 Segment profit shown as "operating profit" and the segment map that never updates
The decks' "operating profit" excludes other expenses (Rp134bn of write-offs in 2024) and the bio-asset line; the segmental chart sums only the four poultry segments (Rp5,715bn for FY2024) beside a consolidated Rp5,213bn without saying so; the pie's "total revenue" switched from net sales to gross segment sales (Rp80.7tn) in FY2023 without a footnote; market shares cite a 2021 study for five years then "2026 data"; two countries' per-capita consumption halved between decks.
